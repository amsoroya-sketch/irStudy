# Copyright Risk Analysis: Textbook-Based MCQ/OSCE Generation

## ⚠️ CRITICAL LEGAL WARNING

**Using copyrighted medical textbooks to generate MCQs and OSCEs creates substantial legal risk.** While your approach has some protective elements, you are operating in a legal gray area that requires careful navigation.

---

## 1. THE CORE LEGAL PROBLEM

### What You Did
```
Medical Textbooks (Copyrighted)
         ↓
   Extracted Content
         ↓
   AI Processing
         ↓
   Generated MCQs/OSCEs
         ↓
   Commercial Platform
```

### Why This Is Problematic

| Issue | Explanation | Risk Level |
|-------|-------------|------------|
| **Derivative Work** | Creating questions from textbook content may constitute a derivative work under copyright law | 🔴 HIGH |
| **Substantial Reproduction** | Medical facts can't be copyrighted, but the *expression* (how concepts are explained, case presentations) can be | 🔴 HIGH |
| **Commercial Use** | Selling content derived from textbooks strengthens copyright holder's case against you | 🔴 HIGH |
| **Originality Threshold** | If questions closely mirror textbook structure/cases, infringement is more likely | 🟡 MEDIUM |

### Australian Copyright Act 1968 (Cth)

**Relevant Sections:**
- **Section 31**: Exclusive rights of copyright owner (reproduction, adaptation)
- **Section 36**: Infringement by doing acts comprised in copyright
- **Section 103A**: Fair dealing for purpose of research or study (LIMITED)

**Key Problem**: Fair dealing in Australia is much narrower than US "fair use." Commercial educational use has very limited protection.

## 2. SPECIFIC RISK FACTORS

### High-Risk Elements in Your Current Approach

```
❌ HIGH RISK:
├── Direct case adaptation from textbooks
│   Example: "A 45-year-old male presents with chest pain..."
│   If this matches a textbook case presentation → RISK
│
├── Using textbook-specific explanations
│   Example: Copying the pedagogical approach or structure
│

│   Even if cited, reproducing copyrighted images is infringement
│
├── Using "recall" questions from actual exams
│   AMC recalls are technically AMC's property
│
└── Creating questions from subscription content
    Example: Therapeutic Guidelines (eTG) requires subscription
    Using their content commercially may violate terms of service
```

### Lower-Risk Elements

```
✅ LOWER RISK:
├── Medical facts and knowledge
│   "Paracetamol is first-line for mild pain" = fact (not copyrightable)
│
├── General clinical scenarios
│   Common presentations are factual, not creative expression
│
├── Citing sources (with proper attribution)
│   Shows good faith, though doesn't eliminate liability
│
├── Creating entirely novel scenarios
│   Not derived from any specific textbook case
│
└── Using open-access sources
    StatPearls, Cochrane (CC BY licenses)
```

## 4. FAIR DEALING ANALYSIS (Australia)

### What Fair Dealing Allows

Under Australian law, fair dealing permits limited use of copyrighted material for:
- Research or study
- Criticism or review
- Parody or satire
- Reporting news
- Giving professional advice (by lawyer/doctor)

### Why Your Use Likely DOESN'T Qualify

```
Fair Dealing Test:
─────────────────────────────────────────────────
Purpose: Educational? ✅
        But COMMERCIAL educational use is borderline
        
Nature of Work: Factual/Medical? ✅
        Factual works get less protection, but still protected
        
Amount Used: Substantial?
        If you're extracting key teaching points → Likely YES
        
Effect on Market: Does it compete with textbook sales?
        If users buy your platform instead of books → YES ❌
        
─────────────────────────────────────────────────
VERDICT: UNLIKELY TO QUALIFY FOR FAIR DEALING
```

### Comparison: US Fair Use (For Reference)

In the US, "fair use" is more flexible but still risky:
- Transformative use helps
- Commercial use hurts
- Using "heart of the work" hurts
- Effect on market is key factor

**Even under US fair use, commercial test prep using textbook content has resulted in lawsuits.**

**Risk Level:** 🟡 MEDIUM

### Scenario D: Image Reproduction

Using textbook images (even with citations):

**Risk Level:** 🔴 CRITICAL
- Images are strongly protected
- Reproducing without license is clear infringement
