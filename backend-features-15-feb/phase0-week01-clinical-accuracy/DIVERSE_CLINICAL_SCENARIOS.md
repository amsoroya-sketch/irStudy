**Created**: 2026-02-15
**Target Users**: Medical students, Backend developers, Clinical Advisor

## Overview

This document provides 3 diverse clinical scenarios for AMC Clinical Exam preparation:

1. **Aboriginal/Torres Strait Islander**: COPD exacerbation with cultural considerations
2. **CALD Patient**: Mental health presentation with interpreter needs
3. **Obstetric Emergency**: Severe pre-eclampsia requiring urgent management

Each scenario demonstrates:
- Cultural competence requirements
- Australian healthcare system context
- Communication strategies
- Management pathways aligned with Australian guidelines
- Scoring examples using AMC 15-mark rubric

## Scenario 1: Aboriginal Patient - COPD Exacerbation

### Clinical Context Card (Student View)

**Patient**: Uncle Billy Williams
**Age**: 65 years
**Gender**: Male
**Ethnicity**: Aboriginal Australian (Wiradjuri Nation)
**Location**: Rural NSW Emergency Department

**Presenting Complaint**: "I can't catch my breath"

**Vital Signs**:
- BP: 145/88 mmHg
- HR: 105 bpm
- RR: 28/min
- SpO2: 88% on room air
- Temp: 37.8°C

**Task**: Take a focused history and formulate an initial management plan (8 minutes)

---

### Full Scenario Details

**History of Presenting Complaint**:
- Increased shortness of breath over 3 days
- Productive cough with green sputum
- Unable to walk from bedroom to kitchen without stopping
- Usually manages 50 meters on flat ground
- No chest pain, no fever until today

**Past Medical History**:
- COPD (diagnosed 10 years ago, spirometry confirmed)
- Type 2 diabetes (HbA1c 8.2% last check)
- Hypertension
- Previous MI 5 years ago

**Medications**:
- Salbutamol inhaler PRN (uses daily)
- Tiotropium (Spiriva) once daily (sometimes forgets)
- Metformin 1g BD
- Aspirin 100mg daily
- Perindopril 4mg daily

**Family History**:
- Father died of "lung problems" age 58
- Mother died of diabetes complications age 62

**Social History**:
- Lives in small town 80km from regional hospital
- Lives with wife in housing commission flat
- Former shearer, retired 10 years ago
- Smoking: 40 pack-years (started age 15, quit 2 years ago)
- Alcohol: Denies current use, previous heavy use in 30s-40s
- Transport: No car, relies on community health transport

**Social Determinants & Barriers**:
- Nearest pharmacy 50km away
- Limited access to GP (bulk-billing clinic 2 days/week)
- Medication costs a barrier (uses Closing the Gap exemption but doesn't always fill scripts)
- Previous negative experiences with healthcare system
- Strong family/community connections but remote location

**Examination Findings**:
- Alert, speaking in short sentences
- Using accessory muscles of respiration
- Bilateral expiratory wheeze throughout chest
- Prolonged expiratory phase
- No cyanosis
- Mild peripheral edema

**Investigation Results** (if ordered):
- **ABG**: pH 7.38, pCO2 48, pO2 58, HCO3 28 (type 2 respiratory failure)
- **CXR**: Hyperinflated lungs, no consolidation, old RLL scarring
- **ECG**: Sinus tachycardia, no acute ischemia
- **FBC**: WCC 13.5 (neutrophils 11.2)
- **CRP**: 45

---

### Cultural Safety Considerations

**Cultural Competence Requirements** (MANDATORY for ≥2/2 Professionalism Score):

1. **Respectful Communication**:
   - Use title "Uncle Billy" if patient prefers (many Aboriginal elders use Uncle/Aunty)
   - Allow extra time for responses (don't rush)
   - Understand communication style may be less direct

2. **Social Determinants Acknowledgment** (Non-Judgmental):
   - ✅ "I understand getting to the pharmacy can be difficult from your town. Let's see what we can do to make this easier."
   - ✅ "Are there any barriers to taking your medications regularly? Cost? Transport?"
   - ❌ "Why don't you just take your medications as prescribed?" (Judgmental - auto-fail professionalism)

3. **Offer Aboriginal Health Services**:
   - "Would you like me to ask an Aboriginal Health Worker to come chat with you?"
   - "Do you have family here or would you like us to contact anyone?"

4. **Closing the Gap PBS Awareness**:
   - "Your medications should be free under the Closing the Gap program. Let's make sure that's set up correctly."

5. **Discharge Planning Considerations**:
   - Transport barriers (80km from home)
   - Follow-up access (GP only 2 days/week)
   - Community support networks
   - Aboriginal health service involvement

**What Constitutes Cultural Safety FAILURE** (Auto-Fail Professionalism):
- Judgmental language about medication adherence, alcohol use, housing situation
- Assumptions about lifestyle without asking
- Dismissive of transport/cost barriers
- Doesn't offer Aboriginal Health Worker when appropriate
- Stereotyping (assuming alcohol use without evidence)

---

### Management Pathway (Australian Guidelines)

**Immediate Management** (eTG Respiratory, COPD Exacerbation):

1. **Oxygen Therapy**:
   - Target SpO2 88-92% (controlled oxygen for COPD, not 94-98%)
   - "Uncle Billy, I'm going to give you some oxygen through these nasal prongs to help your breathing"

2. **Bronchodilators**:
   - Salbutamol 5mg + ipratropium bromide 500mcg via nebulizer
   - "I'll give you a stronger dose of your inhaler through this machine"

3. **Corticosteroids**:
   - Prednisolone 30-50mg PO (or IV hydrocortisone if can't swallow)
   - Check blood glucose (diabetic patient)

4. **Antibiotics** (green sputum + elevated WCC/CRP):
   - Amoxicillin 500mg TDS x 5 days OR
   - Doxycycline 100mg BD x 5 days (if penicillin allergy)
   - As per eTG: COPD exacerbation with purulent sputum

**Investigations**:
- ABG (assess for type 2 respiratory failure)
- CXR (exclude pneumonia/pneumothorax)
- ECG (cardiac history)
- FBC, CRP, U&E (infection markers, renal function before antibiotics)
- Blood glucose (diabetic + steroids)

**Admission Decision**:
- Likely admission (type 2 respiratory failure, unable to manage at home 80km away)
- Medical team referral
- Consider ICU if deteriorates

**Discharge Planning** (if admission not required):
- Aboriginal health service referral for medication support
- Community health transport for follow-up
- GP appointment within 48 hours (arrange through hospital)
- Ensure medications filled before discharge (Closing the Gap PBS)
- Provide action plan for worsening symptoms

**Safety-Netting**:
- "If your breathing gets worse, you feel confused, or you're too breathless to speak, call 000 immediately"
- "Given how far you live, it's safer for you to stay in hospital tonight so we can watch your breathing"

---

### AMC 15-Mark Rubric Application

**Excellent Response Example (14/15)**:

**Communication (3/3)**:
> "Good morning Uncle Billy, my name is Sarah and I'm a medical student. Thank you for coming in today. I can see you're having trouble with your breathing - that must be really frightening. Is it okay if I ask you some questions about what's been happening?"
>
> [Uses SOCRATES for dyspnea, shows empathy, allows time for responses]
>
> "I understand you live about 80 kilometers from here. How did you get to hospital today? Do you have anyone here with you?"
>
> "Getting medications from the pharmacy must be difficult from [town name]. Do you have any problems getting your inhalers regularly?"
>
> [Offers Aboriginal Health Worker involvement without being patronizing]

**Clinical Reasoning (4/4)**:
> "Based on your symptoms and examination, I believe you're having an exacerbation of your COPD - your chronic lung condition is flaring up, likely triggered by a chest infection given the green sputum. The main concerns are:
>
> 1. **COPD exacerbation with infective component** (most likely) - green sputum, fever, increased breathlessness beyond baseline
> 2. **Possible pneumonia** - need chest X-ray to exclude
> 3. **Cardiac causes** - you have a history of heart attack, so need to check your heart isn't contributing
>
> The concerning features are your oxygen levels (88% is lower than we'd like) and breathing rate (28 is quite fast). We need to treat this urgently."

**Information Gathering (3/3)**:
- Systematic SOCRATES for dyspnea
- Baseline functional status ("How far can you usually walk?")
- Medication adherence barriers explored non-judgmentally
- Social history including transport and access barriers
- Red flag screening (chest pain, confusion, worsening)

**Management (2/3)**: (Lost 1 mark for minor gap)
> "Here's what we need to do right now:
>
> 1. I'll give you oxygen to help your breathing - we'll aim for about 88-92% on the monitor
> 2. You'll get a nebulizer with salbutamol and ipratropium - stronger versions of your inhaler
> 3. I'll give you prednisolone 50mg to reduce inflammation in your lungs
> 4. You need antibiotics for the chest infection - amoxicillin 500mg three times a day for 5 days
> 5. We'll do a blood gas test from your wrist, chest X-ray, and some blood tests
>
> I think you'll need to stay in hospital tonight so we can monitor your breathing. The medical team will review you soon.
>
> Your medications should all be free under Closing the Gap - I'll make sure that's sorted before you go home."
>
> **Gap**: Didn't explicitly ask about Aboriginal Health Worker involvement (mentioned cultural considerations but didn't offer AHW)

**Professionalism (2/2)**:
- Used respectful title ("Uncle Billy")
- Non-judgmental about medication adherence
- Acknowledged transport barriers without minimizing
- Maintained dignity and cultural safety throughout

**Total: 14/15 (93%) - Excellent Pass**

---

**Pass Response Example (10/15)**:

**Communication (2/3)**:
- Adequate introduction and rapport
- Some empathy but could be more patient-centered
- Mix of open and closed questions
- Explained plan but used some jargon

**Clinical Reasoning (3/4)**:
> "This sounds like a COPD exacerbation, possibly with a chest infection. I'd want to rule out pneumonia as well."
> [Good core reasoning but less detailed, didn't mention cardiac differential]

**Information Gathering (2/3)**:
- Used SOCRATES but missed 2 components
- Asked about baseline function and medications
- Didn't explore social barriers in depth (asked about transport but not cost/access issues)

**Management (2/3)**:
> "I'll give you oxygen, nebulizers, steroids, and antibiotics. We'll do a chest X-ray and blood tests."
> [Correct core management but lacked detail on specific doses, didn't mention Closing the Gap, basic safety-netting]

**Professionalism (1/2)**:
- Professional overall but didn't offer Aboriginal Health Worker
- Didn't acknowledge social determinants explicitly
- No cultural safety issues but missed opportunity for cultural competence

**Total: 10/15 (67%) - Borderline Pass**

---

**Fail Response Example (6/15 + Cultural Safety Failure = Auto-Fail)**:

**Communication (1/3)**: Minimal empathy, rushed
**Clinical Reasoning (2/4)**: "Probably a chest infection" (vague)
**Information Gathering (1/3)**: Incomplete history
**Management (2/3)**: Basic plan without detail
**Professionalism (0/2)**: **CULTURAL SAFETY FAILURE**

**Critical Error**:
> Student says: "You need to take your medications properly. If you just used your inhalers every day like you're supposed to, you wouldn't be here."

**Auto-Fail Rationale**: Judgmental language that doesn't acknowledge social determinants (transport, cost barriers) = cultural safety violation = auto-fail professionalism domain

**Total: Auto-Fail** (despite 6/15 score on other domains)

## Scenario 2: CALD Patient - Mental Health Presentation

### Clinical Context Card

**Patient**: Mei Chen
**Age**: 42 years
**Gender**: Female
**Ethnicity**: Chinese Australian (Mandarin-speaking, minimal English)
**Location**: GP clinic, metropolitan Melbourne

**Presenting Complaint**: Daughter translates: "Mum hasn't been sleeping and seems very worried"

**Task**: Take a history through an interpreter and formulate initial management plan (10 minutes - includes interpreter setup time)

**Note**: Professional interpreter service available (TIS National 131 450)

---

### Full Scenario Details

**History** (obtained through interpreter):

**Presenting Complaint**:
- 6 weeks of poor sleep (3-4 hours/night)
- Constant worrying, can't "turn off" thoughts
- Loss of appetite, 5kg weight loss
- Fatigue, difficulty concentrating
- No longer enjoys cooking (previous main activity)
- Hasn't left house in 2 weeks except for groceries

**Anxiety Symptoms**:
- Palpitations, trembling hands
- Feeling "on edge" constantly
- Muscle tension (neck/shoulders)
- Difficulty breathing (feels like chest is tight)

**Depression Screening**:
- Low mood most days
- Anhedonia (lost interest in activities)
- Feels hopeless about future
- Denies suicidal ideation when asked directly

**Triggering Event** (requires sensitive questioning):
- Son moved out 2 months ago (got married)
- Feels she "failed as a mother"
- Worries about son, daughter-in-law, future grandchildren
- Husband works long hours (6 days/week), limited support

**Past Psychiatric History**:
- No previous mental health treatment
- Feels "shame" about seeing doctor for this
- Believes mental illness is weakness

**Past Medical History**:
- Hypertension (controlled on amlodipine)
- No other chronic conditions

**Medications**:
- Amlodipine 5mg daily
- No other regular medications

**Family History**:
- Mother had "nervous problems" (untreated)
- No known psychiatric diagnoses in family (stigma around discussing mental health)

**Social History**:
- Migrated from China 15 years ago
- Lives with husband in suburban Melbourne
- Limited English (can say greetings, numbers, but not complex conversation)
- Two adult children (son 25, daughter 22)
- No employment outside home (was accountant in China, qualifications not recognized)
- Isolated - no close friends in Australia, family all in China
- Hasn't spoken to extended family about this (feels ashamed)

**Cultural Context**:
- Traditional Chinese beliefs about mental health:
  - Mental illness = loss of face for family
  - "Just need to try harder" / "be strong"
  - Fear of stigma in Chinese community
- Somatization common (presents with physical symptoms - sleep, appetite - rather than emotional)
- Family-centered (son leaving = existential crisis about role/purpose)
- Medication concerns (prefers "natural" remedies, worried about addiction)

---

### Cultural Safety Considerations

**MANDATORY: Professional Interpreter Use**

**Correct Approach** (Required for pass):
1. Recognize language barrier immediately
2. Offer professional interpreter: "I can see English isn't your first language. I'd like to arrange a Mandarin interpreter so we can understand each other properly. This is a free service and everything will be confidential. Is that okay with you?"
3. Use TIS National (131 450) or on-site interpreter
4. Don't rely on daughter to interpret (confidentiality, family dynamics, medical terminology)

**Interpreter Best Practice**:
- Speak directly to patient (not interpreter): "How are you feeling?" NOT "Ask her how she's feeling"
- Short phrases, allow time for interpretation
- Avoid jargon
- Check understanding frequently

**Auto-Fail if**:
- Proceeds with mental health assessment without offering interpreter
- Uses daughter as interpreter for sensitive mental health discussion
- Says "We don't have time for an interpreter" / "It's too complicated"

**Cultural Competence Requirements**:

1. **Normalize Mental Health**:
   - ✅ "Many people feel this way when there are big changes in their life. It's very common and nothing to be ashamed of."
   - ✅ "This is a medical condition, like high blood pressure or diabetes. It's not weakness."
   - ❌ "You just need to think more positively" (Dismissive - fails professionalism)

2. **Acknowledge Cultural Context**:
   - ✅ "I understand in many cultures there's stigma around mental health. I want you to know this conversation is completely confidential."
   - ✅ "It's understandable you're adjusting to your son leaving - family is very important in Chinese culture."

3. **Address Medication Concerns**:
   - ✅ "I know you might have concerns about medication. The medications we use today are not addictive and can really help. We can start with counseling first if you prefer."
   - ✅ "Would you be open to talking with a counselor who speaks Mandarin?"

4. **Family Involvement** (with consent):
   - ✅ "Would you like your husband to be involved in your care? We can arrange a family meeting with the interpreter."
   - ❌ "I'll just tell your daughter the diagnosis and she can explain to you" (Breaches confidentiality and autonomy)

---

### Management Pathway

**Immediate Management**:

1. **Professional Interpreter** (FIRST STEP):
   - TIS National: 131 450
   - Request Mandarin interpreter
   - Document interpreter used in notes

2. **Mental Health Assessment**:
   - Depression: PHQ-9 or Kessler K10 (culturally validated tools)
   - Anxiety: GAD-7
   - Suicide risk assessment (essential despite stigma)
   - Functional impairment assessment

3. **Diagnosis**:
   - Likely: Major Depressive Disorder (moderate) + Generalized Anxiety Disorder
   - Adjustment Disorder possible (triggered by son leaving)

4. **Management Plan** (Stepped Care Approach):

**Step 1: Psychoeducation & Lifestyle**
- Explain depression/anxiety in culturally appropriate way
- Sleep hygiene
- Physical activity (walking program)
- Social connection (CALD community groups)

**Step 2: Psychological Therapy** (FIRST LINE per RACGP):
- Referral to Mandarin-speaking psychologist
- Mental Health Care Plan (GP Mental Health Treatment Plan - Medicare rebate for 10 sessions)
- Access to Mental Health (ATaPS) program if available

**Step 3: Pharmacotherapy** (if moderate-severe):
- SSRIs (Sertraline or Escitalopram - first line per eTG Mental Health)
- Start low: Sertraline 25mg daily, increase to 50mg after 1 week
- Explain: Not addictive, takes 2-4 weeks to work, can stop when stable
- Address cultural concerns about medication

5. **Referrals**:
   - **Psychologist**: Mandarin-speaking (search via APS "Find a Psychologist")
   - **Community Support**: CALD support groups, Lifeline (13 11 14 - interpreter available)
   - **Mental Health Crisis**: If deteriorates, Emergency Department or Mental Health Crisis Team

6. **Follow-Up**:
   - Review in 1-2 weeks (monitor medication response, suicide risk)
   - Book interpreter for follow-up appointments
   - Give written information in Mandarin (Beyond Blue resources available in multiple languages)

**Safety-Netting**:
> "If you start having thoughts of harming yourself, please call 000 or go to the emergency department immediately. They have interpreters available 24/7. You can also call Lifeline on 13 11 14 - just say 'Mandarin' and they'll connect you to an interpreter."

**Australian Guidelines**:
- RACGP: Guidelines for Preventive Activities in General Practice - Mental Health
- Beyond Blue: Clinical Practice Guidelines for Depression
- Black Dog Institute: Culturally and Linguistically Diverse Communities resources

---

### AMC 15-Mark Rubric Application

**Excellent Response (13/15)**:

**Communication (3/3)**:
> "Hello Ms Chen, my name is Dr. Lee. I can see English isn't your first language. Would it be okay if I arrange a Mandarin interpreter so we can talk properly? This is a free service and everything we discuss will be completely confidential - only between you, me, and the interpreter."
>
> [Waits for TIS interpreter to call back, doesn't proceed without interpreter]
>
> [Through interpreter, speaking directly to patient]:
> "Thank you for waiting. Can you tell me what's been happening that's brought you here today?"
>
> [Shows empathy]: "That sounds really difficult. Many people feel this way when there are big changes in family life."
>
> [Normalizes mental health]: "What you're experiencing is very common. It's a medical condition, not a sign of weakness."

**Clinical Reasoning (3/4)**:
> "Based on what you've told me through the interpreter, I believe you're experiencing depression and anxiety. The symptoms - poor sleep, low mood, loss of interest in activities you used to enjoy, constant worry - are consistent with this. The timing after your son left suggests this was a triggering event."
> [Lost 1 mark: Didn't explicitly mention differential diagnoses like thyroid disorder or other organic causes]

**Information Gathering (3/3)**:
- Used interpreter appropriately throughout
- Systematic mental health assessment (depression + anxiety symptoms)
- Asked about suicide ideation sensitively
- Explored cultural beliefs and medication concerns
- Assessed functional impact

**Management (2/3)**:
> "I'd like to create a mental health care plan for you. This means you can see a psychologist who speaks Mandarin - Medicare will help pay for 10 sessions. I can also prescribe medication if you're comfortable with that, but we can start with counseling first if you prefer."
> [Explained options, involved patient in decision-making, culturally sensitive]
> [Minor gap: Didn't mention specific medication names or dosing]

**Professionalism (2/2)**:
- Used professional interpreter (MANDATORY)
- Maintained confidentiality (didn't use daughter)
- Culturally sensitive throughout
- Normalized mental health to reduce stigma

**Total: 13/15 (87%) - Excellent Pass**

---

**Fail Response with Auto-Fail (3/15 + Interpreter Failure)**:

**Critical Error**:
Student says to daughter: "Can you just translate for me? It's easier than calling the interpreter service."

[Proceeds to ask mental health questions with daughter interpreting, including suicide risk assessment in front of family member]

**Auto-Fail Rationale**:
- Breaches confidentiality (family member present for sensitive mental health discussion)
- Inappropriate to ask daughter to translate suicide risk questions
- Cultural safety failure (doesn't recognize complexity of family dynamics)
- Professional boundary violation

## Scenario 3: Obstetric Emergency - Severe Pre-Eclampsia

### Clinical Context Card

**Patient**: Sarah Thompson
**Age**: 28 years
**Gender**: Female
**Obstetric Status**: G2P1, 34 weeks gestation

**Presenting Complaint**: "I have a terrible headache and my vision is blurry"

**Vital Signs**:
- BP: 170/110 mmHg (severe hypertension)
- HR: 95 bpm
- RR: 18/min
- SpO2: 98% on room air
- Temp: 37.2°C

**Urine Dipstick** (done by midwife):
- Protein: 3+

**Task**: Assess patient and formulate urgent management plan (8 minutes)

---

### Full Scenario Details

**History of Presenting Complaint**:
- Headache started 6 hours ago, now 8/10 severity
- "Throbbing" across forehead and back of head
- Visual disturbances: "Flashing lights" and blurry vision for 2 hours
- Epigastric pain: 6/10, constant, started 1 hour ago
- Nausea, no vomiting
- Swelling of face and hands (noticed this morning - "couldn't get rings off")
- Fetal movements reduced (normally very active, today only 3-4 movements)

**Obstetric History**:
- **Current Pregnancy** (G2P1 at 34 weeks):
  - Uncomplicated until this presentation
  - All antenatal checks normal until 32 weeks
  - BP at 32 weeks: 130/85 (borderline, was monitoring)
  - No protein in urine at previous checks
  - Anomaly scan normal at 20 weeks
  - Due date: 6 weeks from now

- **Previous Pregnancy**:
  - Uncomplicated vaginal delivery 3 years ago
  - Healthy baby girl, now 3 years old
  - No pre-eclampsia or complications

**Past Medical History**:
- No chronic medical conditions
- No previous hypertension

**Medications**:
- Pregnancy multivitamin only
- No other medications

**Allergies**: None known

**Family History**:
- Mother had "high blood pressure in pregnancy" (pre-eclampsia likely)

**Social History**:
- Married, supportive husband (waiting outside)
- Lives 10 minutes from hospital
- Works as primary school teacher (currently on maternity leave)
- Non-smoker, no alcohol, no illicit drugs

**Examination Findings**:
- **General**: Alert, appears uncomfortable
- **BP**: 170/110 mmHg (confirmed on repeat)
- **Reflexes**: Brisk (3+), clonus present (4 beats) - suggests hyperreflexia
- **Edema**: Face (periorbital), hands, pitting edema ankles
- **Abdomen**:
  - Gravid uterus, size consistent with dates
  - Fundal height 32cm (appropriate)
  - Single fetus, cephalic presentation
  - Fetal heart rate: 145 bpm (slightly reduced variability on CTG)
- **Epigastric tenderness**: Present (liver capsule distension)

**Red Flags Present** (CRITICAL):
1. **Severe hypertension**: BP ≥160/110
2. **Headache** (cerebral edema)
3. **Visual disturbances** (posterior reversible encephalopathy syndrome risk)
4. **Epigastric pain** (liver involvement - HELLP syndrome concern)
5. **Proteinuria** 3+ (significant)
6. **Reduced fetal movements** (placental insufficiency)

**Diagnosis**: **Severe Pre-Eclampsia** (34 weeks gestation)

**Differential Diagnoses**:
- HELLP syndrome (need LFTs, platelets urgently)
- Eclampsia (if seizures develop - imminent risk given symptoms)
- Placental abruption (if develops abdominal pain/bleeding)

---

### Management Pathway (Obstetric Emergency)

**IMMEDIATE ACTIONS** (Time-Critical):

1. **Call for Help**:
   - "I need the obstetric team to see this patient urgently - this is severe pre-eclampsia at 34 weeks"
   - Obstetric registrar + consultant obstetrician
   - Anesthetics team (in case emergency cesarean needed)

2. **Maternal Stabilization**:

**Blood Pressure Management** (Target <160/110 to prevent stroke):
- **Hydralazine IV**: 5mg slow IV bolus, repeat every 20 min PRN (first line per RANZCOG)
- OR **Labetalol IV**: 20mg IV, double dose every 10 min (max 300mg) if hydralazine unavailable
- Continuous BP monitoring (every 15 minutes)

**Seizure Prophylaxis** (MANDATORY for severe pre-eclampsia):
- **Magnesium sulfate IV**:
  - Loading dose: 4g IV over 5-10 minutes
  - Maintenance: 1g/hour infusion
  - Monitor reflexes (loss of reflexes = toxicity), respiratory rate (>16/min), urine output (>25ml/hr)
  - Have calcium gluconate ready (antidote if MgSO4 toxicity)

3. **Urgent Investigations**:
- **Bloods**:
  - FBC (platelets - HELLP syndrome, platelets <100 = concern)
  - LFTs (AST/ALT - HELLP syndrome, elevated = severe)
  - U&Es (renal function, creatinine >90 = concern)
  - Coagulation (DIC risk if HELLP)
  - LDH (HELLP syndrome marker)
  - Group & hold (in case bleeding/delivery)
- **Urine**: Protein:creatinine ratio (quantify proteinuria)
- **Fetal Monitoring**: Continuous CTG (assess fetal wellbeing)

4. **Fetal Assessment**:
- Continuous CTG monitoring
- Ultrasound: Estimated fetal weight, amniotic fluid index, Doppler (placental flow)
- Assess for fetal distress (requires emergency delivery if present)

5. **Definitive Management**:
- **Delivery is the ONLY cure for pre-eclampsia**
- **Timing**: 34 weeks gestation = viable but premature
- Decision by obstetric team:
  - If maternal/fetal condition stable after stabilization: Corticosteroids for fetal lung maturity, deliver within 24-48 hours
  - If maternal/fetal condition deteriorating: Emergency cesarean section
- **Corticosteroids** (if time permits before delivery):
  - Betamethasone 12mg IM, repeat in 24 hours
  - Improves fetal lung maturity (34 weeks = preterm)

6. **Multidisciplinary Involvement**:
- Obstetrics (lead team)
- Anesthetics (epidural/spinal for cesarean if needed)
- Neonatology (34-week baby will need NICU)
- ICU (maternal if severe complications)

**Monitoring**:
- Continuous BP monitoring
- Hourly urine output (strict I/O)
- 4-hourly bloods (platelets, LFTs, creatinine)
- Continuous CTG
- Reflexes (MgSO4 toxicity monitoring)

**Safety-Netting** (for patient and family):
> "Sarah, you have severe pre-eclampsia, which means your blood pressure is very high due to the pregnancy. This is serious and we need to stabilize your blood pressure and monitor you and baby very closely. You'll likely need to have the baby delivered in the next 24-48 hours to keep you both safe."
>
> "I'm giving you medication to lower your blood pressure and prevent seizures. The obstetric team is on their way and they'll make the decisions about delivery timing with you."
>
> "If you develop a seizure, severe abdominal pain, heavy bleeding, or baby's movements stop, we'll need to deliver immediately."

**Australian Guidelines**:
- RANZCOG (Royal Australian and New Zealand College of Obstetricians and Gynaecologists): Hypertension in Pregnancy
- Queensland Clinical Guidelines: Hypertensive Disorders of Pregnancy
- NSW Health: Maternal & Neonatal Service Networks - Pre-eclampsia

---

### AMC 15-Mark Rubric Application

**Excellent Response (15/15)**:

**Communication (3/3)**:
> "Hello Sarah, my name is Dr. Emma. I can see you're in pain with this headache. I'm going to ask you some questions and then we'll get you feeling better. The midwife told me your blood pressure is quite high and there's protein in your urine - I'm concerned about pre-eclampsia, which is a serious pregnancy complication. I need to assess you quickly. Is that okay?"
>
> [After assessment]: "Sarah, based on your symptoms - the severe headache, vision changes, high blood pressure, and protein in your urine - you have severe pre-eclampsia. This is serious and means we need to lower your blood pressure urgently and monitor you and baby very closely. The obstetric team is on their way."
>
> [Involves patient]: "You'll likely need to have the baby delivered in the next day or two to keep you both safe. I know this is scary and earlier than expected. Do you have any questions?"

**Clinical Reasoning (4/4)**:
> "The diagnosis is severe pre-eclampsia based on:
> - BP 170/110 (≥160/110 = severe)
> - New-onset headache with visual disturbances (cerebral involvement)
> - Epigastric pain (liver capsule distension - concern for HELLP syndrome)
> - Proteinuria 3+
> - At 34 weeks gestation with no previous hypertension
>
> The concerning features making this an EMERGENCY:
> - Severe range blood pressure (stroke risk)
> - CNS symptoms (headache, vision changes) - risk of eclampsia (seizures)
> - Possible HELLP syndrome (need urgent bloods)
> - Reduced fetal movements (placental insufficiency)
>
> My differentials:
> 1. Severe pre-eclampsia (most likely)
> 2. HELLP syndrome (need LFTs, platelets to rule in/out)
> 3. Imminent eclampsia (very high risk given symptoms)
>
> This requires IMMEDIATE obstetric team involvement and likely delivery within 24-48 hours."

**Information Gathering (3/3)**:
- Focused obstetric history (G, P, gestational age, previous pregnancies)
- Pre-eclampsia red flag symptoms systematically screened:
  - Headache severity, character
  - Visual disturbances
  - Epigastric pain
  - Fetal movements (crucial!)
  - Edema
- Examination: BP repeat, reflexes, edema, abdominal exam
- Recognizes time-sensitive emergency (doesn't waste time on extensive social history)

**Management (3/3)**:
> "I'm calling the obstetric team now - this is an emergency.

> 2. Start an IV and give you medication to lower your blood pressure - hydralazine 5mg IV
> 3. Give you magnesium sulfate IV to prevent seizures - this is standard for severe pre-eclampsia
> 4. Take urgent blood tests to check your liver, kidneys, platelets, and clotting

> The obstetric team will decide on the timing of delivery, but you'll likely need to have the baby in the next day or two. At 34 weeks, baby is viable but will need special care in the neonatal unit. We'll give you steroids to help baby's lungs mature before delivery if there's time.
