### 2. OSCE Stations (8-10 Stations)

#### History Taking Stations (4 stations)
1. **Chest Pain History**
   - Differentiate cardiac vs non-cardiac chest pain
   - Identify red flags (ACS features)
   - Risk stratification (HEART score)
   - Duration: 8 minutes

2. **Palpitations History**
   - Characterize palpitations (rate, rhythm, triggers)
   - Identify concerning features (syncope, family history)
   - Assess for reversible causes
   - Duration: 8 minutes

3. **Dyspnea History**
   - Differentiate cardiac vs respiratory vs other causes
   - Assess severity (NYHA classification)
   - Identify exacerbating factors
   - Duration: 8 minutes

4. **Syncope History**
   - Identify cardiac vs reflex vs orthostatic causes
   - Red flags requiring urgent investigation
   - Risk stratification
   - Duration: 8 minutes

#### Physical Examination Stations (3 stations)
5. **Cardiovascular Examination**
   - Systematic CVS examination
   - Identify murmurs, added sounds
   - Assess JVP, peripheral perfusion
   - Duration: 8 minutes

6. **Peripheral Vascular Examination**
   - Assess for peripheral arterial disease
   - Ankle-brachial index interpretation
   - Identify critical limb ischemia
   - Duration: 8 minutes

7. **ECG Interpretation**
   - Systematic ECG interpretation
   - Identify STEMI, arrhythmias, conduction blocks
   - Clinical correlation
   - Duration: 8 minutes

#### Communication Stations (2 stations)
8. **Explain Statin Therapy**
   - Discuss benefits and risks
   - Address common concerns (muscle aches)
   - Obtain informed consent
   - Duration: 8 minutes

9. **Breaking Bad News - MI Diagnosis**
   - Deliver diagnosis of myocardial infarction
   - Explain prognosis and treatment
   - Address patient concerns
   - Duration: 8 minutes

#### Emergency Management (1 station)
10. **ACS Management**
    - Recognize STEMI on ECG
    - Initiate emergency treatment (MONA-B)
    - Arrange urgent PCI
    - Duration: 8 minutes

## Quality Standards

### Clinical Accuracy
- **100% alignment with Therapeutic Guidelines**
- All management algorithms from eTG
- Drug doses from AMH
- Australian healthcare system context

### Citation Requirements
- **Minimum 2 references per question**
- Must include page numbers
- Primary source: Therapeutic Guidelines
- Secondary sources: Major textbooks, guidelines

### Australian Context Checklist
- ✅ Use Australian medication names (not US brand names)
  - Correct: "ticagrelor" not "brilinta"
  - Correct: "prasugrel" not "effient"
- ✅ Use Australian units (mmol/L not mg/dL)
- ✅ Reference PBS/MBS where relevant
- ✅ Use Australian healthcare system terminology
  - Emergency Department (not ER)
  - General Practitioner (not PCP)
  - Public hospital system

### Validation Checklist
Every question must pass:
- [ ] QA-001 automated validation
- [ ] Citation verification (all refs found with correct pages)
- [ ] Australian context check (100%)
- [ ] Clinical accuracy check (guideline-compliant)
- [ ] Question clarity (no ambiguity)
- [ ] Appropriate difficulty level

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid

### ❌ Don't Use US Guidelines
- **Wrong:** ACC/AHA guidelines
- **Correct:** Australian/ANZCA guidelines, Therapeutic Guidelines

### ❌ Don't Use US Medication Names
- **Wrong:** "Brilinta" (US brand name)
- **Correct:** "ticagrelor" (Australian generic name)

### ❌ Don't Use US Units
- **Wrong:** Cholesterol 200 mg/dL
- **Correct:** Cholesterol 5.2 mmol/L

### ❌ Don't Ignore Australian Healthcare System
- **Wrong:** "Refer to PCP"
- **Correct:** "Refer to General Practitioner"

### ❌ Don't Use Outdated Guidelines
- **Wrong:** "Give aspirin alone for NSTEMI"
- **Correct:** "Give aspirin + ticagrelor/prasugrel for NSTEMI"
