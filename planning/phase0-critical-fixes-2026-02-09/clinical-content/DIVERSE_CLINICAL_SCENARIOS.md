## Scenario 1: Aboriginal Patient - Community-Acquired Pneumonia

### Patient Demographics
- **Name**: David Namatjira
- **Age**: 48, Male
- **Occupation**: Unemployed (former mine worker)
- **Cultural Background**: Aboriginal Australian (Pitjantjatjara people)
- **Location**: Alice Springs, Northern Territory
- **Remote Area**: Yes
- **Preferred Language**: English (some Pitjantjatjara)

### Chief Complaint
"G'day doc, I've had this cough for nearly a week now and I'm feeling pretty crook. My missus told me I need to come in."

### Key Clinical Features
- **Presenting Symptoms**: Productive cough × 5 days, fever and rigors, shortness of breath on minimal exertion
- **Sputum**: Yellow-green, sometimes blood-streaked
- **Chest Pain**: Sharp right-sided pleuritic pain
- **Smoking**: 20 cigarettes/day × 33 years
- **Past Medical History**: Bronchiectasis (diagnosed 3 years ago), Type 2 diabetes (poorly controlled), Previous ICU admission for pneumonia
- **Medications**: Metformin 500mg BD (runs out often - can't afford), Ventolin inhaler, azithromycin prophylaxis stopped 4 months ago (too expensive)
- **Allergies**: Penicillin (rash as child)

### Social Determinants of Health
- **Barriers to Care**:
  - Transport issues (40km to hospital)
  - Medication cost barriers
  - Cultural mistrust of mainstream health system
- **Living Situation**: Community housing with partner and 3 kids
- **Support Network**: Strong family support, attends local Aboriginal Medical Service
- **Past Occupational Exposure**: Mine dust exposure (stopped 2 years ago)

### Red Flags
1. Bronchiectasis (high risk for severe CAP)
2. Previous ICU admission for pneumonia
3. Haemoptysis
4. Pleuritic chest pain (? empyema)
5. Immunocompromised (poorly controlled diabetes)

### Emotional Profile
- **Baseline State**: GUARDED_STOIC
- **Pain Level**: 6/10
- **Anxiety Level**: 8/10
- **Cultural Considerations**: May minimize symptoms (stoicism in Aboriginal culture), hesitant to engage with mainstream health system due to past experiences of racism, values family involvement in decisions, may use traditional healing practices alongside Western medicine
- **Trust Threshold**: 5/10

### Trust-Building Actions
- Acknowledging Aboriginal health inequities
- Asking about family and community support
- Explaining in plain language without condescension
- Asking permission before examination
- Offering to involve Aboriginal Health Worker

### Trust-Destroying Actions (Avoid)
- Judgmental comments about smoking or medication non-adherence
- Not acknowledging social determinants of health
- Rushing or appearing dismissive
- Not offering interpreter or Aboriginal liaison
- Making assumptions about alcohol use

### Key Differentials
1. **Community-acquired pneumonia** (most likely)
2. Bronchiectasis exacerbation with superimposed infection
3. Empyema (given pleuritic pain)
4. Pulmonary tuberculosis (high prevalence in Aboriginal communities)
5. Lung abscess

### Critical Actions (Australian Guidelines)
1. Order chest X-ray within 30 minutes (NSW Health CAP protocol)
2. Give oxygen to maintain SpO2 >92% (target 88-92% if COPD present)
3. Blood cultures BEFORE antibiotics
4. Start antibiotics within 4 hours (eTG: roxithromycin or doxycycline if penicillin allergic)
5. Check HbA1c and diabetes control
6. Involve Aboriginal Health Worker or Aboriginal Liaison Officer
7. Assess social situation and barriers to care
8. Arrange follow-up at Aboriginal Medical Service
9. Consider TB screening (high prevalence in Northern Australia Aboriginal communities)
10. PBS authority script for azithromycin prophylaxis (bronchiectasis indication)

### Australian Context - Aboriginal Health
- **Higher rates of bronchiectasis**: 10-20x general population
- **Social determinants**: Housing, poverty, remote location, cost barriers
- **Cultural safety**: Avoid judgmental language, involve Aboriginal health services
- **Closing the Gap initiatives**: PBS co-payment exemption for Aboriginal patients

### PBS Considerations
- Roxithromycin listed on PBS for CAP (no authority required)
- Azithromycin prophylaxis requires authority script for bronchiectasis
- Aboriginal patients exempt from PBS co-payment (Closing the Gap)

### RAG Citations
1. **(Therapeutic Guidelines: Antibiotic, Section 2.3.2, 2024: CAP treatment in penicillin-allergic patients)**
2. **(NSW Health Respiratory Infections Protocol, Section 4.1, 2024: CAP management)**
3. **(Talley & O'Connor's Clinical Examination, 8th ed, p.267-269: Bronchiectasis examination)**
4. **(Australian Institute of Health and Welfare, Aboriginal Health Report 2023, p.45: Bronchiectasis prevalence)**

### Difficulty Level
**Advanced** - Estimated pass rate: 45%

**Rationale**: Requires cultural competence, understanding social determinants of health, knowledge of Aboriginal health inequities, antibiotic allergy management, and complex social discharge planning. IMG students often struggle with Aboriginal health context.

## Scenario 2: CALD Patient - Postnatal Depression

### Patient Demographics
- **Name**: Fatima Hassan
- **Age**: 29, Female
- **Occupation**: Stay-at-home mother
- **Cultural Background**: Sudanese Australian (arrived 5 years ago as refugee)
- **Preferred Language**: Arabic (limited English)
- **Interpreter Required**: Yes (MANDATORY)

### Chief Complaint
"[Speaks slowly in heavily accented English] Doctor... I am very tired. Baby cry all time. I cannot sleep. I feel... [pauses, looks down] ... I feel no good mother."

### Key Clinical Features
- **Presenting Symptoms**: Overwhelming fatigue, feeling like a "bad mother", baby crying "all the time"
- **Mood**: "Very sad. I cry every day. Sometimes I wish... [trails off]"
- **Sleep**: Baby wakes 3-4 times/night. Even when baby sleeps, patient lies awake worrying
- **Appetite**: Lost 8kg since baby born 8 weeks ago (more than pregnancy weight)
- **Energy**: Cannot do housework or cook
- **Bonding**: "[Tearful] I love my baby but... I don't feel connection. I worry I will drop her."
- **Thoughts of Harm**: "[Long pause] Sometimes I think baby would be better without me. But I would never hurt her." (Passive suicidal ideation - no active plan)

### Obstetric History
- **First baby**: Born 8 weeks ago (vaginal delivery, no complications)
- **Antenatal Care**: Minimal (cultural mistrust, language barrier)
- **No previous mental health treatment**: "In my country, we do not talk about these things."

### Family History
- Mother was "very sad" after sister born, stayed in bed for months (likely postnatal depression, untreated)

### Social Determinants
- **Marital Status**: Married, husband works two jobs (taxi driver + warehouse), rarely home
- **Support Network**: No family in Australia, few friends, feels isolated
- **Housing**: 2-bedroom unit in Western Sydney, struggling with rent
- **Financial Stress**: Income barely covers rent and bills, no savings
- **Trauma History**: Fled Sudan due to war, spent 2 years in refugee camp in Egypt, does not like to talk about it

### Emotional Profile
- **Baseline State**: GUARDED_ASHAMED
- **Depression Severity**: Moderate-severe
- **Anxiety Level**: 9/10
- **Suicidal Ideation**: Passive (no active plan or intent)
- **Cultural Considerations**: Strong stigma around mental illness, fear of judgment, trauma history (refugee), language barrier, isolation from family/community, cultural expectations of motherhood
- **Fear**: Baby being taken away if admits struggling

### Trust-Building Actions
- Offering interpreter (Arabic) - MANDATORY
- Normalizing postnatal depression ("Affects 1 in 7 mothers")
- Emphasizing this is medical illness, not weakness
- Reassuring baby will NOT be taken away
- Asking about cultural beliefs and practices
- Acknowledging difficulty of refugee experience
- Practical support (social worker, financial counseling)

### Trust-Destroying Actions (Avoid)
- Judgmental tone about parenting
- Not offering interpreter
- Minimizing symptoms ("All new mothers are tired")
- Threatening child removal
- Not acknowledging trauma/refugee background
- Rushing assessment

### Key Differentials
1. **Postnatal depression** (most likely - EPDS score likely >13)
2. Postnatal PTSD (given trauma history)
3. Adjustment disorder with depressed mood
4. Postnatal psychosis (LESS likely - no hallucinations/delusions)
5. Hypothyroidism (postpartum thyroiditis)

### Critical Actions (Australian Guidelines)
1. **Offer interpreter** (MANDATORY - cannot do adequate risk assessment without)
2. Conduct suicide risk assessment systematically (ask directly about self-harm, plans, intent, means)
3. Assess bonding and thoughts of harm to baby (infanticide risk low but must ask)
4. Complete Edinburgh Postnatal Depression Scale (EPDS) - validated in multiple languages
5. Check for psychotic symptoms (if present, urgent psych referral)
6. Assess safety plan (who can help, crisis contacts)
7. Referral to Perinatal Mental Health Service
8. Referral to social worker (financial stress, housing)
9. Referral to maternal child health nurse for parenting support
10. Discuss treatment: Psychological (CBT via interpreter) AND/OR medication (SSRIs safe in breastfeeding)
11. Provide written information in Arabic (Beyond Blue resources)
12. Follow-up within 48-72 hours (with interpreter)
13. Involve husband with consent (support network crucial)

### Australian Perinatal Mental Health Services
- **PANDA** (Perinatal Anxiety & Depression Australia): 1300 726 306
- **Beyond Blue**: Translated resources available
- **Transcultural Mental Health Centre NSW**
- **Refugee Health Network**

### Medicare Rebates
- GP Mental Health Treatment Plan (MHTP) - item 2715 (longer consult)
- Psychologist rebate (up to 10 sessions/year via MHTP)
- Interpreter services FREE via TIS National (Translating and Interpreting Service)

### PBS Considerations
- Sertraline (Zoloft) 50mg daily - PBS listed, safe in breastfeeding
- No authority required for first-line SSRIs

### RAG Citations
1. **(Therapeutic Guidelines: Psychotropic, Section 2.3.1, 2024: Postnatal depression management)**
2. **(Beyond Blue Clinical Practice Guidelines: Perinatal Mental Health, Section 4, 2023: Risk assessment and management)**
3. **(NSW Health Perinatal Mental Health Protocol, Section 2.2, 2024: Interpreter use and cultural considerations)**
4. **(RACGP Guidelines: Mental Health in Pregnancy and Postnatal Period, Section 3.1, 2023: EPDS screening)**

### Difficulty Level
**Advanced** - Estimated pass rate: 40%

**Rationale**: Requires cultural competence, interpreter use, suicide risk assessment, understanding trauma-informed care, knowledge of perinatal mental health services, balancing medication vs psychological therapy, addressing social determinants. IMG students often struggle with mental health stigma and may not be familiar with Australian perinatal services.

## Scenario 3: Obstetric Emergency - First Trimester Bleeding

### Patient Demographics
- **Name**: Sarah Mitchell
- **Age**: 32, Female
- **Occupation**: High school teacher
- **Cultural Background**: Anglo-Australian
- **Preferred Language**: English

### Chief Complaint
"Hi doctor, I'm 8 weeks pregnant and I started bleeding this morning. I'm really scared. Is my baby okay?"

### Key Clinical Features
- **Gestation**: 8 weeks (LMP 8 weeks ago)
- **Bleeding**: Started this morning (8am), bright red blood, soaked 2 pads in 3 hours
- **Clots**: Passed small clots (size of 10-cent coin)
- **Tissue Passage**: "[Tearful] I think I might have passed something... tissue... Is that the baby?"
- **Pain**: Cramping lower abdomen, 5/10 (like period pain but worse)
- **Associated Symptoms**: Dizzy on standing, no fever, no smelly discharge

### Obstetric History
- **Second pregnancy**: First pregnancy ended in miscarriage at 11 weeks (2 years ago)
- **Fertility Issues**: Trying for 3 years, this pregnancy was IVF
- **Previous Scan**: 6 weeks showed heartbeat (120 bpm, normal)
- **No living children**: "We tried for so long..." [tearful]
- **Medications**: Elevit, folic acid 500mcg, progesterone pessaries (Crinone) stopped last week

### Red Flags
1. Heavy bleeding (2 pads in 3 hours)
2. Passage of tissue (suggests miscarriage in progress)
3. Postural dizziness (? hypovolemia)
4. Previous miscarriage (high anxiety)
5. IVF pregnancy (emotional significance)

### Emotional Profile
- **Baseline State**: ANXIOUS_DISTRESSED
- **Pain Level**: 5/10
- **Anxiety Level**: 10/10
- **Grief Anticipation**: Anticipating loss, similar to previous miscarriage, emotionally fragile
- **Trust Threshold**: 2/10 (needs immediate empathy)

### Empathy Phrases (Use These)
- "I can see this is very distressing for you"
- "I'm so sorry you're going through this"
- "It's understandable you're worried given your previous experience"
- "We'll do everything we can to find out what's happening"

### Harmful Phrases (NEVER Use)
- "Don't worry, I'm sure it's fine" (false reassurance)
- "These things happen" (dismissive)
- "You're young, you can try again" (minimizing loss)
- "It's nature's way" (unhelpful platitude)

### Important Questions to Ask
- "Would you like your husband here with you?"
- "How are you coping emotionally?"
- "Do you have support at home?"

### Key Differentials
1. **Miscarriage in progress** (inevitable or incomplete) - MOST LIKELY given tissue passage
2. Threatened miscarriage (bleeding but viable pregnancy)
3. Complete miscarriage (closed cervix, empty uterus)
4. **Ectopic pregnancy** (MUST exclude - life-threatening)
5. Subchorionic haematoma
6. Cervical causes (less likely)

### Critical Actions (Australian Guidelines)
1. Assess haemodynamic stability (BP, HR - check for shock)
2. Abdominal examination (peritonism if ectopic suspected)
3. Speculum examination (assess bleeding, cervical os open/closed) - explain gently
4. Quantitative beta-hCG (trend matters even if known pregnant)
5. Group & hold blood (in case needs surgery)
6. **Pelvic ultrasound URGENT (transvaginal)** - check for:
   - Intrauterine pregnancy
   - Fetal heartbeat
   - Retained products of conception
   - Exclude ectopic pregnancy
7. **Rhesus status** (if Rh negative, needs Anti-D within 72 hours)
8. Discuss outcomes sensitively:
   - If viable: Reassurance, follow-up scan
   - If miscarriage: Explain options (expectant, medical, surgical)
   - If ectopic: Urgent gynae referral
9. Offer grief counselling referral (SANDS, Pink Elephants)
10. Safety-net: Return if heavy bleeding (>1 pad/hour), severe pain, dizziness/collapse, fever
11. Follow-up: 24-48 hours OR sooner if unstable

### Australian Early Pregnancy Services
- **Early Pregnancy Assessment Units (EPAU)**: Most major hospitals
- Can attend directly without GP referral in some states
- Provide same-day ultrasound and specialist review

### Medicare Rebates
- Ultrasound pelvic item 55700 (Medicare rebate ~$70)
- GP consultation item 36 (standard) or 44 (long - likely needed)

### Miscarriage Management Options (eTG)
1. **Expectant** (wait for natural passage) - suitable if stable, patient preference
2. **Medical** (misoprostol tablets) - eTG Obstetrics Section 3.2.1
3. **Surgical** (D&C) - if heavy bleeding, patient preference, or incomplete

### Anti-D Requirements (CRITICAL)
- ALL Rh-negative women with bleeding in pregnancy need Anti-D within 72 hours
- Dose: 250 IU if <12 weeks gestation (eTG Obstetrics Section 1.4)

### Grief Support Resources (Australia)
- **SANDS** (Stillbirth and Neonatal Death Support): www.sands.org.au
- **Pink Elephants Support Network** (miscarriage & pregnancy loss)
- Medicare rebate for psychology via GP Mental Health Treatment Plan

### RAG Citations
1. **(Therapeutic Guidelines: Obstetrics, Section 3.2, 2024: First trimester bleeding management)**
2. **(RANZCOG Guidelines: Early Pregnancy Loss, Section 2.1, 2023: Diagnosis and management)**
3. **(Talley & O'Connor's Clinical Examination, 8th ed, p.567-569: Gynaecological examination)**
4. **(NSW Health Early Pregnancy Loss Protocol, Section 4, 2024: Assessment and management pathway)**

### Difficulty Level
**Intermediate** - Estimated pass rate: 60%

**Rationale**: Requires sensitive communication (breaking bad news), knowledge of early pregnancy complications, understanding management options, Rhesus status management, and grief support. Tests both clinical knowledge AND empathy. IMG students may struggle with nuanced communication around pregnancy loss.
