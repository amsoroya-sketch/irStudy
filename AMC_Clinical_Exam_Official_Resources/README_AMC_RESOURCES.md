## 🆘 Common Questions

**Q: I don't have much money. Can I still prepare well?**
A: Yes! Use free resources:
- AMC official documents (downloaded here)
- AMC Wise question bank (free tier)
- Geeky Medics (50 free OSCE stations)
- YouTube clinical videos (University of Nottingham)
- Public library access to textbooks
- Study groups for practice

**Q: What's the ONE thing I must buy?**
A: Talley & O'Connor Clinical Examination textbook (~$200). It's the gold standard for Australian OSCE preparation.

**Q: Should I subscribe to eTG or AMH?**
A: If choosing only one, get **Therapeutic Guidelines (eTG)** - it's referenced more frequently in AMC exams. AMH is better for prescribing specifics.

**Q: How many OSCE stations should I practice?**
A: Minimum 300-400. Recommended 600-900. The more you practice, the more confident you'll be.

**Q: When should I do a mock exam?**
A: Do self-timed mocks every 4 weeks. Do a professional mock (ARIMGSAS) 4-6 weeks before your actual exam.

**Q: I'm weak in communication/ethics. What should I do?**
A:
1. Read AHPRA Good Medical Practice code (already downloaded)
2. Practice breaking bad news scenarios (x 20 times)
3. Watch University of Nottingham communication videos (YouTube)
4. Focus on SPIKES framework and active listening

## ✨ Final Tips

1. **Start early** - Give yourself 6-12 months if possible
2. **Practice OSCEs out loud** - Record yourself
3. **Use Australian guidelines** - AMC tests Australian practice, not international
4. **Time yourself strictly** - 8 minutes per station, no exceptions
5. **Get feedback** - Study partners, tutors, mock exams
6. **Stay consistent** - Daily practice beats cramming
7. **Focus on communication** - It's tested in every station
8. **Know your weak areas** - Track them in the Progress Tracker
9. **Don't neglect ethics** - Read AHPRA code multiple times
10. **Believe in yourself** - Preparation + practice = pass!
