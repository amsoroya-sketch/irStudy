### Procedural Competency Framework

**PROGRESSIVE COMPETENCY LEVELS:**

1. **Level 1 - Observation:** Watches procedure performed by experienced clinician
2. **Level 2 - Assisted Performance:** Performs parts of procedure under direct supervision
3. **Level 3 - Supervised Performance:** Performs complete procedure with supervisor present
4. **Level 4 - Independent Performance:** Performs procedure independently, supervisor available
5. **Level 5 - Teaching Others:** Competent to teach and supervise the procedure

**SIGN-OFF REQUIREMENTS:**
* Minimum number of procedures observed (varies by procedure complexity)
* Minimum number supervised (10-20 for most procedures)
* Supervisor assessment of competency
* Logbook documentation
* No complications during assessment period

### 1. Lumbar Puncture (LP) - Detailed Procedure Guide

```markdown
LUMBAR PUNCTURE - AUSTRALIAN STANDARD TECHNIQUE

**INDICATIONS:**
├─ Suspected meningitis (bacterial, viral, TB, fungal)
├─ Subarachnoid hemorrhage (SAH) - if CT negative, looking for xanthochromia
├─ Idiopathic intracranial hypertension (IIH) - measure opening pressure
├─ Multiple sclerosis workup (oligoclonal bands)
├─ Guillain-Barré syndrome (albuminocytologic dissociation)
├─ Normal pressure hydrocephalus (therapeutic trial)
└─ Intrathecal medication administration (chemotherapy, antibiotics)

**CONTRAINDICATIONS (ABSOLUTE):**
├─ Raised intracranial pressure (ICP) with mass effect → risk of coning/herniation
│   └─ CT head MANDATORY if: GCS <13, focal neurology, papilloedema, seizures
├─ Infection at puncture site (cellulitis, abscess)
├─ Bleeding diathesis (platelets <50, INR >1.4, on anticoagulation)
└─ Patient refusal (no valid consent)

**CONTRAINDICATIONS (RELATIVE):**
├─ Spinal deformity (scoliosis, previous spinal surgery)
├─ Coagulopathy (mild thrombocytopenia 50-100, INR 1.2-1.4)
├─ Suspected spinal epidural abscess
└─ Uncooperative patient (unable to remain still)

**EQUIPMENT SETUP:**
1. Sterile LP kit (spinal needle 20-22G, manometer, collection tubes)
2. Antiseptic (chlorhexidine 2% in 70% alcohol - PREFERRED in Australia)
3. Sterile gloves, drapes, gown
4. Local anesthetic (lignocaine 1%, 5-10 mL, 25G needle for infiltration)
5. Dressings (sterile gauze, adhesive bandage)
6. Sharps container

**SPINAL NEEDLE SELECTION:**
├─ 20G needle: Standard adults, quicker CSF flow
├─ 22G needle: Preferred for most LPs (lower post-LP headache rate)
└─ Length: 90mm standard, 120mm for obese patients

**ANATOMICAL LANDMARKS:**
├─ Tuffier's line: Horizontal line joining highest points of iliac crests = L4 vertebra
├─ Target: L3/L4 or L4/L5 interspace (below conus medullaris at L1/L2)
├─ Midline vs paramedian approach (midline standard)
└─ Surface marking: Palpate spinous processes, identify interspace

**PATIENT POSITIONING:**

**LATERAL DECUBITUS (PREFERRED):**
├─ Patient lies on side (usually left lateral)
├─ Knees pulled to chest (flexes spine, opens intervertebral spaces)
├─ Back at edge of bed (easier access)
├─ Shoulders and hips perpendicular to bed (true lateral position)
├─ Pillow under head (spine horizontal)
└─ Assistant holds patient in position

**SITTING POSITION (ALTERNATIVE):**
├─ Used if obese (easier to palpate landmarks)
├─ Patient sits, leans forward over bedside table
├─ Flexes spine, opens interspaces
└─ **Cannot measure opening pressure** (requires lateral position)

**STERILE TECHNIQUE (CRITICAL):**

**HAND HYGIENE:**
1. Wash hands (5 Moments: before patient contact)
2. Don sterile gloves
3. No-touch technique throughout

**SKIN PREPARATION:**
1. Identify landmarks BEFORE cleaning (can palpate through gloves after cleaning)
2. Chlorhexidine 2% in 70% alcohol (30 seconds contact time, allow to dry)
3. Apply in circular motion, outward from puncture site
4. Cover wide area (15cm x 15cm)

**STERILE FIELD:**
1. Sterile drapes around puncture site
2. Keep all equipment on sterile field
3. Assistant opens sterile packs (no-touch technique)

**LOCAL ANESTHESIA:**
1. Draw up lignocaine 1% (5-10 mL)
2. 25G needle for infiltration (less painful)
3. Raise skin wheal at puncture site (intradermal)
4. Infiltrate deeper tissues (subcutaneous, interspinous ligament)
5. Wait 2-3 minutes for effect
6. Test anesthesia (patient should feel pressure, not pain)

**PROCEDURE STEPS:**

**STEP 1: NEEDLE INSERTION**
├─ Insert spinal needle at midline, L3/L4 or L4/L5
├─ Angle slightly cephalad (toward umbilicus)
├─ Advance slowly through:
│   └─ Skin → Subcutaneous tissue → Supraspinous ligament →
│       Interspinous ligament → Ligamentum flavum → Epidural space →
│       Dura mater → Subarachnoid space
├─ "Pop" sensation when penetrating ligamentum flavum and dura (may not always feel)
├─ Advance 1-2mm at a time, remove stylet frequently to check for CSF
└─ If hit bone:
    ├─ Withdraw to skin, redirect slightly (usually need to angle more cephalad)
    └─ If repeatedly hitting bone, try adjacent interspace

**STEP 2: CSF CONFIRMATION**
├─ Remove stylet, CSF should drip out freely
├─ If no CSF:
│   ├─ Rotate needle 90° (opening may be against nerve root)
│   ├─ Advance 1-2mm further
│   └─ Withdraw slowly (may be too deep, past subarachnoid space)
└─ If blood-stained CSF:
    ├─ Traumatic tap vs subarachnoid hemorrhage (SAH)?
    ├─ Collect 3 sequential tubes
    ├─ If traumatic: Blood clears in later tubes
    ├─ If SAH: Blood uniform across all tubes, xanthochromia present

**STEP 3: OPENING PRESSURE (if indicated)**
├─ Only if patient in lateral decubitus position
├─ Attach manometer to spinal needle
├─ Read height of CSF column (cm H2O)
├─ Normal: 10-25 cm H2O
├─ Elevated: >25 cm H2O (IIH, meningitis, SAH)
├─ Low: <10 cm H2O (CSF leak, dehydration)
└─ CRITICAL: Patient must be relaxed, legs extended (flexion raises pressure falsely)

**STEP 4: CSF COLLECTION**
├─ Collect 10-20 drops per tube (typically 1-2 mL)
├─ **4 tubes standard:**
│   ├─ Tube 1: Microbiology (Gram stain, culture)
│   ├─ Tube 2: Biochemistry (protein, glucose)
│   ├─ Tube 3: Cell count (WCC, RCC)
│   └─ Tube 4: Additional tests (xanthochromia, oligoclonal bands, cytology)
├─ If suspecting SAH: Collect tube 1 and tube 4 for comparison (clear vs persistent blood)
└─ Total volume: 5-10 mL typical (can collect up to 20 mL if needed)

**STEP 5: NEEDLE REMOVAL**
├─ Replace stylet BEFORE removing needle (reduces post-LP headache)
├─ Withdraw needle in one smooth motion
├─ Apply pressure with sterile gauze
├─ Cover with adhesive dressing
└─ Dispose of needle in sharps container IMMEDIATELY

**POST-PROCEDURE CARE:**
├─ Bed rest: 30-60 minutes (controversial, but standard practice in Australia)
├─ Encourage oral fluids
├─ Analgesia if needed (paracetamol)
├─ Advise re post-LP headache (10-30% incidence)
└─ Safety netting: Return if severe headache, fever, leg weakness, bladder/bowel dysfunction

**CSF INTERPRETATION (QUICK REFERENCE):**

| Condition | Appearance | WCC (cells/µL) | Protein (g/L) | Glucose (mmol/L) |
|-----------|------------|----------------|---------------|------------------|
| **Normal** | Clear | 0-5 | 0.15-0.45 | >2.5 (>50% blood glucose) |
| **Bacterial Meningitis** | Turbid/cloudy | 100-10,000 (PMNs) | 1.0-5.0 (high) | <2.5 (low, <50% blood) |
| **Viral Meningitis** | Clear | 10-1,000 (lymphocytes) | 0.5-1.0 (mildly ↑) | Normal (>2.5) |
| **TB Meningitis** | Fibrin web | 10-500 (lymphocytes) | 1.0-5.0 (very high) | <2.5 (very low) |
| **SAH** | Bloody/xanthochromia | RBCs +++, uniform | Elevated | Normal |
| **Guillain-Barré** | Clear | 0-10 (normal) | 0.5-2.0 (high, no cells) | Normal |

**XANTHOCHROMIA:**
├─ Yellow discoloration of CSF supernatant (after centrifugation)
├─ Indicates: Subarachnoid hemorrhage (>12 hours old)
├─ Peak: 12-24 hours post-SAH
├─ Persists: Up to 2 weeks
├─ Distinguishes SAH from traumatic tap (traumatic tap = no xanthochromia)
└─ Sample must be protected from light (bilirubin degrades)

**COMPLICATIONS:**

**COMMON (1-10%):**
├─ Post-LP headache (10-30%)
│   ├─ Mechanism: CSF leak through dura, intracranial hypotension
│   ├─ Onset: 12-48 hours post-LP
│   ├─ Character: Positional headache (worse upright, better lying flat)
│   ├─ Management: Bed rest, fluids, caffeine, analgesia
│   └─ Severe/persistent: Blood patch (epidural autologous blood injection)
└─ Back pain at puncture site (common, self-limiting)

**UNCOMMON (0.1-1%):**
├─ Bleeding (epidural hematoma, spinal subdural hematoma)
├─ Infection (meningitis - rare, 1 in 50,000)
├─ Nerve root irritation (radicular pain down leg)
└─ Traumatic tap (blood in CSF from needle trauma to epidural veins)

**RARE (<0.1%):**
├─ Cerebral herniation/coning (if LP done with raised ICP + mass effect)
├─ Spinal hematoma (if coagulopathic)
├─ Persistent CSF leak
├─ Cranial nerve palsy (abducens nerve palsy = diplopia)
└─ Spinal cord injury (if needle inserted too high, above L3)

**CRITICAL SAFETY POINTS:**
├─ ⚠️ CT head BEFORE LP if: GCS <13, focal neurology, seizures, papilloedema
├─ ⚠️ Check coagulation: Platelets >50, INR <1.4, no anticoagulation
├─ ⚠️ L3/L4 or L4/L5 only (below conus at L1/L2)
├─ ⚠️ Replace stylet before withdrawing (reduces headache)
├─ ⚠️ Protect xanthochromia sample from light
└─ ⚠️ Document: Opening pressure, appearance, tubes sent, complications
```

### 2. Arterial Blood Gas (ABG) Sampling - Detailed Technique

```markdown
ARTERIAL BLOOD GAS SAMPLING - AUSTRALIAN STANDARD

**INDICATIONS:**
├─ Respiratory failure (hypoxemia, hypercapnia)
├─ Acid-base disturbances (metabolic acidosis/alkalosis)
├─ Shock states (lactate measurement)
├─ Severe sepsis (lactate >2 mmol/L diagnostic criterion)
├─ Diabetic ketoacidosis (pH, bicarb, lactate)
└─ Pre-operative assessment (respiratory function)

**SITES (in order of preference):**

1. **RADIAL ARTERY (PREFERRED):**
   ├─ Advantages: Superficial, collateral circulation (ulnar artery), low complication rate
   ├─ Disadvantages: Can be difficult to palpate in shock/hypotension
   └─ **Modified Allen's Test MANDATORY before radial puncture:**
       ├─ Compress radial + ulnar arteries simultaneously
       ├─ Patient makes fist (blanch palm), then opens hand
       ├─ Release ulnar artery (keep radial compressed)
       ├─ Positive test: Palm pinks up within 5-15 seconds (ulnar collateral adequate)
       ├─ Negative test: Palm remains pale >15 seconds (ulnar insufficient - DO NOT use radial)
       └─ If negative: Try other wrist OR use femoral artery

2. **FEMORAL ARTERY (ALTERNATIVE):**
   ├─ Advantages: Large, easy to palpate, consistent anatomy
   ├─ Disadvantages: Higher infection risk, patient discomfort (groin puncture)
   ├─ Indications: Shock (radial not palpable), failed radial attempts, negative Allen's test
   └─ Landmark: Midpoint between ASIS and pubic symphysis (femoral artery midpoint)

3. **BRACHIAL ARTERY (AVOID if possible):**
   ├─ Advantages: Relatively superficial
   ├─ Disadvantages: No collateral circulation (median nerve injury risk), hematoma risk
   └─ Use only if radial and femoral unsuccessful

**EQUIPMENT:**
├─ Pre-heparinized ABG syringe (1-3 mL, 23-25G needle)
├─ Alcohol swab
├─ Sterile gloves
├─ Gauze
├─ Adhesive bandage
├─ Sharps container
└─ Ice (if delayed analysis >10 minutes)

**TECHNIQUE (RADIAL ARTERY):**

**STEP 1: PREPARATION**
├─ Explain procedure to patient
├─ Position: Wrist extended ~30° (dorsiflexed), supported on pillow/towel
├─ Palpate radial artery (medial to radial styloid)
├─ Perform Modified Allen's Test (ensure collateral circulation)
└─ Don sterile gloves

**STEP 2: SKIN PREPARATION**
├─ Clean site with alcohol swab (70% isopropyl alcohol)
├─ Allow to dry (30 seconds)
└─ Do NOT re-palpate artery after cleaning (contaminates site)

**STEP 3: LOCAL ANESTHESIA (OPTIONAL)**
├─ Subcutaneous lignocaine 1% (0.5-1 mL) at puncture site
├─ Reduces pain, especially if multiple attempts expected
└─ Wait 1-2 minutes for effect

**STEP 4: NEEDLE INSERTION**
├─ Hold syringe like a pencil, 30-45° angle to skin
├─ Needle bevel UP (reduces vessel trauma)
├─ Palpate artery with non-dominant index + middle fingers (straddle artery)
├─ Insert needle between palpating fingers, advance toward arterial pulse
├─ **Flashback:** Bright red blood pulsates into syringe (arterial pressure drives plunger)
│   └─ If syringe doesn't auto-fill: May be in vein (dark blood) or not in vessel
├─ Collect 1-2 mL (adequate for analysis)
└─ If no flashback:
    ├─ Withdraw slowly (may have gone through artery)
    ├─ Redirect slightly (adjust angle)
    └─ Maximum 2-3 attempts at one site (then try different site)

**STEP 5: NEEDLE REMOVAL**
├─ Withdraw needle in one smooth motion
├─ Immediately apply firm pressure with gauze (5 minutes minimum)
│   └─ Arterial bleeding: Requires prolonged pressure vs venous sampling
├─ Expel air bubbles from syringe (air dilutes sample, falsely elevates pO2)
├─ Cap syringe (prevents air contamination)
├─ Mix sample gently (heparin prevents clotting)
└─ Dispose of needle in sharps container

**STEP 6: POST-PROCEDURE**
├─ Apply pressure dressing
├─ Check distal perfusion (radial pulse, capillary refill, finger warmth/color)
├─ If hematoma developing: Continue pressure, elevate arm
└─ Safety netting: Return if hand becomes cold, pale, numb, or painful

**SAMPLE HANDLING:**
├─ Analyze within 10 minutes (ideal)
├─ If delayed: Store on ice (slows cellular metabolism, preserves pO2/pCO2)
├─ Label: Patient details, time collected, FiO2 (room air vs supplemental oxygen)
└─ Transport immediately to point-of-care analyzer or lab

**ABG INTERPRETATION (AUSTRALIAN STANDARD):**

**NORMAL VALUES (Room Air, Sea Level):**
├─ pH: 7.35-7.45 (7.40 ideal)
├─ pO2: 80-100 mmHg (10.7-13.3 kPa)
├─ pCO2: 35-45 mmHg (4.7-6.0 kPa)
├─ HCO3: 22-26 mmol/L
├─ Base Excess (BE): -2 to +2 mmol/L
├─ SaO2: >95%
├─ Lactate: <2 mmol/L
└─ Note: Australia uses both mmHg and kPa (kPa = mmHg ÷ 7.5)

**SYSTEMATIC INTERPRETATION:**

**STEP 1: OXYGENATION**
├─ pO2 <60 mmHg (8 kPa) = Hypoxemia (respiratory failure Type 1)
├─ Check FiO2: On room air (FiO2 21%) vs supplemental O2
└─ A-a gradient: Expected pO2 = [FiO2 × (760 - 47)] - (pCO2 ÷ 0.8)
    └─ Widened A-a gradient: V/Q mismatch (pneumonia, PE, pulmonary edema)

**STEP 2: pH**
├─ pH <7.35 = Acidemia
├─ pH >7.45 = Alkalemia
└─ pH 7.35-7.45 = Normal (but check for compensated disorder)

**STEP 3: RESPIRATORY vs METABOLIC**
├─ Look at pCO2 and HCO3:
│   ├─ If pH ↓ and pCO2 ↑ = Respiratory acidosis
│   ├─ If pH ↓ and HCO3 ↓ = Metabolic acidosis
│   ├─ If pH ↑ and pCO2 ↓ = Respiratory alkalosis
│   └─ If pH ↑ and HCO3 ↑ = Metabolic alkalosis
└─ Primary disorder moves pH in same direction as primary abnormality

**STEP 4: COMPENSATION**
├─ **Respiratory acidosis:** Expected HCO3 = 24 + [(pCO2 - 40) × 0.1] (acute) or ×0.4 (chronic)
├─ **Metabolic acidosis:** Expected pCO2 = (1.5 × HCO3) + 8 ± 2 (Winter's formula)
├─ **Respiratory alkalosis:** Expected HCO3 = 24 - [(40 - pCO2) × 0.2] (acute) or ×0.4 (chronic)
├─ **Metabolic alkalosis:** Expected pCO2 = 40 + 0.7 × (HCO3 - 24)
└─ If actual matches expected: Appropriate compensation (single disorder)
    If actual ≠ expected: Mixed disorder

**STEP 5: ANION GAP (if metabolic acidosis)**
├─ Anion Gap (AG) = Na - (Cl + HCO3)
├─ Normal AG: 8-12 mmol/L
├─ High AG metabolic acidosis (AG >12): MUDPILES
│   ├─ Methanol
│   ├─ Uremia (chronic kidney disease)
│   ├─ Diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA)
│   ├─ Paracetamol/Paraldehyde
│   ├─ Iron/Isoniazid
│   ├─ Lactic acidosis (sepsis, shock, metformin)
│   ├─ Ethylene glycol
│   └─ Salicylates
├─ Normal AG metabolic acidosis (AG <12): HARDUPS
│   ├─ Hyperalimentation
│   ├─ Acetazolamide
│   ├─ Renal tubular acidosis
│   ├─ Diarrhea (GI bicarb loss)
│   ├─ Uretero-sigmoidostomy
│   ├─ Pancreatic fistula
│   └─ Saline administration (excess IV normal saline)
└─ Delta-delta: If AG elevated, check if HCO3 drop matches AG rise (mixed disorder)

**STEP 6: LACTATE**
├─ Lactate >2 mmol/L = Lactic acidosis
├─ Type A (tissue hypoxia): Shock, sepsis, cardiac arrest, severe anemia
├─ Type B (no hypoxia): Metformin, malignancy, liver failure, thiamine deficiency
└─ Lactate >4 mmol/L = Severe, requires urgent resuscitation

**CLINICAL EXAMPLES:**

**EXAMPLE 1: Acute Respiratory Failure (COPD Exacerbation)**
├─ pH 7.28 (acidemia)
├─ pCO2 65 mmHg (elevated - respiratory cause)
├─ HCO3 28 mmol/L (mildly elevated - partial compensation)
├─ pO2 55 mmHg (hypoxemia)
└─ **Interpretation: Acute on chronic respiratory acidosis (Type 2 respiratory failure)**

**EXAMPLE 2: Diabetic Ketoacidosis**
├─ pH 7.15 (severe acidemia)
├─ pCO2 20 mmHg (low - respiratory compensation via Kussmaul breathing)
├─ HCO3 8 mmol/L (very low - metabolic cause)
├─ Anion Gap 28 (very high)
├─ Lactate 3.5 mmol/L
├─ Glucose 28 mmol/L, ketones 5.0 mmol/L
└─ **Interpretation: High anion gap metabolic acidosis (DKA) with appropriate respiratory compensation**

**EXAMPLE 3: Pulmonary Embolism**
├─ pH 7.48 (alkalemia)
├─ pCO2 28 mmHg (low - hyperventilation from hypoxemia)
├─ HCO3 22 mmol/L (normal - acute, no compensation yet)
├─ pO2 65 mmHg (hypoxemia)
├─ Widened A-a gradient
└─ **Interpretation: Acute respiratory alkalosis (hyperventilation) with hypoxemia (V/Q mismatch from PE)**

**COMPLICATIONS:**

**COMMON (1-5%):**
├─ Hematoma (arterial bleeding into tissues)
│   ├─ Prevention: Adequate pressure 5+ minutes post-procedure
│   └─ Management: Continued pressure, ice, elevation
├─ Pain/tenderness at puncture site (self-limiting)
└─ Vasospasm (arterial spasm, reduces distal perfusion temporarily)

**UNCOMMON (0.1-1%):**
├─ Arterial occlusion (thrombus formation)
│   └─ Symptoms: Cold, pale, pulseless distal extremity → vascular emergency
├─ Median nerve injury (if brachial artery used)
├─ Infection (rare with aseptic technique)
└─ Pseudoaneurysm (pulsatile hematoma, requires surgical repair)

**RARE (<0.1%):**
├─ Arteriovenous fistula
├─ Digital ischemia/necrosis (if radial artery compromised without ulnar collateral)
└─ Compartment syndrome (if large hematoma in forearm)

**CRITICAL SAFETY POINTS:**
├─ ⚠️ Modified Allen's Test MANDATORY before radial artery puncture
├─ ⚠️ Firm pressure 5+ minutes post-procedure (arterial bleeding)
├─ ⚠️ Check distal perfusion after procedure (pulse, capillary refill, warmth)
├─ ⚠️ Expel air bubbles (air falsely elevates pO2)
├─ ⚠️ Analyze within 10 minutes or ice sample (cellular metabolism alters results)
└─ ⚠️ Document: Site, FiO2, time collected, number of attempts, complications
```

### 3. Central Venous Catheter (CVC) Insertion - CICM Standard

```markdown
CENTRAL VENOUS CATHETER INSERTION - AUSTRALIAN INTENSIVE CARE STANDARD

**INDICATIONS:**
├─ Hemodynamic monitoring (CVP measurement)
├─ Vasopressor/inotrope infusion (noradrenaline, vasopressin - peripheral veins inadequate)
├─ Difficult IV access (no peripheral access)
├─ Large volume resuscitation (major trauma, septic shock)
├─ Hemodialysis/plasmapheresis (large bore dialysis catheter)
├─ Total parenteral nutrition (TPN - high osmolarity, scleroses peripheral veins)
└─ Frequent blood sampling (ICU patients)

**CONTRAINDICATIONS:**
├─ Absolute: Infection at insertion site, SVC obstruction
├─ Relative: Coagulopathy (INR >1.5, platelets <50), anticoagulation, anatomical distortion
└─ Caution: Previous failed attempts (scar tissue), neck irradiation

**SITE SELECTION:**

1. **INTERNAL JUGULAR VEIN (IJ) - PREFERRED:**
   ├─ Right IJ: Straight path to SVC, lower pleural dome
   ├─ Left IJ: Risk of thoracic duct injury
   ├─ Advantages: Low infection rate, ultrasound-guided, avoids subclavian stenosis
   ├─ Disadvantages: Patient discomfort (neck), difficult to dress
   └─ Anatomy: Lateral to carotid artery, under SCM muscle

2. **SUBCLAVIAN VEIN:**
   ├─ Advantages: Low infection rate, patient comfort, stable dressing
   ├─ Disadvantages: Higher pneumothorax risk (1-2%), subclavian stenosis (dialysis patients), NO ultrasound
   └─ Anatomy: Beneath clavicle, anterior to subclavian artery

3. **FEMORAL VEIN:**
   ├─ Advantages: Easy landmark, no pneumothorax risk, good for emergencies
   ├─ Disadvantages: Highest infection rate, DVT risk, patient mobility limited
   └─ Anatomy: Medial to femoral artery (mnemonic: NAVY - Nerve, Artery, Vein, Y-fronts)

**EQUIPMENT (CENTRAL LINE KIT):**
├─ Central venous catheter (triple lumen 7Fr typical)
├─ Guidewire (J-wire, Seldinger technique)
├─ Introducer needle (18G)
├─ Dilator
├─ 10 mL syringe
├─ 0.9% saline flushes
├─ Ultrasound with sterile probe cover (for IJ)
├─ Sterile gown, gloves, drapes, mask, cap
├─ Chlorhexidine 2% (antiseptic)
├─ Local anesthetic (lignocaine 1%, 10-20 mL)
├─ Suture (2-0 silk), scalpel, gauze, dressing
└─ Sharps container

**ULTRASOUND-GUIDED IJ INSERTION (GOLD STANDARD):**

**STEP 1: PATIENT POSITIONING**
├─ Supine, 15° Trendelenburg (head down - distends neck veins, reduces air embolism risk)
├─ Head turned 30° to contralateral side (exposes IJ)
├─ Arm at side
└─ No pillow under head

**STEP 2: ULTRASOUND SURVEY**
├─ Identify internal jugular vein (IJ) and carotid artery
│   ├─ IJ: Lateral to carotid, compressible, thin walls
│   └─ Carotid: Medial, non-compressible, thick walls, pulsatile
├─ Assess patency (no thrombus)
├─ Identify best puncture site (IJ most superficial, away from carotid)
└─ Mark site externally (pen)

**STEP 3: STERILE TECHNIQUE (MAXIMUM BARRIER PRECAUTIONS)**
├─ Hand hygiene (surgical scrub)
├─ Don: Cap, mask, sterile gown, sterile gloves
├─ Full-body patient drape (not just small fenestrated drape)
├─ Sterile ultrasound probe cover + sterile gel
└─ Chlorhexidine 2% skin prep (30 seconds, allow to dry)

**STEP 4: LOCAL ANESTHESIA**
├─ Infiltrate lignocaine 1% (10-20 mL) along needle track
├─ Superficial (skin wheal) → deep (along anticipated vein path)
└─ Aspirate before injecting (avoid intravascular injection)

**STEP 5: VENIPUNCTURE (SELDINGER TECHNIQUE)**
├─ Insert introducer needle (18G) attached to syringe
├─ Real-time ultrasound guidance (in-plane or out-of-plane approach)
├─ Advance needle toward IJ, aspirating continuously
├─ Flashback of dark venous blood confirms entry
├─ **Confirm venous (not arterial):**
│   ├─ Blood dark (not bright red)
│   ├─ Non-pulsatile flow
│   └─ If uncertain: Attach arterial line transducer (venous waveform)
├─ Stabilize needle, remove syringe
├─ Insert guidewire through needle (J-wire)
│   ├─ Advance 15-20 cm (into SVC)
│   ├─ Should pass smoothly without resistance
│   ├─ If resistance: Withdraw wire, reposition needle
│   └─ Monitor ECG: PVCs if wire touches right atrium → withdraw wire 2-3 cm
└─ Remove needle, leave wire in place

**STEP 6: DILATION**
├─ Make small skin nick with scalpel (allows dilator passage)
├─ Advance dilator over wire (dilate tract)
│   └─ Insert 1-2 cm only (excessive dilation increases bleeding)
├─ Remove dilator, leave wire
└─ Apply pressure to puncture site (control bleeding)

**STEP 7: CATHETER INSERTION**
├─ Flush all catheter lumens with saline (remove air)
├─ Thread catheter over wire (hold wire at skin entry)
├─ Advance catheter to appropriate depth:
│   ├─ Right IJ: 15-17 cm (tip at cavoatrial junction)
│   └─ Left IJ: 17-19 cm
├─ Remove guidewire (hold catheter in place)
├─ Aspirate all lumens (confirm blood return)
├─ Flush lumens with saline
└─ Cap lumens

**STEP 8: SECURING CATHETER**
├─ Suture catheter to skin (2-0 silk, two sutures)
├─ Apply sterile occlusive dressing (transparent, dated)
├─ CXR MANDATORY post-insertion (confirm position, exclude pneumothorax)
└─ Document: Site, depth, lumens, complications, CXR result

**POST-INSERTION CXR INTERPRETATION:**
├─ Catheter tip position: Cavoatrial junction (carina level on CXR)
├─ **Malposition:**
│   ├─ Too deep: Right atrium (risk of perforation, arrhythmias)
│   ├─ Too shallow: SVC (risk of thrombosis)
│   ├─ Jugular vein: Kinked, withdraw
│   └─ Contralateral IJ/subclavian: Withdraw, reposition
├─ **Pneumothorax:**
│   ├─ Visceral pleural line with no lung markings beyond
│   ├─ If present: Size? (<20% observe, >20% chest drain)
│   └─ Immediate pneumothorax: Rare with IJ (common with subclavian)
└─ **Hemothorax:** Blunted costophrenic angle, fluid level

**COMPLICATIONS:**

**IMMEDIATE (0-24 hours):**
├─ Arterial puncture (1-10%, higher without ultrasound)
│   ├─ Recognition: Bright red, pulsatile blood
│   ├─ Management: Remove needle, apply pressure 10 minutes
│   └─ If dilated: Vascular surgery consult (pseudoaneurysm risk)
├─ Pneumothorax (1-2% IJ, 2-3% subclavian)
│   └─ CXR mandatory, chest drain if symptomatic/large
├─ Hemothorax (bleeding into pleural space)
├─ Air embolism (if patient inspires during insertion, negative intrathoracic pressure sucks air)
│   └─ Prevention: Trendelenburg position, occlude catheter hub
├─ Arrhythmias (guidewire touches right atrium → PVCs)
│   └─ Management: Withdraw wire/catheter
├─ Nerve injury (phrenic nerve, brachial plexus)
└─ Malposition (wrong vein)

**EARLY (24 hours - 7 days):**
├─ Line-associated bloodstream infection (CLABSI)
│   ├─ Prevention: Aseptic technique, chlorhexidine dressing, daily necessity review
│   └─ Management: Blood cultures, antibiotics, line removal if persistent bacteremia
├─ Thrombosis (catheter-related DVT)
│   └─ Risk factors: Malposition, traumatic insertion, prolonged dwell time
└─ Catheter occlusion (fibrin sheath)

**LATE (>7 days):**
├─ CLABSI (increases with dwell time, remove when no longer needed)
├─ Venous stenosis/thrombosis (especially subclavian - problematic for future dialysis access)
└─ Catheter fracture/migration

**CRITICAL SAFETY POINTS:**
├─ ⚠️ Ultrasound guidance for IJ (CICM standard)
├─ ⚠️ Maximum sterile barrier precautions (cap, mask, gown, gloves, full drape)
├─ ⚠️ Chlorhexidine 2% skin prep (ACSQHC standard)
├─ ⚠️ CXR post-insertion (confirm position, rule out pneumothorax)
├─ ⚠️ Daily necessity review (remove when no longer indicated)
├─ ⚠️ Document: Site, depth, lumens, complications, CXR
└─ ⚠️ If arterial puncture + dilator passed → vascular surgery (urgent)
```

### 4. Suturing Techniques - RACS Standard

```markdown
SUTURING TECHNIQUES - AUSTRALIAN SURGICAL STANDARD

**INDICATIONS:**
├─ Wound closure (trauma, surgical incisions)
├─ Hemostasis (bleeding vessel ligation)
├─ Tissue approximation (fascia, muscle, skin)
└─ Cosmetic repair (facial lacerations)

**SUTURE SELECTION:**

**BY MATERIAL:**
├─ **Absorbable:**
│   ├─ Vicryl (polyglactin 910): 56-70 days absorption, braided
│   ├─ Monocryl (poliglecaprone 25): 91-119 days, monofilament
│   ├─ PDS (polydioxanone): 182-238 days, monofilament
│   └─ Uses: Subcutaneous, deep layers, mucosa
├─ **Non-absorbable:**
│   ├─ Nylon (monofilament): Strong, minimal tissue reaction
│   ├─ Prolene (polypropylene): Monofilament, good for vascular
│   ├─ Silk (braided): Easy to tie, but tissue reactive
│   └─ Uses: Skin (removed 5-14 days), vascular, tendon repair
└─ Choice depends on: Tissue type, tension, cosmetic importance, infection risk

**BY SIZE:**
├─ 6-0, 7-0: Face, delicate skin (small needle, minimal scarring)
├─ 4-0, 5-0: Extremities, scalp, trunk
├─ 3-0: Fascia, muscle, high tension areas
├─ 2-0, 1: Retention sutures, large tissue
└─ Smaller number = thicker suture (e.g., 1 > 3-0 > 5-0)

**NEEDLE TYPES:**
├─ **Cutting:** Triangular cross-section, sharp edges for tough tissue (skin)
├─ **Reverse cutting:** Cutting edge on outer curve (preferred for skin - less tissue tear)
├─ **Taper:** Round body, no cutting edges (viscera, vessels, fascia)
├─ **Curved vs straight:** Curved standard (better control), straight for accessible areas
└─ Size: 3/8 circle, 1/2 circle, 5/8 circle (larger = deeper wounds)

**WOUND ASSESSMENT (BEFORE SUTURING):**
├─ Mechanism: Clean vs contaminated (bite, farm injury, sewage)
├─ Time: <6 hours (face <24 hours) suitable for primary closure
├─ Depth: Superficial (skin only) vs deep (muscle, fascia involved)
├─ Contamination: Clean wound → primary closure, contaminated → delayed closure or secondary intention
├─ Tissue viability: Devitalized tissue must be debrided
├─ Neurovascular status: Check distal sensation, movement, pulses BEFORE anesthesia
└─ Foreign body: Explore wound, remove debris

**LOCAL ANESTHESIA (BEFORE SUTURING):**
├─ Lignocaine 1% (with or without adrenaline)
│   ├─ Plain lignocaine: Max 3 mg/kg (e.g., 70 kg = 21 mL of 1%)
│   ├─ With adrenaline: Max 7 mg/kg (vasoconstriction prolongs duration, reduces bleeding)
│   └─ **NEVER use adrenaline:** Digits (fingers/toes), nose, ears, penis (end-arteries, ischemia risk)
├─ Infiltrate along wound edges (subcutaneous, intradermal)
├─ Wait 5-10 minutes for effect (adrenaline takes 7-10 min for vasoconstriction)
└─ Test anesthesia before starting (patient should feel pressure, not pain)

**SIMPLE INTERRUPTED SUTURES (MOST VERSATILE):**

**TECHNIQUE:**
1. Insert needle perpendicular to skin (90° angle)
2. Entry point: 5 mm from wound edge
3. Advance needle through dermis, exit opposite side at same depth
4. Exit point: 5 mm from wound edge (symmetric)
5. Tie knot (instrument tie or hand tie)
   ├─ Surgeon's knot: First throw doubled (more secure)
   ├─ 3-4 throws total (3 for monofilament, 4 for braided)
   └─ Tightness: Approximate edges, don't strangle (causes ischemia, scarring)
6. Cut tails (leave 3-5 mm, prevents spontaneous untying)

**ADVANTAGES:**
├─ Most secure (individual sutures independent)
├─ If one breaks, others maintain closure
├─ Good for irregular wounds
└─ Each suture can be adjusted individually

**DISADVANTAGES:**
├─ Time-consuming (many knots)
├─ More suture material left in wound
└─ "Railroad track" scarring if too tight or left too long

**SPACING:**
├─ 5 mm between sutures (face: 3 mm, trunk/limbs: 5-10 mm)
├─ Closer spacing = less tension per suture, less wound edge inversion
└─ Eversion of wound edges (slight) promotes flat scar (dermis heals edge-to-edge)

**SUBCUTICULAR (COSMETIC) SUTURES:**

**TECHNIQUE:**
1. Use absorbable suture (Monocryl 4-0 or 5-0)
2. Continuous suture running through dermis (NOT through epidermis)
3. Enter at one end of wound, subcuticular plane
4. Alternate sides: Horizontal bites through dermis, 5 mm apart
5. Exit at opposite end
6. Secure with buried knot or adhesive strips
7. **No skin puncture:** Entire suture buried (no visible suture marks)

**ADVANTAGES:**
├─ Excellent cosmesis (no "railroad tracks")
├─ No suture removal needed (absorbable)
├─ Ideal for face, cosmetically important areas
└─ Distributes tension evenly along wound

**DISADVANTAGES:**
├─ Requires clean, non-infected wound
├─ Difficult in contaminated wounds (absorbable suture = foreign material, infection risk)
└─ Cannot adjust tension after placement

**INDICATIONS:**
├─ Facial lacerations (cosmetic priority)
├─ Surgical incisions (elective, clean)
└─ Areas of minimal tension

**MATTRESS SUTURES (HORIZONTAL AND VERTICAL):**

**HORIZONTAL MATTRESS:**
├─ Technique:
│   ├─ Near-near, far-far
│   ├─ Entry 5 mm from wound edge, exit 5 mm opposite side
│   ├─ Re-enter 5-10 mm lateral to exit, cross wound again
│   └─ Tie knot (creates rectangular suture path)
├─ Advantages:
│   ├─ Excellent wound edge eversion
│   ├─ High tensile strength
│   └─ Good for high-tension areas (scalp, back)
├─ Disadvantages:
│   ├─ Poor cosmesis (wide suture marks)
│   └─ Ischemia risk if too tight
└─ Uses: Scalp, back, high-tension wounds

**VERTICAL MATTRESS:**
├─ Technique:
│   ├─ Far-far (deep bite), near-near (superficial bite)
│   ├─ Deep bite: 8-10 mm from edge, through dermis
│   ├─ Superficial bite: 2-3 mm from edge, through epidermis
│   └─ Tie knot (creates vertical loop)
├─ Advantages:
│   ├─ Excellent wound eversion
│   ├─ Combines deep and superficial tissue approximation
│   └─ Single suture replaces deep + superficial layer
├─ Disadvantages:
│   ├─ Time-consuming
│   └─ Can cause tissue strangulation
└─ Uses: Areas requiring eversion, thick skin (heel)

**WOUND CLOSURE PRINCIPLES:**

**LAYER CLOSURE (MULTI-LAYER WOUNDS):**
├─ **Deep layer (fascia, muscle):**
│   ├─ Absorbable suture (Vicryl 2-0 or 3-0)
│   ├─ Reduces tension on skin
│   └─ Prevents dead space (hematoma/seroma formation)
├─ **Subcutaneous layer:**
│   ├─ Absorbable (Monocryl 3-0 or 4-0)
│   ├─ Further reduces skin tension
│   └─ Inverted knots (bury knot to avoid palpability)
└─ **Skin layer:**
│   ├─ Non-absorbable (Nylon 4-0, 5-0) or subcuticular absorbable
│   └─ Minimal tension (deep layers bear load)

**SUTURE REMOVAL TIMING (AUSTRALIA):**
├─ Face: 3-5 days (excellent blood supply, heals fast, minimize scarring)
├─ Scalp: 7-10 days
├─ Trunk/upper limbs: 7-10 days
├─ Lower limbs: 10-14 days (slower healing, higher tension)
├─ Palms/soles: 14 days (thick skin, high tension)
└─ Over joints: 10-14 days (movement creates tension)

**COMPLICATIONS:**
├─ **Infection:** Contaminated wound, poor technique, retained foreign body
│   └─ Prevention: Irrigation, debridement, prophylactic antibiotics (if high risk)
├─ **Wound dehiscence:** Sutures removed too early, excessive tension, infection
├─ **Poor cosmesis:** Sutures too tight, left too long, inverted edges, infection
├─ **Scarring:** Hypertrophic scar, keloid (genetic predisposition, Afro-Caribbean, Asian)
├─ **Hematoma/seroma:** Dead space not closed, bleeding
└─ **Suture abscess:** Foreign body reaction (especially braided sutures)

**SPECIAL SITUATIONS:**

**DOG BITE WOUNDS:**
├─ High infection risk (Pasteurella, Streptococcus)
├─ Generally: Leave open (secondary intention healing) OR delayed primary closure
├─ Exception: Facial bites (cosmetic priority) - irrigate copiously, primary closure + prophylactic antibiotics
└─ Antibiotics: Amoxicillin-clavulanate (Augmentin) 875/125 mg BD × 5 days

**CONTAMINATED WOUNDS (FARM INJURIES, SOIL):**
├─ Tetanus status: Check immunization (booster if >5 years)
├─ Copious irrigation (1-2 L normal saline, high pressure)
├─ Debridement (remove devitalized tissue, dirt)
├─ Leave open (secondary intention) OR delayed primary closure (3-5 days)
└─ Prophylactic antibiotics if high risk

**FACIAL LACERATIONS:**
├─ Priority: Cosmesis
├─ Primary closure within 24 hours (face has excellent blood supply)
├─ Fine sutures (6-0 Nylon or Prolene)
├─ Remove early (3-5 days)
├─ Consider: Subcuticular closure (no visible sutures)
└─ Steri-Strips post-removal (support wound 5-7 more days)

**CRITICAL SAFETY POINTS:**
├─ ⚠️ Check neurovascular status BEFORE local anesthetic
├─ ⚠️ Never use adrenaline on digits, nose, ears, penis (end-arteries)
├─ ⚠️ Irrigate contaminated wounds (reduces infection 20-fold)
├─ ⚠️ Layer closure for deep wounds (reduces tension, dead space)
├─ ⚠️ Evert wound edges (flat scar), don't invert
├─ ⚠️ Suture removal timing: Face 3-5 days, limbs 10-14 days
└─ ⚠️ Tetanus prophylaxis: Check immunization status
```
