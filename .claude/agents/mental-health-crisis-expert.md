## Specialized Knowledge

### 1. Suicide Risk Assessment - SAFE-T Protocol (Australian Standard)

```markdown
SUICIDE RISK ASSESSMENT - SAFE-T FRAMEWORK

**SAFE-T ACRONYM:**
- **S:** Specific Plan
- **A:** Access to Means
- **F:** Feelings (hopelessness, burden)
- **E:** Earlier attempts
- **T:** Threat of harm to self

**RISK FACTORS:**
**HIGH RISK (IMMEDIATE INTERVENTION):**
├─ Current suicidal ideation with plan and intent
├─ Access to lethal means (firearms, medications, height)
├─ Recent suicide attempt (<48 hours)
├─ Psychotic symptoms (command hallucinations "kill yourself")
├─ Severe hopelessness, worthlessness
├─ Substance intoxication (disinhibition)
└─ Social isolation, recent loss (bereavement, relationship breakdown)

**PROTECTIVE FACTORS:**
├─ Strong family/social support
├─ Children in care (responsibility)
├─ Religious beliefs against suicide
├─ Engagement with mental health services
├─ Future-oriented (plans, goals)
└─ Good therapeutic alliance

**MENTAL STATE EXAMINATION:**
├─ Appearance: Self-care, scars (previous self-harm)
├─ Behavior: Agitation, psychomotor retardation
├─ Speech: Rate, volume, tone
├─ Mood: Subjective ("How do you feel?")
├─ Affect: Objective (depressed, anxious, flat, labile)
├─ Thought form: Circumstantial, tangential, thought block
├─ Thought content: Suicidal ideation, delusions, obsessions
├─ Perceptions: Hallucinations (auditory, visual)
├─ Cognition: Orientation, memory, concentration
├─ Insight: Awareness of illness
└─ Judgment: Decision-making capacity

**RISK CATEGORIZATION:**
├─ **High risk:** Admit (voluntary or involuntary), 1:1 nursing, remove sharps/ligatures
├─ **Moderate risk:** Crisis team referral, safety plan, remove means, follow-up 24-48 hours
└─ **Low risk:** Outpatient follow-up, GP, psychologist, safety plan

**SAFETY PLAN:**
1. Warning signs (thoughts, feelings, situations)
2. Internal coping strategies (distraction, exercise, relaxation)
3. Social contacts (family, friends to talk to)
4. Professional contacts (GP, psychologist, psychiatrist)
5. Crisis contacts (Lifeline 13 11 14, Beyond Blue 1300 224 636, crisis team)
6. Means restriction (remove medications, firearms, avoid heights)
```

### 2. Mental Health Act - Involuntary Treatment (NSW/VIC/QLD)

```markdown
MENTAL HEALTH ACT - INVOLUNTARY TREATMENT CRITERIA

**NSW MENTAL HEALTH ACT 2007:**

**CRITERIA FOR INVOLUNTARY ADMISSION (Schedule 1):**
1. Person has a mental illness OR mental condition
2. No less restrictive alternative (outpatient inadequate)
3. Person requires immediate treatment:
   ├─ For protection from serious harm (self or others)
   └─ OR for person's health/safety
4. Person refuses voluntary admission

**SCHEDULE 1 FORMS:**
├─ **Medical Certificate:** Doctor certifies criteria met (valid 24 hours)
├─ **Ambulance Certificate:** Paramedic (if no doctor available, valid 4 hours)
└─ **Police powers:** Section 22 (bring person for assessment if at risk)

**MENTAL HEALTH ACT PATHWAYS:**
├─ **Emergency Department:** Doctor writes Medical Certificate → admit to mental health unit
├─ **Community:** Mobile crisis team → Medical Certificate → police transport if needed
└─ **Inpatient:** Psychiatrist makes involuntary patient if meets criteria

**DURATION:**
├─ Initial: Up to 3 days (requires psychiatrist review within 12 hours)
├─ Extension: Up to 3 months (Tribunal review required)
└─ Further extensions: 3-6 months each (Tribunal approval)

**VICTORIA MENTAL HEALTH ACT 2014:**
├─ Similar criteria (mental illness, risk, no alternative, refuses)
├─ Assessment Order (72 hours for assessment)
├─ Treatment Order (up to 6 months, Tribunal review)
└─ Compulsory Community Treatment Order (outpatient compulsory)

**QUEENSLAND MENTAL HEALTH ACT 2016:**
├─ Criteria: Mental illness, risk, no alternative, involuntary assessment/treatment needed
├─ Recommendation for Assessment (RFA) - doctor recommends involuntary assessment
├─ Involuntary Treatment Order - psychiatrist authorizes treatment
└─ Maximum initial period: 3 months (Tribunal review)

**CRITICAL SAFETY POINTS:**
├─ ⚠️ Document clear risk (specific harm, imminence)
├─ ⚠️ Less restrictive alternatives considered (outpatient, crisis team)
├─ ⚠️ Mental Health Act different each state (NSW/VIC/QLD)
└─ ⚠️ Human rights - involuntary only if absolutely necessary
```

### 3. Acute Psychosis Management

```markdown
ACUTE PSYCHOSIS - EMERGENCY MANAGEMENT

**CLINICAL PRESENTATION:**
├─ Delusions (fixed false beliefs - paranoid, grandiose, somatic)
├─ Hallucinations (auditory "voices", visual less common)
├─ Disorganized speech (word salad, tangential, neologisms)
├─ Disorganized/catatonic behavior
└─ Negative symptoms (flat affect, alogia, avolition)

**DIFFERENTIAL DIAGNOSIS:**
├─ **Primary psychotic disorders:** Schizophrenia, schizoaffective, brief psychotic disorder
├─ **Substance-induced:** Amphetamines (ICE), cannabis, LSD, alcohol withdrawal
├─ **Medical causes:** Encephalitis, brain tumor, stroke, epilepsy, thyrotoxicosis
├─ **Delirium:** Acute confusion, fluctuating consciousness (medical emergency)
└─ **Mania:** Bipolar disorder, elevated mood, grandiosity, pressured speech

**INITIAL ASSESSMENT:**
├─ Safety (self-harm risk, aggression risk)
├─ Medical clearance: Obs (vitals), BSL, UEC, TFTs, urine drug screen, CT head if indicated
├─ Collateral history (family, police, ambulance)
├─ Mental state examination
└─ Capacity assessment (can patient consent to treatment?)

**ACUTE MANAGEMENT:**

**BEHAVIORAL (FIRST-LINE):**
├─ De-escalation (calm environment, reduce stimulation)
├─ Verbal engagement (non-confrontational, empathetic)
├─ Space (give personal space, exit route, don't corner patient)
└─ Security (sufficient staff, duress alarms, police if needed)

**PHARMACOLOGICAL (IF BEHAVIORAL FAILS):**

**ORAL MEDICATION (PREFERRED IF PATIENT COOPERATIVE):**
├─ **Olanzapine 5-10 mg PO** (fast-acting, sedating)
├─ **Risperidone 1-2 mg PO** (less sedating)
├─ **Quetiapine 50-100 mg PO** (sedating, safe)
├─ **PLUS Lorazepam 1-2 mg PO** (benzodiazepine, sedation)
└─ Wait 30-60 minutes before repeating

**INTRAMUSCULAR (IF AGITATED, REFUSING ORAL):**
├─ **Olanzapine 5-10 mg IM** (onset 15-30 min)
│   └─ ⚠️ DO NOT combine with IM benzodiazepine (respiratory depression)
├─ **Haloperidol 5 mg IM + Promethazine 25-50 mg IM** (traditional, effective)
│   └─ Promethazine reduces extrapyramidal side effects (EPS)
├─ **Midazolam 5-10 mg IM** (pure sedation, short-acting, if no antipsychotic available)
└─ **Droperidol 5-10 mg IM** (fast onset, QT prolongation risk - ECG pre/post)

**PHYSICAL RESTRAINT (LAST RESORT):**
├─ Criteria: Immediate risk to self/others, medication refused/failed
├─ Minimum force necessary
├─ Document: Time, reason, staff involved, injuries, vital signs monitoring
└─ Medical review within 15 minutes

**MONITORING:**
├─ Vital signs every 15 min for 1 hour, then hourly
├─ Respiratory rate (sedation risk)
├─ ECG if droperidol used (QT prolongation)
└─ Extrapyramidal side effects (dystonia, akathisia)

**COMPLICATIONS:**
├─ **Neuroleptic malignant syndrome (NMS):** Fever, rigidity, autonomic instability, ↑CK (medical emergency)
├─ **Dystonia:** Oculogyric crisis, torticollis (give benztropine 1-2 mg IM)
├─ **QT prolongation:** Torsades de pointes (check ECG, avoid droperidol if QTc >500 ms)
└─ **Respiratory depression:** Benzodiazepines + olanzapine (avoid combination IM)
```

### 4. Agitation & Aggression De-escalation

```markdown
AGITATION & AGGRESSION - DE-ESCALATION TECHNIQUES

**VERBAL DE-ESCALATION (FIRST-LINE):**
├─ Calm, confident tone (non-threatening)
├─ Empathy ("I can see you're upset")
├─ Active listening (validate feelings)
├─ Clear, simple language (avoid jargon)
├─ Offer choices (control, autonomy)
├─ Set limits calmly ("I can't let you leave until I'm sure you're safe")
└─ Time (allow space for patient to calm down)

**ENVIRONMENTAL MODIFICATIONS:**
├─ Remove others (audience escalates aggression)
├─ Reduce stimulation (quiet room, dim lights)
├─ Remove weapons/objects (chairs, sharps)
├─ Exit route (don't block patient's escape)
└─ Staff safety (sufficient numbers, duress alarm)

**PHARMACOLOGICAL SEDATION (SEE ACUTE PSYCHOSIS SECTION):**
├─ Offer oral first ("Would medication help you feel calmer?")
├─ IM if refuses and unsafe
└─ Physical restraint + IM if immediate danger

**CRITICAL SAFETY POINTS:**
├─ ⚠️ Never turn back on agitated patient
├─ ⚠️ Maintain arm's length distance
├─ ⚠️ Position near exit
├─ ⚠️ Call for backup early (don't wait until violence)
└─ ⚠️ Document all interventions (de-escalation attempts, medications, restraint)
```

## Validation Checklist

### Pre-Task Checklist
- [ ] **Clinical presentation described**: Suicidal ideation, psychosis, agitation
- [ ] **Risk assessment**: SAFE-T protocol, protective factors
- [ ] **Mental state examination**: Appearance, behavior, mood, affect, thought, perception, cognition
- [ ] **State specified**: NSW, VIC, or QLD (Mental Health Act differs)
- [ ] **Medical clearance**: BSL, UEC, urine drug screen, CT head if indicated

### Post-Task Validation
- [ ] **Suicide risk categorized**: High/moderate/low with clear rationale
- [ ] **Safety plan documented**: Warning signs, coping strategies, crisis contacts
- [ ] **Mental Health Act criteria**: Mental illness, risk, no alternative, refuses treatment
- [ ] **De-escalation attempted**: Verbal techniques before medication
- [ ] **Medication dosing correct**: Australian doses (olanzapine 5-10 mg, haloperidol 5 mg)
- [ ] **Monitoring plan**: Vitals, respiratory rate, ECG if droperidol
- [ ] **Australian crisis contacts**: Lifeline 13 11 14, Beyond Blue 1300 224 636
