## Specialized Knowledge

### 1. Paediatric Assessment Triangle (PAT)

```markdown
PAEDIATRIC ASSESSMENT TRIANGLE - RAPID VISUAL ASSESSMENT

**THREE COMPONENTS (15-SECOND ASSESSMENT):**

**APPEARANCE:**
├─ TICLS mnemonic:
│   ├─ Tone (normal, floppy, rigid)
│   ├─ Interactiveness (alert, recognizes parents, playful vs lethargic)
│   ├─ Consolability (stops crying when comforted vs inconsolable)
│   ├─ Look/gaze (tracks faces, makes eye contact vs vacant stare)
│   └─ Speech/cry (strong cry vs weak whimper)
└─ Abnormal appearance = CNS/metabolic problem

**WORK OF BREATHING:**
├─ Respiratory rate (fast vs slow)
├─ Accessory muscle use (intercostal, subcostal, supraclavicular recession)
├─ Nasal flaring
├─ Grunting (expiratory - airway pressure maintenance)
├─ Head bobbing (severe respiratory distress)
└─ Abnormal work of breathing = respiratory problem

**CIRCULATION TO SKIN:**
├─ Color (pink vs pale/mottled/cyanotic)
├─ Capillary refill time (<2 seconds normal, >2 sec = poor perfusion)
├─ Skin temperature (warm vs cool peripheries)
└─ Abnormal circulation = shock

**INTERPRETATION:**
├─ Normal PAT (all 3 normal) = Stable
├─ 1 abnormal = Respiratory distress, compensated shock, or CNS problem
├─ 2 abnormal = Respiratory failure or decompensated shock
├─ 3 abnormal = Cardiorespiratory failure (imminent arrest)
```

### 2. Paediatric Resuscitation - APLS Algorithm

```markdown
PAEDIATRIC ADVANCED LIFE SUPPORT (APLS)

**PAEDIATRIC BLS:**
├─ 5 initial rescue breaths (children are primarily respiratory arrests)
├─ 15:2 compression:ventilation ratio (2 rescuers)
├─ 30:2 ratio (1 rescuer)
├─ Compression depth: 1/3 chest depth (~4 cm infant, ~5 cm child)
├─ Compression rate: 100-120/min
└─ Hand position: Lower half sternum, 2 fingers (infant), 1-2 hands (child)

**PAEDIATRIC WEIGHT ESTIMATION:**
├─ **Age-based formula:** Weight (kg) = (Age in years × 2) + 8
│   └─ Example: 5-year-old = (5 × 2) + 8 = 18 kg
├─ **Broselow tape:** Length-based (most accurate in emergency)
└─ **Actual weight preferred** if known

**DRUG DOSING (WEIGHT-BASED):**
├─ **Adrenaline (cardiac arrest):** 10 microgram/kg IV (0.1 mL/kg of 1:10,000)
├─ **Adrenaline (anaphylaxis):** 0.01 mg/kg IM (0.01 mL/kg of 1:1000), max 0.5 mg
├─ **Atropine:** 20 microgram/kg IV, min 100 microgram, max 600 microgram
├─ **Amiodarone:** 5 mg/kg IV bolus (VF/pulseless VT)
├─ **Glucose:** 2 mL/kg 10% dextrose IV (hypoglycemia)
└─ **Fluid bolus:** 20 mL/kg 0.9% saline IV (shock)

**SHOCKABLE RHYTHMS (VF/pVT):**
1. CPR 2 minutes
2. Defibrillate 4 J/kg (biphasic)
3. CPR 2 minutes
4. Defibrillate 4 J/kg
5. Adrenaline 10 microgram/kg IV
6. CPR 2 minutes
7. Defibrillate 4 J/kg
8. Amiodarone 5 mg/kg IV
(Continue cycles)

**NON-SHOCKABLE RHYTHMS (Asystole/PEA):**
1. CPR 2 minutes
2. Adrenaline 10 microgram/kg IV (every 3-5 min)
3. Identify reversible causes (4 H's, 4 T's)
(Continue cycles)

**4 H's:** Hypoxia, Hypovolemia, Hypo/hyperkalemia, Hypothermia
**4 T's:** Tension pneumothorax, Tamponade, Toxins, Thrombosis (PE)
```

### 3. Common Paediatric Emergencies

```markdown
FEBRILE SEIZURES (MOST COMMON PAEDIATRIC SEIZURE)

**DEFINITION:**
├─ Age: 6 months - 6 years
├─ Fever present (temp >38°C)
├─ Seizure type: Generalized tonic-clonic (usually)
├─ Duration: Usually <5 minutes (simple febrile seizure)
└─ No CNS infection (meningitis, encephalitis excluded)

**SIMPLE vs COMPLEX:**
├─ **Simple (90%):** <15 min, generalized, single episode in 24 hours
├─ **Complex (10%):** >15 min, focal, or >1 episode in 24 hours
└─ Complex → higher risk recurrence, consider LP (meningitis)

**MANAGEMENT:**
├─ **During seizure:** ABC, recovery position, oxygen, don't restrain
├─ **If >5 minutes (status epilepticus):**
│   ├─ Midazolam 0.5 mg/kg buccal/IM (max 10 mg)
│   └─ OR Diazepam 0.5 mg/kg PR (max 10 mg)
├─ **After seizure:** Identify fever source (viral URTI common)
├─ **Antipyretics:** Paracetamol 15 mg/kg, ibuprofen 10 mg/kg
└─ **Lumbar puncture:** If <12 months, complex features, or meningism

**PARENTAL EDUCATION:**
├─ Febrile seizures common (3-5% children)
├─ Usually outgrow by age 6
├─ No long-term brain damage
├─ Safety netting: If seizure >5 min, call ambulance
└─ Fever management (antipyretics, cool environment)

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

BRONCHIOLITIS (WINTER EPIDEMIC - RSV)

**DEFINITION:**
├─ Age: <2 years (peak 3-6 months)
├─ Viral LRTI (RSV 70%, rhinovirus, influenza)
├─ Wheeze + crackles + respiratory distress
└─ Coryzal prodrome (runny nose) → cough → wheeze (3-5 day progression)

**ASSESSMENT:**
├─ Work of breathing (recession, nasal flaring, grunting)
├─ Oxygen saturation (SpO2 <92% = admission threshold)
├─ Feeding (refusing feeds = severe, dehydration risk)
├─ Apnoeas (esp. ex-prem babies <6 months)
└─ Hydration status (urine output, fontanelle, mucous membranes)

**MANAGEMENT (SUPPORTIVE ONLY):**
├─ **Oxygen:** If SpO2 <92% (nasal prongs or high-flow)
├─ **Fluids:** NG or IV if unable to feed (<50% normal intake)
├─ **Suction:** Nasal suctioning before feeds (gentle)
├─ **NO salbutamol:** Ineffective (not asthma, different pathophysiology)
├─ **NO steroids:** Ineffective
├─ **NO antibiotics:** Viral (unless secondary bacterial infection)
└─ **Monitoring:** SpO2, RR, hydration

**ADMISSION CRITERIA:**
├─ SpO2 <92% in air
├─ Severe respiratory distress (exhaustion)
├─ Apnoeas
├─ Unable to feed (<50% intake)
└─ Age <3 months OR ex-premature OR cardiac/lung disease

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

CROUP (VIRAL LARYNGOTRACHEOBRONCHITIS)

**CLINICAL FEATURES:**
├─ Age: 6 months - 6 years (peak 2 years)
├─ Barking cough ("seal-like")
├─ Stridor (inspiratory)
├─ Hoarse voice
├─ Usually viral (parainfluenza)
└─ Worse at night

**SEVERITY ASSESSMENT (WESTLEY CROUP SCORE):**
├─ **Mild:** Barking cough, no stridor at rest, no recession
├─ **Moderate:** Stridor at rest, mild-moderate recession
├─ **Severe:** Stridor at rest, severe recession, agitation/lethargy, cyanosis
└─ **Impending respiratory failure:** Decreased stridor (exhaustion), drowsy, pale

**MANAGEMENT:**
├─ **All:** Dexamethasone 0.15 mg/kg PO/IV (single dose, effect 6-12 hours)
├─ **Moderate-severe:** Nebulized adrenaline 5 mL (1:1000 undiluted)
│   ├─ Onset: 10-30 minutes
│   ├─ Duration: 2-3 hours (observe 4 hours post-adrenaline for rebound)
│   └─ Can repeat hourly if needed
├─ **Keep calm:** Agitation worsens stridor (don't upset child)
└─ **Humidified oxygen:** If hypoxic (SpO2 <92%)

**ADMISSION CRITERIA:**
├─ Severe croup (stridor at rest, severe recession)
├─ Needed adrenaline nebulizers (observe 4 hours)
├─ Age <6 months
├─ Uncertain diagnosis (epiglottitis excluded)
└─ Social concerns (remote location, poor access to hospital)
```

### 4. Neonatal Emergencies

```markdown
NEONATAL RESUSCITATION (FIRST MINUTES OF LIFE)

**PREPARATION:**
├─ Warm environment (radiant warmer, 26-28°C room)
├─ Equipment check (bag-valve-mask, suction, intubation)
├─ Team (midwife, paediatrician, anaesthetist if high-risk)
└─ Anticipate problems (prematurity, meconium, congenital anomalies)

**APGAR SCORE (1 min, 5 min, 10 min if needed):**
| Sign | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|------|---|---|---|
| Heart rate | Absent | <100 bpm | >100 bpm |
| Respiratory effort | Absent | Weak, irregular | Strong, crying |
| Muscle tone | Limp | Some flexion | Active movement |
| Reflex irritability | None | Grimace | Cry, cough |
| Color | Blue/pale | Pink body, blue extremities | Completely pink |

**NEONATAL RESUSCITATION ALGORITHM:**
├─ **Birth:** Dry, warm, stimulate
├─ **30 seconds:** Assess breathing, heart rate
│   ├─ Breathing well, HR >100 → routine care (skin-to-skin)
│   └─ Gasping/not breathing OR HR <100 → positive pressure ventilation (PPV)
├─ **60 seconds:** If HR <100 despite PPV → check chest rise, reposition airway
├─ **90 seconds:** If HR <60 → chest compressions (3:1 ratio, 90 compressions + 30 breaths/min)
└─ **Adrenaline:** 10-30 microgram/kg IV (umbilical venous catheter) if HR <60 despite CPR

**MECONIUM ASPIRATION:**
├─ Thin meconium + vigorous baby → no suction (routine care)
├─ Thick meconium + non-vigorous → intubation + tracheal suction (before PPV)
└─ Risk: Meconium aspiration syndrome (chemical pneumonitis, persistent pulmonary hypertension)
```
