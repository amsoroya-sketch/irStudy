PROVISIONAL DIAGNOSIS

Because there is no physical examination, cannot distinguish between:

  • Septic arthritis
  • Osteomyelitis

To patient:

“Most likely the cause of your pain is an infection in the joint, which we call septic arthritis, or an infection in the bone, which we call osteomyelitis.”

Reasoning

  • Fever
  • Severe pain
  • Not able to bear weight

IMPLANT-RELATED DIFFERENTIAL

  • Infection around hip implant
  • Loosening of hip implant

🔬 Expert Review & Enhancements

Reviewed by: surgical-skills-expert (Australian surgical educator, FRACS, MSK focus) · fact-checked against the irStudy medical textbook RAG index.

✏️ Corrections

✏️ The case reaches 'septic arthritis or osteomyelitis' but does not name the most specific diagnosis for the scenario: a fever + non-weight-bearing hip in a patient with a total hip replacement is a PROSTHETIC JOINT INFECTION (PJI) until proven otherwise.
State prosthetic joint infection explicitly. Late haematogenous PJI (here plausibly seeded by recent dental work) is a surgical emergency: it requires urgent orthopaedic referral, inflammatory markers (CRP/ESR), blood cultures and image-guided joint aspiration BEFORE antibiotics if the patient is haemodynamically stable, because premature antibiotics reduce culture yield.
✏️ The implied teaching that dental surgery mandated antibiotic prophylaxis for the prosthesis is out of step with current Australian guidance.
Per current Therapeutic Guidelines (eTG) and Australian Dental Association advice, routine antibiotic prophylaxis before dental procedures is NOT recommended solely because a patient has a prosthetic joint. The dental work is relevant as a possible haematogenous source, but it should not be taught as a prophylaxis-failure.

⭐ Suggested Enhancements

⭐ Add explicit escalation/safety-netting: this is an unwell febrile patient who cannot weight bear - if signs of sepsis (fever, tachycardia, hypotension, confusion) treat as a medical emergency, call 000/arrange urgent hospital transfer, and manage along the sepsis pathway. Do not send home.
⭐ Broaden the male-specific groin differential the history should actively exclude and explain to the patient: strangulated/incarcerated inguinal or femoral hernia, testicular torsion or epididymo-orchitis, ureteric colic, and a psoas abscess - all can present as groin pain and some are surgical emergencies.
⭐ Add the initial investigation bundle so the candidate can justify the plan: FBC, CRP/ESR, blood cultures, urate, plain hip/pelvis X-ray (implant loosening/lucency), and urgent orthopaedic-guided aspiration; plus urinalysis if KUB cause considered.
⭐ Note the age/sex tailoring: at 60 with a THR, weight the differential toward implant-related infection/loosening and malignancy (metastatic/prostate) rather than the primary arthritides listed first - re-order the framework to lead with the red-flag/implant causes for this stem.
