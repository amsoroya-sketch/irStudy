Burning Foot Pain - Peripheral Sensory Neuropathy (Diabetes and Alcohol)

Source: Dr. Amir workshop — Med 5 MSK and urology / 3. Ankle, heel, foot (Aug 2026 drop) · Specialty: musculoskeletal

Foot Pain

Case Introduction

Your next patient is a 60 year old man who presents to your general practice complaining of burning pain on his feet.

Task:

  • Take history for 6 minutes
  • (Physical examination will appear on the screen)
  • Explain your diagnosis and differentials

Top two categories to think about:

  • Nerve problems

    – nerve entrapment

    – radiculopathy

    – neuropathy
  • Vascular problems

2. Core Concept for This Case

This is a peripheral sensory neuropathy type case.

Teacher’s question

“If a patient comes to you with pain in the foot and you find neurological deficit, and you think this is peripheral sensory neuropathy, what are your differentials other than diabetes, alcohol, B12, chemotherapy?”

Mentioned differentials:

  • Hypothyroidism
  • Uremia
  • Spinal nerve root pressure (radiculopathy)

History-Taking Framework

3.1 Opening

Start with:

  • Open-ended question
  • Address the patient’s concern

3.2 Pain Exploration – SIQORAA

You must explore pain using SIQORAA:

  • S – Site
  • I – Intensity
  • Q – Quality
  • O – Onset
  • R – Radiation
  • A – Aggravating / relieving factors
  • A - Affect in life

Also ask:

  • Timing
  • Pattern
  • Pain at night
  • Worse in morning or end of day
  • Worse with rest or activity
  • Limping
  • Ability to bear weight

3.3 General Musculoskeletal Question

Ask:

  • Swelling
  • Redness and rashes
  • Stiffness
  • Deformities
  • Difference between sides
  • History of trauma or injuries

4. Red Flags First

Even though it is burning pain:

Rule out:

  • Malignancy questions
  • Infection questions

5. Neurological Pathway (Main Focus of This Case)

5.1 Core Neurological Symptoms

Ask:

  • “Have you had any numbness or pins and needles in your foot?”
  • “Have you noticed any weakness in moving your ankle or foot?”

5.2 Look for Causes of Neuropathy

Back / Radiculopathy

“Do you have any back pain?”

(To rule out lumbar radiculopathy)

Diabetes

“Do you have any previous H/O diabetes?”

If No, screen:

  • “Are you passing more urine lately?”
  • “Are you feeling more thirsty?”

Alcohol

“Do you drink alcohol?”

If yes →

“How much?”

“How long have you been drinking?”

Vitamin B12 Deficiency

Ask about:

  • Diet

    “Are you a vegetarian?”
  • Past gastric surgery (from PMH)

Thyroid (Hypothyroidism)

“Do you have any weather preference?”

“Any cold intolerance?”

6. If Diabetes is Present

Once the patient says yes:

Ask:

  • “How long have you had diabetes?”
  • “What treatment are you on?”
  • “Are you compliant with your medications?”
  • “Do you have regular follow-ups and blood tests?”

Look for complications:

  • Retinopathy

    “Do you have any blurring of vision?”

7. Vascular Pathway

Think arterial disease.

Ask:

  • “Is the pain worse on walking?”
  • “Does it get better when you stop or rest?”
  • “Have you noticed any pallor or paleness in your legs?”

Risk factors:

  • Smoking
  • Alcohol (already asked)

8. Soft Tissue / Nerve Entrapment

Morton’s Neuroma

(Key soft-tissue cause of burning pain)

Ask:

  • “Do you wear tight shoes?”
  • “Is the pain worse when you wear shoes?”
  • “Is the pain better when you take them off?”

Also ask:

  • Excessive exercise
  • Occupation
  • Repetitive movements

Example used:

  • Common peroneal nerve entrapment in strawberry pickers → foot drop

9. Restless Leg Syndrome

Already asked about night pain.

Add:

“Does moving your legs or walking make the pain better at night?”

10. Other Systemic Causes

Liver Disease

Ask:

  • Yellowish discolouration of skin
  • Dark urine
  • Pale stools

Kidney Disease (uraemia of chronic kidney disease can cause neuropathy)

Ask:

  • Past history of kidney disease
  • Have u noticed a Decrease in amount of urine passed(late CKD)

Geriatric History (Because Patient Is Old)

Foot pain + neuropathy → falls risk

Ask:

  • “Have you had any falls?”
  • Home safety:
    • Stairs
    • Rugs
    • Lighting

Also:

  • Do u have any Vision problems
  • Do u have any Hearing problems
  • Support at home

History Findings

  • Known case of diabetes, not compliant to meds
  • Drinks 5-6 beers every day

Physical examination findings

  • Loss of sensation in both legs
  • Loss of vibration sense in both legs
  • Decreased lower-limb reflexes

Diagnosis

Do NOT label it as just diabetic neuropathy.

Correct diagnosis:

Sensory neuropathy

Because:

  • Diabetes
  • Alcohol

Both contribute.

Explanation to Patient (Example Dialogue)

“You most likely have a condition called sensory neuropathy.

This means the nerves in your feet and legs are damaged.

In your case, the most likely reasons are your high blood sugar levels from diabetes and your high alcohol intake.

These have damaged the nerves, and that is why you are getting this burning pain.

On examination, I also found that you have lost sensation and vibration in your legs, which supports this diagnosis.”

15. Differential Diagnosis List

A. Causes of Sensory Neuropathy

  • Diabetes
  • Alcohol
  • Vitamin B12 deficiency
  • Uremia
  • Hypothyroidism
  • Chronic liver disease

B. Other Causes of Burning Foot Pain

Nerve

  • Nerve entrapments (e.g. tarsal tunnel)
  • Lumbar radiculopathy
  • Traumatic nerve injury

Vascular

  • Peripheral arterial disease
  • Chilblains

Soft tissue

  • Morton’s neuroma

Others

  • Cancers (bone, malignancy)
  • Infections
  • Tendinitis
  • Bursitis
  • Other foot soft-tissue problems (make long list)

🔬 Expert Review & Enhancements

Reviewed by: surgical-skills-expert (Australian surgical educator, RACS/MSK focus) · fact-checked against the irStudy medical textbook RAG index.

✏️ Corrections

✏️ The neuropathy cause list is good but omits several common and reversible Australian causes and does not specify confirmatory investigations.
Add HbA1c and fasting/random glucose (diagnose/quantify diabetes), serum B12/folate, TSH, UEC/eGFR (uraemia), LFTs, and consider serum protein electrophoresis in older patients (paraproteinaemia). This converts the case's diet/history screen into a defensible workup.
✏️ The example 'common peroneal nerve entrapment in strawberry pickers -> foot drop' is offered within a burning/sensory neuropathy case.
Common peroneal palsy is predominantly a motor lesion (foot drop) with a sensory patch over the dorsum of the foot/lateral leg - it is not a cause of diffuse burning sensory neuropathy. Keep it as a contrasting mononeuropathy example, but do not conflate it with the length-dependent sensory polyneuropathy that is the actual diagnosis here.
✏️ No management or foot-protection plan is given for a non-compliant diabetic with established sensory loss.
Add: optimise glycaemic control (the single best measure to slow progression), address alcohol (brief intervention/referral), high-risk diabetic foot care (daily inspection, footwear, podiatry review), and neuropathic pain management per eTG (duloxetine, amitriptyline or pregabalin/gabapentin) with falls-risk mitigation in older patients.

⭐ Suggested Enhancements

⭐ Add a diabetic-foot risk stratification and screening tool (10 g monofilament + 128 Hz tuning fork vibration + pedal pulses/ABI) to the PEFE interpretation - the case already reports lost sensation/vibration and reduced reflexes, so naming the bedside tests strengthens the answer.
⭐ Add explicit falls-risk and PBS/eTG prescribing detail: pregabalin is PBS-listed for neuropathic pain; amitriptyline should be used with caution in the elderly (anticholinergic/orthostatic effects, falls). Include driving/sedation safety-netting.
⭐ Add the safety point that a diabetic with sensory neuropathy is at high risk of neuropathic ulceration and Charcot foot - build ulcer surveillance and urgent-review criteria (new ulcer, redness, deformity, warmth) into the plan.
