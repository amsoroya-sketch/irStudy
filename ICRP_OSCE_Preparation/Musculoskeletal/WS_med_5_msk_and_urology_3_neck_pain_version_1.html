Neck Pain (Version 1)

CASE STEM

Your next patient is a 40 year old lady who has presented to your General Practice complaining of neck pain for 2 days.

Task:

  • Take history from the patient - you will have a prompt time at 6 minutes
  • Physical examination will appear on the screen at 6 minutes
  • Explain your diagnosis and differentials to the patient
  • (Investigation has been asked in some exams)

Exam-Focused Differential Framework

Four Groups

1. Spine

  • Mechanical / non-specific neck pain
    Vertebral dysfunction
  • Cervical radiculopathy
  • Vertebral fracture

2. Red Flags

  • Cervical myelopathy
  • Malignancy & metastasis
  • Infection
  • Multiple myeloma (elderly)

3. Arthritis

  • Osteoarthritis (cervical spondylosis)
  • Rheumatoid arthritis
  • Psoriatic arthritis
  • Ankylosing spondylitis
  • Reactive arthritis

4. Referred Pain

  • Head
    • Meningitis
    • Tension headache
    • Subarachnoid hemorrhage
  • Cardiovascular
    • Angina
    • MI
    • Ischemic heart disease
  • Shoulder
    • Polymyalgia rheumatica
    • Rotator cuff disorders

KEY CONDITION REVISION

Cervical Myelopathy

  • Midline disc herniation
  • Compresses entire spinal cord
  • Red flag – needs urgent decompression
  • Features (in legs)
    • Walking difficulty
    • Balance issues
    • Leg pain
    • Weakness
    • Incontinence

Cervical Spondylosis

  • Degenerative disc & joint disease
  • Osteophytes
  • Same as osteoarthritis
  • Worse in morning
  • Improves with movement
  • Worse with heavy activity

Cervical Radiculopathy

  • Nerve root compression
  • Causes:
    • Disc prolapse
    • Tumor
    • Osteophytes (spondylosis)
  • Key feature: neurological deficit

Spinal Metastasis

  • Occurs in ~10% of cancer patients
  • Cervical spine = 15%
  • Most common:
    • Breast
    • Prostate
    • Lung

HISTORY-TAKING STRUCTURE

Opening

Doctor:

“How can I help you today?”

Patient:

“I’ve had neck pain for two days, it’s on the back of my neck, it’s so bad I can’t sleep and I’m worried.”

Doctor response (communication skill)

“I understand your concern. You did the right thing coming in. I’ll ask a few questions and examine you to find the cause.”

Block 1 – Explore the Pain

SIQORAA

Site

  • Front or back of neck?

Intensity

  • “On a scale of 1–10, how bad is the pain?”

Quality

  • Dull / sharp / burning?

Onset

  • When did it start?
  • Continuous or on / off?
  • Getting worse?
  • First episode or previous?

Radiation

  • Does it travel anywhere?

Alleviating & Aggravating

  • What makes it better or worse?
  • Coughing or sneezing?

Activity impact

  • Work and daily activities?

Pattern

  • Night pain?
  • Worse in the morning or end of day?
  • Worse after activity or rest?

Block 2 – General Musculoskeletal

  • Swelling around that area?
  • Redness?
  • Rashes?
  • Stiffness (must ask)
  • Recent trauma or injury?

Block 3 – Red Flags

Cervical Myelopathy

  • Difficulty walking?
  • Balance problems?
  • Loss of bladder or bowel control?
  • Leg weakness, numbness, pain?

Malignancy

  • Weight loss?
  • Loss of appetite?
  • Lumps and bumps?
  • Have you been feeling tired or Fatigue?
  • Previous cancers?
  • Long-term cough?
  • Previous history of Breast lumps?

Infection

  • Fever?
  • Chills?

Block 4 – Spine Differentials

Mechanical Neck Pain

  • Occupation?
  • Does your job involve Repetitive neck movements?
  • Any Excessive exercise u have been doing lately?

Cervical Radiculopathy

  • Pins and needles in hands?
  • Numbness?
  • Weakness in moving his arms and legs?

Block 5 – Arthritis

Osteoarthritis

  • Any grating or cracking sound when u move your neck

Rheumatoid Arthritis

  • Other joint pain?
  • Family history?

Psoriatic

  • Rashes on elbows or knees?

Ankylosing Spondylitis

  • Any Stiffness or Difficulty moving your neck?

Reactive Arthritis / IBD

  • Diarrhea?
  • Blood in stool?
  • Genital discharge?

Block 6 – Referred Pain

Head

  • Headache?

HISTORY FINDINGS

  • Pain on the back of the neck
  • After painting her house
  • Radiates to the right arm
  • Also feeling tingling and numbness in her hand
  • No redflags

→ Diagnosis: Cervical radiculopathy

PHYSICAL EXAMINATION

  • Limitation & restriction of neck movements on rotation and lateral flexion due to pain
  • Decreased sensation on thumb and outer forearm
  • Weak elbow flexion

Spurling Test

  • Lateral flexion + axial compression
  • Reproduces arm pain
  • Suggests cervical radiculopathy

DIAGNOSIS EXPLANATION

“You most likely have cervical radiculopathy.

This means a nerve exiting your spine is under pressure, usually from a tear in a cushion-like structure called a disc.

I think this because:

  • Your pain goes into the arm
  • You have numbness
  • I found weakness and sensory loss on examination.”

INVESTIGATIONS

Two key investigations

  1. Cervical spine MRI
  2. Cervical spine X-ray

Other tests for other differentials:

  • ESR
  • CRP
  • Rheumatoid factor
  • HLA-B27

(MRI + X-ray = main scoring investigations)

🔬 Expert Review & Enhancements

Reviewed by: surgical-skills-expert (Australian surgical educator, RACS/FRACS, MSK focus) · fact-checked against the irStudy medical textbook RAG index.

✏️ Corrections

✏️ The case states cervical myelopathy is caused by 'midline disc herniation compressing the entire spinal cord' and lists only leg symptoms.
In adults the commonest cause of cervical myelopathy is degenerative cervical spondylotic myelopathy (multilevel spondylosis/canal stenosis), not primarily acute midline disc herniation. Its hallmarks are UPPER motor neuron signs - hyperreflexia, positive Hoffmann and Babinski signs, clonus - together with loss of hand dexterity ('clumsy hands') and a broad-based unsteady gait, in addition to lower-limb symptoms. Add these to history and examination.
✏️ Spinal metastasis figures are muddled: 'occurs in ~10% of cancer patients / cervical spine = 15%'.
The cervical spine is the LEAST common site of spinal metastasis. Approximately 70% of spinal metastases involve the thoracic spine, ~20% the lumbar spine and only ~10% the cervical spine. Reword so the cervical spine is correctly presented as an uncommon (not 15%) site.
✏️ The Spurling test is described as 'lateral flexion + axial compression' only.
The Spurling manoeuvre is neck EXTENSION plus lateral flexion (and rotation) TOWARD the symptomatic side, then gentle axial compression; reproduction of radicular arm pain is positive. Omitting the extension/ipsilateral component makes the description technically incomplete.
✏️ Investigations state 'Cervical spine MRI + X-ray = main scoring investigations' for a 2-day history of radiculopathy with no red flags.
Per RACGP/eTG, acute cervical radiculopathy without red flags does not require early imaging - most settle within 6-12 weeks with conservative care. Reserve MRI (imaging of choice) for red flags, myelopathy, or progressive/severe or non-resolving neurological deficit after ~6 weeks. Plain X-ray adds little in this setting. Frame imaging as conditional, not routine.

⭐ Suggested Enhancements

⭐ Add the cervical myelopathy examination bundle (Hoffmann sign, Babinski, hyperreflexia, clonus, tandem gait, Romberg) and explicit safety-netting: suspected myelopathy is a red flag requiring urgent neurosurgical/spinal referral and MRI, since it is the can't-miss diagnosis in this framework.
⭐ Add carpal tunnel syndrome and thoracic outlet syndrome as differentials for the hand tingling, with bedside tests (Phalen and Tinel) to distinguish a peripheral median-nerve lesion from a C6/C7 nerve-root lesion - directly relevant given the thumb/lateral-forearm symptoms.
⭐ Add explicit dermatomal/myotomal mapping: thumb and lateral forearm sensory loss with weak elbow flexion (biceps) localises to the C6 root - teaching the candidate to name the root level strengthens the diagnosis-explanation task.
⭐ Add a management section for cervical radiculopathy (regular NSAID/paracetamol-plus-NSAID analgesia, activity modification, physiotherapy, reassurance about favourable natural history) since the current case stops at diagnosis and investigations.
⭐ Tag as a randomised multi-version station (Version 1 = radiculopathy) and cross-link to the Version 2 neck-pain case so the platform can present alternate findings on the same history framework.
