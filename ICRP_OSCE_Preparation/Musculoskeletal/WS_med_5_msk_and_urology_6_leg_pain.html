LEG PAIN

1. HOW TO THINK ABOUT LEG PAIN

1.1 Concept of Leg Pain in OSCE

  • Leg pain is not just knee, hip, foot or ankle pain
  • When the patient says “my whole leg”, you must think claudication
  • This case is built around:
    • Vascular claudication
    • Neurogenic claudication

Claudication = pain on walking

6. CLAUDICATION

6.1 Definition

  • Leg pain worse on walking, better on rest

6.2 VASCULAR CLAUDICATION (aortoiliac arterial disease)

Features

  • Worse walking uphill
  • Gets better immediately when standing still
  • Due to arterial disease

Key Questions are ruling out CVD risk factors.

  • Smoking
  • Alcohol
  • Diet
  • Exercise
  • Obesity
  • Family history of vascular disease
  • Medication: beta blockers

6.3 NEUROGENIC CLAUDICATION (Spinal canal stenosis)

Features

  • Pain on walking (uphill and downhill)
  • Better on:
    • Leaning forward
    • Bending
    • Lying down
  • Neurological deficit:
    • Numbness
    • Weakness

6.4 VENOUS DISORDER

Symptoms

  • Pain
  • Aching
  • Swelling
  • Skin dryness and itchiness
  • Muscle cramps
  • tightness

Signs

  • Dilated veins
  • Leg edema
  • Skin changes
  • Skin ulceration

Onset & Course

“How long have you had it?”

“Is it getting worse?”

Radiation

Aggravating / Alleviating

“Anything that makes it better or worse?”

Specific:

  • Is pain worse on Sitting or Standing?
  • Does it get better on Leaning forward?

Affect in life

“How is this affecting your walking and daily activities?”

HISTORY FINDINGS

  • Pain for 2 months, on both legs
  • Worse on walking
  • Better with rest
  • Pain at night
  • Has to wake up and hang legs
  • Smoker
  • On beta blockers

Physical Examination

  • On inspection legs are pale, hair loss seen
  • CRT more than 3 sec
  • Absent pulses on both legs
  • Both feet are cold
  • + Buerger's test
  • + femoral bruits

9. DIAGNOSIS

Peripheral Arterial Disease

10. EXPLANATION TO PATIENT

“You most likely have a condition called peripheral arterial disease. The blood tubes in your legs have been damaged by your diabetes, blood pressure, smoking and high cholesterol. Reasons: That’s why you get pain on walking and relief when you rest. On examination, your pulses were weak and a special test called Buerger’s test was positive.”

  • CRT more than 3 sec
  • Absent pulses on both legs
  • Both feet are cold
  • + Buerger's test
  • + femoral bruits
  • You can also see a rash on the leg with pus and discharge
  • Office test + gluc

DIAGNOSIS

PAD + Cellulitis

Note: this is a mixed case that can be presented in exam, other possibility can be PAD plus diabetes sensory neuropathy. Because the risk factor for peripheral artery disease and diabetic neuropathy is the same, and also when we get an ulcer with peripheral artery disease, it can get infected and also cause cellulitis. So all these factors are similar, that’s why this mixed case can be present.

EXPLAINING TO PATIENT

“Explain peripheral artery disease, same as previously. For cellulitis, explain : I also found features of an infection in soft tissues of your legs that we call cellulitis.”

🔬 Expert Review & Enhancements

Reviewed by: surgical-skills-expert (Australian surgical educator, RACS/MSK focus) · fact-checked against the irStudy medical textbook RAG index.

✏️ Corrections

✏️ Case Three states spinal canal stenosis is 'most likely caused by a ruptured disc'.
Lumbar spinal canal stenosis in this age group is predominantly degenerative (facet joint osteoarthritis, ligamentum flavum hypertrophy and disc bulging/spondylosis), producing neurogenic claudication. An acute ruptured (herniated) disc typically causes a radiculopathy, not the classic posture-dependent neurogenic claudication described. Reword the patient explanation to 'wear-and-tear narrowing of the spinal canal'.
✏️ The physical examination names 'Buerger's test' correctly here but the linked PEFE case (case 8) spells it 'Berger's test'.
The correct eponym is Buerger's test (elevation pallor / dependent rubber in critical limb ischaemia), named after Leo Buerger. Standardise spelling across the linked cases to avoid confusion with Berger disease (IgA nephropathy).
✏️ PAD is diagnosed clinically with no investigation or secondary-prevention plan stated.
Add Australian-standard PAD workup and management: resting ABI (and exercise ABI if borderline), arterial duplex ultrasound, and cardiovascular secondary prevention per eTG/RACGP - smoking cessation, a statin, antiplatelet therapy, BP and glycaemic control, and a supervised exercise program; urgent vascular referral if critical limb ischaemia (rest pain, ulceration, gangrene).

⭐ Suggested Enhancements

⭐ Add the vascular-vs-neurogenic claudication discriminators as an examinable table: vascular = calf pain after a fixed walking distance, relieved by standing still, worse uphill, absent pulses/bruits/ABI < 0.9; neurogenic = pain with standing/extension, relieved by sitting/flexion (shopping-trolley sign), normal pulses, positive on spinal flexion.
⭐ For Case Two (PAD + cellulitis) add eTG-aligned cellulitis management: mark the border, elevate, and treat with flucloxacillin (or cefalexin), plus urgent review given the ischaemic limb and diabetes (higher risk of necrotising infection / poor healing) - the case names the diagnosis but gives no antibiotic plan.
⭐ Add 'pain at night relieved by hanging the legs out of bed' as the cardinal symptom of critical limb ischaemia (ischaemic rest pain) and flag it as a red flag mandating urgent vascular referral - the history findings describe exactly this.
