Heel Pain with Prior Ankle Fracture (Post-traumatic Osteoarthritis)

Source: Dr. Amir workshop — Med 5 MSK and urology / 3. Ankle, heel, foot (Aug 2026 drop) · Specialty: musculoskeletal

HEEL PAIN

Exam Case Stem

Your next patient is a 40 year old man who presents to you complaining of heel pain. He has a past history of ankle injury/fx 10 years ago.

Task:

  • Take history for 6 minutes
  • Physical examination will appear on the screen at 6 minutes
  • •Explain diagnosis and differentials to the patient

3. Top Differentials for Heel Pain

From the general foot & ankle differential list:

Top three you MUST mention

  1. Tender Heel pad (tender fat pad)
  2. Plantar fasciitis
  3. Achilles tendonitis

Heel pain is not always the sole.

It can be back of heel (Achilles) or bottom of heel (sole)

All other:

  • Arthritis
  • Infection
  • Malignancy

    …and so on

HISTORY FINDINGS

  • Heel looks bigger and wider on the affected side
  • Fracture 10 years ago
  • Pain worse on walking
  • Better with rest

Physical Examination

  • Heel is widened
  • Range of motion restricted and painful in all directions
    • Inversion: 20°
    • Eversion: 10°
    • Plantarflexion: 20°
    • Dorsiflexion: 20°

Why This Is NOT Tendonitis

Tendonitis only affects one movement direction

  • Achilles → plantarflexion
  • Tibial → inversion
  • Peroneal → eversion

But here:

ALL FOUR movements are painful and restricted

So this is a JOINT problem, not tendon

Key Diagnostic Logic

Restricted and painful movement in all directions

  • Joint widened (deformity)
  • Old fracture

    = Post-traumatic osteoarthritis

Final Diagnosis

Post-traumatic osteoarthritis of the ankle/heel

How to Explain to the Patient

“Your old fracture damaged the joint.”

“That damage increases the risk of osteoarthritis.”

“Osteoarthritis is wear and tear of the joint.”

“Because the joint is damaged, it becomes inflamed and painful more easily.”

Justification to Examiner

  • Heel is deformed (widened)
  • Previous fracture
  • All movements painful and restricted
  • This means joint inflammation, not tendon or muscle

Investigations (from early recalls)

  • X-ray → arthritis
  • Ultrasound → ankle ligaments

    (MRI possible but not required)

🔬 Expert Review & Enhancements

Reviewed by: surgical-skills-expert (Australian surgical educator, RACS/MSK focus) · fact-checked against the irStudy medical textbook RAG index.

✏️ Corrections

✏️ The physical examination lists 'restricted and painful movement in all directions' but records Dorsiflexion 20 degrees, which is a normal ankle dorsiflexion value.
Normal ankle ROM is roughly dorsiflexion 15-20 degrees and plantarflexion 40-50 degrees, with subtalar inversion ~30 degrees and eversion ~15-20 degrees. Of the values given, plantarflexion 20 degrees and inversion 20 degrees are genuinely reduced, but dorsiflexion 20 degrees is within normal limits. The teaching point ('all four directions restricted') should be reconciled with the recorded numbers or the numbers corrected.
✏️ Top-three heel differentials list 'tender heel pad' first and imply equal weighting.
For inferior (plantar) heel pain the single most common cause in Australian general practice is plantar fasciitis; it should be framed as the lead probability diagnosis, with tender fat pad and calcaneal stress fracture as alternatives. Plantar fasciitis is a clinical diagnosis - imaging is not routinely required.
✏️ Given a prior ankle fracture and heel pain worse on walking, calcaneal stress fracture is not listed.
Add calcaneal/tarsal stress fracture to the differential (positive squeeze test, night/rest pain, X-ray may be normal early - MRI or delayed X-ray confirms). This is a relevant 'not to be missed' cause in a patient with prior bony injury and weight-bearing pain.

⭐ Suggested Enhancements

⭐ Add investigation rationale aligned to the final diagnosis: weight-bearing ankle/foot X-ray is the key test for post-traumatic osteoarthritis (joint space narrowing, osteophytes, prior fracture deformity); note MRI/US are for soft-tissue or occult stress-fracture uncertainty, not routine.
⭐ Add a management/counselling limb the case omits: analgesic ladder (paracetamol first-line, topical/oral NSAID if tolerated per eTG), weight management, footwear/orthotic and podiatry referral, physiotherapy, and orthopaedic referral for refractory post-traumatic ankle/subtalar OA (e.g. fusion/arthroplasty discussion).
⭐ Clarify the calcaneal-widening finding: a widened heel with prior fracture suggests malunited calcaneal fracture leading to subtalar post-traumatic OA - naming the subtalar joint sharpens the diagnostic reasoning for the examiner.
