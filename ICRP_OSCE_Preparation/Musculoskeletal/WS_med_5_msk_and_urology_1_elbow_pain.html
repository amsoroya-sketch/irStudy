Elbow Pain (Olecranon Bursitis)

Source: Dr. Amir workshop — Med 5 MSK and urology / 2. MSK UL LL (Aug 2026 drop) · Specialty: musculoskeletal

ELBOW PAIN

CASE STEM

Your next patient is a 30 year old man who has presented to your General Practice complaining of pain in his elbow.

Task:

  • Take history for 6 minutes
  • Physical examination will appear on the screen at 6 minutes
  • Explain your diagnosis and differentials

DIFFERENTIAL FRAMEWORK

You must organise elbow pain into 4 groups

  1. Soft tissue
  2. Joint
  3. Red flags
  4. Referred pain

1. SOFT TISSUE

  • Medial epicondylitis
  • Lateral epicondylitis
  • Olecranon bursitis
  • Biceps tendonitis
  • Osteochondritis

2. JOINT (ARTHRITIS)

  • Osteoarthritis
  • Rheumatoid arthritis
  • Psoriatic arthritis
  • Gout
  • Inflammatory bowel disease–reactive arthritis

3. RED FLAGS

Malignancy

  • Bone tumors
  • Metastasis
  • Lung cancer
  • Pancost tumor

Infections

  • Septic arthritis
  • Osteomyelitis
  • Cellulitis

4. REFERRED PAIN

  • Cervical radiculopathy
  • CVS: Ischemic heart disease
  • Respiratory (pancoast tumor)
  • Upper limb DVT (acute effort thrombosis)

HISTORY-TAKING STRUCTURE

1. INTRODUCTION

Doctor

“Hi, my name is Amir. Can you tell me more about your elbow pain today?”

Patient

“Doctor, I’ve had pain for a few days, it’s at the back of my elbow and it’s getting worse.”

Empathy

“I’m sorry to hear that. I understand your concern. I’ll ask you a few questions and try to find the cause.”

2. CHARACTERISTICS OF PAIN

Site

  • Front / back / side of elbow

Intensity

“On a scale of 1 to 10?”

Quality

“Is it dull, sharp, burning?”

Onset (4 questions)

  • When did it start?
  • Is it constant or on and off?
  • Is it getting worse?
  • First episode or previous episodes?

Radiation

“Does it travel anywhere?”

Aggravating / Relieving

“What makes it better or worse?”

Affect on life

“How is this affecting your work and daily activities?”

3. PATTERN OF PAIN (Inflammatory vs Mechanical)

  1. Night pain

“Does it wake you up at night?”

  1. Morning vs evening

“Is it worse in the morning or later in the day?”

  1. Rest vs activity

“Is it worse after resting or after activity?”

4. GENERAL MUSCULOSKELETAL QUESTIONS

  • Have u noticed any Swelling in elbow?
  • Redness or rash?
  • Stiffness?
  • Trauma or injury?

5. RED FLAGS

Malignancy

  • Weight loss
  • Loss of appetite
  • Lumps & bumps
  • Fatigue

Metastasis

  • Chronic cough
  • Breast lump
  • Urinary symptoms> do u have any problem with urination? > just 1 question

Infection

  • Fever
  • Chills

6. SOFT TISSUE QUESTIONS

Occupation

“What do you do for work?”

Repetitive wrist movement> condylitis

“Do you do repeated wrist or elbow movements?”

Pressure on elbow> bursitis

“Do you lean on your elbow for long periods?”

Exercise

“Any heavy lifting or sports?”

Osteochondritis

“Any locking of the elbow?”

Gout trigger

“Does red meat or alcohol trigger the pain?”

7. JOINT-SPECIFIC

Reactive arthritis / IBD

  • Diarrhea
  • Blood in stool
  • Vaginal or penile discharge
  • STI history: are you sexually active? Do you practice safe sex and use condom?

8. REFERRED PAIN

Cervical spine> cervical radiculopathy

  • Any Neck pain>
  • Shoulder pain
  • Pins and needles
  • Numbness
  • Weakness in moving arms and hands

Cardiac

  • Chest pain
  • Shortness of breath on exertion

Respiratory (pancoast)

  • Cough
  • Hemoptysis: have you ever coughed up blood?

Upper limb DVT

  • Have u had any recent Repetitive upper-limb movements

9. ENDING HISTORY

  • Smoking
  • Alcohol
  • Drugs
  • Medications (statins)
  • Past medical history
  • Family history

Lateral Epicondylitis (topic review)

  • Occupation: Carpenter, cleaner, gardener, dentist
  • Symptoms: Rest pain and pain at night, pain on gripping, picking up objects, turning door handles
  • Signs: Pain on passive wrist flexion
  • Pain on resisted wrist extension

History Findings

  • Severe pain in his left elbow
  • Bumped his elbow in the door knob
  • Cant play golf
  • Hx of gout attacks in his foot
  • No other injuries

Physical Examination

  • Tender fluctuant swelling on olecranon
  • Limited elbow flexion

PROVISIONAL DIAGNOSIS

Olecranon bursitis

EXPLANATION TO PATIENT

“You most likely have a condition called olecranon bursitis.

A bursa is a cushion-like structure around the joint that absorbs pressure.

Repeated movements, direct pressure or injury can inflame it.

Your gout also increases the risk of bursitis.

On examination, the swelling and pain over the back of your elbow explains your pain.”

🔬 Expert Review & Enhancements

Reviewed by: surgical-skills-expert (Australian surgical educator, FRACS, MSK focus) · fact-checked against the irStudy medical textbook RAG index.

✏️ Corrections

✏️ The case lists an acute, tender, fluctuant olecranon swelling in a patient with gout but gives no plan to exclude SEPTIC olecranon bursitis, which is the key not-to-be-missed diagnosis in a red/hot/tender bursa.
In Australian practice a hot, tender, fluctuant olecranon bursa should be aspirated for MC&S, Gram stain and crystal examination before deciding management. Septic bursitis (commonly Staph aureus) and gouty/crystal bursitis are the two leading acute causes and cannot be distinguished clinically. Empirical antibiotics per eTG (e.g. di/flucloxacillin) only after aspiration if infection is suspected.
✏️ Spelling and referral logic: 'Pancost tumor' is misspelt and a Pancoast (superior sulcus) tumour classically causes shoulder/medial arm and C8-T1 pain, not isolated elbow pain.
Correct spelling is 'Pancoast tumour'. It is a legitimate apical-lung red flag to screen for in referred UPPER-LIMB pain, but frame it as shoulder/arm/hand (ulnar) pain with Horner syndrome and wasting of intrinsic hand muscles rather than elbow-specific pain.

⭐ Suggested Enhancements

⭐ Add an investigation/management step: serum urate (note urate is often normal during an acute gout flare), FBC/CRP if infection suspected, bursal aspiration, and plain elbow X-ray if trauma or to exclude a bony spur/loose body. Management of non-septic olecranon bursitis = rest, avoid pressure/leaning, compression, NSAID or paracetamol, aspiration if tense; treat underlying gout.
⭐ Add explicit red-flag safety-netting and the not-to-be-missed elbow diagnoses missing from the framework: septic olecranon bursitis, septic arthritis of the elbow, and referred cardiac ischaemia (left arm) — with advice to call 000 if chest pain/breathlessness develops.
⭐ Include a brief structured examination checklist (look-feel-move, resisted wrist extension for lateral epicondylitis, resisted flexion/pronation for medial epicondylitis, palpate olecranon and epicondyles, assess ulnar nerve at cubital tunnel) so the candidate can interpret the PE findings shown at 6 minutes.
⭐ Tag the internal teaching inconsistency: the 'Lateral Epicondylitis topic review' block sits inside a case whose history findings and PE (fluctuant olecranon swelling, gout) point to olecranon bursitis — clarify for learners that the review is background, not the diagnosis.
