1. HOW THIS CLUSTER IS ORGANISED

Acute vs Chronic in Musculoskeletal

  • Musculoskeletal cases can be divided into:
    • Traumatic / acute
    • Non-traumatic / chronic

In this cluster we are doing traumatic knee pain.

2. CASE FRAME

Case 1 – Opening Stem

  • 20-year-old patient
  • Right knee pain
  • On open-ended question, you expect trauma

Tasks

  • 6-minute history
  • Physical examination
  • Physical exam is confusing in acute knee injuries

3. WHY HISTORY IS THE KEY

Murtagh says history is the key to diagnosis

Why not physical examination?

  • Patient was tackled two hours ago
  • Severe pain
  • You cannot touch the knee
  • Special tests cannot be done
    • Anterior drawer
    • McMurray
    • Lachman

➡ So diagnosis comes from HISTORY

4. WHAT PART OF HISTORY IS MOST IMPORTANT

Mechanism of Injury

You must get a careful description of how the injury happened

These mechanisms point to different injuries:

  • Twisting → meniscus
  • Certain patterns → ACL

5. INJURY MECHANISM – EXACT QUESTIONS

General

“Explain in detail what happened.”

Ask:

  • Was it painful?
  • Was there a direct blow?
  • Did you jump and land awkwardly?
  • Did you twist your leg?

ACL-specific

  • Did you hear a popping or snapping sound?
  • Did you feel wobbly or unsteady?
  • (Optional) Did your bones separate momentarily?

Swelling

How soon after the injury did swelling appear?

This is critical

6. WHY SWELLING TIMING MATTERS

  • Knee swelling = bleeding in the joint
  • Ligaments are vascular
  • ACL tear → rapid bleeding → rapid swelling

Timeframe

  • Rapid painful swelling (< 30 minutes) → ACL tear
  • Meniscus
    • Swelling can happen
    • Minimal
    • Late (after a few hours)

7. MECHANISM OF MENISCAL TEAR

  • Twisting
  • Rotatory movement
  • Semi-flexed, weight-bearing knee

“If someone twisted their leg → most of the time it’s a meniscus.”

8. MENISCAL SPECIAL TESTS (WHY THEY’RE NOT USEFUL ACUTELY)

  • Apley grinding
  • McMurray
  • Thessaly

A patient with acute knee trauma will not let you do these

9. MENISCAL TEAR – HISTORY FEATURES

  • Joint line pain
    • Medial meniscus → medial joint line
    • Lateral meniscus → lateral joint line
  • Locking
  • Restricted movement
  • Late swelling

Torn meniscus behaves like a foreign body in the joint

11. ACL TEAR

Why it matters

  • Unstable knee
  • Rapid surgery in Australia
  • Prevents traumatic osteoarthritis

12. ACL MECHANISMS

Three patterns

  1. Sudden deceleration + change of direction
  2. Jump and land awkwardly
  3. Direct blow → valgus + hyperextension

13. ACL CLINICAL FEATURES

  • Severe pain
  • Cannot bear weight
  • Popping sound
  • Immediate large swelling
  • Giving way / instability
  • Diffuse joint line tenderness
  • Large effusion

14. ACL SPECIAL TESTS

  • Anterior/ posterior drawer
  • Lachman

Lachman = anterior drawer in a more extended knee

15. COLLATERAL LIGAMENTS

  • Mechanism: direct valgus or varus blow
  • Joint line pain
  • ± effusion
  • Test: Varus / Valgus stress

16. OTTAWA RULES – KNEE

X-ray if:

  • Older age
  • Fibular head tenderness
  • Isolated patellar tenderness
  • Cannot flex knee 90°
  • Cannot bear weight immediately AND now

17. HISTORY TAKING STRUCTURE

Opening> how I can help u today?

“How can I help you today?”

Patient: I was playing football/ rugby and fell after that I have pain in my knee

“I was playing soccer and I injured my knee.”

Doctor: address concern, do u need painkillers now

“I understand your concern. I’ll ask a few questions to understand what’s happening.”

18. PAIN – SOCRATES

  • Site (front / side / back)
  • Onset
  • Character
  • Radiation (hip / ankle)
  • Severity
  • Time
  • Exacerbating / relieving
  • Weight-bearing> r u able to bear weight?
  • Limping

19. KEY PART – INJURY DETAILS

Ask: can u describe the injury in detail

  • Was it a Direct blow?
  • Did u twist your leg?
  • Did u Jump and land awkwardly?
  • Did u hear a Popping sound?
  • How soon after the injury did swelling appear?
  • Were u Able to bear weight immediately after injury and now?
  • did u feel as if your bones separated?

20. GENERAL MUSCULOSKELETAL

  • Swelling on knee
  • Redness on knee
  • Rash
  • Have u noticed any Deformity when u compare right and left knee?
  • Stiffness or difficulty in moving

21. THREE CRITICAL QUESTIONS

  1. Does your knee Give way / does your knee feel unstable
  2. Have u noticed any Locking in the knee?
  3. Did u hear any Clicking / cracking / grating sound on moving your knee?

OTHER INJURY(MSK)> did u have any injury to your head? Any headache?, LOC

Any hip pain, back pain?

22. NEUROVASCULAR

  • Weakness in moving your legs?
  • Pins & needles and Numbness in legs?
  • Have u noticed any Colour change in knee or leg

23. CASE 1 – MENISCAL TEAR

History

  • Twisting injury
  • Pain on the inner side of knee

Exam

  • Swelling on knee
  • Flexed knee
  • Tenderness on Medial joint line
  • Movements restricted
  • Special Tests not possible

Provisional Diagnosis

Meniscal tear

Explanation to patient

Meniscus is a cushion like structure in the knees that absorb pressure, in your case it is torn

“The meniscus is a cushion-like structure that absorbs pressure in the knee. You’ve most likely torn it.”

Reasoning

  • Twisting mechanism injury
  • I can feel pain on one side of joint

24. CASE 2 – ACL

History

  • Playing netball, Suddenly turn to right
  • Heard Popping sound
  • Severe pain
  • Immediate swelling
  • Could not bear weight

Exam

  • Big effusion
  • Patella tap positive
  • Movements restricted

Provisional Diagnosis

ACL tear

Explanation

A ligament is a rope like structure that connects two bones to each other, in your case ACL ligament is torn.

“A ligament is a rope-like structure that connects two bones. You’ve most likely torn your ACL.”

Reasons

  • Rapid swelling
  • Mechanism of injury
  • Instability

25. OTHER ACL VERSIONS

  • Lachman positive
  • Anterior drawer positive

26. TRAUMATIC KNEE DIFFERENTIALS

  • Meniscal tear
  • ACL / PCL tear
  • MCL / LCL tear
  • Patellar dislocation
  • Patellar tendon rupture
  • Tendon sprain / strain
  • Fractures (tibia, femur, knee joint)
  • Ruptured Baker cyst

✏️ Corrections

✏️ The Ottawa Knee Rule is listed with a vague 'older age' criterion.
The Ottawa Knee Rule specifies age 55 years or older (not simply 'older'). The full rule = knee X-ray if ANY of: age >=55; isolated patellar tenderness; fibular head tenderness; inability to flex the knee to 90 degrees; or inability to bear weight (4 steps) both immediately after injury AND in the assessment setting.
✏️ 'ACL tear -> rapid surgery in Australia' overstates urgency and misrepresents current practice.
Acute ACL rupture is NOT a surgical emergency. Standard Australian care is initial RICE, analgesia, and orthopaedic referral; reconstruction is an ELECTIVE decision made after swelling settles and a period of prehabilitation, and is prioritised for younger/active patients or ongoing instability. Many patients are managed with structured rehabilitation. Reword to avoid implying immediate operation.

⭐ Suggested Enhancements

⭐ Add a first-line management plan the station reward: RICE, paracetamol +/- short NSAID course for analgesia, knee immobiliser/crutches with protected weight-bearing, and orthopaedic/physiotherapy referral; MRI to confirm meniscal/ACL injury when clinically indicated after acute swelling settles.
⭐ Explicitly apply the Ottawa Knee Rule to the two worked cases to justify (or withhold) X-ray, and add fracture screening (tibial plateau, patella) to the differential list already provided.
⭐ Sharpen the swelling-timing teaching: a rapid, tense HAEMARTHROSIS within ~2 hours strongly suggests ACL rupture (or osteochondral fracture/patellar dislocation), whereas a delayed effusion over hours favours meniscal injury - and note the classic triad of ACL rupture: a 'pop', immediate swelling and giving-way/instability.
⭐ Add a neurovascular-and-not-to-be-missed prompt for knee trauma: always document distal pulses and peroneal nerve function, and consider knee dislocation (with popliteal artery injury) after high-energy trauma or gross instability as a limb-threatening emergency requiring urgent transfer/000.
