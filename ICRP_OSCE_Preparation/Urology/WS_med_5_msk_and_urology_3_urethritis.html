Urethritis (Dysuria and Urethral Discharge)

Source: Dr. Amir workshop — Med 5 MSK and urology / 5. urology (Aug 2026 drop) · Specialty: urology

URETHRITIS

Exam Stem

Your next patient is a 25 year old boy who has presented to your General Practice wanting to discuss about his urinary problems.

Task:

  • Take history for 6 minutes, you will have a prompt time at 6 minutes
  • Explain your diagnosis and differentials to the patient

Differential Diagnosis

Group 1: Urinary Tract Infections (UTIs)

  • Lower UTI → Cystitis
  • Upper UTI → Pyelonephritis

Reason for terminology:

  • Similar presentation
  • Very different severity and management

Group 2: STI-Related Infections

  • Urethritis
  • Epididymo-orchitis (EO)

Preferred terminology over “STIs”

  • Chlamydia, Gonorrhoea → urethritis
  • Deeper spread → epididymo-orchitis

Group 3: Other Important Causes

  • Prostatitis
  • Ureteric colic
  • Skin conditions & skin cancers
  • Other STIs with skin lesions (e.g. herpes)
  • Balanitis / contact dermatitis

History-Taking Structure

Initial Clarification from Patient

Open-Ended Question

Example dialogue

“How can I help you today?”

“Can you tell me more about your urinary problem?”

Patient’s Response

  • Pain, burning, and stinging when passing urine
  • Duration: 7 days
  • Not improving
  • Patient is worried

1. Addressing Patient’s Concern

Example dialogue

“I understand your concern. I can imagine how stressful this is. Thank you for coming in. Let me ask you a few questions so we can figure out what’s happening and make a good plan together.”

2. Exploring the Presenting Complaint (Dysuria)

Pain Character / Quality

  • Burning or stinging while passing urine
  • Difficult, painful urination with straining (suggests prostatitis)

Example questions

  • “Is it a burning sensation when you pass urine?”
  • “Is it difficult or painful to empty your bladder?”

Timing

  • Onset: When did it start?
  • Duration: 7 days
  • Pattern:
    • On and off or Every time passing urine
  • Progression:
    • Getting worse day by day?

Aggravating & Alleviating Factors

  • Asked routinely as part of complaint exploration

3. UTI-Focused Questions

Cystitis Features

  • Frequency
    • “Are you passing urine more frequently?”
  • Urgency
    • “Do you feel a sudden urge to pass urine?”
  • Suprapubic pain
    • “Do you have any pain in your lower abdomen?”

Pyelonephritis Features

  • Fever and chills
  • Nausea and vomiting
  • Flank / loin pain

4. Urethritis & Epididymo-Orchitis

Key Differentiating Feature

  • Penile discharge

Example question

  • “Have you noticed any discharge from your penis?”

Epididymo-Orchitis

  • Scrotal swelling
  • Scrotal redness
  • Scrotal or testicular pain

5. KEY PASS COMPONENT – Sexual History

Must be detailed

Not 1–2 questions

Failure to do this properly → fail the case

Introductory Sensitivity & Confidentiality Statement

Example dialogue

“I need to ask you some sensitive questions about your sex life. Is that okay with you?”

“I want to reassure you that everything we discuss today will be kept confidential.”

Sexual History – Structured Groups

A. Sexual Activity

  • “Are you sexually active?”
  • Backup:
    • “Have you ever been sexually active?”

B. Partner History

  • “Do you have a stable partner?”
  • “Have you ever had multiple partners?”

C. Partner Gender

  • “Are your partners male or female?”

Importance:

  • Men who have sex with men → higher STI risk
  • Different screening and management

D. Protection / Safe Sex

  • “Do you use condoms?”
  • “Do you practice safe sex?”

E. Type of Sexual Activity

  • Do you practice anal / vaginal / oral sex?

Importance:

  • Determines sites for swabs (urethral, throat, anal)

F. High-Risk Sexual Behaviour

  • Have u ever had Sex under influence of alcohol or drugs

  • Have u ever had Sex with sex workers

6. Prostatitis Assessment

Lower Urinary Tract Symptoms

  • Any Dribbling of urine
  • Weak stream
  • Difficulty emptying bladder
  • Do u Feel your bladder does not empty completely

Deep Pelvic Pain

  • “Do you feel any pain around your back passage?”

7. Ureteric Colic

  • Hematuria > blood in urine
  • Flank / loin pain
  • Past history of kidney stones
  • Family history of kidney stones

8. Other STIs with Skin Lesions

Herpes / Painful Ulcers

  • “Have you noticed any rashes on your penis?”
  • “Have you noticed any painful lumps in your groin?”

9. Skin Cancers & Systemic Features

  • Weight loss
  • Loss of appetite
  • Lumps in groin or elsewhere

Also relevant for HIV considerations

10. Balanitis / Contact Dermatitis

  • R u using any New shampoo or body wash
  • New underwear material
  • Redness of skin or foreskin

11. Closing the History

General Questions

  • Smoking
  • Alcohol
  • Drugs
  • Medications (can cause skin eruptions)
  • Past medical history
  • Family history

History Findings

  • Pain on passing urine
  • Frequent urination
  • I had discharge a week ago but got better by itself
  • Sexually active
  • I have multiple partners
  • Never use condoms

Diagnosis

Correct Diagnosis

  • Acute urethritis

How to Explain to the Patient

Example explanation

“Most likely, you have a condition called urethritis. This is an infection of the urethra, which is the tube that carries urine out of the body. This infection is usually caused by a sexually transmitted infection.”

Reasoning for Diagnosis

  • U have pain passing urine
  • Increased frequency
  • History of penile discharge
  • High-risk sexual behaviour:
    • Multiple partners
    • No condom use

Explaining Differentials (Brief & Selective)

UTI (Cystitis / Pyelonephritis)

  • Infection of bladder or kidney
  • Less likely:
    • No fever
    • No chills
    • No abdominal pain

Epididymo-Orchitis

  • Infection of testis and tubes
  • Less likely:
    • No scrotal swelling
    • No scrotal redness

Other Differentials (List Form)

  • Prostatitis
  • Kidney stones
  • Balanitis (skin irritation of penis / foreskin)
  • Skin cancers

🔬 Expert Review & Enhancements

Reviewed by: surgical-skills-expert (Australian surgical educator, FRACS) · fact-checked against the irStudy medical textbook RAG index.

✏️ Corrections

✏️ The causative-organism list for urethritis names only Chlamydia and Gonorrhoea and is now incomplete/outdated.
Non-gonococcal urethritis is increasingly caused by Mycoplasma genitalium (a recognised, macrolide-resistant pathogen) and also Trichomonas vaginalis and adenovirus. Australian STI guidelines now recommend testing for M. genitalium (with macrolide-resistance testing) when NGU does not respond to first-line therapy.
✏️ The case ends at explaining the diagnosis and does not mention partner notification/contact tracing, which is a mandatory component of STI management in Australia.
For any diagnosed or strongly suspected STI, arrange partner notification/contact tracing (patient-referral or provider-referral, or the Let Them Know service) and treat contacts empirically - this is expected best practice and is often scored.

⭐ Suggested Enhancements

⭐ Add the Australian empirical management the station's diagnosis implies (per Therapeutic Guidelines / ASHM): first-pass urine NAAT for chlamydia/gonorrhoea plus urethral swab MCS; treat presumptively - doxycycline 100 mg BD for 7 days for chlamydia/NGU, and ceftriaxone 500 mg IM stat plus azithromycin 1 g for gonorrhoea if suspected. Offer a full STI screen (HIV, syphilis serology, hepatitis B) and check hepatitis B immune status.
⭐ Add reactive arthritis and Mycoplasma genitalium to the differential/complication discussion (the RAG dysuria table explicitly lists reactive arthritis and adenovirus urethritis as pitfalls), and prompt the candidate to advise abstinence until treatment completed and contacts treated.
⭐ Metadata to add: tag confidentiality/consent handling for the sensitive sexual history, and cross-link to the penile ulcer and scrotal swelling stations which share the sexual-history framework.
