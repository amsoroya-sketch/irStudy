Tiredness – Physical Examination From Examiner (PEFE)

Time Management Emphasis

  • History: ~4 minutes
  • Physical Examination from Examiner (PEFE): ≤ 2 minutes
  • Diagnosis + Differentials: ~2 minutes
  • Rule:
    • If PEFE reaches 2 minutes → STOP and move on
    • Failure to reach differentials = high risk of failing

Key Principle of PEFE

  • Examiner will NOT give findings unless specifically requested
  • Vague requests lead to:
    • “What are you looking for?”
    • Time wastage
  • Always ask specific, closed-ended questions

Concept Review: Focused vs Complete Examination

  • In tiredness:
    • “Focused” is confusing
    • Examiner expects a complete but fast screening structure
  • Purpose of examination:
    • Tick off key differentials
    • Even if diagnosis seems obvious (e.g. pneumonia), still screen others

Overall Strategy

  • Build a FAST BASIC STRUCTURE
  • Same structure used in all tiredness cases
  • Add key points later depending on provisional diagnosis

BASIC PHYSICAL EXAMINATION STRUCTURE (CORE FRAMEWORK)

1. General Appearance

How to ask (specific):

  • “On general appearance, I’m looking for:”
    • Pallor
    • Jaundice
    • Rashes
    • (Optional) Cachexia

2. Vital Signs

Priority focus:

  • Temperature (MOST IMPORTANT)

Then, if relevant:

  • Blood pressure
  • Pulse rate
  • Respiratory rate
  • Saturation

Common mistake:

  • Asking all vitals but forgetting temperature

3. Neck Examination

a. Thyroid Examination

  • MUST be included
  • Often forgotten—even in thyroid cases
  • Always ask:
    • “I’d like to examine the thyroid”

b. Full Lymph Node Examination

  • Ask globally:
    • “I want to examine all lymph nodes for lymphadenopathy”

Easy memory trick: Group it with neck examination

4. Cardiovascular Examination (Screening)

Closed-ended screening questions:

  • “Is S1 and S2 normal?”
  • “Are there any murmurs or added sounds?”

Why it’s done:

  • Cardiac causes of tiredness
  • Heart failure
  • Infective endocarditis

5. Respiratory Examination (Screening)

Screening questions:

  • “Is air entry equal bilaterally?”
  • “Are there any added sounds?”
  • If not a respiratory case → keep it brief
  • Ticks off pneumonia and respiratory differentials

6. Abdominal Examination

ONLY one key question:

  • “Is there hepatosplenomegaly?”

Do NOT ask:

  • Masses
  • Tenderness
  • Detailed abdominal findings

Why:

  • Liver disease
  • Malignancy
  • Hematological causes

7. Office Tests

Mandatory

  • Blood sugar level
  • Urine dipstick

Why urine dipstick is always included:

  • Diabetic ketoacidosis
  • Chronic kidney disease causing tiredness

EXAMPLE OSCE DIALOGUE (EXACT FLOW)

“James, just give me a moment. I’ll speak to my examiner and come back to you.”

General Appearance

  • “On general appearance, are there any rashes?” → No
  • “Is there pallor or jaundice?” → No
  • “Is there cachexia?” → No

Vital Signs

  • “What is the temperature?” → 36.7
  • “Blood pressure?” → 110/70
  • “Pulse rate?” → 88

Neck

  • “I’d like to examine the thyroid.” → Normal
  • “I’d like to examine all lymph nodes for lymphadenopathy.” → None

Cardiovascular

  • “Is S1 and S2 normal?” → Yes
  • “Any murmurs or added sounds?” → No

Respiratory

  • “Is air entry equal?” → Yes
  • “Any added sounds?” → No

Abdomen

  • “Is there hepatosplenomegaly?” → No

Office Tests

  • “Blood sugar level?” → 6.5
  • “Urine dipstick?” → Normal

Why This Works

  • Closed-ended questions
  • Examiner cannot interrupt
  • Prevents “What are you looking for?”
  • Works even with difficult examiners

ADDING KEY POINTS (ONLY AFTER BASIC STRUCTURE)

  • Basic structure is done in ALL tiredness cases
  • Never skip it
  • Extra examination points depend on provisional diagnosis

Examples

  • Hypothyroidism:
    • Proximal myopathy
    • Reflexes
  • Obstructive sleep apnea:
    • Neck circumference
    • BMI
    • Mallampati score

⚠️ These are NOT part of the basic structure

⚠️ Added only when case clearly points that way

🔬 Expert Review & Enhancements

Reviewed by: history-taking-expert (Australian clinical supervisor) · fact-checked against the irStudy medical textbook RAG index.

✏️ Corrections

✏️ Vital signs are presented as temperature first with blood pressure, pulse, respiratory rate and saturation only ‘if relevant’.
In an Australian setting a full vital-sign set (temperature, HR, BP, RR, SpO2) plus a lying-and-standing (postural) BP should always be requested in an undifferentiated fatigue PEFE — postural drop is a common, examinable cause/associate of tiredness (medications, dehydration, autonomic, anaemia). Frame them as a complete set rather than optional, and record values in Australian units (temperature °C, BP mmHg, glucose mmol/L).
✏️ The single abdominal question is limited to ‘Is there hepatosplenomegaly?’ with masses and tenderness explicitly excluded.
Retaining a quick check for a palpable mass and for signs of chronic liver disease (as well as hepatosplenomegaly) is low-cost and clinically justified given malignancy and haematological/hepatic causes of tiredness; screening for splenomegaly alone can miss relevant findings.

⭐ Suggested Enhancements

⭐ Add anaemia-specific inspection to general appearance (conjunctival/palmar pallor, koilonychia, glossitis) and, when suggested by history, angular stomatitis — directly relevant to a very common cause of tiredness.
⭐ Extend the office-test rationale: a random BSL is diagnostic of diabetes at ≥11.1 mmol/L and the urine dipstick screens glucose, ketones, protein, blood and leucocytes/nitrites (CKD, UTI, DKA) — state the interpretive thresholds so candidates know what a positive looks like.
⭐ For the disease-specific add-ons, expand beyond hypothyroidism (proximal myopathy, slow-relaxing reflexes, bradycardia) and OSA (BMI, neck circumference, Mallampati) to include heart-failure signs (raised JVP, displaced apex, basal crepitations, ankle oedema) since cardiac causes are explicitly in the differential.
