Unwell After Sauna Exposure on PrEP (Hepatitis with High STI Risk)

Source: Dr. Amir workshop — Med workshop 2 - 2026 / CLASS 5 UNWELL (Aug 2026 drop) · Specialty: general_practice

Case Overview

A young man ('Max') feels unwell for a few weeks. Three lead points anchor the stem: (1) sauna exposure with friends also affected (clustered recreational-water infection), (2) multiple people affected (common-source outbreak), and (3) PrEP use (implies MSM, STI risk, and renal monitoring). Tasks: 6-minute history, examination on card, diagnosis + differentials. Leading diagnosis: viral hepatitis on a background of high STI risk.

Quick differentials from the stem: Legionella (atypical pneumonia), Hepatitis A/B/C, Pseudomonas (hot-tub folliculitis), and STIs including HIV seroconversion.

Opening and Exploring 'Unwell'

  • Open-ended question; respond with empathy
  • Define 'unwell': low energy / sleepy / feverish (tired, run-down, a bit feverish)
  • Timing (2-3 weeks), constant vs intermittent, progression, impact on life

Lead Point - The Sauna

  • How often attended; symptoms in friends; specific activities (pools, body treatments)

Rule: if it is specified in the stem, it is there for a reason - do not skip it. Sauna/spa exposure raises Legionella, Hepatitis A and Pseudomonas.

Lead Point - PrEP

  • Indication for PrEP (confirms MSM without assuming)
  • Adherence - PrEP only protects if taken as prescribed
  • Regular GP follow-up and blood tests (renal monitoring)
  • Vaccination status (Hepatitis A, Hepatitis B, HPV)

Recreational-Water Infection Questions

Legionella: fever/chills, cough, shortness of breath, diarrhoea (classic accompaniment).

Hepatitis: abdominal pain (probe if present), nausea/vomiting, jaundice (yellow skin/eyes - positive), dark urine, pale stools, itch.

Pseudomonas (hot-tub folliculitis): rash anywhere on the body.

Sexual History (MSM) and STI Symptoms

Sexual history is mandatory. Screen (sexually active, condoms), then risk-assess partners (stable relationship, multiple partners, gender), method (anal/oral), and prior STIs.

STI symptom screen - HIV seroconversion (sore throat, rash, night sweats, lymphadenopathy, fever), syphilis/herpes (genital rash/ulcer), urethritis/epididymo-orchitis/prostatitis (penile discharge, scrotal swelling/redness).

Other Differentials and HEMI ADCOP

Malignancy (weight/appetite loss, lumps, night sweats, family history), travel history, mood, drugs. Dr Amir's HEMI ADCOP framework: Hepatic, Environmental/exposure infections, MSM/sexual transmission, Infections (systemic), Anaemia/Autoimmune, Drugs, Cancer, Organ disease (CKD - relevant on PrEP), Psychological.

Diagnosis and Explanation

Examination card: tender hepatomegaly, jaundice, urine dipstick bilirubin positive. Explain hepatitis as infection and inflammation of the liver, most likely viral - do not name the specific type clinically; blood tests confirm. State reasons (abdominal pain, jaundice, tender enlarged liver, bilirubinuria).

Differentials communicated: Legionella and Pseudomonas from the sauna; HIV and syphilis from the sexual history; malignancy; other viral/travel infections; and CKD screening because of PrEP.

✏️ Corrections

✏️ The case frames nephrotoxicity as the 'main side effect' of PrEP.
Clinically significant nephrotoxicity is uncommon with standard tenofovir disoproxil/emtricitabine PrEP, and the newer tenofovir alafenamide (TAF) formulation has less renal and bone effect. Renal function (eGFR) should be monitored per current ASHM/Australian guidance (baseline then periodically, typically 3-12 monthly depending on risk), but it should be described as an uncommon adverse effect requiring monitoring rather than the predominant side effect.
✏️ Legionella is grouped with person-transmissible recreational-water infections when explaining why 'multiple people are affected'.
Legionella is acquired by inhaling contaminated aerosols (spa/sauna water, cooling towers) and is not spread person-to-person; a cluster points to a common environmental source, which is important for public-health notification and source control rather than person-to-person contact tracing.

⭐ Suggested Enhancements

⭐ Apply SOCRATES to the abdominal pain once elicited (site, character, radiation, severity) to separate hepatitic RUQ discomfort from biliary or other causes.
⭐ Specify the confirmatory work-up: acute viral hepatitis serology (Hep A IgM, HBsAg/anti-HBc IgM, anti-HCV + HCV RNA, Hep E IgM), Legionella urinary antigen and sputum/PCR if respiratory features, and a full site-specific STI screen (throat/anorectal/urine NAAT) plus HIV Ag/Ab with HIV RNA if seroconversion is suspected.
⭐ Add public-health actions: Legionellosis and Hepatitis A/B/C are notifiable in NSW - notify and support source investigation of the sauna/spa.
⭐ Reinforce PrEP counselling: confirm dosing schedule (daily vs on-demand), adherence support, and that PrEP does not protect against other STIs, hence condom advice.
