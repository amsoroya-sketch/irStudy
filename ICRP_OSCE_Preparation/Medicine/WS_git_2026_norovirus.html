Norovirus - Fitness for Discharge (Cruise Ship)

Source: Dr. Amir workshop — GIT 2026 / class 7 Lower GI (Aug 2026 drop) · Specialty: gastroenterology

Overview &amp; Scenario

A frustrated patient has been isolated on a cruise ship in the harbour for several days with gastroenteritis (norovirus outbreak context) and demands to go home. The station tests agenda exploration, empathic communication, a focused fitness-for-discharge assessment, and a twist (a cannula-site infection) requiring safe management and safety-netting.

Opening &amp; Exploring the Agenda

Open with "How can I help you today?" Acknowledge frustration: "I understand you'd like to go home today, and I can see this has been frustrating. I'll ask a few quick questions to make sure it's safe to discharge you, and then we'll decide together."

Explore the concern (very important for marks): "What's making you want to get home urgently? If you tell me, I might be able to arrange some support." Let the patient speak, then validate: "That sounds really stressful - let's see how I can help."

Focused Diarrhoea History (short)

  • When did the diarrhoea start? Is it still ongoing or stopped? (Resolution is the key discharge criterion.)
  • How many times are you going now? What is the consistency - watery or back to normal?
  • Any blood in the stools?

Symptom Check for Discharge

  • Any fever or chills?
  • Any abdominal pain?
  • Any nausea or vomiting?
  • Any dizziness or lightheadedness?
  • Critical: Are you able to eat and drink normally now? (If not tolerating oral intake - NOT safe to discharge.)

Risk Factors &amp; On-board Treatment

  • Any medical conditions affecting immunity (diabetes, cancer)? Occupation?
  • Any regular medications, especially immunosuppressants?
  • What treatment have you received on the ship so far?
  • If time: travel activities, unusual food or exposures, pre-travel GP visit / vaccinations.

Assessment (turn to examiner)

"Based on my assessment, I would like to check the following before discharge:"

  • Vital signs - especially fever and postural (orthostatic) blood pressure.
  • Signs of dehydration.
  • Abdominal examination for tenderness.

Discharge Criteria

Consider fit for discharge if:

  • No diarrhoea for the required symptom-free interval (see correction).
  • No fever.
  • No abdominal pain or vomiting.
  • Tolerating oral intake.
  • Vitals stable and no dehydration.
  • Not immunocompromised.

The Twist - Cannula-Site Infection

There is redness at the cannula site suggesting a possible local infection, but the patient is otherwise clinically stable (normal vitals, no fever, alert, no systemic symptoms) - most consistent with a mild cannula-site infection or superficial thrombophlebitis.

Management: remove the cannula if still in place; for mild infection, manage with an oral antistaphylococcal antibiotic (e.g. flucloxacillin) as per soft-tissue infection guidelines; provide local care and advice.

When NOT to discharge: fever, tachycardia, hypotension or systemic unwellness suggests more severe infection needing IV antibiotics and admission.

Final Decision &amp; Closing

As the infection appears mild and the patient is otherwise stable and meets gastroenteritis discharge criteria, discharge with oral antibiotics and safety-netting. Close to the patient: "It looks safe for you to go home. I'll give you clear instructions - if you develop fever, worsening pain, or the diarrhoea returns, please seek care immediately."

🔬 Expert Review & Enhancements

Reviewed by: history-taking-expert (Australian clinical supervisor) · fact-checked against the irStudy medical textbook RAG index.

✏️ Corrections

✏️ The notes give an internally inconsistent symptom-free interval: 'no diarrhea' as the key criterion in the history but '48-72 hours' in the discharge criteria list. For norovirus in an outbreak/institutional (cruise ship) setting the isolation exit rule needs to be stated clearly and consistently.
State a single, evidence-based rule: for norovirus the patient should remain isolated and is considered non-infectious for discharge/return to communal settings once they have been symptom-free (no diarrhoea or vomiting) for at least 48 hours, per Australian public-health guidance for viral gastroenteritis in institutional outbreaks.
✏️ The case does not mention public-health notification or outbreak control, which is central to a cruise-ship norovirus scenario in Australian waters.
Add that a suspected norovirus outbreak in a cruise ship/institution is notifiable to the local public health unit; management includes cohorting/isolation, environmental cleaning, food-handler exclusion, and hand hygiene with SOAP AND WATER (alcohol-based hand rub is less effective against norovirus).

⭐ Suggested Enhancements

⭐ Add explicit dehydration and oral-rehydration management (oral rehydration solution first-line; norovirus is self-limiting and does not require antibiotics or antivirals) so the candidate does not over-investigate.
⭐ State that flucloxacillin dosing/choice should follow eTG (e.g. flucloxacillin 500 mg orally 6-hourly for mild cellulitis; use cephalexin or clindamycin if penicillin-allergic) and document the allergy history before prescribing.
⭐ Add a safety-net differential for the cannula site: escalate if spreading erythema, tracking, fever or suppurative thrombophlebitis - consider blood cultures and IV therapy.
⭐ Add metadata flagging this as primarily a communication/discharge-decision station (agenda exploration, shared decision-making) rather than a diagnostic one, and cross-link to gastroenteritis and cellulitis topics.
