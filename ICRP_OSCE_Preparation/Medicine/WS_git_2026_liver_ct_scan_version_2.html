Single Liver Lesion with Prior Bowel Cancer - History, Investigation & Differentials

Source: Dr. Amir workshop — GIT 2026 / class 5 liver cases (Aug 2026 drop) · Specialty: gastroenterology

Focused History - Colon Cancer

After open-ended questions, take the colon-cancer history:

  • When were you diagnosed?
  • What treatment did you receive? → "Surgery, chemotherapy and radiotherapy as needed."
  • Have you had regular follow-up with your GP and specialists? → "No, I haven't seen them since the surgery."
  • Complications - general cancer screen: weight loss, loss of appetite, lumps/bumps (lymph nodes), tiredness/light-headedness/dizziness; and yellow discolouration, itchiness.

Explaining the CT

Pick up the image. "We did a CT scan of your abdomen and are looking at a cross-sectional image of your liver. I can see a circular lump in your liver, which is an abnormal finding."

Diagnosis & Differentials

Explanation to patient: "With your history of bowel cancer, I am concerned about the possibility of spread of the bowel cancer to your liver. The second condition I'm considering is a primary liver cancer (HCC) putting pressure on your tubes and causing your symptoms."

Differentials for a single liver lesion:

  1. Hepatocellular adenoma
  2. Liver abscess
  3. Liver cyst
  4. Haemangioma

Other conditions causing pain and the urinary/stool changes considered: hepatitis, cholangitis, cholecystitis, pancreatitis and pancreatic cancer.

Investigation of the Lump

  1. MRI
  2. Biopsy
  3. Colon cancer follow-up - CEA - and PET scan
  4. Find the cause and treat the cause

✏️ Corrections

✏️ The lesion is attributed to metastasis vs HCC, but the history notes no follow-up after colon cancer surgery without flagging this as an abnormal care gap.
Australian colorectal cancer surveillance (Cancer Council/eviQ) requires structured follow-up after curative resection - serial CEA, surveillance colonoscopy and CT - so the absence of follow-up is itself an abnormal finding raising the pre-test probability of recurrence and should be documented and acted on.
✏️ A single liver lesion with prior colorectal cancer is framed primarily around confirming metastasis.
A solitary colorectal liver metastasis may be resectable with curative intent, so characterisation (contrast MRI liver), full staging (CT chest/abdomen/pelvis) and MDT discussion are required before assuming palliative intent; this changes both work-up and counselling tone.

⭐ Suggested Enhancements

⭐ Add explicit red-flag/obstructive-jaundice screening (fever/rigors for cholangitis, degree of jaundice, pale stool/dark urine, pruritus) and a cholangitis safety-net, as the differential list includes cholangitis.
⭐ Expand the lesion characterisation pathway: triphasic/contrast-enhanced MRI is the key characterising test (haemangioma shows peripheral nodular fill-in; simple cyst shows no enhancement), and note AFP for HCC in addition to CEA for colorectal recurrence.
⭐ Add a management/safety-net and communication frame (this may be a mini-BBN): the diagnosis is unconfirmed pending biopsy/MRI, outcomes range from potentially curative surgery to palliative care, and urgent specialist referral is needed.
