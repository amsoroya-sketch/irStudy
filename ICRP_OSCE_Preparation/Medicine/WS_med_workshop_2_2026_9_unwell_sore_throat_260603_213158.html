Unwell with a Prolonged Sore Throat (Pharyngitis - EBV vs Gonococcal / STI)

Source: Dr. Amir workshop — Med workshop 2 - 2026 / CLASS 5 UNWELL (Aug 2026 drop) · Specialty: general_practice

Case Overview

A 30-year-old man presents to general practice feeling unwell. Task: take a history for 6 minutes, then explain the diagnosis and differentials. The complaint declares itself as a sore throat - but because it is an 'unwell + sore throat' case lasting 3 weeks, this is not simple viral pharyngitis and serious conditions must be actively considered. Use the HEMI ADCOP framework for the undifferentiated unwell patient.

Diagnosis depends on the sexual history: with a normal sexual history the likely diagnosis is pharyngitis due to EBV; with a high-risk history (MSM, oral sex, no condoms) consider STI-related pharyngitis (gonococcal/chlamydial) and HIV.

Opening and Exploring 'Unwell'

  • Sit, open-ended question, respond to concern
  • Define 'unwell' (feverish / sleepy / low energy) - patient reports a sore throat
  • Timing (onset, constant vs intermittent, worsening), aggravating and relieving factors

Exploring the Sore Throat

  • Key question - one side or both? Unilateral (e.g. left-sided) pain suggests quinsy/peritonsillar abscess; whole-throat pain suggests generalised pharyngitis
  • Intensity (1-10); radiation (care if radiating to the neck)
  • Aggravating and relieving factors

Serious-throat red flags (examiner expects these): drooling and difficulty swallowing (physical obstruction from a retropharyngeal/peritonsillar abscess), change in voice ('hot-potato' voice from peri-laryngeal swelling threatening the airway), neck pain (meningitis and deep neck abscess), headache and vision problems (meningitis), rash (EBV with severe respiratory infection).

ENT, Lower Respiratory and GI Screen

Upper respiratory/ENT: fever/chills, ear pain/discharge, runny nose, facial pain, post-nasal secretion, neck lumps (positive).

Lower respiratory/pneumonia: cough, shortness of breath, noisy breathing.

GI: nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea (Legionella/atypical pneumonia can cause GI symptoms).

History Findings

  • Feels unwell and tired
  • Sore throat for 3 weeks (no longer a normal sore throat)
  • Body aches and joint pains; no travel; no IV drug use; no weight loss
  • Neck swelling / neck lump that is tender (near the ear); no rash
  • Otherwise essentially nothing positive - the key point is the prolonged course and tender lymphadenopathy

Characterise the lump: site, size, and whether painful on touch (tender - suggests reactive lymphadenitis/infection rather than malignancy).

Malignancy, Sexual History and RHD Screen

Malignancy (important as sore throat >3 weeks): weight loss, appetite loss, lumps/bumps, night sweats.

Sexual history (always relevant): sexually active, safe sex/condoms, stable relationship, multiple partners, partner gender, and specifically oral sex (gonococcal/chlamydial pharyngitis). AMC favours HIV cases - HIV seroconversion can present as sore throat.

Rheumatic heart disease: ask about past or family history, given the link between streptococcal sore throat and rheumatic heart disease.

Diagnosis and Differentials

Diagnosis: pharyngitis (cannot differentiate tonsillitis without examination). Explain it as a throat infection that can be viral (Epstein-Barr virus), bacterial (streptococcal), or occasionally sexually transmitted (gonococcal). Reasons: severe long-standing (3-week) sore throat unusual for ordinary viruses/bacteria; associated body aches and a tender neck lump (sign of infection).

If sexual history normal - most likely EBV. If sexual history high-risk - consider gonococcal infection and HIV.

Differentials: laryngitis, bacterial tracheitis, peritonsillar abscess (quinsy); pneumonia/TB/atypical pneumonia; malignancy (pharyngeal/tonsillar carcinoma, leukaemia, lymphoma); HIV seroconversion; STI-related pharyngitis (chlamydia, gonorrhoea). In HEMI ADCOP, malignancy and infection are the priorities.

✏️ Corrections

✏️ The case discusses treating pharyngitis without warning against empirical aminopenicillins when EBV is possible.
Do not give amoxicillin/ampicillin empirically for an undifferentiated sore throat when infectious mononucleosis (EBV) is a possibility - it precipitates a florid maculopapular rash in a high proportion of patients. If a bacterial (Group A streptococcal) cause is confirmed, the recommended agent is phenoxymethylpenicillin (penicillin V), reserving broader agents for specific indications.
✏️ The case links a 'worse-than-usual sore throat' loosely to rheumatic heart disease.
The correct causal chain is that Group A streptococcal pharyngitis can lead to acute rheumatic fever and subsequently rheumatic heart disease. Australian guidance (RHDAustralia/eTG) prioritises antibiotic treatment of GAS pharyngitis to prevent acute rheumatic fever specifically in high-risk groups - notably Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples and others from high-incidence settings - which should be identified in the history.

⭐ Suggested Enhancements

⭐ Add a validated decision tool for GAS: the Centor/McIsaac criteria (fever, tonsillar exudate, tender anterior cervical nodes, absence of cough, age) to rationalise throat swab and antibiotic decisions.
⭐ Specify investigations: throat swab MCS, EBV serology (EBV IgM / heterophile antibody) and FBC with film for atypical lymphocytes, HIV Ag/Ab (plus HIV RNA if seroconversion suspected), and a pharyngeal NAAT for gonorrhoea/chlamydia if the sexual history is high-risk.
⭐ Add explicit airway safety-netting: drooling, stridor, trismus or 'hot-potato' voice indicate a threatened airway (epiglottitis/quinsy) requiring 000 and emergency ENT/anaesthetic involvement - do not examine the throat with instruments if epiglottitis is suspected.
⭐ Add a head and neck cancer red-flag pathway: a persistent (over 3 weeks) unilateral sore throat, or a persistent neck mass, especially if hard/fixed/non-tender, warrants urgent ENT referral for suspected head and neck malignancy.
