Bowel Cancer Presenting as 'IBS-like' Abdominal Discomfort — The 'Strange' 2025 Case

Source: Dr. Amir workshop — GIT 2026 / 2.Abdominal pain case (Aug 2026 drop) · Specialty: gastroenterology

Station Stem and Tasks

This is a 2025 case that candidates started calling "the strange case".

Your next patient is a 60-year-old man who has presented to you complaining of abdominal discomfort.

  • Task 1: Take history for 6 minutes.
  • Task 2: Explain diagnosis and differentials to the patient.

Approach: go in, apply the standard structure — abdominal discomfort, explore the pain, general GI questions, red flag questions.

History Findings

  • Pain in the lower abdomen
  • Bouts of diarrhoea alternating with constipation (change in bowel habit)
  • Unintentional weight loss
  • Passing fresh blood per rectum
  • Family history of bowel cancer (aunt)
  • Stress and other features that superficially resemble IBS
  • Reports a normal bowel cancer screening test done 12 months ago

Why This Case Is 'Strange' — and Why the Answer Is Still Bowel Cancer

Diagnostic principle: no matter what the age is, with these findings the first thing on top of the differential list is bowel cancer.

When Dr Amir asked candidates what was strange about this patient, the answer given was: "the patient had IBS findings." Dr Amir's response: if there are ANY red flags, it is not IBS. IBS is a diagnosis that cannot stand in the presence of red flags, regardless of how many IBS-type criteria (such as stress-related symptoms) are also present.

Candidates were also confused by the patient reporting a normal bowel cancer screening test 12 months ago. This does not exclude cancer: a normal screening result last year does not mean the patient is fine now. The most likely diagnosis remains bowel cancer.

Explaining the Diagnosis

Diagnosis to the patient: "I am very concerned about you having bowel cancer."

Reasons:

  • Bouts of diarrhoea and constipation — a change in bowel habit
  • Unintentional loss of weight
  • Passing fresh blood
  • Family history of bowel cancer (aunt)

This is not IBS, even though other IBS-type features such as stress are present. The screening test 12 months ago being normal does not rule out a current cancer.

⭐ Suggested Enhancements

⭐ Add the Australian screening context that makes the '12 months ago' distractor teachable: the National Bowel Cancer Screening Program uses a 2-yearly immunochemical FOBT (iFOBT), now available from age 45 (on request 45–49, invited 50–74). iFOBT is a screening test for asymptomatic people with imperfect sensitivity — any SYMPTOMATIC patient (rectal bleeding, change in bowel habit, unexplained weight loss, iron-deficiency anaemia) requires diagnostic colonoscopy regardless of a recent negative screen (RACGP Red Book; Cancer Council Australia clinical practice guidelines).
⭐ Add the expected next steps for the closure: urgent referral for colonoscopy (Optimal Care Pathway for colorectal cancer targets prompt diagnostic evaluation of red-flag symptoms), plus FBE and iron studies to look for iron-deficiency anaemia, and CEA only after diagnosis (not as a diagnostic test).
⭐ Add a named red-flag checklist to the notes so candidates can verbalise it: age >50 with new bowel symptoms, rectal bleeding, unexplained weight loss, nocturnal symptoms, anaemia, palpable mass, family history of colorectal cancer — and state explicitly that ANY of these excludes a positive diagnosis of IBS (consistent with Rome criteria requiring absence of alarm features).
⭐ Add differentials that should still be verbalised to the patient after cancer: inflammatory bowel disease, diverticular disease, haemorrhoids/anal fissure (for the fresh bleeding), infective colitis — each with a one-line reason it is less likely, mirroring the structure used in the other stations of this bundle.
