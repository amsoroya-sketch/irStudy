Acute Hepatitis in a Returned Traveller: Focused History and LFT Patterns

Source: Dr. Amir workshop — GIT 2026 / class 5 liver cases (Aug 2026 drop) · Specialty: gastroenterology

Case 1 - Returned Traveller with RUQ Pain (Hepatitis A)

Step 1 - Opening: confirm haemodynamic stability if not given (BP, PR, RR, temperature); ID check, hand hygiene, introduction. Open question - patient reports right upper quadrant pain after returning from South-East Asia. Acknowledge, empathise, reassure.

Step 2 - Pain assessment (SIQORAA): Site (if RLQ, ask where it started/shifted), Intensity (0-10), Quality (sharp vs dull), Onset/timing (constant vs intermittent, worsening), Radiation, Alleviating/aggravating factors (worse after eating - screens PUD, pancreatitis, cholecystitis; positional).

Travel history (ABCDEFG): Activities and destination; Bushwalking/insect bites; Contact with sick people/animals; Drugs; Exposure (tattoos, piercing, transfusion, procedures); Food (street food - positive); GP pre-travel vaccination/malaria prophylaxis.

Case 1 - Differentials Screen and Diagnosis

General GI: nausea/vomiting (upper), diarrhoea/constipation (lower), bloating/wind (mid), fever (none).

Red flag GI: weight loss, appetite loss, lumps/nodes, tiredness/dizziness, family history of bowel cancer.

Hepatitis: jaundice (positive), itch, dark urine/pale stool. Gallbladder: fatty-food pain. Surgery.

Hepatitis risk factors (ABP FIT NO SEX): Alcohol/Antibiotics, Blood, Piercing, Food, IV drugs, Tattoo/Travel, Needle-stick/Occupation, Sexual history (safe sex, sex overseas).

Positive findings: RUQ pain, jaundice, no fever, street food, travel.

Diagnosis: acute hepatitis - liver inflammation - most likely hepatitis A (faeco-oral) acquired via street food overseas.

Differentials: hepatitis B and C (blood/sexual transmission); malignancy (liver, GB, pancreas); gallstones causing cholecystitis/obstructive jaundice/cholangitis; antibiotic/alcohol-induced hepatitis; pancreatitis; PUD; GORD; ACS/lower-lobe pneumonia; travel-related infection (malaria, dengue, EBV, CMV, chikungunya).

Case 2 - Contact with a Sick Person (Hepatitis A)

Same structured screen as Case 1. Diagnosis: acute hepatitis, most likely hepatitis A acquired via faeco-oral contact with a sick person. Differentials as above (hepatitis B/C, malignancy, gallstone disease, drug/alcohol-induced hepatitis, pancreatitis, PUD, GORD, ACS/pneumonia, travel-related infection).

LFT Patterns

  • Hepatocellular: ALT/AST/GGT markedly raised (up to ~10x), with a small rise in ALP.
  • Cholestatic: ALP markedly raised (up to ~10x), with small rises in AST/ALT/GGT.
  • Isolated hyperbilirubinaemia: raised bilirubin with normal AST/ALT/ALP (e.g. Gilbert syndrome, haemolysis).

✏️ Corrections

✏️ The case reaches a viral hepatitis A diagnosis but does not mention confirmatory serology or public-health obligations.
Confirm with hepatitis A IgM serology and a full acute viral hepatitis panel (anti-HAV IgM, HBsAg/anti-HBc IgM, anti-HCV/HCV RNA), plus LFTs and coagulation. Hepatitis A is a NOTIFIABLE disease in NSW - notify public health, and offer hepatitis A vaccine or normal human immunoglobulin to close contacts.
✏️ Pre-travel prevention is only touched on within the travel history.
Reinforce Australian pre-travel advice per Smartraveller/immunisation guidance: hepatitis A and typhoid vaccination and food/water hygiene for South-East Asia, plus malaria chemoprophylaxis where indicated - a common AMC counselling extension of this case.

⭐ Suggested Enhancements

⭐ Add explicit management for uncomplicated hepatitis A: it is self-limiting and supportive - rest, hydration, avoid alcohol and hepatotoxic drugs (including paracetamol dose caution), monitor for the rare fulminant course (encephalopathy, coagulopathy) needing urgent transfer to a liver unit.
⭐ Add a paracetamol-safety teaching point relevant to any hepatitis station: in significant liver disease reduce the maximum paracetamol dose and avoid alcohol - important in the Australian setting where paracetamol is the default simple analgesic.
⭐ Add a red-flag/escalation note: jaundice with confusion, bruising or a rising INR signals acute liver failure - escalate immediately for retrieval to a tertiary liver service from a rural hospital.
⭐ Structure improvement: separate 'risk-factor screen' (ABP FIT NO SEX) from 'exposure/travel screen' (ABCDEFG) so learners avoid duplicating the blood/exposure questions.
⭐ Add metadata linking to viral-hepatitis serology interpretation and to a returned-traveller fever work-up (malaria thick/thin films).
