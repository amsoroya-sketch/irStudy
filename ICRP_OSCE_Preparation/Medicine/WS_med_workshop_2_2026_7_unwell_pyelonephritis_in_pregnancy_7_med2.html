Unwell in Pregnancy at 20 Weeks (Acute Pyelonephritis)

Source: Dr. Amir workshop — Med workshop 2 - 2026 / CLASS 5 UNWELL (Aug 2026 drop) · Specialty: general_practice

Case Overview

A 25-year-old woman at 20 weeks' gestation presents to the GP feeling unwell. Four tasks under time pressure: history (~4 min), examination from examiner, explain diagnosis + differentials, and explain management. Key examination card findings: positive renal angle tenderness and urine dipstick nitrites - i.e. an upper urinary tract infection / acute pyelonephritis in pregnancy.

History - Opening and Exploring 'Unwell'

  • Open-ended question, empathic response, then work efficiently
  • Describe 'unwell' (energy / sleepy / feverish - patient feels hot = fever, the main complaint)
  • Timing, progression, aggravating/relieving factors - pivot to infection cluster

Infection Cluster

Fever: measured temperature, chills, medications and response.

UTI / pyelonephritis (priority): dysuria, frequency, abdominal/flank pain (which side), nausea/vomiting. Right flank pain + dysuria + frequency = upper UTI pattern.

STIs (priority 2): genital rash/ulcer (herpes - important in pregnancy for birth-canal transmission), vaginal discharge or bleeding (chorioamnionitis).

Other infections: cough/sore throat (RTI), headache/rash (meningitis), travel.

Second-Half Pregnancy Screen

Pre-eclampsia: antenatal screening/results, BP and BSL checks, headache, visual blurring, leg swelling.

Gestational diabetes: tiredness, polyuria, polydipsia.

Cholestasis/liver disease of pregnancy: jaundice, dark urine, pale stools, itch.

Anaemia: chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations on exertion.

Physical Examination and Office Tests

General appearance (jaundice, pallor, rash) and vital signs - BP and temperature are the critical vitals. ENT, respiratory, CVS. Abdomen: RUQ/liver tenderness, uterine tenderness, kidney ballotting; percussion for renal angle tenderness (positive); bowel sounds. Inspect genitalia for rash; no speculum/bimanual without vaginal symptoms.

Obstetric: always check fetal heart rate. Office tests: BSL; urine dipstick - leucocytes + nitrites (UTI confirmed) + protein (pre-eclampsia screen); positives here include nitrites, leucocytes and blood.

Diagnosis and Differentials

Explain pyelonephritis as a kidney infection, giving a full 'pack of reasons' (fever, dysuria, frequency, right flank pain, nausea, tender kidney on percussion, urinary signs of infection).

Differentials: other infections (herpes, pneumonia, gastroenteritis, meningitis); second-half pregnancy conditions (pre-eclampsia, gestational diabetes, cholestasis/acute fatty liver, anaemia); plus thyroid disorder, psychological and malignancy from the HEMI ADCOP list.

Management Plan

Admit via ED (call ambulance) - a severe kidney infection with high fever in a pregnant woman is taken seriously.

  • Investigations: urine MCS (organism + sensitivities); bloods - FBC, ESR, CRP, U&E, LFTs, blood cultures
  • Treatment: IV antibiotics, antipyretic, IV fluids and monitoring
  • Follow-up: post-treatment urine MCS in 1-2 weeks (mandatory in pregnancy after any UTI/asymptomatic bacteriuria/pyelonephritis)

✏️ Corrections

✏️ The plan states 'IV antibiotics: 10-14 days total', implying the full course is intravenous.
Per Therapeutic Guidelines, acute pyelonephritis in pregnancy is treated with IV antibiotics until the patient is clinically improving and afebrile for 24-48 hours, then switched to an appropriate oral agent guided by susceptibilities to complete a total course of about 10-14 days. The IV component is usually only the first 1-3 days, not the whole course.
✏️ The case states 'nitrites + leucocytes = sufficient for UTI diagnosis'.
In pregnancy a positive dipstick is not sufficient on its own - urine microscopy, culture and sensitivities are mandatory to confirm infection, identify the organism and guide safe antibiotic choice, and to arrange test-of-cure. Dipstick nitrites/leucocytes support but do not replace culture in this group.

⭐ Suggested Enhancements

⭐ Name eTG-consistent empirical therapy: IV ceftriaxone (or ampicillin plus gentamicin) is first-line for pyelonephritis in pregnancy; note pregnancy-specific cautions (avoid trimethoprim in the first trimester and nitrofurantoin near term).
⭐ Add fetal and sepsis assessment: continuous fetal monitoring/CTG as appropriate for gestation, early obstetric involvement, and application of a sepsis pathway given fever plus tachycardia in pregnancy (blood cultures and antibiotics within one hour if septic).
⭐ Highlight prevention: screen for and treat asymptomatic bacteriuria in pregnancy (routine MSU screening), which prevents progression to pyelonephritis - an important teaching point to add.
⭐ Consider VTE risk assessment and thromboprophylaxis on admission, and document analgesia/antipyresis with paracetamol as the preferred agent in pregnancy.
