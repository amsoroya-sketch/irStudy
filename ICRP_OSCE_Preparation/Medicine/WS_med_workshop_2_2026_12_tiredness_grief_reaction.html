Tiredness in a Child — Grief Reaction

Source: Dr. Amir workshop — Med workshop 2 - 2026 / CLASS 4 TIREDNESS (Aug 2026 drop) · Specialty: general_practice

Introduction

Introduce yourself and gain the mother's cooperation: “I understand you're concerned about your daughter, but I need to ask some more questions to get an idea about what's going on. Is that okay?”

Presenting Complaint — Explore Tiredness

Clarify what is meant by tired: low energy? sleepy? feverish? Timing (how long), alleviating/aggravating factors. Effect on function: impact on daily activities and, importantly, school performance. Finding: she used to be a good student, but her grades have been dropping since about two months ago.

Differential Screening

  • Endocrine: diabetes and thyroid screening questions.
  • Malignancy: weight loss, loss of appetite, lumps or bumps; consider leukaemia/lymphoma.
  • Infection: fever/chills, rashes, recent viral symptoms, recent travel; sexual activity (mother: no).
  • Autoimmune and anaemia: screening questions.
  • Drugs and medications: any medications, any drug use.
  • Psychological: mood, stresses at home, bullying at school. Finding: grandmother died 4 months ago.
  • Other: coeliac disease, cardiopulmonary, obstructive sleep apnoea screening.

Physical Examination

Ask examiner for: general appearance, vital signs, thyroid examination, lymph node examination, cardiovascular and respiratory examination, abdominal examination for hepatosplenomegaly, and office tests (BSL and urine dipstick).

Diagnosis and Reasoning

Most likely diagnosis: grief reaction. Grandmother died 4 months ago — an emotional response to a significant stressor. Children often present with tiredness, poor school performance and atypical/somatic symptoms rather than obvious mood symptoms.

Differentials

  1. Grief reaction (most likely)
  2. Major depressive disorder
  3. PTSD (if the death was particularly traumatic)
  4. Post-viral tiredness (EBV, COVID, other viral illness)
  5. Leukaemia/malignancy (less likely — no weight loss, no loss of appetite, no night sweats, no concerning examination findings; lymph node reactive)

Explanation to the parent should note that the cervical lymph node is not concerning because it is less than one centimetre, soft and mobile, consistent with a normal/reactive post-viral node.

✏️ Corrections

✏️ The psychosocial assessment of an adolescent is taken almost entirely from the mother, and sexual activity is asked of the mother (‘Do you think she might be sexually active?’).
Adolescents should be offered time interviewed alone with an explanation of confidentiality and its limits (RACGP adolescent/HEEADSSS approach). Sensitive topics (sexual activity, drug use, mood, self-harm) are best explored directly with the young person, respecting Gillick/mature-minor competence, not answered by proxy.
✏️ In a bereaved adolescent with a two-month decline in school performance the case does not document a risk (self-harm/suicide) assessment.
Any adolescent with functional decline plus a significant loss must be screened for depression and suicidality (mood, hopelessness, thoughts of self-harm, plan, protective factors). Recent bereavement is a recognised risk factor and its omission is a safety gap; add an explicit HEEADSSS + risk assessment.

⭐ Suggested Enhancements

⭐ Distinguish normal grief from prolonged grief disorder and adjustment disorder using a timeline: at 4 months, persistent functional impairment warrants active follow-up rather than pure reassurance; add DSM-5-TR/ICD-11 framing and a safety-net review.
⭐ Add red-flag safety net for the malignancy differential: fixed/hard/matted or >1 cm supraclavicular nodes, drenching night sweats, hepatosplenomegaly, unexplained bruising/petechiae or pallor should prompt urgent FBE/film and paediatric referral.
⭐ Add first-line paediatric fatigue investigations (FBE, film, iron studies, TSH, EBV serology/monospot, coeliac serology) and note school liaison, grief counselling/bereavement support and involvement of the whole family as management.
⭐ Add metadata: patient_age_group (adolescent/child), key_diagnosis (grief reaction), and confidentiality/consent flag for adolescent interviewing.
