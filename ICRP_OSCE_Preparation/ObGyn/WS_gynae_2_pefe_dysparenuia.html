Pelvic and Focused Examination for Dyspareunia (Examiner-Led)

Source: Dr. Amir workshop — gynae / 2. intercourse and cervical problems (Aug 2026 drop) · Specialty: obstetrics_gynaecology

Physical Examination From Examiner – Dyspareunia Case

General Appearance

  • Assess for cachexia because of cancers in the differential list.
  • Assess for dehydration as a sign of infection (due to PID being in the differentials).

Vital Signs

  • Ask for all four:
    • Blood pressure
    • Pulse rate
    • Respiratory rate
    • Temperature is critical (because infections like PID are on the list)

Abdominal Examination

  • Do a quick abdominal examination.
  • Check for:
    • Masses
    • Tenderness
  • Done to be on the safe side.

Pelvic Examination

  • Mention the two Cs:
    • Consent of the patient
    • Chaperone presence

Inspection

  • Rashes (important because dermatosis/dermatitis skin conditions can cause dyspareunia)
  • Redness
  • Bleeding (for problems like cancers)
  • Discharge (for PID)
  • Rare differential: traumatic intimate/partner violence
    • Ask for bruises
    • Ask for traumatic signs on private parts

Speculum Examination

Follow regular structure with one extra point.

  • Cervix:
    • Any Masses or abnormal growths on cervix
    • Ectropion
    • Bleeding
    • Discharge
  • Vaginal wall:
    • Recall: atrophic vaginitis will be painful
  • Extra question:
    • Ask examiner: Is speculum insertion painful for the patient?
      • Relevant for vaginismus and vulvodynia

Bimanual Examination

Perform the usual structure:

  • Cervical motion tenderness
  • Uterus size & tenderness
    • If large, ask for borders
  • Adnexal masses and tenderness

Rectal Examination

  • Very important due to the differential of endometriosis
  • Look for:
    • Tenderness
    • Nodularity on the pouch of Douglas
      • AMC often gives this finding in endometriosis cases.

Conclusion

  • This completes the simple PFE for the dyspareunia case.
  • Structure is the same as previous PFEs with only small additions depending on differentials.
  • Just add a few additional findings as required—that’s all.

🔬 Expert Review & Enhancements

Reviewed by: clinical-documentation-expert (Australian clinical supervisor, O&G / GP standards) · fact-checked against the irStudy medical textbook RAG index.

✏️ Corrections

✏️ General appearance advises assessing 'for dehydration as a sign of infection (due to PID)'.
Dehydration is not a reliable or characteristic sign of PID. Signs pointing to pelvic infection are fever, tachycardia, lower abdominal/adnexal tenderness, cervical motion tenderness and purulent discharge. Reword to assess for systemic features of sepsis (temperature, pulse, peritonism) rather than dehydration.
✏️ The examination sequence does not mention a pregnancy test before proceeding to bimanual/pelvic examination.
In any woman of reproductive age a urine beta-hCG must be obtained before pelvic examination and before hormonal or radiological management, as ectopic pregnancy is a not-to-be-missed cause of pelvic pain. Add urinalysis and urine beta-hCG to the 'ask the examiner' list.

⭐ Suggested Enhancements

⭐ Add STI screening at speculum: endocervical or self-collected vaginal NAAT (PCR) for chlamydia and gonorrhoea, plus high vaginal swab/microscopy for candida, BV and trichomonas - directly relevant to the infective differentials the case lists.
⭐ State that examination should stop if it causes pain and that findings of an intolerable/guarded speculum insertion themselves support vaginismus/provoked vestibulodynia - this reframes 'is speculum insertion painful?' as a diagnostic finding, not just a question.
⭐ Document the 'two Cs' more completely as consent + chaperone + offer of analgesia/gentle technique and the patient's right to decline; add that the chaperone's name/role should be recorded in the notes (Australian medicolegal standard).
⭐ Add offer of a cervical screening/co-test at the same visit if the patient is due, and vulvoscopy/skin mapping for the dermatosis differentials (lichen sclerosus, lichen planus).
