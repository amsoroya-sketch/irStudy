Primary Postpartum Haemorrhage (Telephone / Rural HMO)

Source: Dr. Amir workshop — obstetrics / class 7 (Aug 2026 drop) · Specialty: obstetrics_gynaecology

PRIMARY POSTPARTUM HEMORRHAGE (PPH)

  • Bleeding in the first 24 hours after delivery

Brainstorming / Causes

  • Causes and possibilities of bleeding in first 24 hours
  • Four T's:
    • Tone
    • Trauma
    • Thrombin
    • Tissue

Four T’s Explained

1. Tone

  • Uterine atony
  • After delivery uterus should contract to stop bleeding
  • Risk factors mentioned:
    • Multiparous
    • Prolonged delivery
    • Polyhydraminos
    • Macrosomia
    • Uterine overdistension (e.g., multiple pregnancy)

2. Trauma

  • Trauma to birth canal:
    • Uterine rupture(imp)
    • Cervical tear
    • Vaginal tear
    • Perineal tear
    • Episiotomy bleeding

3. Tissue

  • Retained placenta or membranes
  • Uterus unable to contract if tissue remains

4. Thrombin

  • Bleeding disorders
  • DIC

Primary Postpartum Hemorrhage as Significant Blood Loss

  • Must understand initial resuscitation
  • Primary survey
    • Priority: save mother first
    • ABC: airway, breathing, circulation

Primary Survey Steps

  • Call for help (registrar/consultant)
  • Airway & Breathing
    • Assess airway
    • Assess breathing
    • High-flow oxygen
  • Circulation
    • Two large-bore IV cannulas
    • Send bloods:
      • Full blood count
      • Blood group and crossmatch
      • Coagulation studies
    • Start fluids while arranging blood
  • Vital signs
    • Pulse
    • Blood pressure
    • Oxygen saturation
    • Urine output

Management Principle

  • Manage by addressing the cause
  • Don’t wait to identify cause before starting treatment

Adding a Fifth T

  • Theatre
  • Most cases end up requiring surgical procedure
  • Assessments and managements are manual and need operating theatre

Management by Cause

TONE

Mechanical

  • Uterine massage on abdomen
  • Bimanual uterine compression (fist inside + external pressure)

Pharmacological

  • First line: Oxytocin
  • If not effective:
    • Ergometrine
    • Misoprostol
    • Carboprost

TRAUMA

  • Thorough assessment with speculum
  • Examine:
    • Cervix
    • Vagina
    • Perineum
  • Repair tears / episiotomy
  • Important point:
    • If shock with minimal visible bleeding → consider ruptured uterus
    • Higher risk in women with previous cesarean sections

TISSUE

  • Retained placenta or membranes continue bleeding
  • If placenta delivered:
    • Examine placenta and membranes for completeness
  • If placenta not delivered or not delivered easily with controlled cord traction (CCT):
    • Empty bladder
    • Transfer to theatre for manual removal
  • Persistent PPH → Uterine exploration

THROMBIN

  • Bleeding disorder or DIC
  • Tranexamic acid
    • IV
    • Should be given as soon as possible after bleeding onset

Surgical / Theatre Interventions

  • Balloon tamponade (e.g., Foley catheter inflated)
  • Arterial ligation
  • Hysterectomy (if bleeding uncontrolled and family complete)

Massive Transfusion Protocol

  • Mentioned when bleeding ~1.5 liters
  • Significant blood loss (≈ one third to one fourth blood volume)
  • Packed red cells alone not enough
  • Must consider:
    • Red blood cells
    • Platelets
    • Plasma (FFP)
  • Activate massive transfusion protocol with bleeding ≥1.5 L
  • If blood not ready → O negative can be transfused

Order / Flow Emphasis

  • Emergency case becomes messy if not structured
  • Suggested order:
    • First: Oxytocin
    • Then: Tranexamic acid and Ergometrine
    • Later: Misoprostol / Carboprost

Key Expectation in Case

  • Show understanding of causes of primary postpartum hemorrhage

You are a HMO in a rural hospital, you receive a call from the birth suite, The nurse informs you of a 29 year old lady who delivered via NVD 15 minutes ago, delivering a 3.7kg baby who is having severe vaginal bleeding and has lost 1.5lit of blood. You are 5 minutes away from the birth suite.

Task:

• Talk to the nurse and take history - You will have a prompt time at 5mins

• Give advice about management steps she needs to take before you get there


  • This is a telephone case:
    • Talk to the nurse on the phone
    • Take history (including exam findings)
    • Then advise management steps

Nature of Telephone OSCE Cases

  • There are telephone cases (not only in O&G):
    • Telehealth red eye in medicine
    • Nurse calls for hyponatremia, etc.
  • Good thing:
    • Can use medical jargon freely
  • Important:
    • History from nurse must include examination findings
      • E.g., “Is uterus contracted or lax?”
  • Bad thing:
    • Management must be extremely detailed
    • Must explicitly name medications (not “just give a medication”)
    • Not required to know doses, but must know names

KEY POINTS & CRITICAL ERRORS (FROM HANDBOOK / EXAM EXPECTATION)

  • Throughout the discussion, your objective is to show:
    • You understand the causes of primary PPH
    • You manage all 4 T’s together
  • Because bleeding is massive and urgent:
    • You assess and manage simultaneously
  • Other causes cannot be excluded early, even if atony looks likely
  • You must:
    • Inspect placenta
    • Do speculum examination
    • Notify consultant/registrar
    • Prepare for likely surgical exploration under anesthesia

Critical Errors:

  1. Failure to consider cervical/vaginal laceration just because perineum is intact
    • Failing to indicate a speculum examination → critical error
  2. Failure to consider retained products of conception
    • Failing to indicate examination under anesthesia for possible retained tissue → critical error

Speculum examination:

    • “Speculum examination is NOT contraindicated and is MUST”

STRUCTURED APPROACH – THREE STEPS

  1. Primary survey and resuscitationDRS ABC
  2. Information gathering
    • Combination of:
      • History
      • Physical examination findings
      • Investigations
  3. Management of bleeding
    • Find the cause
    • Treat the cause
    • Explain management of all Four T’s

STEP 1 PRIMARY SURVEY IN THIS CASE – DRS ABC (ADJUSTED FOR TELEPHONE)

You are talking to a nurse, but still using DRS ABC:

  • D – Danger
    • No specific danger in the birth suite mentioned
  • R – Response
    • Ask nurse:
      • “Is the patient alert and responsive?”
    • Assess level of consciousness
  • S – Send for help
    • Key point:
      • “Please call my OG consultant or my obstetric registrar”
      • They need to be there now

Then ABC:

  • A – Airway
    • Ask: “Is the airway patent / open?”
  • B – Breathing
    • Ask: “Is the patient breathing?”
  • C – Circulation
    • Ask nurse to:
      • Check blood pressure
      • Check pulse rate
      • Check oxygen saturation
      • Start continuous monitoring of BP, PR, SpO₂

IV Access & Bloods

  • Take two large IV lines
  • Do two things:
    1. Start normal saline immediately
      • Some references mention 2 litres, or simply “start normal saline”
    2. Send bloods:
      • Blood group and cross match (most important)
      • Full blood examination
      • Coagulation studies / screening:
        • INR
        • APTT
        • Fibrinogen
        • etc.

Other Immediate Measures

  • Start oxygen
  • Put in a catheter:
    • Empty bladder
    • Start urine output monitoring
  • Activate massive transfusion protocol:
    • For cases with > 1.5 litres blood loss

(Step 2 – information gathering – is done in the case itself, not expanded here in detail.)

STEP 3 MANAGEMENT – FOUR T’s IN OSCE ANSWER

In management, regardless of information gathered, you must explain management of all Four T’s.

1 TONE (Atony) – Management

  • If not already done:
    • Ask to insert a catheter and empty the bladder
  • Remove clots
  • Start uterine massage
  • Pharmacological steps:
    • Oxytocin
    • Ergometrine
    • Misoprostol or carboprost (either/both as third/fourth options)
      • As long as oxytocin and ergometrine are mentioned, carboprost is less critical
  • If bleeding not controlled:
    • Ask midwife to perform bimanual compression
       (no need to explain technique; midwife is trained)
    • Prepare patient for theatre (operation theatre)

2 TISSUE – Management

  • Structure based on placenta status:

If placenta is NOT delivered

  • Ask to:
    • Perform controlled cord traction (CCT) – gentle traction
    • Continue controlled gentle traction to deliver placenta

Once placenta is delivered (or if already delivered)

  • Ask nurse/midwife to:
    • Check placenta for any missing parts
    • If any risk that part of placenta or membranes may have been left inside:
      • Prepare for manual removal
      • Prepare for uterine exploration in operation theatre

3 TRAUMA – Management

  • Ask nurse to:
    • Inspect perineum / genital area
    • Do a speculum examination first
  • Look for:
    • Any tears in the birth canal:
      • Cervical tears
      • Vaginal tears
      • Perineal tears
    • Episiotomy:
      • Check if it is bleeding
  • If any trauma found:
    • Ask to apply pressure to stop bleeding
    • Plan to repair it / stitch it (you will do it when you arrive; for now, pressure)

4 THROMBIN – Management

  • Start tranexamic acid
  • Give:
    • Platelets
    • FFPs
  • If bleeding very bad / massive:
    • Consult a hematologist
    • In massive transfusion protocol, typically haematologist is involved
  • Antiemetics:
    • Can give antiemetics (e.g., ondansetron)
    • Considered the least important part; can be mentioned briefly

Overall:

  • Primary PPH OSCE case is about:
    • Showing structured approach
    • Demonstrating differentials (Four T’s) clearly
    • Doing a proper primary survey and resuscitation
    • Gathering information efficiently from the nurse/midwife
    • Explaining detailed, specific management for each T, including:
      • Resuscitation
      • Uterotonics
      • Speculum examination
      • Placenta check and uterine exploration
      • Massive transfusion protocol
      • Theatre and surgical options

You are a HMO in a rural hospital, you receive a call from the birth suite, The nurse informs you of a 29 year old lady who delivered via NVD 15 minutes ago, delivering a 3.7kg baby who is having severe vaginal bleeding and has lost 1.5lit of blood. You are 5 minutes away from the birth suite.

Task:

• Talk to the nurse and take history - You will have a prompt time at 5mins

• Give advice about management steps she needs to take before you get there

  • You are an HMO in a rural hospital
  • Face-to-face exam setup:
    • You enter the room, there is a phone in the middle of the station
    • You pick up the phone and start talking to the nurse/midwife

1.1 ISBAR-STYLE INTRODUCTION

  • This is very similar to ISBAR – structured communication with colleagues
  • First step when you pick up the phone:
    • Introduce yourself:
      • “My name is …, I’m the HMO / medical officer.”
    • Clarify who you are talking to:
      • “Who am I talking to?”
    • The examiner (as midwife/nurse) responds:
      • “I’m Jane, I’m the midwife / nurse.”
    • Acknowledge:
      • “Nice to meet you.”

1.2 SEMI-OPEN-ENDED OPENING QUESTION

  • Do not jump straight into “Check vital signs, do this, do that.”
  • Start naturally, like with a patient:
    • Use a semi-open-ended question to assess the situation:
      • “Can you tell me more about what has happened or what has happened so far?”
  • The midwife gives an opening statement (similar to a role-player’s script):
    • Example:
      • “Doctor, I have this 29-year-old lady, first pregnancy. She delivered 15 minutes ago with a vaginal delivery, a 3.7 kg baby. Since then she’s bled 1.5 liters and the bleeding is not stopping. We want you to come and see this patient.”

2. STRUCTURE OF YOUR APPROACH (DIVIDED INTO STEPS/PHASES)

  • To avoid making things messy, divide the case into clear steps:
  1. Step / Phase 1: Resuscitation or Primary Survey
  2. Step / Phase 2: Information gathering
    • History + examination findings (via the midwife) + any investigations
  3. Step / Phase 3: Managing the bleeding
    • Find the cause
    • Treat the cause
    • Address all Four T’s

3. STEP / PHASE 1: RESUSCITATION / PRIMARY SURVEY (DRS ABC)

  • You are doing the DRS ABC that you already know, adjusted to a telephone OSCE:

3.1 D – Danger

  • No specific danger in the birth suite mentioned in this scenario

3.2 R – Response

  • Ask the midwife:
    • “Is the patient responsive and alert?”
  • You are assessing the level of consciousness

3.3 S – Send for Help

  • Key point and exam expectation:
    • “I want you to send for help.”
    • “Please notify the consultant or registrar.”
  • One of the consultants always available:
    • Obstetric consultant

3.4 A – Airway

  • Ask:
    • “Can you please first check the airway?”
    • “Is the airway patent/open?”

3.5 B – Breathing

  • Ask:
    • “Can you please check if she’s breathing?”
  • Even if she is responsive and talking (which implies breathing and patent airway), you still mention airway and breathing as part of the structured approach:
    • “Even if she’s responsive, can you just make sure that her airway is patent and that she’s breathing?”

3.6 C – Circulation

  • Circulation gives you a lot to talk about:
    • “Can you tell me the blood pressure?”
    • “Can you tell me the pulse rate?”
    • “Can you tell me the oxygen saturation?”
  • Example of findings given:
    • BP: 80/50
    • Pulse: 120
    • Saturation: 98%
  • Interpretation:
    • Unstable patient, almost going into shock
  • Instructions to midwife:
    • “I want you to take two IV lines, two large IV lines.”
    • “I want you to take some blood for investigations – I’ll tell you the investigations later.”
    • “Once you take your blood, I want you to immediately start normal saline and fluids for this lady.”
      • You may specify 2 litres or simply “start normal saline.”
    • “I would like you to continuously monitor the vital signs.”
      • Continuous monitoring of:
        • Blood pressure
        • Pulse rate
        • Oxygen saturation
  • At this point you have essentially done resuscitation/stabilisation:
    • “Please start giving her fluids immediately.”
    • “I want you to continuously monitor her blood pressure and pulse rate and see how that goes.”
  • Note on not mixing everything:
    • You could start mentioning oxygen, bladder emptying, etc., here, but this is where people start mixing steps.
    • The lecturer stops here and treats this as resuscitation only, not yet detailed management.

4. STEP / PHASE 2: INFORMATION GATHERING

  • After stabilising:
    • “We did our resuscitation and we’re good. Let’s start our history.”
  • Again, this is still through the midwife:
    • This phase includes both history and examination findings.

4.1 Start with the Complaint – Explore the Bleeding

  • First group of questions: about the bleeding
  • Example:
    • “Tell me about the bleeding.”
  • Ask specifically:
  1. Colour:
    • “Can you describe the color to me?”
    • “Is it bright red or dark red?”
  2. Content / Clots:
    • “Is she passing any clots?”
  3. Consistency / Volume:
    • “Is blood pouring out?”
    • Is it just a small amount / few drops, or a significant amount?
    • Is it a gush of blood?
    • Any wording to clarify ongoing severe bleeding is acceptable.
  4. Blood Group:
    • “ can you tell me the blood group?”

4.2 Delivery (Obstetric) History – Focus on This Episode of Labour

  • Main focus: delivery history first, not whole antenatal history.

Ask:

  • “How long did the labor last?”
    • Looking for prolonged labor (risk factor for atony)
  • “Did you use any instruments?”
  • “Did you need to do an episiotomy?”
    • You can directly use the term episiotomy since you’re talking to a health professional
  • “Was this a single pregnancy?”
    • Single vs multiple → risk factor for atony
  • “Any previous history of cesarean sections or uterine surgeries?”
    • Starting to worry about rupture
  • “Has the placenta been delivered?”
    • If yes:
      • “Can you check it and make sure it’s complete?”
      • The midwife may respond:
        • “Yes, doctor, I’ve already checked it and it is complete. It’s not missing any parts or any membranes.”

4.3 Thrombin – Bleeding Disorder Questions

Ask about bleeding disorders and clotting:

  1. “Does this lady have any past medical history or family history of bleeding disorders?”
  2. “Is this lady taking any blood thinners – any type of blood thinner that can increase the risk of bleeding?”
  3. “Can you check if the blood is clotting?”
    • There is blood all over the place:
      • “Is the blood clotting or not?”
    • This gives you an idea about deep bleeding disorders.

5. STEP / PHASE 2 CONTINUED: EXAMINATION VIA THE MIDWIFE

    • So far: all history / questions (information gathering).
    • Now: start examining the lady, still via the midwife.

5.1 Uterine Examination

Ask the midwife to palpate the uterus:

  • “Can you feel the uterus?”
  • “Tell me the size.”
    • Example response: “It’s at the level of the umbilicus, about 20 weeks.”
  • “Is the uterus lax and soft?”
  • “Is it painful on palpation or painful to touch?”
    • You’re looking for clues to rupture:
      • E.g., tenderness/pain.

Typical finding in this case:

  • “Yes, it is kind of soft.”
  • “No, it’s not painful.”

5.2 Looking for Trauma – Perineum and Speculum

  • “I want you to start looking for tears step by step.”
  1. Inspect perineum:
    • “Inspect the perineum.”
    • “Do you see any tears or lacerations?”
    • Midwife might say: “No, I can’t see any.”
  2. Speculum examination:
    • “Put in a speculum, please.”
    • “Tell me, do you see any tears in the cervix or the vagina?”
    • Yes or no.
  • This completes your targeted examination component of information gathering.

6. STEP / PHASE 3: MANAGEMENT – GENERAL MEASURES AND FOUR T’s

  • After information gathering, you move into management:
    • Important rule:
      • “In your management, please talk about all Four T’s.”
      • “I don’t care what finding you get, I don’t care what they give you tomorrow – talk about all Four T’s.”
      • “One is more probable, but the rest still need addressing.”

6.1 Ongoing Monitoring and Support

  • First, general instructions:
  1. Vital signs:
    • “I want you to continue monitoring the vital signs.”
    • “I need continuous, close monitoring of the vital signs of this lady.”
  2. Catheter and fluid chart:
    • “Please put in a catheter.”
    • “I want you to keep an eye on the urine output.”
    • “Make a fluid chart for her because I want to see how much fluid we’re giving this patient.”
  3. Blood tests (specific):
    • “I need you to send bloods for:
      • Full blood examination (looking at haemoglobin)
      • Blood group and cross-match (maybe the most important)
      • Coagulation screening:
        • “Get me an INR, APTT, and so on.”
  4. Massive transfusion protocol: (MTP)
    • Reminder:
      • “This lady has bled 1.5 litres of blood.”
    • “I want you to arrange or activate the massive transfusion protocol.”
    • “Prepare blood for her.”
    • Preferably:
      • O-negative blood until group & cross-match is back.
  5. Oxygen:
    • “Start giving this lady oxygen.”
    • Can be mentioned here or earlier in resuscitation, but keep it clearly separated in your structure.

7. MANAGEMENT BY T’S – DETAILED INSTRUCTIONS TO THE MIDWIFE

7.1 TONE – Uterine Atony

  • Mechanical measures:
    • “I want you to start massaging the uterus.”
    • “Please remove any clots.”
  • Pharmacological measures (stepwise):
    • “I want you to start this lady on oxytocin.”
    • “If the bleeding doesn’t stop, you can give her ergometrin.”
    • “Prepare the next line for me too – prepare misoprostol, carboprost, whichever one you’ve decided to mention as your third line.”
  • If bleeding still doesn’t stop:
    • “Please proceed to a bimanual compression by the time I get there.”
  • Overall, as you are 5 minutes away:
    • “Start massaging.”
    • “Remove clots.”
    • “Start giving her medication – initially oxytocin.”
    • “If bleeding hasn’t stopped, give ergometrin.”
    • “You can even proceed to a bimanual compression.”

7.2 THROMBIN – Tranexamic Acid & Coagulation

  • “I want you to give her tranexamic acid.”
  • Reminder:
    • You have already sent coagulation screening.
  • In massive bleeding, you will also be giving:
    • Blood products (platelets, FFPs) as part of the massive transfusion protocol.

7.3 FIFTH T – THEATRE (Uterine Exploration)

  • “Maybe the most important key thing that you should not forget is your fifth T.”
  • Repeat instruction to call senior help:
    • “Tell the midwife one more time, I want you to call the registrar or the consultant.”
  • Theatre preparation:
    • “I want you to tell them to prepare to take this patient to the theatre.”
    • Reason:
      • “Because there is a chance that we will need a uterine exploration.”
  • In uterine exploration:
    • “We may need to do a manual compression to stop the bleeding.”
    • “Exploration will be looking for tears and repairing.”
    • “Looking for tissue.”
    • “If there’s atony, we’re going to do a manual compression.”

You are the HMO in OB ward, a 30 year old lady has just delivered a 3.7kg baby 15minutes ago. The midwife was doing a controlled cord traction but the cord snapped and the placenta was left inside. She has already bled 1.5 lit.

• Task:

• Talk to the nurse over the phone

• Advice on Immediate management

KEY FOCUS OF THIS VERSION

  • This lady has a problem with Tissue
    • Retained placenta
  • Immediate management:
    • We need to deliver the placenta
  • Theatre is required, but:
    • Cannot take her to theatre immediately
    • Need to manage bleeding until then

What to do before theatre:

  • Put in a speculum
  • Ask: “Are you seeing the cord?”
    • If yes:
      • Clamp the cord
        • This stops the bleeding for now
      • If there is any length of cord left:
        • With the same clamp, start controlled gentle traction
    • If you are not able to deliver the placenta:
      • Go for manual delivery of the placenta in theatre
        • Put your hand inside and pull it out

STRUCTURE

1. Start the Case – Introduction & Opening Question

  • Same as version 1:
    • Introduce yourself:
      • Name, role (HMO / medical officer)
    • Clarify who you’re talking to:
      • “Who am I talking to?”
      • Midwife replies: “I was pulling the cord, doctor, it suddenly snapped and she started having this very severe bleeding.”
  • Ask a semi-open-ended question:
    • “Can you tell me what has happened so far?”
    • Midwife gives opening story:
      • Controlled cord traction
      • Cord snapped
      • Severe bleeding

2. Phase 1 – Resuscitation / Primary Survey (DRS ABC)

  • Same DRS ABC as previous version:

D – Danger

  • Same structure; no specific external danger highlighted

R – Response

  • “Is the patient responsive and alert?”

S – Send/Seek for Help

  • “Please notify the consultant or registrar.”

A – Airway

  • “Check the airway.”

B – Breathing

  • “Check if she’s breathing.”

C – Circulation

  • “Tell me the blood pressure.”
  • “Tell me the pulse rate.”
  • “Tell me the saturation.”
  • “Please continuously monitor this for me.”
  • Instructions:
    • “Take two IV lines.”
    • “Grab the blood – I’ll tell you later what to send them for.”
    • “Immediately start normal saline for me.”
    • If you want, you can add oxygen here as well.

NOTE ON “DANGER” AND CLAMPING THE CORD

  • Some may try to treat the snapped cord as a danger under D:
    • E.g., clamp the cord there
  • Lecturer’s view:
    • Does not like doing this at Danger
    • If airway is not patent, you will open the airway first, not clamp the cord
    • So:
      • Does not consider snapped cord as a “danger” step
      • Prefers to do clamping later in the structure

3. Phase 2 – Information Gathering

  • Then move to information gathering phase:

History

  • Do the same delivery history as version 1
  • Ask the same thrombin questions
  • Ask the same tissue questions
  • Difference here:
    • You already know the placenta has not been delivered

Examination

  • Do it very similar to version 1:
  1. Tone:
    • “I want you to check the tone.”
    • “Feel the uterus, tell me the size.”
    • “Is it soft and lax?”
    • “Is it painful?”
    • Important teaching:
      • Retained placenta is one good important reason for uterine atony
  2. Trauma:
    • “Go for inspection.”
    • “Look for tears.”
    • “Put in a speculum.”
  3. Speculum – Cord and Tears:
    • After you look for tears:
      • “Do you see the cord?”
      • Midwife says: “Yes, I can see it inside.”
      • You say:
        • “Can you please clamp it?”
        • Midwife: “Okay doctor, I’ve clamped it.”
        • Bleeding stops.
      • Then:
        • “Now that you’ve clamped it, can you please gently apply a cord traction and see if you are able to remove it?”
        • Midwife: “I’ll start doing that.”
    • If she can’t deliver it:
      • “If you’re not able to deliver the placenta, please call the consultant.”
      • From this point:
        • You are stepping into management
        • “We need to prepare her for theatre or a manual surgical removal of the placenta.”

4. Phase 3 – Management

4.1 Tissue – Core Action in This Case

  • If you see the cord:
    • Clamp it
    • Start pulling it gently (controlled traction)
    • If you are able to deliver the placenta:
      • “Please check if the placenta has been delivered completely.”
    • If you are not able to deliver it:
      • “Please call the consultant.”
      • “We need to prepare this lady for operation theatre.”
      • “We’re going to go for a manual removal of the placenta.”

4.2 Tone / Medications

  • “I’ll do everything else.”
    • “I want you to start this lady on oxytocin.”
    • “You can give this lady antiemetics.”
    • “I want you to give this lady tranexamic acid.”
    • “If the bleeding doesn’t stop, we may consider doing other medications like:
      • Ergometrin
      • Carboprost
      • Misoprostol too.”

4.3 If Bleeding Continues After Placenta Delivered

  • “If the bleeding doesn’t stop after delivering the placenta:
    • We are going to do a bimanual compression.”
  • Or consider surgical methods:
    • “Maybe the best one is arterial ligation.”

5. TEACHING POINT – STILL USE ALL FOUR T’s

  • Even though the stem is very obvious:
    • Tissue / retained placenta / placenta problem
  • You still talk about your Four T’s:
    • Tone
    • Tissue
    • Trauma
    • Thrombin

✏️ Corrections

✏️ Primary PPH is defined only by timing ('bleeding in the first 24 hours after delivery') with no blood-loss threshold.
RANZCOG/RCOG define primary PPH as blood loss >=500 mL from the genital tract within 24 hours of birth (>=1000 mL or any loss causing haemodynamic compromise = major/severe PPH). Document the estimated/measured blood loss (here 1.5 L = major PPH) as it triggers massive transfusion protocol activation.
✏️ Uterotonic sequence lists oxytocin, ergometrine, misoprostol, carboprost without noting contraindications.
Ergometrine (and Syntometrine) is contraindicated in hypertension/preeclampsia, and carboprost (prostaglandin F2-alpha) is contraindicated in asthma. These contraindications must be checked and documented before administration (eTG/RANZCOG PPH pathway).

⭐ Suggested Enhancements

⭐ Use Australian spelling (haemorrhage, haematologist, oedema, caesarean, labour) and spell out drug/units safely per ACSQHC (write 'units', avoid 'U'); tranexamic acid dose is 1 g IV (repeat once if bleeding continues) per the WOMAN trial - give within 3 hours of onset.
⭐ Structure the telephone handover explicitly as ISBAR and document it as such (Identify/Situation/Background/Assessment/Recommendation) - the case already uses ISBAR-style intro; labelling it meets clinical-handover documentation standards.
⭐ Record the 4 T's assessment findings, not just the framework: Tone (uterine tone/fundal height), Trauma (perineal + speculum for cervical/vaginal tears), Tissue (placenta and membranes complete), Thrombin (clotting/coagulation screen) - and the massive transfusion 1:1:1 (RBC:FFP:platelets) plan with O-negative until crossmatch returns.
⭐ Add named critical-error safeguards to the record: speculum examination is NOT contraindicated and is mandatory; consider examination under anaesthesia for retained products; escalate early to obstetric registrar/consultant and prepare theatre (the 5th T).
