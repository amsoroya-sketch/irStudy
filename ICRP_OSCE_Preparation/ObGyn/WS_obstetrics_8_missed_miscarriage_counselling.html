Missed Miscarriage - Breaking Bad News and Management Counselling

Source: Dr. Amir workshop — obstetrics / class 3 (Aug 2026 drop) · Specialty: obstetrics_gynaecology

MISSED MISCARRIAGE COUNSELLING

Your next patient is a 25 year old lady, GA 8 weeks. Presented to you with vaginal bleeding.

On examination: Cervical os is closed

USD: No fetal cardiac activity

Task:

• Explain the diagnosis to her (Break the bad news? )

• Counsel regarding further management

  • Pregnancy has stopped, no longer viable
  • Best indication: no fetal cardiac activity
  • Pregnancy sac still inside uterus
  • Cervical os closed
  • No passage of tissue
  • Small vaginal bleeding may occur
  • Products of conception have stayed inside the womb

1. Management Overview

Three options:

  1. Expectant management (waiting)
  2. Medical management (misoprostol + mifepristone)
  3. Surgical management (suction curettage / D&C)
  • Decision depends on gestational age and thresholds for termination
  • 7–9 weeks → expectant / medical / surgical
  • ≥ 9 weeks → preferably surgery
  • Very early (e.g., 6 weeks) → may repeat scan in 1 week
  • Ultimately: patient-centered decision-making

2. Pros and Cons

Expectant Management

Pros:

  • No medication → no side effects
  • No surgery → avoids complications (ectopic risk, placenta previa, perforation, infection, etc.)

Cons:

  • Unpredictable timing: may occur tomorrow or in 2 weeks
  • Ongoing pain and bleeding
  • If picked up at 8 weeks and wait 2 weeks → cross 9-week threshold → may need surgery
  • If body does not pass tissue → need surgical management

Medical Management

Indicated when:

  • Lady does not want to keep products inside
  • Wants to avoid surgical/anesthetic complications
  • Can avoid hospital admission
  • Uses misoprostol + mifepristone (more effective than misoprostol alone per some guidelines)

Pros:

  • A few pills
  • Avoids surgery
  • Can be done at home

Cons:

  • Still unpredictable
  • Pain and bleeding will occur
  • Medication side effects: nausea, vomiting, diarrhea
  • Small chance of failure → may need surgery

Surgical Management (D&C / Suction Curettage)

Procedure:

  • Day admission
  • Vaginal approach, tube inserted into womb
  • Removes products of pregnancy

Pros:

  • Planned procedure
  • Predictable timeframe
  • Immediate relief from symptoms
  • Less blood loss
  • Shorter duration of bleeding
  • Less pain

Cons:

  • Risks of surgery (infection, perforation, bleeding, injury to organs, future ectopic pregnancy)

3. Breaking Bad News – Review

Key Concepts

  • Spikes protocol is UK-based; exam requires a simplified approach
  • Steps needed acc to john murtag:
    • Assess patient’s interest
    • Establish patient’s belief
    • Provide accurate info
    • Monitor patient’s feelings
    • Involve family if possible

SPIKES Mnemonic (discussed but not exam focus)

  • Setting
  • Perception
  • Invitation
  • Knowledge
  • Emotions
  • Summary & strategy

Simplified Exam Approach

  • Mini-preparation (no lengthy build-up)
  • Offer: “Would you like anyone to be with you?”
  • Open-ended question → “What has happened so far?”
  • Assess perception → “Did the doctor tell you why we want to do a scan?”
  • Use burger technique: normal findings → bad news → normal if available
  • Warning shot: “I’m so sorry, I don’t have good news today.”
  • Deliver diagnosis clearly
  • Allow silence (count to 10)
  • Provide empathy statements
  • Offer tissue/water if crying
  • Ask: “Would you like to discuss the management later in another consultation?” if needed
  • Must proceed with management regardless of ongoing crying

OSCE Scripted Consultation Flow (need really good acting)

Start

  • Introduce yourself
  • Begin with: “Can you tell me what has happened so far?”
  • Patient explains her story

Assess Perception

  • “Did the other doctor tell you why we want to do a scan?”

Start with Normal Findings

  • “on your examination , I saw the opening of your womb is still closed.”
  • “You have not passed any products of pregnancy.”

On USG results Warning Shot

  • “on your USG scan, I’m so sorry to tell you that I don’t have good news today. Do u want someone to be with u?”

Deliver the Bad News on USG

  • “The USG has seen the Sac but Not able to detect fetal cardiac activity”
  • “This suggests you have had a miscarriage.”

Emotional Response Handling

Empathy statements:

  • “I’m so sorry that I had to give you bad news today.”
  • “I can imagine how difficult this can be.”
  • “I wish I had better news today.”

Other points:

  • Offer tissue only if crying
  • Offer water
  • Allow silence (count to 10)
  • Once emotions are addressed, reassure:
    • “You are still young and can have healthy pregnancies and healthy babies.”

Offer Support Person (two points where this may occur)

  • After warning shot
  • After emotions settle

If patient continues crying

  • Ask: “Would you like to discuss the management later in another consultation?”
  • But still proceed to management because required for station

Diagnosis Explanation

  • “as the Products of pregnancy have not passed yet, we call it missed miscarriage
  • We have Three options:
    1. Expectant management (wait & watch)
    2. Medical management
    3. Surgical management

Management Explanation

Option 1: Expectant Management:

“once the pregnancy stops, the body passes the products of pregnancy in 2 weeks time, we can wait for this to happen which Will cause period-like bleeding and Passing some tissue is expected”

Pros:

  • Avoids medication side effects
  • Avoids surgical complications

Cons:

  • Unpredictable timeline
  • Ongoing pain + bleeding
  • If does not pass →we will need to proceed for surgery
  • If wait 2 weeks from 8 weeks → becomes 10 weeks → no medical option

Option 2: Medical Management

  • We give u some Medications, misoprostol + mifepristone
  • They Triggers period-like bleeding and u pass products of pregnancy.

Pros:

  • Avoids surgery
  • Only takes a few tablets
  • Can be done at home
  • No need for admission

Cons:

  • Unpredictable
  • Side effects: nausea, vomiting, diarrhea
  • ~5% failure → surgical management

Option 3: Surgical Management

  • Which is called “Suction curettage” or “dilatation and curettage”

  • Tube inserted into womb through Vagina to remove products of pregnancy

Pros:

  • Predictable
  • Immediate relief
  • Less blood loss
  • Shorter bleeding duration
  • Less pain

Cons:

  • Risks of surgery: infection, perforation, bleeding, injury to organs, future risk of ectopoc pregnancy.

Support

  • Support groups (example given): Miscarriage Australia
  • I Can arrange meeting with partner
  • I want to Reassure you “You can still have healthy and normal pregnancies in the future.”

If extra time

  • Assess emotion: “How are you feeling?”
  • Assess understanding: “Do you have any questions?”

Important Clarification from Lecturer

  • Recall missing details: the stem did not mention sac presence
  • For diagnosis: sac must be seen + no cardiac activity
  • Expect exam to give full info (e.g., “uterus still large” or “sac seen but no cardiac activity”)

🔬 Expert Review & Enhancements

Reviewed by: clinical-documentation-expert (Australian clinical supervisor, communication and O&G standards) · fact-checked against the irStudy medical textbook RAG index.

✏️ Corrections

✏️ The note states the SPIKES protocol is 'UK-based'. SPIKES was developed by Baile, Buckman and colleagues (North American - MD Anderson/Canada), not UK.
Correct the origin: SPIKES (Setting, Perception, Invitation, Knowledge, Emotions, Strategy/Summary) is a North American breaking-bad-news framework. This does not change the exam approach but the factual attribution should be fixed.
✏️ Anti-D prophylaxis is not mentioned anywhere in the missed-miscarriage management, despite surgical and medical evacuation being sensitising events.
Add: for Rh(D)-negative women, give Rh(D) immunoglobulin 250 IU (first trimester) within 72 hours when undergoing surgical or medical management of miscarriage (and for significant bleeding). Blood group/antibody screen should be documented as part of workup.
✏️ The rigid gestational-age rule '>=9 weeks -> preferably surgery' is presented as a firm cut-off.
Reframe as patient-centred: expectant, medical (mifepristone then misoprostol) and surgical management are all valid options across the first trimester; surgical evacuation is preferred for heavy bleeding, haemodynamic instability, infection, patient choice or failed medical management, rather than a strict 9-week threshold.

⭐ Suggested Enhancements

⭐ Document confirmation-of-diagnosis governance: an incidental first-scan finding of no cardiac activity should ideally be confirmed (repeat scan or second sonologist, applying CRL >=7 mm / MSD >=25 mm criteria) before definitive counselling - the lecturer's own 'important clarification' hints at this and it should be stated explicitly.
⭐ Add practical safety-netting for whichever option is chosen: expected bleeding/pain, when to seek help (heavy soaking bleeding, fever, offensive discharge, severe pain -> present/000), and analgesia (paracetamol +/- as advised).
⭐ Add bereavement and follow-up care: offer written information, GP/EPAS follow-up, discussion of histopathology, contraception/future pregnancy planning, and correct the informal empathy scripting ('u', 'coz') into professional documentation language.
⭐ Reference RANZCOG 'Management of Miscarriage', eTG, and the Australian support resources (e.g. SANDS, Miscarriage Australia, Pink Elephants) as metadata.
