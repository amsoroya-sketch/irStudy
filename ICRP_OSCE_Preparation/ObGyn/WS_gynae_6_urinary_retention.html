Acute Urinary Retention (Genital Herpes; Adnexal Mass Variant)

Source: Dr. Amir workshop — gynae / 3. Vulvar problems (Aug 2026 drop) · Specialty: obstetrics_gynaecology

URINARY RETENTION

CASE STEM

  • 24-year-old lady
  • Presented to ED with urinary retention
  • Cannot pass urine due to pain
  • Tasks: History, physical exam on screen, explain diagnosis & differential, management

GENERAL DISCUSSION

  • Old AMC case with several versions
  • Common recall: herpes case, but must know differentials
  • Do NOT jump to management before at least touching/looking for a distended bladder
  • Must still take a history first, even with urgency
  • In OSCE: follow the order given in the task, not your own order

DIFFERENTIALS

1. Outflow Obstruction – Pelvic Masses

  • Cancers
  • Ovarian cancer / ovarian cyst
  • Large fibroids
  • Pelvic masses pressing on the bladder
  • Old version: adnexal mass instead of herpes rash

2. Infections

  • Herpes (famous case)
  • UTIs → irritation, cystitis, pain
  • Bladder stone (rare)

3. Neurological Causes

  • Spine trauma
  • Fall with massive spine trauma
  • Epidural abscesses

4. Traumatic Causes

  • Pelvic trauma
  • Back trauma

HISTORY TAKING FRAMEWORK

Opening

  • Start with open-ended question: “How can I help you?”
  • Expect distress (pain, can’t pass urine for 18 hrs)

Addressing Urgency (as taught)

  • “I’ll arrange someone to prepare a painkiller and prepare to empty the bladder, but I need to ask you a few questions first.”

1. TIMING OF COMPLAINT

  • When was the last time you passed urine? → “18 hours ago”
  • Is this the first time?
  • Did it happen suddenly or gradually?

2. PAIN ASSESSMENT

  • Site
  • Intensity
  • Radiation
  • Better/worse factors
  • Show you are doing a pain assessment

3. SERIOUS DIFFERENTIAL – CANCERS

  • Any recent loss of weight?
  • Any changes in appetite?
  • Any lumps/bumps?
  • Any tiredness?
  • Any family history of cancers?

4. FIBROID ASSESSMENT

  • Period assessment
    • When was your last period?
    • Heavy periods?
    • Excessive bleeding?
    • Painful periods?
  • Abdominal pain and pressure
    • Any abdominal pain or pressure sensation?

5. OVARIAN CANCER QUESTIONS

  • Any bloating?
  • Any changes in bowel motions?
  • Constipation / impaction can also give retention

6. UTI QUESTIONS

(Ask these “before this happened” because patient is not passing urine now)

  • Before this happened, did you have pain on passing urine?
  • Before this happened, were you passing urine more frequently?
  • Before this happened, any blood in urine?
  • Fever or chills?

7. HERPES HISTORY

  • Any rashes? → Patient will say yes
  • For the rash:
    • Can u Describe the rash, is it Red patch? Painful ulcer? Blisters?
    • Any discharge?

Sexual history

  • Are you sexually active?
  • Are you in a stable relationship?
  • Any multiple partners?
  • Do you practice safe sex / use condoms?
  • Any previous STIs?

Key point in herpes history: Sexual history

8. TRAUMA HISTORY

  • Any trauma or injuries to private parts?
  • Any trauma to back?

9. SPINAL / NEUROLOGICAL QUESTIONS

  • Any back pain?
  • Any weakness or numbness in legs?

10. FIVE Ps (remaining items)

  • Period history: already done
  • Partner history: covered in sexual history
  • Previous pregnancies
  • Previous surgeries down below (adhesions)
  • Cervical screening (not relevant but still “nice to do”)

PHYSICAL EXAMINATION (from screen card)

  • Suprapubic mass = distended bladder
  • Genital exam: typical genital herpes
    • Photo shows ulcerations = ruptured vesicles
    • Still herpes

DIAGNOSIS (as explained)

  • “Most likely you have a condition called genital herpes.
  • This is a sexually transmitted infection.
  • It causes painful blisters and ulcers on the private parts.
  • The severe pain affects your bladder and causes retention.”

(Do not over-explain sphincter contraction; keep it simple as taught.)

MANAGEMENT

1. Treat URINARY RETENTION

  • Do not catheterize if active herpes lesions:
    • Will push herpes inside
    • Extremely painful
  • we are going to empty your bladder by a method called suprapubic aspiration:
    • “Insert a small needle into the lower abdomen to empty the bladder.”
    • Reassure: “This is a very safe procedure.”

2. Treat HERPES

Pain relief

  • Painkillers
  • Lignocaine gel for local numbness, especially when passing urine

Antivirals

(Do not need to memorize doses, but lecture provides:)

  • Valacyclovir (preferred) 500 mg twice a day
  • Acyclovir or Famciclovir also acceptable
  • Duration 5–10 days for initial episode
  • Shorter duration for recurrent episodes

OTHER VERSION (OLD RECALL) – ADNEXAL MASS

  • Suprapubic mass + adnexal mass
  • Suspect:
    • Ovarian cancer
    • Massive ovarian cyst (benign or malignant)
  • Management:
    • Imaging, start with ultrasound
    • Look at characteristics of ovary / cyst
    • Likely need surgical removal of mass or cyst

🔬 Expert Review & Enhancements

Reviewed by: clinical-documentation-expert (Australian clinical supervisor, O&G / AMC OSCE) · fact-checked against the irStudy medical textbook RAG index.

✏️ Corrections

✏️ Management of the retention states 'empty your bladder by a method called suprapubic aspiration (insert a small needle into the lower abdomen)'. A single needle aspiration does not treat ongoing retention (18 hours, distended bladder) - it will re-fill.
Definitive management of acute urinary retention is continuous bladder drainage. Where urethral catheterisation is too painful due to active herpetic lesions, a suprapubic CATHETER (indwelling), not a one-off aspiration, is the appropriate option; alternatively a urethral catheter inserted with copious lignocaine gel is often feasible. Provide analgesia, admit/observe, and document post-void residual and renal function.
✏️ Antiviral names use American spelling 'Valacyclovir/Acyclovir'.
Use Australian approved names: valaciclovir and aciclovir. eTG first-episode genital herpes: valaciclovir 500 mg orally BD for 5-10 days, OR famciclovir 250 mg orally TDS, OR aciclovir 400 mg orally TDS, for 5-10 days (until lesions healed).
✏️ The neurological differential (spine trauma, epidural abscess) is listed but cauda equina syndrome is not named as the key emergency to exclude.
Explicitly screen for and name cauda equina syndrome: retention with saddle (perineal) anaesthesia, bilateral leg weakness/numbness and altered bowel/anal tone requires urgent MRI and emergency neurosurgical referral - it is a can't-miss cause of retention.

⭐ Suggested Enhancements

⭐ Add that acute painful herpetic retention is often neurogenic (sacral radiculopathy / herpetic sacral autonomic dysfunction, Elsberg syndrome) as well as pain-mediated - useful for the patient explanation and for justifying catheter drainage rather than a single aspiration.
⭐ Add the immediate ED bundle: analgesia (paracetamol +/- opioid), bladder scan to confirm/quantify retention, urinalysis/MSU, and beta-hCG in any woman of reproductive age (24-year-old) before imaging or treatment.
⭐ For the adnexal-mass variant, add tumour markers (CA-125, and in a young woman LDH/AFP/beta-hCG for germ-cell tumours) alongside pelvic ultrasound, and note ovarian torsion as a surgical emergency to exclude.
⭐ Add STI/public-health follow-up for confirmed herpes: full STI screen (HIV, syphilis, chlamydia/gonorrhoea), partner discussion, condom advice, and counselling on recurrence and antiviral suppression options.
⭐ Add metadata and cross-link to the vaginal discharge/STI station and the vulvar lump station which share sexual-history and genital-examination structure.
