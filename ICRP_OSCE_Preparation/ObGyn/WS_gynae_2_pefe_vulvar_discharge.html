Pelvic Examination From Examiner - Vaginal Discharge

Source: Dr. Amir workshop — gynae / 3. Vulvar problems (Aug 2026 drop) · Specialty: obstetrics_gynaecology

PHYSICAL EXAMINATION FROM EXAMINER – VAGINAL DISCHARGE

1. GENERAL APPEARANCE

  • Main concern in vaginal discharge cases is PID.
  • PID = “mother of all STIs”, severe infection (endometritis, cervicitis, salpingitis), can progress to sepsis and require ED + IV antibiotics.
  • Because of this severity → treat aggressively when suspected.

What to assess

  • Septic features
  • Alert or not
  • Dehydration

These relate back to concern about PID and severe infection.

2. VITAL SIGNS

  • All vital signs important because they help categorize PID:
    • Severe PID if abnormal vitals.
  • Look specifically for:
    • Temperature (fever)
    • Blood pressure
    • Pulse rate
    • Respiratory rate (to complete the set)

3. ABDOMINAL EXAMINATION

  • Although bimanual exam overlaps with abdominal + pelvic components, still do a separate abdominal exam.
  • Reason: looking for abdominal features relevant to discharge cases.

Check for

  • Masses
  • Tenderness
  • Make this part very quick.

4. PELVIC EXAMINATION

(With consent and chaperone.)

4A. Inspection

Looking for:

  • Bleeding
  • Discharge
  • Redness and rashes (many discharge cases are STI-related)
  • Bartholin abscesses
  • Skin abscesses / skin problems
  • Swelling or lumps
    • Especially Bartholin abscess
  • All “skin issues that give you discharge” and “STIs that give you rashes”.

4B. Speculum Examination

Stick to structure: cervix + vaginal wall

Vaginal Wall

  • Important for atrophic vaginitis
  • If you don’t ask, examiner won’t volunteer:
    • dry, thin, pale

Cervix

  • Important because most important differential in discharge = cervical cancer
  • Look for:
    • Any growth
    • Abnormal masses
    • Ectropion (AMC “loves” this)
    • Discharge

If discharge present

  • Tell examiner: “I’d like to take a swab.”
  • Although borderline between exam vs investigation, do it now (don’t remove speculum and return later).
  • Swab types not discussed unless your task is investigations.

4C. Bimanual Examination

CMT (Cervical Motion Tenderness)

  • Important because positive in PID

Uterus

  • Tenderness (most important for PID)
  • Also: size and borders

Adnexa

  • Adnexal masses
  • Adnexal tenderness

Closing Teaching Points

  • Having a good PFAS structure means you automatically tick key points in gynecology cases.
  • Vaginal wall should always stay in your structure:
    • Included specifically so you don’t miss atrophic vaginitis, which appears in AMC.
  • Overall, a simple case as long as you recall the list of differentials explained in the lecture and understand why each step is done.

🔬 Expert Review & Enhancements

Reviewed by: clinical-documentation-expert (Australian clinical supervisor, O&G / AMC OSCE) · fact-checked against the irStudy medical textbook RAG index.

✏️ Corrections

✏️ PID is described as the 'mother of all STIs'. PID is not itself an STI - it is a clinical syndrome (endometritis/salpingitis/tubo-ovarian involvement) that is a complication most often caused by ascending STIs (chlamydia, gonorrhoea, Mycoplasma genitalium) or vaginal flora.
Frame PID as an ascending upper-genital-tract infection/complication of STIs and vaginal organisms, not an STI in its own right. This distinction matters for contact tracing and counselling.
✏️ The examination card omits an objective sepsis-screening framework despite emphasising 'septic features'.
Structure the general/vital-signs assessment around a recognised deterioration tool (e.g. Between the Flags / qSOFA: RR >=22, altered mentation, systolic BP <=100 mmHg) so 'severe PID' is quantified objectively, and state a temperature >38 degrees C plus tachycardia as escalation triggers.

⭐ Suggested Enhancements

⭐ State swab specifics when discharge is seen: high vaginal swab for candida/BV/trichomonas microscopy, and endocervical (or self-collected vaginal) NAAT for chlamydia/gonorrhoea/Mycoplasma genitalium - the card says 'I'd like to take a swab' but does not specify type.
⭐ Add Fitz-Hugh-Curtis syndrome (perihepatitis) as a right-upper-quadrant tenderness finding to actively check for in suspected PID.
⭐ Document consent, chaperone and offer of analgesia BEFORE the pelvic examination, and note the examination should be deferred/limited if the patient is haemodynamically unstable (resuscitate first).
⭐ Add an explicit note that a friable/irregular cervix or contact bleeding on speculum mandates urgent colposcopy/gynaecology referral (cervical cancer is the key not-to-be-missed differential emphasised for the cervix).
⭐ Add metadata (station_type physical_examination) and cross-link to the vaginal discharge diagnosis-management station and the prolapse PEFE station which share the PFAS pelvic-exam structure.
