Gestational Diabetes Mellitus (GDM) - History and Management

Source: Dr. Amir workshop — obstetrics / class 3 (Aug 2026 drop) · Specialty: obstetrics_gynaecology

Background Notes on Gestational Diabetes (GDM)

Definition

  • GDM = no pre-existing diabetes.
  • Detected on routine screening around 24–28 weeks, or earlier in some situations.

Postpartum & Next Pregnancy

  • At 6 weeks post-delivery → do diabetes testing again (not GDM screen).
  • Most GDM improves after delivery; some continue to have diabetes.
  • Next pregnancy → do first trimester diabetes screening.
  • OGTT needed 6–12 weeks postpartum + early OGTT in next pregnancy.

Management Overview

  • Start with non-pharmacological management (diet, physical activity).
  • Some mild cases controlled only with lifestyle.
  • Medications: insulin or metformin.
  • Emphasize: diet, physical activity, weight gain control, blood sugar monitoring.
  • All women with hyperglycemia in pregnancy should see a diabetic educator.

Metformin Notes

  • Some specialists recommend against metformin because it crosses placenta.
  • But RCTs show no complications; can be used with insulin.
  • Consider metformin when:
    • needle phobia
    • refuses insulin
    • poor adherence to insulin

Diagnostic Cut-offs – RANZCOG vs RACGP

  • RANZCOG: fasting >5.1
  • RACGP prefers >5.5
  • Memorise: FPS 5.5, 1 hr 10, 2 hr 8–8.5
  • But normal ranges usually given in exam stem.

Risk of Recurrent GDM

  • 40% recurrence risk.
  • Therefore: OGTT after delivery + early screening next pregnancy.

Reason OGTT Was Done Early in This Case

  • Because of previous GDM history.
  • Must be identified in history taking.
  • Routine antenatal schedule: OGTT is not done this early.

HISTORY TAKING STRUCTURE

1. Opening

  • Open-ended question.
  • “Has this been a planned pregnancy?”
  • Congratulate.

2. Five Ps

Period

  • LMP
  • Cycle regularity

Pregnancy symptoms

  • Tiredness
  • Nausea/vomiting (how much?)

Partner

  • Stable relationship?
  • Supportive?
  • Home situation

STIs

  • Routine ask

Pregnancies

  • “Is this your first pregnancy?”
  • If not → details of previous pregnancies.

3. Previous Pregnancy History (Key Focus of Case)

Ask about complications

  • High blood pressure?
  • High blood sugar?
  • Patient answers: Yes, gestational diabetes.

Important follow-up questions (as listed in lecture)

  1. Any history of high blood sugar before pregnancy?
    • To determine GDM vs pre-existing diabetes.
  2. What treatment did they give you?
    • Patient answer: controlled with lifestyle.
  3. Any problems with delivery?
    • Long delivery, big baby, obstructed delivery.
  4. Have you had follow-up blood sugar testing after delivery?
    • Should have been done at 6-week check.
    • Patient answer: No.

4. CST

  • “Are you up to date with your cervical screening?”

5. Additional Medical History

  • Any other medical conditions?
  • Any medications currently?

6. Diabetes Symptoms

  • “Are you passing more urine?”
  • “Any increase in thirst?”

7. Family History

  • Ask routine family history questions.

8. Lifestyle History

  • Diet
  • Exercise
  • “Are you having a healthy diet?”
  • “Are you doing regular exercise?”

DIAGNOSIS (EXPLAINING RESULTS)

  • “We did an oral glucose tolerance test and checked your blood sugar level three times.”
  • “Your fasting blood sugar level is mildly increased / slightly higher than normal.”
  • This means:
    → You have gestational diabetes again.
  • i.e., high blood sugar level in pregnancy.

MANAGEMENT

Multidisciplinary team

  • Refer to high-risk pregnancy clinic.
  • Involve:
    • Endocrinologist
    • Diabetic educator
    • Dietician

Lifestyle modifications

  • We will start with lifestyle modifications. Start immediately since FPS mildly elevated (5.6).
  • Healthy diet → dietician input.
  • Regular exercise (moderate intensity 30–40 min).
  • Continuous blood sugar monitoring at home:
    • at least 4 times a day.

Medication Discussions

  • If sugars not controlled: start medication.
  • Best medication = insulin.
  • Other option = metformin (if avoiding injections).

Routine antenatal counselling (brief mention only)

  • Ask about Down syndrome screening at 12 weeks.
  • Morphology scan at 20 weeks.
  • May need extra growth scans & heart scans depending on course.
  • Plan delivery if sugars high (risk of big baby).

Key Take-Home Points (as stated by lecturer)

  • Focus on:
    • previous complications
    • previous delivery details
    • previous treatment
    • whether she completed follow-up testing after pregnancy
  • At this point, unclear whether this is recurrent GDM or undiagnosed diabetes, but numbers not in diabetic range.

✏️ Corrections

✏️ The note states 'RACGP prefers fasting >5.5' and to 'memorise FPS 5.5, 1 hr 10, 2 hr 8-8.5', treating the fasting cut-off as 5.5 mmol/L. This is outdated for Australian practice.
Use the national ADIPS/RANZCOG 75 g OGTT diagnostic thresholds (any one abnormal value diagnoses GDM): fasting >=5.1 mmol/L, 1-hour >=10.0 mmol/L, 2-hour >=8.5 mmol/L. The FPG cut-off is 5.1, not 5.5.
✏️ Postpartum testing timing is inconsistent - the note says '6 weeks post-delivery' in one place and '6-12 weeks' in another.
Recommend a 75 g OGTT at 6-12 weeks postpartum (not the fasting/HbA1c-only 6-week check), then lifelong screening every 1-3 years given the elevated future type 2 diabetes risk. Early OGTT (first trimester) in a subsequent pregnancy is correct.

⭐ Suggested Enhancements

⭐ Add fetal/neonatal and maternal GDM risks explicitly to strengthen counselling: macrosomia, shoulder dystocia, neonatal hypoglycaemia, polyhydramnios, pre-eclampsia and increased caesarean rate - these justify the monitoring plan.
⭐ Quantify glucose self-monitoring targets (e.g. fasting <5.0-5.3 mmol/L, 1-hour post-prandial <7.4 mmol/L, 2-hour <6.7 mmol/L) rather than only 'monitor 4 times a day'.
⭐ Document the risk-factor basis for early screening (previous GDM, previous macrosomia, BMI >35, age >=40, PCOS, family history, high-risk ethnicity) and reference the KEMH/ADIPS risk-stratified screening pathway.
⭐ Use Australian spelling (anaemia, dietitian) and add RANZCOG/ADIPS GDM guideline and eTG references, plus postnatal contraception and breastfeeding advice, as metadata.
