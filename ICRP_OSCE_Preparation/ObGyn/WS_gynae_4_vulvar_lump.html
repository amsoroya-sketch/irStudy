Vulvar Lump (Bartholin Cyst/Abscess and Vulval Malignancy Differentials)

Source: Dr. Amir workshop — gynae / 3. Vulvar problems (Aug 2026 drop) · Specialty: obstetrics_gynaecology

VULVAR LUMP

INTRODUCTION

  • 30-year-old lady with vulva lump (“lump down below”) and discomfort.
  • Tasks:
    1. Take a history
    2. Explain diagnosis and differentials
    3. Explain management plan
  • Common exam issue: candidates focus only on Bartholin recall and fail because they don’t rule out wide differentials.

DIFFERENTIALS (GROUPED AS IN VIDEO)

1. Skin Conditions

  • Skin cancers (especially SCC: raised, ulcerated lesions)
  • Warts
  • Skin abscesses: folliculitis, boils

2. Bartholin Problems

  • Bartholin cyst
  • Bartholin abscess
  • Bartolinitis mentioned as related term

3. GI / Perianal Differential Group

  • Perianal abscess
  • Hernias (femoral, inguinal)

4. Prolapse

  • Pelvic organ prolapse may present as lump

5. Lymphadenopathy

  • Enlarged glands in groin

6. Other (Emphasis on Open-Minded Approach)

  • Avoid assuming Bartholin only
  • Must consider full list

HISTORY TAKING FRAMEWORK

1. Opening

  • Open-ended question: “How can I help you today?”
  • Reassure patient
  • Explore the lump

2. Site of Lump

  • “Is it close to your groin, upper part, or lower part of your vagina/vulva/private parts?”
  • Reminder: Bartholin glands at 4 o’clock and 7–8 o’clock (lower part)

3. Timing

  • “When did you first notice it?”

4. On/Off / Size Questioning

  • “Is the lump always present, or does the size change?”
  • “Is it increasing in size?”

5. Alleviating/Aggravating Factors

  • “Have you noticed anything make it better or worse?”
  • Specific prompts:
    • Position (standing/lying)
    • Valsalva (coughing, sneezing)

6. Impact on Life

  • “How is this affecting your life?”

RED FLAG QUESTIONS (CANCER)

  • Loss of weight
  • Loss of appetite (optional)
  • “Any lumps or bumps or enlarged glands in the groin?”
  • Tiredness

DERMATOLOGY / ABSCESS QUESTIONS

  • Do u have Pain in private parts? Quality (burning/dull), radiation, intensity
  • Redness or rashes
  • Ulcers or abnormal skin lesions
  • Fever and chills

GI DIFFERENTIAL QUESTIONS

  • Any Pain on passing bowel motions / opening bowels
  • Have u noticed Changes in bowel habits (form, frequency, pain)

PROLAPSE ASSESSMENT

1. Obstetric History

  • Have u had any Pregnancies?
  • If yes:
    • Delivery route (vaginal or not)
    • Complications: prolonged labour, instrumental delivery, big babies, cuts

2. Chronic Increased Intra-Abdominal Pressure

  • Chronic cough
  • Chronic constipation

WARTS(caused by hpv) / STI HISTORY

  • Sexually active?
  • Stable relationship?
  • Multiple partners?
  • Condom use? Safe sex?
  • Previous STI diagnoses?
  • Dyspareunia questions can be asked here or in 5Ps

FIVE-P’S SECTION (BRIEF GYN SCREEN)

Period

  • LMP
  • Problems with the period?

Partner

  • Dyspareunia (pain on sex)
  • Post-coital bleeding

Pregnancy

  • Already covered earlier

Protection

  • Contraception use?

Pap Smear

  • Up-to-date cervical screening?

Finish with SADMA / PMH / FH

CASE EXAMPLES FROM LECTURE

CASE 1 — CLASSIC BARTHOLIN ABSCESS

History Features

  • Fever
  • Extreme pain (unable to sit)
  • Swollen, red area
  • Lower part of vagina

Diagnosis

  • Top differential: Bartholin abscess
  • Could also be a skin abscess (similar)

Explanation to Patient

  • There are Two glands on the lower part of vaginal wall called Bartholin glands
  • Normally provide lubrication
  • If the Outlet is obstructed it cause a cyst formation and it can become Infected and filled with pus which we call abscess

Management (Bullet Steps from Lecture)

  1. I will need u to Refer to ED
  2. They will perform a procedure called Incision and drainage. Small cut and drain pus (essential step)
  3. Antibiotics
  4. Small blood tests (ensure no sepsis)
  5. Further options:
    • As part of treatment they can put catheter inside the gland called Word catheter which is small tube inserted to drain pus/fluid
    • As 2nd procedure called Marsupialization: cut made, edges stitched to the side to keep the outlet open

PHOTO CASE REMINDER

  • Bartholin abscess expected on lower half of vagina
  • Must combine history + location + photo
  • Do not diagnose from photo alone

MARCH 2024 RECALL — IRREGULAR LESION CASE

History

  • Lump getting worse
  • No pain
  • Brownish discharge / spotting

Photo Description (as taught)

  • Irregular
  • Ulcerated
  • Raised edges
  • Fungating whitish discharge

Diagnosis

  • Top differential: Skin cancer (SCC)
  • “Ugly, irregular, ulcerating” lesion → rule out cancer even without systemic features

CASE — BARTHOLIN CYST

  • Lump for two months
  • No pain
  • No fever
  • Photo resembles abscess but without inflammatory features

Diagnosis

  • Bartholin cyst

Explanation

  • Two normal glands
  • Outlet obstruction → cyst filled with secretion
  • Not infected; not painful

FINAL TEACHING POINTS

  • Main reason for failing: not ruling out differentials
  • Problem is not the cyst/abscess distinction — it is forgetting red flags and alternative diagnoses
  • Skin colour considerations: redness may be harder to see
  • Hernias can mimic appearance when no inflammation is present

✏️ Corrections

✏️ Bartholin abscess management lists 'Antibiotics' as a routine step alongside incision and drainage.
For an uncomplicated Bartholin abscess, drainage is the definitive treatment; antibiotics are NOT routinely required. Reserve antibiotics (guided by swab, covering possible gonorrhoea/chlamydia and MRSA where relevant) for surrounding cellulitis, systemic sepsis, pregnancy, immunocompromise, recurrence, or age >40. Simple incision and drainage alone has a high recurrence rate, so Word catheter insertion or marsupialisation is preferred over simple I&D.
✏️ The 'MARCH 2024 RECALL' irregular ulcerated fungating lesion is labelled only as 'Skin cancer (SCC)' with photo-based reasoning.
Any suspicious, irregular, ulcerated or non-healing vulval lesion (especially with bloody/brown discharge) requires urgent gynae-oncology referral and diagnostic (incisional/punch) biopsy for definitive histology - vulval SCC, VIN, melanoma and Paget disease cannot be reliably distinguished clinically. Do not diagnose or exclude malignancy on appearance alone.

⭐ Suggested Enhancements

⭐ Add HPV context to the warts/SCC differential: recommend Gardasil 9 vaccination (National Immunisation Program), and note vulval intraepithelial neoplasia (VIN) as a premalignant precursor requiring biopsy.
⭐ Broaden the anterior/groin lump differentials to include cyst of the canal of Nuck, lipoma, inguinal/femoral hernia and inguinal lymphadenopathy (infective vs malignant), reinforcing the case's 'don't anchor on Bartholin' teaching point.
⭐ Add red-flag safety-net for a Bartholin abscess progressing to systemic sepsis (fever, rigors, spreading cellulitis) requiring ED review and IV antibiotics; and note recurrent Bartholin abscess in a woman >40 warrants biopsy to exclude carcinoma of the gland.
⭐ Add examination/investigation detail: describe the lesion (site by clock position, size, tenderness, fluctuance, discharge), take a lesion swab if discharging, and arrange biopsy for any solid/fixed/ulcerated lesion; include analgesia (paracetamol +/- NSAID) counselling.
