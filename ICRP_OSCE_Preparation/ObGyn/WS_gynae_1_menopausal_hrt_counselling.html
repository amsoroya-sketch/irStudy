Menopausal Hormone Therapy (MHT/HRT) Counselling

Source: Dr. Amir workshop — gynae / 4. Counselling cases (Aug 2026 drop) · Specialty: obstetrics_gynaecology

HRT COUNSELLING

Q. A 48-year-old lady who has presented to your GP and wants to discuss

HRT for menopause.

- Take history from the patient and counsel the patient about HRT.

Symptoms of menopause

- Vasomotor symptoms such as hot flushes, night sweats

- Urogenital symptoms such as vaginal dryness, painful sex, urine frequency,

dysuria, noturia, urinary incontinence

- Sleep disturbance

- Low libido

- Psychological symptoms (memory and concentration problems, anxiety,

depression)

Central body fat accumulation

- Irregular vaginal bleeding

- Mastalgia

Menopause

- Usually clinical diagnosis.

- Based on menstrual bleeding pattern, presence of menopausal symptoms

and age.

- Assessment of hormone conc is not routinely done (FSH – high around 25

unit per litre and low estradiol < 100 pmol)

- Take 2 FSH and estradiol measurements 6 weeks apart of menopause is not

clear.

Menopausal hormonal therapy

- Treat symptoms of menopause

- Treat or prevent osteoporosis (not used primarily for this purpose)

- Prevent cardiovascular disease in individuals with premature ovarian

failure or early menopause.

Non-hormonal option for menopause

- SSRI, SNRI, tibolone, gabapentin, others

- Relaxation, mindfulness, CBT for mood problems

Hormonal therapy

- Estrogen only (only in women who do not have a uterus)

- Combined (estrogen + progesterone)

Cyclical combined – 28 days of estrogen and 10-14 days of progesterone

in perimenopausal women that means had their LMP less than 1 year ago. Can

get withdrawal bleeding after 14 days of progesterone but it will get lighter with

time. (estrogen pills + progesterone pill/Mirena or estrogen patch + progesterone

pill/Mirena)

Continuous combined – estrogen and progesterone every day in

postmenopausal women that means had their LMP more than 1 year ago. Avoid

a monthly withdrawal bleed.

CI of HRT

- Age 60 years or older

- Previous VTE

- Previous TIA, stroke, AMI

- Uncontrolled hypertension

- Estrogen-dependent cancer (breast cancer, endometrial cancer)

- Undiagnosed vaginal bleeding

High risk of breast cancer (PMH, BRCA positive)

- Significant liver disease

- Porphyria or SLE

CI specific to oral estrogen

- Risk factors for VTE (obesity, smoking, thrombophilia)

- Risk factors for CVS disease

- Elevated triglycerides

- Liver disease or gallbladder disease

(if any of these CI is present, transdermal estrogen is recommended)

Benefits

- Reduce risks of fractures, DM, colorectal cancer, vasomotor symptoms.

Disadvantages

- Increase the risk of invasive breast cancer, stroke, VTE, gallbladder

disease, dementia, coronary heart disease, urinary incontinence.

Review

- 6-8 weeks after starting MHT, then again at 6 months, and continue to

review every 6-12 months.

- For persistent vasomotor symptoms increase estrogen dose, review

progesterone dose to ensure adequate endometrial protection.

- Prompt investigation of vaginal bleeding is only needed if the bleeding is

heavy, irregular, post-coital, or occurs in individuals at increased risk of

endometrial cancer.

Side effects:

Breast tenderness, nausea, progesterone intolerance, bloating

Stopping HRT

- No specific recommended duration of HRT.

Contraception during HRT

- Condoms, Mirena, copper IUD, POPs, implant up to age of 55 if you

have menopausal symptoms, will add HRT.

- COCs or depot if < 50 years

- Contraception is required for 12 months after the final menstrual period if

the FMP occurs after the age of 50 and if FMP occurs before the age of 50, 24

months of contraception is required.

Perimenopause is the 4–8-year transitional stage of increasingly irregular

menstrual cycles leading up to final menstrual period.

What if a contraception user has perimenopausal symptoms – irregular bleeding?

- Use low dose combined pills

If only atrophic vaginitis is present, just go for topical estrogen cream

Positive findings

- has irregular periods, hot flushes, no CI, wants to go on HRT

Counselling

- In women > 45 years of age, the hormone starts decreasing in your body

and we call this menopause.

- Menopausal symptoms

When you start getting close to menopause, you might have irregular

period bleeding, hot flushes, sleep problems, mood problems and dryness in

vagina.

- CI

You do not have CI of HRT such as breast cancer, liver disease, VTE

- As you have symptoms, I will give you HRT.

- Benefits of HRT (same as above)

- Risk of HRT (same as above)

Minor Breast tenderness, nausea, progesterone intolerance, bloating

Major VTE, heart attack, breast cancer, dementia

HRT

We can do hormonal cyclical combined treatment which means we will

give you 28 days of estrogen and 14 days of progesterone. We can use estrogen

in pill and patch form. You may get withdrawal bleeding every month and it will

get lighter with time.

- Review in 6-8 weeks to check the symptoms.

- Contraception

HRT is not contraception, and you still can fall pregnant.

You can use condoms or copper IUD or we can give you COCPs up to 50

years of age.

- Other non-hormonal therapy instead of HRT

SSRI, SNRI, CBT, relaxation techniques, herbal medicine

Q. A 52-year-old lady for HRT. Take history, PEFE, counsel the patient.

Positive findings

- Friends recommended, only has vaginal dryness, no hot flushes, LMP 18

months ago

2 options

- Topical estrogen cream (less side effects)

- HRT (higher risks)

- (no need to take contraception)

Another case

- Previous history of breast cancer, fully treated, no issue on regular f-up,

treated 10 years ago.

Answer

- We have alternative methods such as medications such as SSRI, CBT,

relaxation and herbal medicine.

- No to HRT

Another case

Q. 47-year-old lady, currently on COCs. Want to take HRT

Positive findings

- No menopausal symptoms, has been on COCPS for 5 years, friend told me

about HRT

Answer

- As you have no symptoms, no indication for HRT.

- I would like you to continue COCPs up to 50 years and come back and I

will review you.

- Other options POP pills, Mirena or Implanon, barriers – condoms. If you

develop menopausal symptoms, will start HRT.

Q. A 57-year-old lady for a repeat HRT. She is on HRT for the last 6 years

and was started due to hot flushes.

Answer

- There is no specific duration for HRT.

- Want you to understand 2 important points.

- Consider the risks of HRT such as increased risk of clotting, CVD and

breast cancer

- Another thing is MP symptoms usually settle after a few years from your

FMP.

- We can trial stopping and if symptoms return, we can restart HRT or choose

non-HRT treatment.

Another case

Q. A 47-year-old lady. She was taking COCPs since she was 30 years but stopped

5 weeks ago after hearing about the harmful side effects of the pills. She had a

breakthrough bleed after she stopped her COCPs 5 weeks ago. FSH normal, LH,

TSH – normal, BHCG – negative. Explain results, history, PEFE – normal,

counsel.

Investigation explanation

- Not pregnant, LH and TSH – normal

- We have also done hormonal test for you and that FSH is also normal,

which means you are still not in menopause.

Positive findings

No menopausal symptoms, no CIs.

Answer

- There is no indication for HRT as you have no symptoms.

- “I am worried about the SE of COCps” HRT has the same hormone and

same SE profile. The only difference is that it usually contains lower doses.

- Options

I will give you reading materials about COCPs which can be continued up

to 50 years of age.

Or you can stay off COCPs and monitor for menopausal symptoms and if

it occurs, come back to me and we can further discuss about it and give you HRTs.

Q. A 47-year-old lady with hot flushes and irregular periods for HRT, FSH

and LH normal, CST normal.

- Explain results, order necessary investigations and tell her if she can start

HRT.

DDx of hot flushes and irregular periods

- Hyperthyroidism TSH

- Pheochromocytoma urine and plasma metanephrine

- Cancer such as leukaemia FBE and blood film

- Prolactinoma prolactin level

- Carcinoid syndrome CXR. Looking for bowel cancer

Answer

- As your hormonal level is normal, you are still not in menopause, no

indication for HRT.

- We need to find out the possible causes by further investigations as

mentioned above.

🔬 Expert Review & Enhancements

Reviewed by: clinical-documentation-expert (Australian clinical supervisor, O&G / RACGP standards) · fact-checked against the irStudy medical textbook RAG index.

✏️ Corrections

✏️ The note lists 'Age 60 years or older' as an absolute contraindication to HRT.
Age is not an absolute contraindication. Per current Australian guidance (Jean Hailes / RACGP / AMS), MHT is most favourable when started before age 60 or within 10 years of the final menstrual period ('window of opportunity'). Starting after this window increases cardiovascular/VTE risk and shifts the risk-benefit balance, but is not absolutely prohibited if symptoms are severe and risks are individually assessed. Frame it as a caution requiring specialist input, not an absolute contraindication.
✏️ Spelling uses US forms 'estrogen' and 'estradiol' throughout, and 'FSH high around 25 unit per litre and low estradiol < 100 pmol' omits the unit denominator.
Use Australian spelling: oestrogen, oestradiol. Oestradiol should be expressed as pmol/L (e.g. oestradiol < 100 pmol/L). FSH for menopause is typically > 25-30 IU/L; note hormone testing is unreliable in perimenopause and in women on hormonal contraception, so diagnosis remains clinical.
✏️ The differential for 'hot flushes and irregular periods' states 'Carcinoid syndrome - CXR. Looking for bowel cancer.'
Carcinoid syndrome is investigated with 24-hour urinary 5-HIAA and chromogranin A, not CXR, and it is a neuroendocrine tumour, not bowel cancer. Recommend correcting this DDx workup; phaeochromocytoma (plasma/urine metanephrines) and thyrotoxicosis (TSH) are the more relevant secondary causes of flushing to exclude.

⭐ Suggested Enhancements

⭐ Add explicit VTE-risk stratification guidance: transdermal oestradiol (patch/gel) does not carry the increased VTE risk seen with oral oestrogen and is the preferred route for women with obesity, migraine, smoking, or raised VTE risk - the note mentions this but should state transdermal is first-line in these groups.
⭐ Add the PBS/prescribing detail: micronised progesterone (Prometrium) and oestradiol patches/gels are available in Australia; specify that a progestogen (oral micronised progesterone, or a 52 mg levonorgestrel IUD/Mirena) is mandatory for endometrial protection in any woman with a uterus.
⭐ Add a red-flag / safety-netting line: any postmenopausal bleeding, or new persistent/irregular bleeding on continuous combined MHT after the first 3-6 months, warrants transvaginal ultrasound +/- endometrial biopsy to exclude endometrial cancer before attributing to MHT.
⭐ Add metadata linking to related irStudy topics (OCP counselling, Mirena counselling, abnormal uterine bleeding) and cite the AMS/Jean Hailes MHT position statements as the current Australian evidence source.
