Chest Discomfort in Late Pregnancy (Gastro-oesophageal Reflux)

Source: Dr. Amir workshop — obstetrics / class 4 (Aug 2026 drop) · Specialty: obstetrics_gynaecology

PREGNANCY CHEST DISCOMFORT

Your next patient is a 30 year old lady who is 36 weeks pregnancy and is here to see you for her routine pregnancy visit. She is complaining of chest pain.

Task：

• Take History for 6 minutes

• Physical Examination will be given to you on a card

• Explain your diagnosis and differentials to the patient

Purpose of the Case

  • Differentials.
  • Pregnant lady + chest pain → need full differential diagnosis.
  • Still a chest pain case → all adult non-pregnancy differentials apply.
  • PLUS pregnancy-specific differential: preeclampsia.

Differential Groups Required

  1. Cardiovascular
  2. Respiratory
  3. GI
  4. Musculoskeletal
  5. Psychogenic
  6. Pregnancy-related / Obstetric (OB)
    • Preeclampsia as extra differential.

“Whatever third trimester case you have, I want to hear preeclampsia questions.”

HISTORY TAKING STRUCTURE (6 minutes)

Step 1 – Entry + Vitals

  • Enter, ID check, sanitize hands.
  • Ask examiner for:
    • Blood pressure
    • Pulse rate
    • Respiratory rate
    • Temperature
    • (O₂ saturation optional)
  • If abnormal → say i want to shift to treatment room/resuscitation room and move on with history taking
  • If stable → continue.

Step 2 – Open-Ended Question + Empathy Statement

  • “How can I help you today?”
  • Patient: expresses distress (“I want the pregnancy to end…”)
  • Required empathic line:
    • “I understand how stressful, overwhelming and concerning this can be, but you are doing a great job as a mother.”
  • Continue reassurance:
    • “Let me ask a few questions… we’ll find the cause… best management plan.”

**Step 3 – Explore the Complaint

(SIQORAA for chest pain/discomfort)**
Purpose: differentiate typical vs atypical chest pain.

SIQORAA

  1. Site
    • Is it a point or an area?
    • Point tenderness → atypical.
  2. Intensity (1–10 scale)
  3. Quality
    • Burning / dull ache / pressure / constricting.
  4. Onset
    • Duration, constant/on-off, worsening?
  5. Radiation
    • Left jaw/left shoulder/left arm.
  6. Alleviating/Aggravating Factors
    • General: “Anything makes it better or worse?”
    • Then specific questions:
      • Worse on activity, better with rest? (typical cardiac)
      • Better leaning forward? (pericarditis)
      • Related to breathing/eating? (pleuritic or GI)

Note: Shortness of breath asked later due to pregnancy framework.

Step 4 – OB History (MUST NOT FORGET)

“It’s a little bit not nice if you forget OB history.”

  • “How’s the pregnancy going so far?”
  • Routine blood tests (early pregnancy) → normal?
  • Dating scan?
  • Down syndrome screening?
  • 18–20 week ultrasound?
  • Sweet drink test?
  • Baby kicking normally?

Preeclampsia Questions (critical)

  • Headache?
  • Blurring of vision?
  • Dizziness?
  • Decrease in urine output?
  • Swelling in legs?
  • Previous pregnancies?
    → Any complications during pregnancy/delivery?

Step 5 – Differential-Directed Questions

1. Cardiovascular

  • Ischemic heart disease
    • SOB on exercise?
    • Sweating, nausea, vomiting?
  • Valvular/Arrhythmias
    • Racing heart?
    • Previous heart problems?
  • Heart failure
    • Swelling legs (already asked)
    • SOB, orthopnea, number of pillows used.

2. Respiratory

  • Pulmonary embolism (top respiratory differential)
    • Symptoms of DVT: leg swelling or pain?
    • Risk factors: recent long travel?
    • Family history or past history of clots?
  • Pneumonia
    • Fever or chills?
    • Cough?
    • Contact with anyone sick?

3. Gastrointestinal

  • Gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD)
    • Any bitter acidic taste in mouth?
    • Any sudden increase of saliva (“water brush”)?
  • Peptic ulcer disease
    • Worse after eating?
  • Cholecystitis / Biliary colic
    • Nausea, vomiting?
    • Worse after large fatty meals?

Note: progesterone relaxes everything thats why pregnancy have constipation, cholecystitis, bile stones etc.

4. Musculoskeletal

  • Any rashes/redness on chest?
  • Any recent chest injury?
  • Pain worse on touching or moving?

5. Psychogenic

  • Mood lately?
  • Any stresses at home?
  • Coping with pregnancy?

Step 6 – PMHx / FHx (if time)

  • “If you have time, do it; if not, totally fine.”

Key Points for History

  • Purpose:
    1. Rule out differentials.
    2. Characterize discomfort.
  • “Please do not forget OB history.”
  • “Please don’t forget preeclampsia.”
  • “Don’t show me you know the diagnosis.”

HISTORY FINDING

  • Feels chest discomfort, not chest pain
  • Burning pain
  • Also has shortness of breathe
  • Discomfort worse after meals
  • 3rd pregnancy
  • Antenatal screening normal

PHYSICAL EXAMINATION CARD FINDINGS

  • BP 120/80
  • No rash on chest
  • Respiratory examination normal
  • On CVS examination: Mid systolic murmur at left sternal edge
  • BMI 32
  • Urine dipstick normal

Important Teaching Point

  • Soft systolic murmurs are physiologic in pregnancy.
  • Mitral stenosis is rumbeling mid diastolic murmur nit systolic

DIAGNOSIS

  • Gastroesophageal Reflux Disease (GERD)
     Despite systolic murmur and SOB.
    Murmur is physiologic.

PATIENT EXPLANATION

  • “Most likely you have a condition called gastroesophageal reflux disease.”
  • “This means the acid of your stomach is irritating the food pipe.”
  • “This is common in pregnancy because the gateway at the end of the food pipe is looser due to pregnancy hormone.”

Regarding the Murmur

  • “I heard an extra sound in your heart examination.”
  • “These systolic murmurs are usually normal during pregnancy.”

DIFFERENTIAL DIAGNOSIS LIST

Top differential:

  • Preeclampsia (ruled out based on history/exam)

Cardiovascular:

  • Valvular problems (e.g., mitral stenosis)
  • Ischemic heart disease
  • Heart failure
  • Arrhythmias

Respiratory:

  • Pulmonary embolism
  • Pneumonia

GI:

  • Peptic ulcer disease
  • Cholecystitis / Biliary colic

MSK:

  • Costochondritis

Psychogenic:

  • Diagnosis of exclusion

EXAM CALLBACK VERSION (April) – Variation

  • Stem does not mention chest discomfort initially.
  • Patient begins with dramatic statement:
    • “I just want the pregnancy to end.”
  • Candidate must be dramatic and empathetic until patient reveals chest discomfort on the 4th open question if needed.
  • Everything else same: reflux with water brush, acidic taste, worse on eating.

🔬 Expert Review & Enhancements

Reviewed by: clinical-documentation-expert (Australian teaching hospital clinical supervisor) · fact-checked against the irStudy medical textbook RAG index.

✏️ Corrections

✏️ The patient reports shortness of breath alongside burning chest discomfort, and the case settles confidently on GERD, effectively attributing the dyspnoea to reflux ('GERD despite SOB').
Dyspnoea in a 36-week pregnant woman must not be dismissed. Pulmonary embolism/VTE is the leading direct cause of maternal death in Australia and must be actively excluded before reassurance. Also list physiological dyspnoea of pregnancy (very common, diaphragmatic splinting + progesterone-driven hyperventilation) as a benign differential. Document that PE, pre-eclampsia and cardiac causes were considered and excluded, rather than presenting GERD as certain.
✏️ American spellings and terms used throughout ('Ischemic heart disease', 'Gastroesophageal', 'esophagus/food pipe', 'edema').
Australian clinical documentation requires British/Australian spelling: ischaemic heart disease, gastro-oesophageal reflux disease (GORD), oesophagus, oedema. Standardise terminology for AHPRA-compliant notes.
✏️ No management is offered for the confirmed reflux, and legacy teaching may suggest ranitidine.
Add a management plan and note that ranitidine was withdrawn in Australia in 2020 (NDMA contamination) and should not be recommended. First-line is lifestyle advice plus antacids (calcium/magnesium-based, e.g. Gaviscon/Mylanta); if inadequate, a proton pump inhibitor such as omeprazole is considered acceptable in pregnancy (eTG).

⭐ Suggested Enhancements

⭐ Explicitly add physiological dyspnoea of pregnancy and peripartum cardiomyopathy to the differential list - a late-pregnancy woman with SOB and a murmur warrants consideration of new cardiac disease, not automatic 'physiological murmur'.
⭐ Add a structured reflux management ladder: small frequent meals, avoid eating within 3 hours of lying down, elevate head of bed, avoid triggers; antacids first-line; PPI if refractory (eTG reference).
⭐ Strengthen safety-netting: advise immediate presentation / 000 for pleuritic pain, acute breathlessness, calf swelling, haemoptysis, syncope, or pre-eclampsia features (severe headache, visual disturbance, epigastric pain) - the leading obstetric emergencies behind this presentation.
⭐ Add aortic dissection as a rare but life-threatening chest-pain differential in pregnancy (increased risk in connective-tissue disorders), and mention it in the 'not to be missed' group.
⭐ Add metadata linking to related irStudy chest-pain and VTE-in-pregnancy topics, and note the AMC critical-error emphasis on eliciting pre-eclampsia symptoms in any third-trimester station.
