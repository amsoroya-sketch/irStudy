Secondary Amenorrhoea (Functional Hypothalamic Amenorrhoea)

Source: Dr. Amir workshop — gynae / 1. period problems (Aug 2026 drop) · Specialty: obstetrics_gynaecology

SECONDARY AMENORRHEA

Your next patient is a 24 year old lady who has presented to your general practice complaining of Amenorrhea for 18 months. Investigations have been done and show:

FSH low, LH low, Estrogen Low

TSH normal, Prolactin normal

Task:

  • Take history from the patient for 5 minutes
  • Explain your diagnosis and differentials to the patient
  • Explain the implications (Risks) to the patient

STRUCTURE FOR SECONDARY AMENORRHEA HISTORY

1. Opening

  • Open-ended question: “How can I help you today?”
  • Patient response: No periods for 18 months.
  • Reassure: “I understand your concern. I’ll ask a few questions to find out what’s happening.”

2. Explore the Complaint – PERIOD HISTORY (Five Ps)

P1: Period

  • When was your last period? → 18 months ago.
  • Before stopping:
    • Were periods regular or irregular?
    • Any heavy bleeds?
    • Cycle length?
    • Bleeding duration?
    • Excessive pain?
    • Irregular bleeding between periods?

P2: Partner History

  • Are you sexually active?
  • Any chance you might be pregnant?
  • Ask for pregnancy symptoms:
    • Tiredness.
    • Morning sickness.
    • Any Recent breast pain.

P3: Pills History

  • Have you ever been on contraception?
  • What type?
  • Especially consider injectables (Depo).
  • Ask about contraception now or before.

P4: Pregnancy History

  • Have you ever had any pregnancies?
    • Explore outcome.
    • Ask about complications:
      • Cesarean section.
      • Pregnancy or delivery complications.
  • Any surgeries down below?
    • Any Hx of Surgical terminations of pregnancy?.
    • Regular D&C.
    • Looking for Asherman syndrome/adhesions.
  • Are you currently breastfeeding?

P5: Pap Smear

  • Have you started cervical screening?
  • Are you up to date?
  • If <25: Ask about HPV vaccination.
  • No relevance to diagnosis, but still ask as part of structure.

    • Home.

  • Exercise:

  • Diet:
    • Describe your diet.

      • Do u use Laxatives.

      • Crash dieting.

Pituitary – Prolactinoma

  • Headache.
  • Vision problems.
  • Milky breast/nipple discharge.

Thyroid

  • Weather preference.
  • Heat/cold intolerance.
  • Constipation/diarrhea (optional for completeness).

Ovarian / Lower Causes

  • PCOS:
    • Any Acne.
    • Excessive hair growth on body.
    • Recent weight gain.
  • Premature ovarian failure / Perimenopause:
    • Have u started getting Hot flushes.

Medications and Medical History

  • Any medications currently?
  • Any chronic illnesses that can cause secondary amenorrhea?

Family History

  • Optional:
    • PCOS.
    • Thyroid disease.

Wedding coming up in a few months,, exercising 4 hours every day

Had one curettage bcz of miscarriage 6 years ago

New version:

Athlete, training for the Olympics, exercising few hours every day, has a professional dietician

MOST LIKELY DIAGNOSIS

  • Suppressed hypothalamic function causing secondary amenorrhea.
  • Due to:
    • Excessive exercise.
    • Plus/minus strict dieting.
  • Explanation:
    • Exercise and dieting suppress normal hormone production of an area in the brain called hypothalamus.
  • Reasoning with investigations:
    • I can see your blood tests that u have Low levels of hypothalamus hormones like LH and FSH confirm this.

RISKS / IMPLICATIONS OF SECONDARY AMENORRHEA

  1. Infertility
    • If it continues, pregnancy is not possible.
  2. Osteoporosis
    • Weak/fragile bones due to abnormal hormone levels.
  3. Cardiovascular disease
    • Increased risk of heart disease (heart attack, stroke) due to loss of protective female hormones.

KEY EXAM REMINDER

  • Don’t jump straight to exercise.
  • You must rule out differentials to pass.

✏️ Corrections

✏️ Under risks the note states 'If it continues, pregnancy is not possible.'
This is too absolute. Functional hypothalamic amenorrhoea causes reversible anovulatory infertility; fertility usually returns once energy availability is restored (reduced exercise load, increased caloric intake, weight gain). Ovulation can also be induced (e.g. pulsatile GnRH or gonadotrophins) if spontaneous recovery does not occur. Document it as reversible subfertility, not permanent infertility.
✏️ American spelling ('estrogen', 'Cesarean') and informal contractions ('u', 'bcz') in a clinical teaching note.
Use Australian spelling and formal terminology in documentation: oestrogen, oestradiol, caesarean section, dilatation and curettage (D&C). Avoid text-speak.

⭐ Suggested Enhancements

⭐ Add explicit management guidance per eTG/RANZCOG for functional hypothalamic amenorrhoea: multidisciplinary approach (dietitian +/- psychologist), reduce exercise and restore energy balance as first-line, and consider transdermal oestradiol with cyclical progestogen (preferred over the COCP) for bone protection if amenorrhoea is prolonged.
⭐ Add a bone-health investigation: DEXA (bone mineral density) scan is indicated given >6 months of hypo-oestrogenaemia and osteoporosis risk - currently the note lists osteoporosis as a risk but orders no bone assessment.
⭐ Name the working diagnosis precisely as 'functional hypothalamic amenorrhoea' (the low FSH/low LH/low oestrogen pattern with normal TSH/prolactin fits this) and add screening for the female athlete triad / relative energy deficiency in sport (RED-S), including a validated eating-disorder screen (e.g. SCOFF).
⭐ Add red-flag safety net: if headache, visual field defect, or galactorrhoea are present, image the pituitary (MRI) to exclude a prolactinoma or other sellar lesion despite the currently normal prolactin.
