Physical Examination for Dysmenorrhoea / Endometriosis (PEFE)

Source: Dr. Amir workshop — gynae / 1. period problems (Aug 2026 drop) · Specialty: obstetrics_gynaecology

Physical Examination From Examiner – Dysmenorrhea (Endometriosis Case)

1. General Appearance

  • Ask examiner if the lady has cachexia (for cancers).
  • Ask if the patient is in pain.
  • Nothing else relevant here.

2. Vital Signs

  • As a habit, do all four: blood pressure, pulse rate, temperature, respiratory rate.
  • Temperature is important because PID is on the list.

3. Abdominal Examination

  • Reason: thinking about endometriosis → deposits may be outside the uterus (tubes, ovaries, anywhere in the abdomen).
  • Look for distension.
  • Ask examiner: “I’d like to feel for any masses and tenderness.”

4. Pelvic Examination

  • “Examiner, I’d like to do a pelvic exam with the consent of my patient and the presence of a chaperone.”

A. Inspection

  • Ask examiner:
    • Any bleeding or discharge?
    • Any rashes or redness?
    • If you want, ask for swelling or lumps.

B. Speculum Examination

  • Look at the cervix. Ask examiner:
    • Any abnormal growths or masses?
    • Any ectropion? (AMC loves ectropion)
    • Any cervical stenosis?
  • Ask also about the vaginal wall.

C. Bimanual Examination

  • Start with cervical motion tenderness (PID on list).
  • Uterus:
    • Ask examiner for size (e.g., “10 weeks”).
    • Ask for border (regular/irregular).
    • Ask for tenderness (PID can give tender uterus).
  • Adnexa:
    • Ask for adnexal masses.
    • Ask for adnexal tenderness.

5. Rectal Examination (Key Point)

  • Important because AMC endometriosis cases commonly have normal abdominal and bimanual exams.
  • This case requires a rectal examination.
  • Ask examiner:
    • Tenderness and nodularity in the pouch of Douglas.
  • This finding helps confirm endometriosis.

6. Additional Notes

  • In past AMC endometriosis cases (2018, 2019):
    • Abdominal exam: normal.
    • Bimanual exam: normal.
    • DRE is the key positive finding.
  • If someone wants to add a urine pregnancy test, acceptable but not necessary.

✏️ Corrections

✏️ Consent/chaperone documentation is a single spoken line.
Per AHPRA/RANZCOG standards for intimate examination, document explicit informed consent, a recorded offer of a chaperone (accepted/declined) with the chaperone's name/role, an explanation of what will be done, and that the patient may stop at any time.
✏️ American spelling 'diarrhea'-style usage and 'gynecology' in accompanying material.
Use Australian spelling: gynaecology, tumour, oedema.

⭐ Suggested Enhancements

⭐ Add that the digital rectal examination for pouch-of-Douglas nodularity/tenderness must itself be preceded by explanation and consent, and document that a normal pelvic/bimanual examination does NOT exclude endometriosis (a key AMC teaching point already implied but worth stating explicitly).
⭐ Add that transvaginal ultrasound is the first-line imaging investigation to correlate examination findings (endometrioma, deep infiltrating nodules) and that laparoscopy is the definitive but no-longer-mandatory diagnostic step - links the examination to the modern RANZCOG diagnostic pathway.
⭐ Add sign-to-diagnosis mapping for the bimanual findings the note lists (cervical motion tenderness -> PID; fixed retroverted tender uterus with nodularity -> endometriosis; bulky uterus -> fibroids/adenomyosis; adnexal mass -> endometrioma/ovarian pathology/ectopic).
⭐ Add that temperature and inflammatory markers help separate PID from endometriosis, and that a beta-hCG should be documented as considered to exclude ectopic pregnancy in any woman of reproductive age with pelvic pain.
