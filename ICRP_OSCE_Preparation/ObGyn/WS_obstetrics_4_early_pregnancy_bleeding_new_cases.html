Early Pregnancy Bleeding - New Cases (Pregnancy of Unknown Location, Threatened Miscarriage)

Source: Dr. Amir workshop — obstetrics / class 3 (Aug 2026 drop) · Specialty: obstetrics_gynaecology

EARLY PREGNANCY BLEEDING NEW CASES

CASE 1

Your next patient is a 25 year old lady who has presented to you complaining of vaginal bleeding. She has missed her periods and her LMP is 8 weeks ago. Home pregnancy tests hase been done and is positive.

Task:

• Take history for 5 minutes

• Investigation will appear on the screen

• Explain your diagnosis

History Taking

  • Use your usual antenatal/adapted structure (not detailed here in transcript)
  • Key findings mentioned:
    • LMP = eight weeks ago
    • Some candidates mention she smokes weed
      • Speaker notes: “I'm not really sure where the smoke weeds comes up because in my structure, I don't ask about drugs.”
      • May be volunteered by the patient
    • Bleeding started today
    • She hasn’t passed any tissue

Investigations (given on a card)

  • Ultrasound performed
  • Gestational sac seen and size correlates to eight weeks
  • Fetal cardiac activity and FHR is normal

How to Explain Diagnosis to the Patient (exact explanation from transcript)

  • Start with your concern:
    • “I was concerned about miscarriage. That’s why I did the investigation for you, not an examination today.”
  • Provide reassurance based on results:
    • “Based on the investigation finding, your pregnancy is growing normally and I don’t have any concerns for your pregnancy.”
    • “That’s a really good sign.”
  • Define the condition:
    • “Whenever we have bleeding in the first few weeks of pregnancy, but the pregnancy continues and is growing normally, we call that a threatened miscarriage.”
  • Important emphasis:
    • “As much as I call it a miscarriage, it’s not really a miscarriage.”
    • “It’s just a threatened condition.”
    • “And it’s because of the bleeding that we call it a miscarriage.”

CASE 2

Your next patient is a 25 year old lady who has presented to you complaining of vaginal bleeding. She has missed her periods and her LMP is 6 weeks ago. Home pregnancy test has been done and is positive.

Task:

• Take history

• Investigation will appear on the card

• Explain your diagnosis

• Explain your management

History

  • LMP = six weeks
  • Patient is bleeding
  • Patient has pain

Investigation Findings (as given in the card/screen)

  • Os is closed
  • Nothing in the adenexa
  • Uterus is empty and normal in size
  • No fluid in the pouch of Douglas
  • No sac seen in the uterus
  • 1.5 cm corpus luteum cyst
  • Beta-HCG is low for this aging pregnancy
  • Reminder: LMP six weeks ago

Interpretation Discussion - Corpus luteum cyst

  • Explanation:
    • Rupture in follicle → corpus hemorrhagicum
    • In pregnancy, someone must produce progesterone
    • Corpus luteum produces progesterone
    • Totally normal in pregnancy
    • This cyst keeps producing progesterone in the first trimester
    • Nothing abnormal, nothing incidental, totally fine
    • Expected to see corpus luteum in pregnancy

Key point

  • Initially looks like a complete miscarriage
  • BUT: LMP six weeks → too early
  • Do you expect to see a sac with beta-HCG of 900?
    • Transvaginal ultrasound requires ≥ 1000
    • Transabdominal ultrasound requires ≥ 5000

Why the case asks for “management for complete miscarriage”

  • Because one possibility is complete miscarriage
  • BUT there is a second possibility with higher likelihood
  • There are two possibilities:
    1. Complete miscarriage
    2. Too early to see the sac → could be normal or ectopic

Diagnostic Reasoning

Two possibilities

  1. Complete miscarriage
  2. Too early → could be normal pregnancy OR ectopic pregnancy

Why diagnosis cannot be confirmed

  • “Too early”
  • “LMP may not be exactly six weeks, maybe five weeks and a few days”
  • Empty uterus gives findings but does not confirm anything
  • You cannot make a confirmed diagnosis

How to Explain Diagnosis to the Patient

You say to the lady:

“ after examining u and looking at the USG, there are 3 possibilities”:

Possibility 1 – Complete miscarriage

  • Empathy:
    • “I’m really sorry to tell you there is a chance… there is a chance… that you’ve had a complete miscarriage.”
  • Meaning:
    • “Pregnancy tissue has been passed.”
    • “and Pregnancy has stopped.”
  • Reasons:
    • “Your uterus size is normal and the uterus is empty.”
    • “On ultrasound I cannot see a sac.”
  • Extra note from lecturer:
    • “People who made the diagnosis of complete miscarriage didn’t pass.”

Possibility 2 – Too early to detect (viable pregnancy still possible)

  • Your pregnancy hormone is less than (Beta-HCG) < 1000
  • When it is so low,
    • “We cannot see the pregnancy sac on ultrasound.”
  • Therefore:
    • “There is a possibility that it’s too early to see the pregnancy sac.”
    • “This can be a normal pregnancy that is too early to detect.”

Possibility 3 – Ectopic pregnancy

  • “There is a chance the pregnancy sac can be outside of the womb, which we call ectopic pregnancy.”
  • Lecturer’s note:
    • “I’m not a fan of this, but you still mention it.”

Overall explanation to the patient (combined speech taught in video)

  • After examining and looking at the findings,
    • “There are a few possibilities.”
    • Top two:
      • Complete miscarriage
      • Too early but normal pregnancy
    • And also:
      • Ectopic pregnancy
  • Reasons:
    • “Womb is small and empty.”
    • “Not seeing pregnancy sac.”
    • “Pregnancy hormone less than a thousand—too early to detect.”

Management

1. Repeat transvaginal ultrasound

  • “I will repeat your ultrasound in seven to ten days.”
  • Purpose:
    • “We will look for the pregnancy sac.”
    • If sac is in womb → normal pregnancy
    • If no sac → chance of miscarriage

2. Serial beta-HCG: the 2nd thing I will do is beta HCG

  • Check beta-HCG in two days
  • “We expect it to normally double every 48 hours.”
  • If increasing normally →
    • “Most likely a sign your pregnancy is continuing normal.”
  • If same or dropping →
    • “It can mean you’ve had a miscarriage.”

General practice style explanation (from lecture)

  • Very early pregnancy (five to five-and-a-half weeks)
  • “There is a chance you have had a miscarriage, but we’ll still repeat ultrasound and beta-HCG to confirm because we are very early.”

Corpus Luteum (How to explain to patient)

  • “I’m seeing a cyst in your ovary.”
  • “That is the normal cyst that forms during pregnancy.”
  • “No concerns about it.”
  • “That corpus luteum cyst is expected in pregnancy.”
  • “I’m not concerned about it.”



CASE 3

Your next patient is a 25 year old lady who has presented to you complaining of vaginal bleeding. She has missed her periods and her LMP is 8 weeks ago. Home pregnancy tests has been done and is positive.

Task:

• Take history for 5 minutes

• Investigation will appear on the screen

• Explain your diagnosis

INVESTIGATION

BHCG 70000

USD: Gestational sac seen, correlates to 6 weeks, FHR 80.

UPT+

Initial Diagnostic Thinking

Key Question

  • “Is this normal?”

Clarification from the lecturer

  • Normal fetal HR in early pregnancy can be lower than third-trimester ranges.
  • But 80 bpm is definitely not normal for an 8-week fetus.
  • Pregnancy test positive.

Differentials (as written and discussed in the lecture)

  1. Threatened miscarriage
  2. Molar pregnancy
  3. Normal pregnancy
  4. Incomplete miscarriage (introduced later during reasoning)
  5. Others later mentioned:
    • Complete miscarriage
    • Ectopic pregnancy
    • Bleeding from cervix/vagina

Excluding/Reasoning Through Differentials

Molar pregnancy

  • Not likely because:
    • You should not have a fetus
    • No cardiac activity in molar pregnancy

Complete miscarriage

  • Not likely because:
    • “Your uterus is still full.”
    • “Still haven’t passed anything out of that.”

Threatened miscarriage

  • Less likely because:
    • Threatened miscarriage expects normal size
    • Here size is smaller than expected

Incomplete miscarriage

  • Considered but excluded because:
    • “Do you get an incomplete miscarriage with cardiac activity?”
    • Heartbeat still present at 80 bpm → cross it out

Normal pregnancy vs abnormal

  • β-HCG 70,000 fits within 7–8 week range
  • Sac is small
  • Heart rate low
  • May reflect wrong dates
  • Lecturer emphasises uncertainty: “No good answer.”

Top Diagnosis (as taught in the lecture)

“A normal viable pregnancy, but I have some concerns.”

  • Viable pregnancy:
    • There is a sac
    • There is cardiac activity
  • Concerns:
    • Size smaller than expected
    • Fetal HR lower than expected
  • Possible explanation: wrong dates
  • Acknowledgment:
    • “This could have been a threatened miscarriage.”
    • “Could have been other types of miscarriage.”
    • “Could have been molar… ectopic… etc.”
  • The case is intentionally designed to keep you “in limbo.”

Final Explanation to Patient (exact framework used in video)

Main statement

  • “After asking you Questions and looking at your investigations, I believe you have a normal viable pregnancy, and the episode of bleeding is called a threatened miscarriage.”

Stress this clearly

  • “This is not a miscarriage.”
  • When you have bleeding in early pregnancy but features of viable pregnancy → “we call it a threatened miscarriage.”

BUT there is a big BUT

  • “But, I have some concerns.”
  • Concerns about:
    • Size of pregnancy sac / tissue
    • Fetal heart rate
  • “Both lower than expected.”

Possible explanation

  • “This can be because of wrong dates.”

Next step

  • “The only way I can confirm it is with a repeat ultrasound.”
  • Repeat in a few days or a week to ensure:
    • Growth
    • FHR normalising
    • Pregnancy continuing normally

Second-line Differentials to Mention (as lecturer states)

  • I was thinking about other conditions like,
  • Incomplete miscarriage
    • Less likely because cardiac activity detected
    • Would need to examine os later
  • Molar pregnancy
  • Complete miscarriage
  • Ectopic pregnancy
  • Bleeding from cervix / vagina

Closing Points (from lecture)

  • Case designed to create “uncertainty.”
  • Unlike typical AMC OSCE structure (finding → reason → diagnosis), this case has no certain answer.
  • Most reasonable, logical explanation:
    • Normal viable pregnancy
    • Threatened miscarriage
    • Concerns about size & heart rate
    • Possibility of wrong dates
    • Need repeat ultrasound

🔬 Expert Review & Enhancements

Reviewed by: clinical-documentation-expert (Australian clinical supervisor, O&G documentation standards) · fact-checked against the irStudy medical textbook RAG index.

✏️ Corrections

✏️ Case 2 quotes the discriminatory zone as 'transvaginal ultrasound requires >=1000, transabdominal >=5000' beta-hCG to see a sac. These figures are outdated/imprecise for Australian practice.
Use the current discriminatory zone: an intrauterine sac should normally be visible on transvaginal ultrasound once beta-hCG is approximately 1500-2000 IU/L (transabdominal approximately 6000-6500 IU/L). Below this level an empty uterus is a 'pregnancy of unknown location' and cannot distinguish very early viable IUP, complete miscarriage or ectopic - the correct label rather than committing to complete miscarriage.
✏️ Case 2 non-viability reasoning ('no fetus seen in a sac large enough') is stated qualitatively without the objective cut-offs, and Case 3 asserts FHR 80 as merely a 'concern' without naming it.
Add objective criteria: mean sac diameter >=25 mm with no yolk sac/embryo, or CRL >=7 mm with no cardiac activity = non-viable (confirm on repeat/second opinion). Embryonic bradycardia (FHR <100-110 bpm at 6-7 weeks, and clearly <90 bpm) is a poor prognostic sign for miscarriage, not simply 'wrong dates'.

⭐ Suggested Enhancements

⭐ Introduce and document the term 'pregnancy of unknown location (PUL)' for Case 2 - this is the correct Australian terminology when hCG is below the discriminatory zone with an empty uterus, and it drives the serial beta-hCG (48-hourly) plus interval TVS pathway the case already recommends.
⭐ Add explicit ectopic safety-netting for the PUL case: written advice to present immediately/000 for shoulder-tip pain, syncope, severe or one-sided pain, or heavy bleeding while awaiting repeat imaging/hCG.
⭐ Correct/qualify the drug-history teaching: cannabis and alcohol ARE relevant in antenatal history (fetal risk, safety) - the transcript's dismissal ('I don't ask about drugs') should be enhanced to include brief substance-use screening and cessation advice.
⭐ Add anti-D guidance where relevant (250 IU for Rh(D)-negative women if a sensitising event/instrumentation occurs) and reference RANZCOG early pregnancy and eTG guidelines as metadata.
