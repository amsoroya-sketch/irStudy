Antenatal Counselling: Chronic Hypertension and Obesity in Pregnancy

Source: Dr. Amir workshop — obstetrics / class 4 (Aug 2026 drop) · Specialty: obstetrics_gynaecology

HISTORY TAKING STRUCTURE

Your next patient is a 32 year old lady who is 14 weeks pregnant and has come for her antenatal check. Her BMI is 31 and she has a history of a miscarriage. Her BP is high.

Task:

Take history for 4 minutes

Physical examination will be given to you on a card

Counsel the patient

1. Open-ended question

  • “I’m 14 weeks pregnant, here for pregnancy checks.”
  • Ask: Is it a planned pregnancy?
  • Congratulate.

2. Five Ps

  1. Periods – LMP, regularity
  2. Pregnancy symptoms
  3. Partner – stable relationship, supportive partner, doing well at home, no previous STIs
  4. Previous pregnancies – is this first pregnancy? history of miscarriage
  5. Pap smear / cervical screening

3. Past Medical History

  • She has high blood pressure → ask 4 questions:
    1. How long have you had high blood pressure?
    2. What treatment are you on?
    3. Compliance – are you taking it regularly?
    4. Are you having regular follow-ups?

4. Complications assessment (MOST IMPORTANT PART)

  • Cardiovascular complications:
    • Chest pain
    • Shortness of breath (esp. on exercise)
    • Swelling of legs
    • SOB on lying down (optional but acceptable)
  • Blurring of vision
  • Kidney issues: urine questions (least important)

5. Family history, lifestyle, mood

(As time allows)

PHYSICAL EXAMINATION FINDINGS PROVIDED

  • Cardiovascular exam: normal
  • Respiratory exam: normal
  • High BP
  • Urine dipstick: no protein

COUNSELLING

Three components:

  1. Hypertension component
  2. Obesity component
  3. Routine antenatal component

1. HYPERTENSION COMPONENT

A. Examination

  • “I’ll examine you and make sure there are no complications of hypertension.”
  • Mainly check heart.
  • Examine eyes.

B. Pharmacological treatment

  • She is on perindopril / ramipril / amlodipine (examples) → must change medication.
  • Change to safer medications in pregnancy:
    • Labetalol
    • Methyldopa
    • Nifedipine acceptable (optional mention)

C. Close BP monitoring

  • Need close control because:
    • Changing medication → adjust dose
    • Keep BP controlled to reduce complications
  • Home blood pressure monitoring
    • Appropriate time
    • Appropriate place
    • Suitable device
    • Suitable cuff size
    • Fact sheet will be given for full instructions (don’t explain how to do it in detail you will run out of time)
    • Not after meals, walking, tea/coffee
    • Sit in quiet room, feet on floor
    • Take several readings, ignore the first, record second and third

D. Target BP

  • Goal: < 135/85

2. OBESITY COMPONENT

A. High-dose folic acid

  • Start high-dose folic acid (5 mg) for first 3 months.

B. Iodine

  • Give iodine as routine supplement.

C. Aspirin

  • Because high weight + high BP → risk of preeclampsia
  • May give aspirin to prevent it.

D. Early OGTT

  • Do an early OGTT.
  • Repeat later as needed.

E. Down syndrome screening

  • Definitely recommend Down syndrome screening
  • Mention higher chance of genetic disorders.

F. Additional ultrasounds

  • Routine 18–20 week morphology scan.
  • Extra growth scans to check fetal growth.

G. Weight gain monitoring

  • Weight gain depends on initial BMI:
    • Underweight → higher expected gain
    • Obese → ~5–9 kg expected
  • Monitor weight gain carefully.

3. ROUTINE ANTENATAL CARE COMPONENT

Most important part to mention first

  • Team care
    • Will see heart specialist
    • Obstetrician
    • Dietician (diet advice; salt restriction for hypertension)

Routine items (brief)

  • Vaccination
  • Avoids
  • Other standard antenatal counselling items

(Discussed already in prior sessions; include but keep brief due to time.)

SUMMARY FRAMEWORK

Hypertension

  • Examine heart and eyes
  • Change to safer medications (labetalol / methyldopa ± nifedipine)
  • Close BP monitoring + home readings
  • Suitable time, place, device, cuff
  • Not after meals/tea/coffee/walking
  • Ignore first reading, record 2nd and 3rd
  • Target < 135/85

Obesity

  • High-dose folic acid 5 mg
  • Iodine
  • Possible aspirin
  • Early OGTT
  • Down syndrome screening
  • Morphology scan + extra growth scans
  • Weight gain monitoring

Routine

  • Team care: cardiologist, obstetrician, dietician
  • Vaccination, avoids, usual routine care

🔬 Expert Review & Enhancements

Reviewed by: clinical-documentation-expert (Australian teaching hospital clinical supervisor) · fact-checked against the irStudy medical textbook RAG index.

✏️ Corrections

✏️ Aspirin is offered only tentatively ('may give aspirin') without dose or timing.
This woman has multiple high-risk factors for pre-eclampsia (chronic hypertension + obesity BMI 31 + prior miscarriage), so low-dose aspirin should be actively recommended, not optional. Specify 150 mg nocte commenced before 16 weeks and continued until ~36 weeks (SOMANZ / eTG). At 14 weeks she is still within the window. Consider calcium supplementation if dietary intake is low.
✏️ Down syndrome screening is justified by stating obesity gives a 'higher chance of genetic disorders'.
Obesity does not itself increase the risk of fetal aneuploidy (Down syndrome). What it does is reduce the accuracy of nuchal translucency/serum and morphology ultrasound and increase neural tube defect risk (the rationale for 5 mg folate). Reframe: offer first-trimester combined screening or NIPT to all women regardless of age; NIPT may be preferred where obesity limits ultrasound quality.
✏️ The case does not order booking bloods to establish a baseline for later superimposed pre-eclampsia detection.
In chronic hypertension add baseline booking investigations - FBC, UEC/creatinine, LFTs, serum urate and a urine protein:creatinine ratio - so that superimposed pre-eclampsia (proteinuria/rising urate/deranged bloods) can be recognised later. Confirm the antihypertensive is pregnancy-safe: perindopril/ramipril (ACE inhibitors) are teratogenic and must be ceased.

⭐ Suggested Enhancements

⭐ State the aspirin regimen explicitly (150 mg nocte before 16 weeks) and add calcium supplementation for pre-eclampsia prevention in high-risk women (eTG/SOMANZ).
⭐ Add a VTE risk assessment - obesity plus pregnancy elevates thromboembolic risk; assess for LMWH thromboprophylaxis and reinforce mobility/hydration.
⭐ Add mental-health screening with the Edinburgh Postnatal Depression Scale at booking (KEMH routine), particularly given the previous miscarriage, with clear support pathways.
⭐ Add micronutrient detail: iodine 150 microgram daily and vitamin D assessment, and reinforce that folic acid 5 mg should continue at least until 12 weeks.
⭐ Add safety-netting for pre-eclampsia: advise urgent review / 000 for severe headache, visual disturbance, epigastric or right-upper-quadrant pain, rapid swelling or reduced fetal movements - the key deterioration signs in a hypertensive obese pregnancy.
