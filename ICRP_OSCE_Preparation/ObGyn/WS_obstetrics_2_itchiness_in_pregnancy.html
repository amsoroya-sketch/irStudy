Differential Framework

 2. Non-pregnancy differentials

Non-Pregnancy Differentials

  • Any liver disease → itchiness.
  • Eczema (allergic dermatitis).
  • Contact dermatitis.
  • Urticaria.
  • Infections:
    • Mildly itchy: tinea.
    • Severely itchy: scabies.

Pregnancy-Specific Serious Problems / Rashes

  1. Intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy
    • Liver disease.
    • “Smaller brother” of:
      • Preeclampsia.
      • Acute fatty liver disease.
    • Preeclampsia can occasionally cause itchiness → still in the list.
  2. Polymorphic eruption of pregnancy
    • Also called:
      • Pruritic urticarial papules and plaques of pregnancy.
      • P-U-P-P.
    • Elevated rashes: urticarial papules and plaques.
    • Famous characteristics:
      • Starts around abdominal striae/stretch marks.
      • Peri-umbilical sparing (does not involve around umbilicus).
    • Differentiates it from pemphigoid.
  3. Pemphigoid gestationis
    • Vesicular lesions and bullae.
    • Very specific: happens around umbilicus.

Figure. Pemphigoid gestationis: plaques and vesicles that begin on the abdominal wall before spreading, with periumbilical involvement

  1. Cholestasis of pregnancy
    • Should not give rashes.
    • If itchiness with no skin findings → think beyond skin.
    • All skin conditions (eczema, contact dermatitis, urticaria, polymorphic eruption, pemphigoid) have rashes.
    • Cholestasis has none.
  2. Atopic eruption of pregnancy
    • A version of eczema in pregnancy.

EXPLORING THE MAIN COMPLAINT: ITCHINESS

BOX 1: TIMING

  • When did it start?
  • On and off or constant?
  • Is it getting worse?
  • First time or previous history? (urticaria, eczema)

BOX 2: PATTERN

  • Site: “Where do you feel itchy?”
    • e.g., belly button/stretch marks/palms/soles
  • Night: “Does it wake you up at night?” (red flag)

ALLEVATING / AGGRAVATING FACTORS

  • Anything making it better or worse?
  • Specifically:
    • Worse at night (reminder)
    • Worse with warm shower/warm water
      • Relevant to scabies in 2025

ASSOCIATED FEATURES

  • Rashes: ask early
    • Any rashes?
    • Any redness?
    • Any blisters?
  • Secondary bacterial infection
    • Any fever?
    • Any discharge on the skin?

  • Past history of liver problems or gallstones?

2025 VERSION (NEW VERSION)

  • Itchy and tired
  • Second pregnancy
  • First pregnancy fine
  • Antenatal checks done
  • Itchy on palms and soles
  • No rash
  • No travel
  • No preeclampsia features

Investigations

  • ALT/AST mildly increased
  • GGT normal
  • ALP mildly increased
  • Bilirubin normal (or mildly elevated—no difference)
  • Bile acids high
  • Pattern discussed:
    • Hepatocellular vs cholestasis pattern
    • Hepatocellular 10x AST and ALT increase
      Cholestasis +/- AST/ALT increase, bilirubin/ ALP increase
    • Main thing to look for: bile acids

DIAGNOSIS

  • Cholestasis / intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy
  • Lay explanation:
    • This is liver problem in pregnancy. Liver does not release bile properly
    • Causes itchiness, abdominal pain, nausea, and other positives

MANAGEMENT

Polymorphic Eruption of Pregnancy

  • Topical corticosteroid creams
  • Antihistamines to control itchiness

Cholestasis

  • 36 weeks pregnant → plan for delivery
  • Consider birth (36–37 weeks depending on results)
  • Medication possible, but start planning for birth
  • Similar logic to preeclampsia (stop pregnancy)

✏️ Corrections

✏️ Management of intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy (ICP) states 'plan for delivery, consider birth 36-37 weeks' as a fixed rule, mirroring preeclampsia ('stop pregnancy').
Current RANZCOG/RCOG (2022) guidance individualises timing of birth by serum bile-acid level: severe ICP (bile acids >=100 micromol/L) - consider birth around 35-36 weeks; moderate (40-99) - around 38-39 weeks; mild (<40) - around 39-40 weeks. It is not a blanket 36-37 week induction. Document the actual bile-acid value driving the decision.
✏️ No first-line symptomatic/therapeutic agent is named for ICP itch.
Ursodeoxycholic acid (UDCA) is commonly used for maternal pruritus, though the PITCHES RCT showed it does not improve perinatal outcomes; it should be documented as symptom-directed, not neonatally protective. Emollients/menthol in aqueous cream and antihistamines for sleep are adjuncts.

⭐ Suggested Enhancements

⭐ Use Australian spelling (oesophageal, paediatric context) and the Australian drug name aciclovir/UDCA; keep 'paracetamol' for analgesia.
⭐ Add the key ICP red flag to the note: bile acids are the diagnostic and prognostic marker (itch with NO rash, worse on palms/soles and at night); document fetal surveillance plan and advise reporting reduced fetal movements.
⭐ List a fuller differential with discriminators: PUPPP (periumbilical sparing), pemphigoid gestationis (periumbilical vesicles/bullae), atopic eruption of pregnancy, scabies (worse at night/warm shower, web-space burrows), and preeclampsia - and record a LFT + bile-acid + preeclampsia screen (BP/urinalysis) as investigations.
⭐ Reference eTG antenatal guidance for pruritus in pregnancy and note that ICP confers increased risk of stillbirth, supporting the delivery-planning rationale.
