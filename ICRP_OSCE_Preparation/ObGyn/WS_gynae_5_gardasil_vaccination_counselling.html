Gardasil 9 (HPV) Vaccination Counselling - Adolescent / NIP

Source: Dr. Amir workshop — gynae / 2. intercourse and cervical problems (Aug 2026 drop) · Specialty: obstetrics_gynaecology

GARDASIL VACCINATION COUNSELLING

Context of the Case

  • 13-year-old teenager brought in by the mother
  • Offered Gardasil vaccination at school as part of the National Immunization Program
  • Mother comes to discuss the vaccine and express concerns
  • Full 8-minute counselling station

Common Question Raised

  • If a patient already has HPV, will you still do Gardasil?
    • Yes
    • Gardasil 9 covers nine strains
    • Even if infected with one, protects against the rest

Who Should Be Vaccinated

  • Younger people (better immune system, stronger antibody response, more effective vaccine)
  • Immunocompromised people
  • Men who have sex with men (higher risk of HPV causing anal cancers, warts)
  • Not only for ladies; men can get it too

What Gardasil 9 Covers

  • Protects against nine high-risk HPV types
  • Includes 16 and 18 (high-risk cervical cancer types)
  • Remaining strains also cause cervical cancer and some genital warts

Nature of the Vaccine

  • HPV vaccine is not live
  • Made from a tiny protein
  • Does not contain live virus, killed virus, or DNA
  • Does not cause illness

Timing and National Immunization Program

  • Part of NIP
  • Free for ages 12–25
  • Usually given at 12–13 years, year 7 in school
  • If missed at school → can still get free up to age 25

Upper Age Limits

  • Women: can do up to 45
  • Men: up to 25–26, unless MSM

HPV-Related Diseases

  • Low-risk types: cutaneous warts, genital warts
  • High-risk types: cervical cancers, anal cancers, cancers in oral cavity, penis, oropharynx, vulva, vagina
  • Anal cancers significant concern for men

Types of Gardasil in Australia

  • Two-valent version (rarely used)
  • Gardasil 9 preferred (nine strains, more efficient)

Vaccinating People Who Already Have HPV

  • Vaccine efficacy is lower but still can vaccinate
  • Gardasil is prophylactic, not treatment
  • Benefits:
    • Protects against other strains
    • Protects against reinfection

Change in Doses

  • Previously two-dose schedule
  • Now one dose under NIP for 9–25 years with normal immune system
  • Three doses if:
    • Age >26
    • Severe immunocompromised state

Key Age

  • School vaccination at 12–13 years

Myths

  • Fertility concerns: myth, not true
  • Only contraindications:
    • Anaphylaxis
    • Yeast allergy
    • Severe bleeding disorders (depends on type)

STRUCTURE FOR THE OSCE COUNSELLING

1. Opening Question

  • “How can I help you today?”
  • Mother explains concern about daughter getting Gardasil vaccine that it might encourage her to start sexual activity sooner

2. Acknowledge and Explore Understanding

  • “How much do you know about the vaccine?”
  • “Do you have any specific concerns you’d like me to address?”

3. Identify Agenda

  • Mother usually states concern:
    • Worry that vaccine at this age will encourage early sexual activity

4. Start Explanation (Basics First)

Vaccines

  • “Vaccinations are a useful prevention method for many infections.”

Gardasil Vaccine

  • Protects against HPV
  • Prepares immune system to fight virus more efficiently

5. Explain HPV

  • HPV is sexually transmitted infection which is Transmitted during sex and is the Main cause of cervical cancers and warts in private parts

6. Explain How the Vaccine Works

  • Vaccine is Made of small proteins Which are Similar to proteins on outer surface of HPV. The vaccine Does not contain live virus, any killed virus and Does not cause infection or symptoms.

7. What Gardasil Covers

  • Covers nine high-risk strains of HPV
  • Mention 16 and 18 (high-risk cervical cancer strains)

8. Timing of Vaccination

  • We give One dose as part of NIP
  • Given at 12–13 years in school
  • She still Can catch vaccine up to the age of 25
  • Vaccine Works best if given before exposure to the virus as the vaccine is preventive vaccine and prevents infection in the event of coming into contact with virus.

9. Why Given Early

  • Vaccine works best before exposure
  • It is Most effective when given at 9–14 years
  • As the Immune system is stronger and gives a better antibody response as compared to older individuals

10. Address Sexual Activity Concern

  • Vaccine is not meant to encourage sexual activity
  • Teenagers still receive sexual education
  • Teach safe sex and importance of consent
  • Explain immune system and timing reasons again

11. Side Effects

Minor

  • Pain
  • Swelling
  • Redness at injection site

Major (rare)

  • Severe allergic reactions (anaphylaxis)
  • Same as any vaccine
  • Very rare

12. Contraindications

  • Anaphylaxis to HPV vaccines
  • Yeast allergy
  • Severe bleeding disorders

13. Closing the Consultation (Patient-Centred)

  • I will Provide u reading materials / fact sheets:
    • Australian Immunization Handbook
    • Department of Health
    • Royal Children’s Hospital
  • “I want you to read them and think about it.”
  • Offer follow-up family meeting with daughter or partner
  • “You can make the best decision for your daughter.”
  • Final question:
    • “Any other questions or any other concerns you’d like me to address?”

14. Counselling Principles

  • Patient-centred approach
  • Doctor’s role: give information (good, bad, side effects, benefits)
  • Patient makes informed decision
  • If mother decides not to vaccinate, that is her decision

✏️ Corrections

✏️ The case repeatedly states Gardasil 9 'protects against nine high-risk HPV types'.
Gardasil 9 covers 9 HPV types but they are not all high-risk. It covers 7 oncogenic (high-risk) types - 16, 18, 31, 33, 45, 52 and 58 - plus 2 low-risk types - 6 and 11 - which cause approximately 90% of anogenital warts. Describe it as '7 cancer-causing types plus 2 wart-causing types', not 'nine high-risk types'.
✏️ Upper age limits state 'Women can do up to 45; Men up to 25-26 unless MSM'.
Gardasil 9 is TGA-registered for both males and females up to 45 years of age; the vaccine is not restricted to age 25-26 in men. The 12-25 age band is the National Immunisation Program (NIP)-funded/catch-up range; people aged 26-45 of either sex can still be vaccinated privately (not NIP-funded) after individual benefit discussion. Correct the statement so the age-45 upper limit and NIP-funding distinction apply equally to men and women.

⭐ Suggested Enhancements

⭐ Reference the authoritative Australian sources explicitly in the note: the Australian Immunisation Handbook (HPV chapter) and the NIP schedule - and state the current single-dose schedule (since February 2023) for immunocompetent people aged 9-25, with a 3-dose schedule for immunocompromised individuals and for catch-up in those aged 26 and over.
⭐ Add consent/Gillick-competence documentation: for a 13-year-old, record parental consent and consider the adolescent's own views/assent; note that school-program vaccination uses parental consent forms - important medicolegally in the counselling station.
⭐ Strengthen the evidence-based reassurance on the 'encourages early sexual activity' concern by citing that population data show HPV vaccination does not increase sexual activity or lower age of sexual debut, and quantify benefit (marked falls in HPV 16/18 prevalence, genital warts and high-grade cervical abnormalities in vaccinated Australian cohorts).
⭐ Add practical safety-netting: observe 15 minutes post-injection for anaphylaxis, syncope precautions in adolescents (vaccinate seated/lying), and report adverse events to the state adverse-events service - not currently stated.
