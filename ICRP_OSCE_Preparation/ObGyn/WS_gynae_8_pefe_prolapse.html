Pelvic Examination From Examiner - Pelvic Organ Prolapse

Source: Dr. Amir workshop — gynae / 3. Vulvar problems (Aug 2026 drop) · Specialty: obstetrics_gynaecology

Physical Examination From Examiner - PROLAPSE

GENERAL APPEARANCE

  • Assess the BMI
    • Important anyway for the relation of being obese and having prolapses.
  • Vital signs (routine structure)
    • Ask blood pressure, pulse rate, maybe temperature, and skip the rest.

ABDOMINAL EXAMINATION

  • Look for any masses in the abdomen.
  • Look for any tenderness in the abdomen.
  • Look for scars
    • Especially relevant when given hysterectomies; nice to ask scars to get a heads up.
  • Can ask for distention or swelling
    • Makes abdomen examination more complete.

PELVIC EXAMINATION

Consent + Chaperone

  • As always, triggered by patient consent and presence of a chaperone.

INSPECTION

  • First question in a prolapse case:
    “Do I see a prolapse on inspection?”
    • High-grade prolapse → everything sitting outside.
  • If examiner says no:
    • Ask: “I would like to ask the lady to do a Valsalva maneuver / to strain. Do I see any prolapse?”
  • Rash and redness:
    • AMC gives rash and redness with prolapse cases.
    • Always ask to show you are doing a complete examination.
  • Bleeding and discharge – still do them.
  • Lumps already assessed by asking prolapse; if you want to ask swelling, you can.
  • Incontinence check:
    • Grab a pad, place on the vulva, ask patient to cough.
    • Rationale: prolapse and incontinence always together.
    • “A prolapse case: ask me about incontinence. Incontinence: ask me about prolapse.”

SPECULUM EXAMINATION

  • Insert speculum and ask:
    “When I put in the speculum, do I see any prolapse?”
  • Look specifically for uterine prolapse.
  • In hysterectomy cases:
    • Ask if there is prolapse of the vaginal vault.
  • If cervix present:
    • Ask for bleeding from the cervix.
    • Ask for any masses on the cervix.
  • Look at the vaginal wall.
    • Prolapse cases are menopausal → look for atrophic vaginitis.

BIMANUAL EXAMINATION

  • Cervical motion tenderness
  • Uterus size
  • Uterus borders
  • Tenderness
  • Adnexal masses and adnexal tenderness

VALSALVA MANEUVER (KEY POINT)

  • In 99% of old versions (2017–2018):
    • Ask: “I would like to ask the lady to strain / push down / do a Valsalva maneuver.”
    • Valsalva explained: pinch nose, blow out with mouth closed like popping ears.
    • You are looking for prolapse.

SIMS POSITION + SIMS SPECULUM

  • Famous exam scenario:
    • Examiner says no prolapse with Valsalva.
    • Candidate must ask:
      “I would like to examine the lady in a SIMS position with a SIMS speculum.”
    • The examiner then says: now you see the prolapse.
  • SIMS position/speculum shows prolapse better.
  • If prolapse already seen on regular inspection:
    • Mentioning SIMS is not a key point anymore, but still nice to mention.
    • Expect to see the same prolapse.

CONCLUSION

  • This case has only small additions.
  • Nothing different from the regular structure done previously.

🔬 Expert Review & Enhancements

✏️ Corrections

✏️ The examination has no objective staging system for the prolapse that is identified.
Quantify the prolapse using a recognised system - POP-Q (Pelvic Organ Prolapse Quantification) is the RANZCOG/international standard, or the simpler Baden-Walker grading - and specify the compartment (anterior/cystocele, posterior/rectocele, apical/uterine or vaginal-vault). Stating 'I see a prolapse' without staging/compartment is incomplete.
✏️ American spelling 'maneuver' is used for the Valsalva manoeuvre.
Use Australian spelling: Valsalva manoeuvre.

⭐ Suggested Enhancements

⭐ Add that the examination is ideally performed with the patient straining maximally, and consider standing examination if prolapse is symptomatic but not demonstrated supine/in Sims - to reproduce the true maximal descent.
⭐ Add a brief management/counselling bridge so the station is exam-complete: conservative options (pelvic floor muscle training, weight loss, treat constipation/chronic cough, vaginal pessary) and surgical options for failed conservative care - and topical vaginal oestrogen for the coexisting atrophic vaginitis noted on speculum.
⭐ Add explicit documentation of consent, chaperone and patient comfort/analgesia before the pelvic examination, and offer of a female examiner/support person per Australian informed-consent standards.
⭐ Add the objective cough stress test detail (visualise urine leak from the urethra on cough with a comfortably full bladder) to link prolapse with stress urinary incontinence, reinforcing the case's 'prolapse and incontinence together' teaching point.
⭐ Add metadata and cross-link to the urinary frequency/incontinence station and the PEFE vaginal discharge station which share the abdominal-plus-pelvic (PFAS) examination structure.
