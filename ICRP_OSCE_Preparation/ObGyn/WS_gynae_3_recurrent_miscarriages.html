Recurrent Miscarriage - History, Differentials and Investigations

Source: Dr. Amir workshop — gynae / 5. other cases (Aug 2026 drop) · Specialty: obstetrics_gynaecology

RECURRENT MISCARRIAGES

  • Definition: More than two (≥2) pregnancy losses before 24 weeks.
  • Importance: Need list of differentials and key investigations.
  • Guidelines vary; topic is controversial, but aim is to build a good structure.

Differentials

1. Uterine causes

  • Fibroids
  • Adenomyosis
  • Intrauterine adhesions (Asherman syndrome)
  • Müllerian anomalies (septated uterus + other anomalies)

2. Endocrine problems

  • Thyroid problems
  • Polycystic ovarian syndrome
  • Diabetes (if uncontrolled)
  • Hyperprolactinemia

3. Immunological problems

  • Antiphospholipid antibodies
  • SLE
  • Thrombophilia (placed in same “weird conditions” group)

4. Infections

  • STIs
  • TORCH infections (controversial but included)

5. Genetic factors

  • Chromosomal abnormalities in otherwise normal-phenotype parents
  • Reason for parental karyotyping + analysing products of pregnancy

6. Environmental factors / risk factors

  • Stress
  • Smoking
  • Alcohol
  • Caffeine (cut down coffee if high intake)
  • Age
  • Previous pregnancy loss

INVESTIGATIONS

  • Karyotyping for both parents
  • Uterine assessment
    • Start with ultrasound
    • Hysterosalpingography
  • Antiphospholipid antibodies
    • Anticardiolipin
    • Lupus anticoagulant
  • Thyroid function test (focus on TSH)
  • Thrombophilia screening
  • Diabetes
  • Prolactin
  • Progesterone levels (in some references)
  • Baseline bloods: FBC, UEC, LFT (not as essential as key investigations)
  • Tricky stem: “trying for pregnancy for six years” appeared like infertility but was actually recurrent miscarriages.

HISTORY TAKING STRUCTURE

1. Open-ended question + Address emotional impact

  • “How can I help you today?”
  • Acknowledge emotional burden:
    • Stressful, overwhelming, concerning
    • Reassure: safe hands, will try to find cause and treat it, hope for normal pregnancy and healthy baby

2. Explore the Complaint (Miscarriages)

  • How many miscarriages have you had?
  • At what age of pregnancy did it happen?
    • Early → genetic
    • Later → immunological (example given)
  • Any tests done on products of pregnancy?

3. Pregnancy History

  • Have you completed any pregnancies in the past?
  • Any surgical procedures down below?
    • Surgical termination, uterine surgery, procedures affecting endometrium/uterus

4. Five Ps (adapted for this case)

Period History

  • When was last period?
  • Regular or irregular?
  • Excessive pain or bleeding during period?
  • Pain or bleeding between periods?

Partner History

  • Replace sexual activity question with: Is your partner supportive?
  • STI screening:
    • Any previous diagnosed STIs?
    • Do you practice safe sex/condoms?
    • Any multiple partners?
  • Two famous partner questions:
    • Post-coital bleeding?
    • Dyspareunia?

Pills

  • Skip pills question (not relevant to miscarriage cause)

Pap smear

  • Are you up to date with cervical screening?

(Other Ps not applied/mentioned)

5. Explore Differential Groups Through Targeted Questions

Endocrine

Thyroid

  • Any weather preference? (heat/cold intolerance)

Diabetes

  • Polyuria
  • Polydipsia (“drinking more than usual?”)

PCOS

  • Excessive hair growth on face/body
  • Acne
  • Recent weight gain

Hyperprolactinemia

  • Headache
  • Milky discharge from breast/nipples
  • (Optional: blurry vision – mentioned only as “anyone wants to ask extra”)

Immunological problems

  • Joint pain
  • Rashes
  • Previous history of clotting (DVT, PE)
  • Family history of miscarriages

Infections

  • STIs already covered
  • TORCH-related: Any pets at home like cats? (toxoplasmosis)

Environmental causes

  • Stress at home/work
  • How is this affecting your life/relationship/function?

SADMA

  • Smoking
  • Alcohol
  • Drugs
  • Coffee/tea/caffeinated drinks intake
  • Medications
  • Any specific past medical history or family issues (already mostly asked)

Management (as stated briefly)

  • Finding the cause and treating the cause
  • Case typically focuses on history, differentials, and investigations, not diagnosis.

Old recall stories (as stated):

  • A card with investigations where karyotyping was missing and candidate had to mention it.
  • Tricky stem: “trying for pregnancy for six years” appeared like infertility but was actually recurrent miscarriages.

🔬 Expert Review & Enhancements

Reviewed by: clinical-documentation-expert (Australian clinical supervisor, O&G / RACGP standards) · fact-checked against the irStudy medical textbook RAG index.

✏️ Corrections

✏️ The definition is given as 'more than two (>=2) pregnancy losses before 24 weeks', which is internally inconsistent ('more than two' vs '>=2') and uses a 24-week cut-off.
Clarify the definition and the gestational cut-off. In Australia miscarriage is pregnancy loss before 20 weeks' gestation. Recurrent miscarriage is variably defined - RANZCOG/RCOG traditionally use 3 or more consecutive losses, while ESHRE uses 2 or more - so state 'two or more (ESHRE) or three or more consecutive (RCOG/RANZCOG) pregnancy losses' and use the 20-week Australian threshold rather than 24 weeks.
✏️ Routine TORCH screening and serum progesterone are listed among investigations.
Routine TORCH serology is NOT recommended in the recurrent miscarriage work-up (low diagnostic yield and does not change management); it should be removed or flagged as not routine. Serum progesterone is not a standard recurrent-miscarriage investigation. The evidence-based core work-up is: parental karyotype +/- products-of-conception cytogenetics, pelvic ultrasound (+/- hysteroscopy/HSG for uterine anomalies), antiphospholipid antibodies (lupus anticoagulant and anticardiolipin, repeated 12 weeks apart), TSH/thyroid antibodies, and testing for diabetes; inherited thrombophilia screening only in selected cases.

⭐ Suggested Enhancements

⭐ Add the key treatable cause and its management: antiphospholipid syndrome is the most important treatable cause and is managed in pregnancy with low-dose aspirin plus prophylactic heparin (LMWH) - naming the treatment demonstrates management-level knowledge, not just a differential list.
⭐ Add preconception and general advice: high-dose/standard folic acid, optimise thyroid function and glycaemic control, smoking/alcohol/caffeine reduction, and progesterone support (vaginal micronised progesterone) for women with recurrent miscarriage and early pregnancy bleeding per current guidance.
⭐ Add explicit communication/psychosocial scoring: recurrent pregnancy loss is highly distressing - acknowledge grief, offer supportive counselling and early-pregnancy reassurance scanning in the next pregnancy, and refer to a recurrent-miscarriage/early-pregnancy clinic.
⭐ Add related metadata cross-linking to infertility, thyroid disease in pregnancy and antiphospholipid syndrome, and cite RANZCOG guidance on investigation of recurrent miscarriage as the Australian reference.
