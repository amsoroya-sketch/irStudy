Ovarian Cancer - History and Diagnosis of Bloating/Abdominal Distension

Source: Dr. Amir workshop — gynae / 5. other cases (Aug 2026 drop) · Specialty: obstetrics_gynaecology

OVARIAN CANCER

Your next patient is a 60 year old lady who has presented to your general practice complaining of bloating and constipation.

Task:

History for 6 minutes

PE and Investigation will appear on the screen at 6 minutes

Explain diagnosis and differentials

GENERAL TEACHING POINTS FROM LECTURE

  • Approach the case based on the stem complaint (bloating + constipation).
  • Even if you know the recall, do not jump to ovarian cancer; ask ovarian cancer questions at the end.
  • Ovarian cancer = “silent killer” because symptoms are non-specific.

Typical Ovarian Cancer Features

  • Pelvic & Abdominal symptoms: Bloating and distension
  • Urin Urgency & frequency
  • Difficulty eating, nausea, anorexia, early satiety
  • Pelvic and abdominal pain
  • Vaginal bleeding
  • Ascites
  • Bowel obstruction
  • Pleural effusion

DIFFERENTIAL DIAGNOSIS LIST FOR BLOATING + DISTENSION

GI causes

  • Lactose intolerance
  • Celiac disease
  • IBS
  • Functional dyspepsia
  • Bowel obstruction (adhesion bands, surgeries)
  • Infections (Giardia)

Systemic / Other

  • Diabetes (autonomic neuropathy → GI symptoms)

Malignancies

  • Bowel cancer
  • Ovarian cancer

Other

  • Ascites
  • Gastroenteritis, giardia
  • Obesity

HISTORY STRUCTURE

1. Open-ended Question

  • Let patient talk freely
  • Address concerns, reassure

2. Explore the Main Complaints

Timing

  • How long u have been experiencing bloating and costipation ?
  • On/off or continuous?
  • Getting worse?

Aggravating / Alleviating

  • Anything making it better or worse?

Food-related

  • “Does any specific food make it worse?”

    (Used to screen GI causes like lactose intolerance / celiac)

3. Differential Diagnosis Questions

A. Bowel Cancer (FIRST)

  • Weight loss
  • Appetite loss
  • Lumps and bumps
  • Tiredness
  • Blood in stools
  • Dark stools
  • Abdominal pain, nausea, vomiting
  • Family history of bowel cancer
  • Bowel cancer screening (if age >50)

B. IBS

  • Abdominal pain/cramps
  • Is Pain/bloating better after opening your bowels?
  • Have u noticed Any change in frequency of opening your bowels?
  • Stress:
    • “How is everything at home?”
    • “Any stresses at work?”

C. Functional Dyspepsia

  • Abdominal/epigastric pain
  • Bloating
  • One reflux question: “Any heartburn?”

D. Previous Surgeries

  • Any previous abdominal surgeries?

    (Assessing for adhesion bands → bowel obstruction)

E. Diabetes

  • Polyuria: “Are you passing more urine lately?”
  • Polydipsia: “Have you noticed an increase in your thirst?”

F. IBD

  • Mouth ulcers
  • Joint pain
  • Blurring of vision
  • Rashes
  • (Blood in stools already asked)

G. Ovarian Cancer (LAST)

1. Period History

  • When was your last period?
  • Any spotting or abnormal vaginal bleeding?

2. Routine Screenings (age >50)

  • Mammogram up to date?
  • Cervical screening up to date?

3. Family History

  • Breast cancer
  • Ovarian cancer

4. Early Satiety

  • “Do you feel full after eating a small amount of food?”

5. Urinary Symptoms

  • Frequency: “Are you passing urine more frequently?”
  • Urgency: “Do you get a sudden urge to pass urine?”

6. Other

  • Shortness of breath
  • Tiredness

4. SADMA + PMH + FH

Wrap up with standard closure.

PHYSICAL EXAMINATION FINDINGS

  • On Physical examination: Shifting dullness and acites present
  • On USD a right ovarian mass seen with septations

EXPLANATION TO PATIENT

Use the standard format:

  • “Your symptoms can be due to a number of causes or conditions.”
  • “But in your case I am concerned about the possibility of ovarian cancer.”
  • Reasoning:
    • Ovarian cancers usually present with unspecific symptoms (bloating, constipation, urinary problems).
    • Ultrasound shows a mass on the ovary.
    • There is fluid collection in the abdomen.

Differentials to share

  • IBS
  • Functional dyspepsia
  • Surgeries → adhesion bands → bowel obstruction
  • IBD
  • Diabetes
  • Bowel cancer

DIFFERENTIAL LIST FOR DISTENSION

GI

  • Lactose intolerance
  • Constipation
  • Bowel obstruction (d/t surgeries)
  • Giardiasis

Metabolic

Gynecological

Ascites Causes

Other

HISTORY STRUCTURE (New Case)

1. Open-ended Question + Address Concerns

2. Explore the Distension

Clarify Meaning

  • “Can you describe what you mean by that?”
  • “Do you feel bloated or gassy?”
  • “Or have you noticed the waist/abdominal circumference actually increasing?”

Timing

  • When did you first notice it?
  • On/off or always present?
  • Getting worse?

Food-related

  • “Is it related to eating? Is it worse after eating?”
  • “Is it related to any specific food?”

3. General Symptoms (Ask Early)

  • Nausea
  • Vomiting
  • Constipation
  • Diarrhea
  • Passing wind normally?
  • Fever
  • Dizziness

4. Differential Diagnosis Questions

A. Bowel Cancer (Most Important)

  • Weight loss
  • Appetite loss
  • Lumps and bumps
  • Tiredness
  • Blood in stools
  • Dark stools
  • Family history
  • Bowel cancer screening
  • Changes in bowel habits

B. IBS

  • Abdominal pain/cramps
  • Pain/distension better after opening bowels?
  • Change in frequency of bowel opening
  • Stress at home
  • Stress at work

C. IBD

  • Joint pain
  • Mouth ulcers
  • Blurring of vision
  • Rashes
  • (Blood in stools & diarrhea already asked)

D. Celiac Disease

  • Have u had Greasy stools that are :
    • Hard to flush?
    • Stick to pan?
    • Float on water?

E. Lactose Intolerance

  • Relation to dairy products?

F. Surgeries / Bowel Obstruction

  • Any previous abdominal surgeries?

G. Gastroenteritis / Infections

  • Any recent travel?

H. Ascites – Liver Conditions

  • Yellowish discoloration of skin
  • Dark urine
  • Pale stools

I. Ascites – Heart Failure

  • Chest pain
  • Shortness of breath on exercise
  • Leg swelling
  • (Pillows question acceptable)

J. Ovarian Cancer (Leave at End)

  • Family history: breast + ovarian cancer
  • Urinary frequency
  • Urgency
  • Early satiety: “Do you feel full after eating a small amount of food?”
  • Tiredness
  • Shortness of breath

K. Obesity

  • “Do you follow a healthy, balanced diet?”
  • “Do you exercise?”
  • Any weight gain?
    • Weight gain possible in ovarian cancer due to mass + fluid

L. Diabetes

  • Polyuria
  • Polydipsia

5. Wrap-up

  • SADMA
  • Past medical history
  • Family history

Physical EXAMINATION FINDINGS

  • Adnexal mass (right or left iliac fossa)

EXPLANATION TO PATIENT

  • “There can be a number of causes giving you these symptoms that I have ruled out.”
  • “But I am very concerned about the possibility of ovarian cancer.”
  • Reasoning:
    • Ovarian cancer gives unspecific symptoms such as distension and bloating.
    • Examination found a mass in the lower abdomen.


🔬 Expert Review & Enhancements

Reviewed by: clinical-documentation-expert (Australian clinical supervisor, O&G / RACGP standards) · fact-checked against the irStudy medical textbook RAG index.

✏️ Corrections

✏️ The investigation/management section is essentially absent - after finding an adnexal mass, ascites and 'shifting dullness', the note stops at 'explain diagnosis' without stating the key tumour marker or referral pathway.
For a postmenopausal woman with a complex/septated adnexal mass and ascites, add serum CA-125 (plus CEA and CA19-9 to help distinguish GI primaries) and calculate a Risk of Malignancy Index (RMI = ultrasound score x menopausal status x CA-125). Australian practice mandates urgent referral to a gynaecological oncologist / specialist multidisciplinary team, not routine gynaecology, for a suspected ovarian malignancy.
✏️ The imaging finding is written as 'On USD a right ovarian mass seen with septations' and 'acites' (typographical/abbreviation artefacts).
Use full terms in the record: transvaginal/transabdominal ultrasound (US) showing a complex right ovarian mass with septations, and ascites (correct spelling). Avoid the non-standard abbreviation 'USD' for ultrasound.

⭐ Suggested Enhancements

⭐ Add family-history and genetic risk stratification: ask specifically about breast AND ovarian cancer in first-degree relatives and consider BRCA1/BRCA2 and Lynch syndrome - relevant to both diagnosis and onward genetics referral.
⭐ Add a concise 'not-to-be-missed' framing when explaining to the patient and safety-netting: the combination of persistent bloating/early satiety/urinary urgency in a woman over 50 for more than a few weeks warrants prompt investigation (the 'silent killer' point should be paired with an explicit early-referral action).
⭐ Add tumour-marker and staging logic to the teaching: CA-125 can be raised in benign conditions (endometriosis, fibroids, ascites of other cause) so it supports but does not confirm the diagnosis; definitive diagnosis/staging is surgical/histological.
⭐ Add related metadata cross-linking to abdominal distension/ascites work-up and colorectal cancer, and cite Cancer Australia's ovarian cancer clinical practice guidance as the Australian reference.
