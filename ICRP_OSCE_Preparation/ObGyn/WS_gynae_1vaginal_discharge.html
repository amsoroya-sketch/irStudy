Source: Dr. Amir workshop — gynae / 3. Vulvar problems (Aug 2026 drop) · Specialty: obstetrics_gynaecology

VAGINAL DISCHARGE

Non-sexual causes

- Non- STI causes Candida, bacterial vaginosis

- Hormonal contraception, cervical ectropion, cervical polyps, malignancy, FB – retained tampon, dermatitis, erosive lichen planus.

STI Chlamydia, mycoplasma genitalium, Neisseria, trichomonas, herpes simplex.

PID

Types of discharge

Candida – thick white non-offensive

Gono – yellowish purulent foul-smelling

Trichomoniasis - offensive green and yellow, scanty to profuse and frothy

BV – thin, grey-white, offensive and fishy odour

BV

- Thin, white homogeneous discharge

- Vaginal fluid PH > 4.5

- Clue cells on wet preparation

- Fishy odour when adding alkali (potassium hydroxide 10%) to discharge.

- Treatment – metro 400 mg 12 hourly for 7 days

Trichomoniasis metronidazole 2 g as a single dose (5 tablets)

✏️ Corrections

✏️ Trichomoniasis is treated with 'metronidazole 2 g as a single dose'. In current eTG (Antibiotic) the preferred regimen for symptomatic trichomoniasis in women is metronidazole 400 mg orally twice daily for 7 days; the 2 g single dose is an alternative (better adherence) but has higher failure rates.
State: metronidazole 400 mg orally, 12-hourly for 7 days (preferred), OR metronidazole 2 g orally as a single dose (alternative). Treat sexual partner(s) concurrently and advise abstinence until both treated. Trichomoniasis is a notifiable STI in several jurisdictions.
✏️ Recurrent candidiasis dose written as 'Fluconazole 3 tabs on day 1, 4, and 7 and then one weekly for 6 months' is ambiguous.
eTG: fluconazole 150 mg orally on days 1, 4 and 7 (induction), then fluconazole 150 mg orally once weekly for 6 months (maintenance). Confirm species/susceptibility on culture (non-albicans species e.g. C. glabrata may be azole-resistant) and screen for diabetes if 4+ glycosuria.
✏️ Spelling/terminology errors: 'Gardanella' and 'Gadasil'.
Correct spellings are Gardnerella vaginalis and Gardasil (Gardasil 9 HPV vaccine, funded on the National Immunisation Program).
✏️ Gonorrhoea/PID empirical regimen lists ceftriaxone 500 mg IM without diluent/route detail and does not mention gonococcal resistance surveillance.
eTG PID regimen is appropriate: ceftriaxone 500 mg (in 2 mL of 1% lignocaine) IM as a single dose, PLUS doxycycline 100 mg orally BD for 14 days, PLUS metronidazole 400 mg orally BD for 14 days. Perform gonococcal culture (not just NAAT) where possible for antimicrobial susceptibility. Arrange contact tracing and test of reinfection.

⭐ Suggested Enhancements

⭐ Add explicit red-flag/safety-netting for PID progressing to tubo-ovarian abscess or sepsis (persistent fever, severe pain, haemodynamic instability) requiring admission and IV antibiotics; also counsel on long-term sequelae (infertility, chronic pelvic pain, ectopic pregnancy risk).
⭐ For the 62-year-old with postmenopausal brownish discharge, emphasise that any postmenopausal bleeding/blood-stained discharge is endometrial cancer until proven otherwise and mandates transvaginal ultrasound (endometrial thickness) +/- pipelle/hysteroscopy before attributing to atrophic vaginitis.
⭐ Add investigation structure using self-collected or clinician-collected vaginal swab for CT/NG/TV NAAT (now standard in Australia), plus offer full STI screen (HIV, syphilis serology, hepatitis B/C) and document contact tracing/partner notification.
⭐ Add PBS/eTG references and Australian NCSP note: Cervical Screening Test (HPV-based) commences at age 25 and every 5 years; correct 'CST if not done' to specify HPV primary screening.
