Early Pregnancy Bleeding Cases - Part 2 (Adnexal Tenderness, Molar Pregnancy)

Source: Dr. Amir workshop — obstetrics / class 3 (Aug 2026 drop) · Specialty: obstetrics_gynaecology

Early Pregnancy Bleeding With Adnexal Tenderness

Given Findings (as presented)

  • LMP six weeks ago
  • Vaginal bleeding started today
  • Ongoing pregnancy symptoms (early morning sickness, tiredness)
  • Cervical motion tenderness: negative
  • Uterus: six weeks in size
  • Adnexal tenderness: right side
  • UUPT: positive
  • Os: closed

Key Teaching Point

  • Do NOT jump to ectopic pregnancy.
  • This is NOT a standalone ectopic pregnancy because the uterus is full and size correlates with LMP.

Reasoning

  • There is a normal pregnancy happening inside the uterus.
  • The os is closed, and the size of uterus matches six weeks → reassuring for normal pregnancy.
  • There is something happening in the ovaries / tubes (adnexal tenderness).

Differentials (as listed in lecture)

  • Heterotopic pregnancy
  • Ovarian cyst / ruptured hemorrhagic cyst
  • Ovarian torsion
  • Ovarian mass (including cancer)
  • Pelvic inflammatory disease

Preferred Provisional Diagnosis (as lecturer states)

  • “Normal pregnancy with an ovarian pathology (ovarian possibly tube pathology).”
  • You do not have good reasons to label it heterotopic, cyst, torsion, cancer, PID specifically.
  • It is not ectopic pregnancy.

How to Explain to the Patient (lecture wording & order)

Two main possibilities:

Possibility 1 — Heterotopic Pregnancy

  • A normal pregnancy in the womb
  • A second pregnancy sac outside the womb
  • Usually located in the tubes
  • Meaning: two pregnancy sacs, one normal, one outside the womb

Possibility 2 — Normal Pregnancy + Ovarian/Tubal Problem

  • Normal pregnancy in the womb
    • Os closed
    • Size of womb exactly as expected
  • Problem occurring in the ovaries / tubes causing pain and bleeding
    • Ovarian cyst / ruptured hemorrhagic cyst
    • Ovarian torsion (ovary twisting on itself)
    • Ovarian mass (e.g., cancer)
    • Tubal infection (pelvic inflammatory disease)

Important Clarification

  • This case is definitely NOT ectopic pregnancy.
  • Ectopic pregnancy in the exam had:
    • Positive CMT
    • Empty uterus (key feature)
    • Adnexal tenderness

CASE 2 — Early Pregnancy Bleeding With Enlarged Uterus & Hyperemesis

Given Findings

  • LMP six weeks
  • Vaginal bleeding
  • Passing tissue (may or may not be “grape-like”)
  • Exaggerated pregnancy symptoms
    • Nausea/vomiting
    • Vomiting many times → hyperemesis
  • Cervical motion tenderness: negative
  • Uterus: 10 weeks in size (but LMP only six weeks)
  • Adnexa: normal

Top Diagnosis (as per lecturer)

  • Molar pregnancy
  • Still top diagnosis even without grape-like tissue.

Why not twin pregnancy as top?

  • Twin/multiple pregnancy does not always bleed.
  • Molar pregnancy always bleeds (as per lecturer’s teaching).
  • Miscarriage in multiple pregnancy is still a differential, not the top.

How to Explain Molar Pregnancy to the Patient (exact framework, lay terms)

  • Start with empathy:
    • “I’m so sorry that I have to give you this news today.”
  • “You most likely have a condition called molar pregnancy.”
  • Explanation in layman terms:
    • The pregnancy does not grow normally.
    • The pregnancy sac forms with no fetus inside it.
    • It is a type of abnormal pregnancy from the beginning.
    • Because it is abnormal, it cannot survive.
    • It ends with vaginal bleeding and passing tissue.
    • The sac has no baby parts inside.

Differentials (as listed by lecturer)

  • Complete miscarriage
  • Incomplete miscarriage
  • Threatened miscarriage
  • Ectopic pregnancy
  • Bleeding from the cervix
  • Bleeding from the vagina

Case Summary

  • This is an old exam case that occasionally appears.
  • Uterus size > expected + hyperemesis + bleeding → Molar pregnancy as top diagnosis.

✏️ Corrections

✏️ Case 2 teaches 'molar pregnancy always bleeds' as an absolute rule to prefer it over twin/multiple pregnancy. This is an over-generalisation; the discriminating features are the enlarged (>dates) uterus, markedly raised beta-hCG, exaggerated symptoms/hyperemesis and characteristic ultrasound, not an absolute 'always bleeds' rule.
State the diagnostic combination for hydatidiform mole: uterus large-for-dates, very high beta-hCG, hyperemesis, and 'snowstorm'/vesicular pattern with no fetus on ultrasound. Confirmation is histopathology of products, and management requires referral with serial beta-hCG follow-up to detect gestational trophoblastic neoplasia.

⭐ Suggested Enhancements

⭐ For the adnexal-tenderness case, add ovarian torsion as a time-critical must-not-miss (sudden severe unilateral pain, nausea/vomiting) requiring urgent ultrasound with Doppler and gynaecology referral - the note lists torsion but does not flag its urgency.
⭐ Add that molar pregnancy requires anti-D consideration only after tissue passage/evacuation in Rh(D)-negative women, referral to a gynaecological oncology/trophoblast service, avoidance of pregnancy and reliable contraception during beta-hCG surveillance.
⭐ Add explicit investigation plan (serum beta-hCG, transvaginal ultrasound, blood group/antibody screen, FBE) to the molar case which currently jumps to explanation without documented workup.
⭐ Add eTG and RANZCOG 'Gestational Trophoblastic Disease' references as metadata.
