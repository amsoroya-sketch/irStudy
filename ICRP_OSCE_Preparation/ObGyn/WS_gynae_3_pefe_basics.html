Gynaecological Pelvic Examination - PEFE Basics

Source: Dr. Amir workshop — gynae / 1. period problems (Aug 2026 drop) · Specialty: obstetrics_gynaecology

PEFE BASICS

1. GENERAL APPEARANCE

  • Basics.
  • Bleeding cases: pallor.
  • Cancer on your list: cachexia.
  • PCOS / hyperandrogenism: hirsutism, acne, being overweight.
  • PCOS appears in many differentials (menorrhagia, amenorrhea, others).
  • Check hirsutism, acne, BMI/overweight on general appearance.

2. VITAL SIGNS

  • Do them as a habit.
  • Do in all cases.
  • Vital signs are part of the examination.
  • May tick off key points even when not thinking about them.
  • Example: OCP counseling → baseline blood pressure (COCP complication is hypertension).
  • Infections are also reasons to do vital signs.

3. PELVIC EXAMINATION

Always state:

  • I want to do Pelvic examination with consent
  • With chaperone

Step 1: Inspection

Ask about:

  • Bleeding and discharge
  • Skin:any redness or rashes
  • Any Swelling or lumps / masses

Relevant across:

  • Menorrhagia (bleeding)
  • PID / STIs (discharge)
  • Redness/rashes cases
  • Lumps for Bartholin cases

Step 2: examiner, I want to do Speculum Examination. “ what do u want to know?”

Habit: I want to assess cervix and vaginal wall

Cervix

  • Bleeding
  • Discharge
  • Any abnormalities on the cervix:
    • Masses
    • Abnormal growths
    • Ectropion
    • Redness
    • Polyps
      (depending on the case)

Vaginal Walls

  • Expect normal
  • Exception: atrophic vaginitis → dry, thin, pale

Step 3: Bimanual Examination

Always ask 3 questions:

  1. Cervical motion tenderness
  2. Uterus
    • Size
    • Tenderness
    • Border
      • Large uterus significant in gynecology
      • Menorrhagia + large uterus → think fibroid
      • Check if border is regular or irregular
      • Tenderness relevant in PID
  3. Adnexa
    • Masses
    • Tenderness

Summary

  • A basic structure repeated for all cases.
  • Same pelvic examination each time.
  • Only small additions depending on differentials.

🔬 Expert Review & Enhancements

Reviewed by: clinical-documentation-expert (Australian clinical supervisor, physical examination) · fact-checked against the irStudy medical textbook RAG index.

✏️ Corrections

✏️ American spelling 'gynecology' used throughout.
Australian documentation requires 'gynaecology' / 'gynaecological'.
✏️ The consent/chaperone statement is brief ('with consent, with chaperone').
Australian medicolegal standard (AHPRA / RANZCOG) for an intimate examination requires: explicit informed consent for the specific examination, a documented offer of a chaperone, recording the chaperone's name/role, explanation of what will be done and why, and that the patient may stop at any time. Document all of these, not just that a chaperone was present.

⭐ Suggested Enhancements

⭐ Add a documentation template line for intimate examinations (offer of chaperone accepted/declined, chaperone identity, consent obtained, patient able to stop at any time) so trainees record it consistently - this is a frequent AMC and medicolegal mark.
⭐ Add that inspection should include the external genitalia/vulva and perineum (not only 'skin, bleeding, discharge, lumps') and that bimanual findings of cervical motion tenderness plus adnexal tenderness/mass raise PID or ectopic pregnancy - link the exam step to its differential.
⭐ Add the Australian reasoning for baseline BP before combined hormonal contraception (a genuine COCP contraindication/monitoring point) and that a bulky, irregular, non-tender uterus with menorrhagia suggests fibroids while a tender bulky uterus suggests adenomyosis - explicit sign-to-diagnosis mapping strengthens the exam station.
