Physical Examination from Examiner (PEFE) - OCP Counselling Case

Source: Dr. Amir workshop — gynae / 4. Counselling cases (Aug 2026 drop) · Specialty: obstetrics_gynaecology

Physical Examination From Examiner – OCP Counselling Case

1. General Appearance

  • Look for BMI → obesity important.
  • Cachexia may matter because cancers are on the list.

2. Vital Signs

  • Blood pressure is super important.
  • Can still ask for pulse rate, respiratory rate, temperature.

3. CVS Examination (imp)

  • CVS is super necessary due to contraindications (ischemic, valvular, congenital heart disease).
  • Simple approach:
    • S1, S2
    • Murmurs or added sounds
  • Optional additions (time-efficient):
    • Ask for raised JVP
    • Ask for displaced apex beat
    • Not necessary to do a full detailed exam, but allowed if desired.

4. Respiratory Examination

  • Because respiratory findings can reflect cardiac problems (heart failure, congenital heart disease).
  • Do it short and fast:
    • Air entry equal
    • Any added sounds

5. Breast Examination (imp)

  • With consent of patient and presence of a chaperone.
  • Purpose: look for any breast lumps.

6. Abdominal Examination (imp)

  • Ask for:
    • Distension (ascites)
    • Masses
    • Hepatomegaly
    • Splenomegaly
    • tenderness

7. Pelvic Examination

  • References say routine pelvic exam is not needed for initiating COCP.
  • But for AMC OSCE, lecturer will still do it.

When to Skip

  • If girl is a virgin → only inspection / looking at hymen.
  • If never sexually active or no partner → skip full pelvic exam.
  • If concerns exist (heavy periods, discharge, other problems) → do full pelvic examination.

Pelvic Examination Steps

  • Consent and chaperone
  • Inspection
    • Look for bleeding, discharge, rashes, redness, lumps, swelling
  • Speculum examination
    • Only if she is not a virgin
    • Look at cervix
    • Look at vaginal walls
  • Bimanual examination
    • Cervical motion tenderness
    • Uterus (size, border, tenderness)
    • Adnexal mass and tenderness

8. Urine Pregnancy Test

  • Last step.
  • Depends on history.
  • If virgin, not sexually active, zero chance of pregnancy → do not do it.
  • If sexually active lady → preferably ensure she is not pregnant before starting contraception.

Key Don’ts / Must-Remembers

  • Don’t forget blood pressure
  • Don’t forget obesity
  • Don’t forget CVS and liver examination
  • Breast examination → yes
  • Pelvic examination depends on sexual activity and concerns
  • If virgin / never sexually active → no pelvic exam
  • If concerns (discharge, bleeding, pain on sex) → do pelvic exam

✏️ Corrections

✏️ The note acknowledges 'routine pelvic exam is not needed for initiating COCP' but then instructs to perform pelvic and breast examination anyway 'because the lecturer will do it', potentially teaching unnecessary/inappropriate examination.
Per current Australian/RACGP evidence, initiating the COCP requires only blood pressure and BMI/weight; routine pelvic (speculum/bimanual) and breast examination are NOT indicated for asymptomatic women starting the pill and should only be performed if the history flags a specific concern (abnormal bleeding, discharge, dyspareunia, palpable lump). The station should reward the candidate for correctly stating these examinations are not routinely required and for gaining consent + chaperone if a targeted exam is justified.
✏️ The note directs speculum examination to be skipped 'only if she is a virgin' and to 'look at the hymen'.
Decisions about speculum/pelvic examination should be based on clinical indication and the woman's comfort/consent, not on virginity status or hymenal inspection, which is not clinically appropriate. Reframe as: offer examination only when clinically indicated, with informed consent and a chaperone, and never as a precondition for prescribing contraception.

⭐ Suggested Enhancements

⭐ Add explicit consent and chaperone documentation as a scored step for any intimate (breast/pelvic) examination - an AHPRA/medicolegal expectation that also earns OSCE communication marks.
⭐ Add that cardiovascular examination should specifically look for signs relevant to COCP contraindications (murmurs suggesting valvular disease, evidence of hypertension) and that a raised BP reading must be repeated/confirmed before withholding or prescribing.
⭐ Add a structured hand-off line: on completing PEFE the candidate should summarise pertinent positives/negatives (BP, BMI, no murmur, no hepatomegaly, no breast lump) and link them explicitly to the decision to prescribe or defer, improving clinical-reasoning marks.
