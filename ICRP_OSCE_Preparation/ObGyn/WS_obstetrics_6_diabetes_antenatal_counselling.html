Pre-Existing Diabetes - Pre-Pregnancy and Antenatal Counselling

Source: Dr. Amir workshop — obstetrics / class 3 (Aug 2026 drop) · Specialty: obstetrics_gynaecology

DIABETES ANTENATAL COUNSELLING

Routine Screening

  • Routine gestational diabetes screening already discussed.
  • Done at 26–28 weeks.
  • OGTT is performed.
    • Glucose challenge test no longer recommended in Australia.
  • HbA1c is not accurate in pregnancy → OGTT is best.
  • OGTT = FBS, 1-hour, 2-hour post-glucose BSL.
  • Will discuss normal/abnormal ranges with new case.

Case 1: Pre-Existing Diabetes (Pre-Pregnancy)

Lady with pre-existing diabetes: “I have diabetes and want to fall pregnant.”

Type of Diabetes

  • Type 1 vs Type 2 does not matter → managed similarly.
  • Guidelines also treat them the same.

Multidisciplinary Team

  • Endocrinologist involvement.
  • High-risk pregnancy clinic because diabetes ↑ complications for mother and baby.

Contraception

  • Appropriate contraception if time is required to optimize diabetes control before conception.

Key Point: Optimal Sugar Control Before Pregnancy

  • Essential to decrease complications at conception and during pregnancy.

Pregnancy Outcomes

  • Similar for Type 1 and Type 2.
  • Type 1 has more maternal risks due to longer duration of diabetes.

Most Important Numbers (Must Mention)

  • Pre-pregnancy HbA1c targets:
    • Type 1: <7%
    • Type 2: <6%
  • Must tell patient these numbers (case may give HbA1c 9 → needs improvement before pregnancy).

Complications of High HbA1c at Conception

  • Miscarriage
  • Fetal malformation
  • Other complications
  • High HbA1c also associated with:
    • Preeclampsia
    • Preterm delivery
    • Perinatal mortality

Mnemonic Mentioned

  • Baby complications = “Bs”
  • Maternal complications = “Ps”

Second Key Point: Screen for Diabetic Complications

  • Chronic kidney disease
  • Fundoscopy: retinopathy (some types worsen in pregnancy)
  • Gastroparesis
  • Autonomic neuropathy
  • Macrovascular disease
  • Other autoimmune conditions associated with diabetes → particularly thyroid problems
    • (Celiac mentioned but “at least thyroid” considered)

Once Sugar Is Controlled and Pregnancy Achieved

Key Point

  • High-dose folic acid for women with pre-pregnancy diabetes.

General Antenatal Advice

  • Stop smoking
  • Limit alcohol
  • Diet and exercise → more critical in diabetes
  • Weight control important
  • Healthy diet (not only avoidance)
  • Regular exercise for sugar control
  • Rest similar to usual antenatal counselling

Management of Diabetes During Pregnancy

Applies to both pre-existing diabetes and GDM.

Medications

  • Traditionally insulin preferred.
  • Previously metformin contraindicated, but guidelines changing:
    • Metformin can be continued in pregnancy.
  • Choice between insulin vs metformin is individualized.
  • Stop all other agents except:
    • Insulin (first line)
    • Metformin (second line)
  • Stop: DPP-4, SGLT-2, GLP, others.

Insulin Regimen

  • Basal–bolus insulin preferred.

Multidisciplinary Team

  • Endocrinologist/diabetes physician
  • Diabetes educator
  • Dietitian
  • Weight optimization pre-pregnancy + weight monitoring in pregnancy

Contraindications to Pregnancy

  • Poorly controlled sugar
  • Retinopathies (depends on severity)
  • Nephropathy (depends on severity)
  • Macrovascular disease (type and severity need assessment)

First Visit in Pregnancy (Diabetic Patient)

  • Refer to high-risk pregnancy/diabetic clinic
  • Routine antenatal screening
  • Advice about nausea and vomiting; diabetic women may have more hyperemesis
  • Self-blood glucose monitoring: at least 4×/day
    • 1 fasting
    • 3 post-prandial (after each meal)

Other Advice

  • Avoid hyperglycemia
  • Adequate exercise
  • Dietitian involvement

HbA1c in Pregnancy

  • Check at first visit; repeat every 4–6 weeks
  • Pre-pregnancy HbA1c 7 = equivalent to 6.5 in first trimester
  • HbA1c interpretation challenging in pregnancy

Aspirin

  • Yes, consider aspirin.
  • Pre-existing diabetes = high-risk factor for preeclampsia → start aspirin.
  • No need to state dose

Other Antenatal Care

  • Screen for genetic disorders (Down syndrome)
  • Higher frequency of visits (especially 3rd trimester)
  • Obstetrician + diabetes physician involvement
  • Fundoscopy already discussed

Ultrasounds

  • Routine:
    • Nuchal translucency
    • Anatomy scan at 20 weeks
  • Additional scan: fetal echocardiogram at 24 weeks (due to cardiac malformation risk)

Delivery Planning

Main Concern

  • Avoid big baby → difficult delivery and complications.

Timing

  • If optimal sugar control and no complications → deliver around 38 weeks.
  • Do not allow up to 40 weeks due to ↑ fetal size.
  • If macrosomia detected → consider delivery before 38 weeks.

If Delivering Before 35 Weeks

  • Give betamethasone (because of RDS risk).

Mode of Delivery

  • If fetal weight <90th percentile → vaginal delivery possible.
  • If very large baby → consider elective caesarean.

Post-Delivery

  • Insulin requirements drop dramatically.
  • Management returns to pre-pregnancy regimen and adjusted according to sugar levels.

Summary (Therapeutic Guideline Highlights)

  • Optimize sugar before pregnancy
  • Screen and optimize diabetic complications before pregnancy
  • Fundoscopy; micro/macrovascular review
  • Plan pregnancy + optimize diabetes before conception
  • Start high-dose folic acid 1 month before pregnancy
  • Refer to multidisciplinary team
  • Do extra scans (especially fetal echo)
  • Start aspirin unless contraindicated; calcium may be used
  • Monitor for complications

HISTORY TAKING STRUCTURE – PRE-EXISTING (Type 1 / Type 2) DIABETES ANTENATAL COUNSELLING

1. Start of History Taking

  • Open-ended question.
  • Ask if it is a planned pregnancy.
  • Congratulate her.

2. Extra Component: Diabetes History

Stick to the 4 main questions taught in all workshops:

1) When were you diagnosed with diabetes?

2) Treatment

  • What treatment are you on?
  • Which medication/insulin?
  • Ask about compliance.

3) Regular follow-ups

  • Do you have regular follow-ups with your GP and your specialist?

4) Blood sugar check

  • When was your last blood sugar level check and was it normal?
  • Key point: HbA1c < 7%.
  • Ask if she knows her HbA1c.

Complications Screening

Complications are important because you must screen even if she doesn’t mention them.

  • Retinopathy → Any blurring of vision / eye problems?
  • Neuropathy → Any numbness / pins and needles in the legs?
  • Macrovascular (most concerning: cardiovascular) → Any history of chest pain?

Key Points to Ask

  • Medication the lady is taking
  • Regular follow-ups
  • Whether sugar is controlled
  • Complications (must check)

3. Continue with Routine Antenatal Counselling Structure (Five Ps)

1) Period

  • When was the last one?
  • Is it regular?

2) Pregnancy Symptoms

  • Tired?
  • Vomiting? (looking for hyperemesis)

3) Partner History

  • Are you in a stable relationship?
  • Is he supportive?
  • How are things at home?
  • Any STIs?

4) Previous Pregnancies

  • Look for complications during pregnancy.
  • Look for complications around delivery.
  • Could have had C-section or hard delivery due to large baby.
  • Ask CSD.

5) Past Medical History / Medications / Family History

  • Ask all usual questions.

4. Lifestyle (more important in this case)

Give priority to diet and exercise (because this is diabetes counselling).

  • Tell me, are you following a healthy diet?
  • Tell me more about your diet.
  • Are you doing any exercise?

Then ask:

  • Alcohol
  • Drugs
  • Coffee
  • Anything else in your structure

5. Psychological Assessment

  • How’s your mood?
  • How are you coping with pregnancy?

COUNSELLING KEY POINTS

Divide into three levels:

A. Pre-pregnancy / Before Pregnancy

1) HbA1c

  • Need HbA1c < 7% in type 1
  • 6% in type 2
  • Need strict blood sugar control before pregnancy

2) Check for complications

  • Chronic kidney disease → eGFR + urine ACR (proteins)
  • Optometrist for eyes
  • Examine for sensory neuropathies
  • Thyroid tests (hypothyroidism + autoimmune association)

3) Routine antenatal screening done before pregnancy

Many women present while planning pregnancy.

  • Full body examination (anemia)
  • Urine MCS (asymptomatic bacteriuria)
  • Rubella IgG (may vaccinate now)
  • Check STIs
    Routine antenatal screening before she falls pregnant because not all problems have answers during pregnancy.

B. Once She Falls Pregnant

1) High-dose folic acid (KEY POINT)

    • Start one month before pregnancy
    • Continue three months in pregnancy
    • Emphasized not to forget this.

2) Regular monitoring of blood sugar

  • 4 times/day once pregnant

3) Multidisciplinary team

  • High-risk pregnancy clinic or not
  • Endocrinologist
  • Diabetic educator
  • Dietician
  • Midwives
  • Obstetricians
  • GP

4) Aspirin throughout pregnancy

5) Look for malformations

Routine testing:

  • Down syndrome screening
    • NIPT
    • NT scan
  • Morph scan
  • Fetal echocardiogram in second trimester (around 24 weeks)

6) Monitor blood sugar and HbA1c throughout pregnancy

  • Need strict control
  • Adjust/change medication if needed (usually already insulin)
  • Most changes ideally done before pregnancy

C. Delivery Planning

  • Plan based on baby size and weight
  • Too early to decide now
  • Check closer to delivery
  • Around 38 weeks if normal-sized baby
  • Earlier if large baby
  • If normal-sized → can try NVD
  • If large baby → prefer C-section
  • Decision made together closer to delivery

D. Postpartum

  • After delivery, insulin requirement drops
  • Adjust medication doses
  • Do not continue same insulin dose as before delivery

Your next patient is a 21 year old teacher who is a known case of Diabetes type 1. She is currently using Novorapid and Lantus. Her recent HbA1C is 9%. She has come to you today to discuss about planning for pregnancy.

Task:

• Take history to explore reasons for her poor diabetes control

• Counsel regarding management plan including postnatal period

HISTORY

Opening

  • Start with open-ended question: “How can I help you today?”
  • Patient says she has diabetes type 1, on insulin, and is planning pregnancy.

Acknowledgement / Appreciation

  • “Thank you for coming in today.”
  • “It is important to plan for pregnancy in diabetes because we can prevent complications and risks.”
  • “I really appreciate that you’ve come in today, Jenny.”

Focused Diabetes History

1. Diagnosis

  • “When were you diagnosed with di

2. Treatment

  • “What treatment are you currently using?” (Insulin)

3. Key points to explore reasons for poor control

a. Compliance

  • “Are you compliant? Do you use it regularly?”

b. Side effects

  • “Have you ever had any side effects and drops in your blood sugar level?”
    • Looking for hypos.

c. Technique

  • “Did they teach you the correct technique of injection?”

d. Follow-ups

  • “Are you having your regular follow-ups with your GP and your specialist?”
    • (HbA1c already given as 9%)

f. Lifestyle

  • Exercise: “Are you exercising?”

Additional Antenatal History (if extra time)

  • Switch on 5 Ps:
    • Past medical history & medications
    • Family history
    • Lifestyle
    • Psychological component
    • (As usual)
  • But priority = explore reasons for uncontrolled sugar.

COUNSELLING

Opening / Positive Environment

  • Appreciate again:
    • “I really appreciate that you’ve come to discuss your diabetes and blood sugar levels before falling pregnant.”

Explaining Complications (Sensitively)

  • Diabetes can increase the risk of pregnancy complications for mother and baby.
  • But if we control sugar before pregnancy and during pregnancy, we can reduce the risk.

Mother – the P’s

  • Polyhydramnios
  • Preeclampsia
  • Placental abruption
  • Preterm labour
  • Premature rupture of membranes

Baby – the B’s

  • Big baby
  • Birth defects
  • Hypoglycemia after delivery
  • Breathing problems

Explaining gently

  • Risk of high BP (preeclampsia)
  • Increased fluid
  • Earlier labour / breaking water early
  • Larger babies → delivery challenges
  • Need for extra scans
  • Risk of hypoglycemia and breathing issues after delivery

PRE-PREGNANCY MANAGEMENT

1. Avoid/Postpone Pregnancy

  • Must avoid pregnancy for now until sugar controlled.
  • HbA1c target <7%.
  • Adjust insulin dose.
  • Address compliance issues (e.g., pain with injections).
  • Can provide contraception until control achieved.
  • “No pregnancy before we have this under control.”

2. Screen for Complications

  • Eye exam (retinopathy)
  • Nerve exam (sensory neuropathy)
  • Kidney blood tests (eGFR, urinary ACR)
  • Heart examination

3. Routine Antenatal Screening

  • Full blood examination (treat anemia)
  • Urine test (treat infections)
  • Check immunity to rubella (vaccinate if not immune)
  • STI screening (treat if positive)

ONCE SHE FALLS PREGNANT

Key Points

1. Start Folic Acid

  • High-dose folic acid
  • Start one month before pregnancy and continue for first three months.

2. Start Aspirin

  • To reduce risk of high BP and preeclampsia.

3. Diabetes Management (in pregnancy)

  • Multidisciplinary team: endocrinologist, dietician, diabetic educator.
  • Adjust insulin dose as needed.
  • Blood glucose monitoring 4 times/day.
  • Regular HbA1c throughout pregnancy.

Extra investigations

  • Down syndrome screening
    • Either NIPT or combined test
  • Morphology scan at 20 weeks
  • Fetal echocardiogram at ~24 weeks

And routine counselling if time allows

DELIVERY PLANNING

Plan delivery based on:

  • Size of the baby

If normal-sized baby

  • Can try normal vaginal delivery
  • Plan around 38 weeks (not beyond)

If large baby

  • Prefer caesarean section
  • Delivery earlier than 38 weeks

POSTPARTUM (POST-DELIVERY)

Mother

  • Insulin dose will drop → adjust dose
  • Continue sugar checks

Baby

  • Monitor for complications:
    • Low blood sugar
    • Breathing problems
  • Close monitoring
  • Prefer delivery in tertiary hospital (need ICU support)

ROUTINE COUNSELLING

(As per lecture: keep brief, bullet-point style)

  • Healthy diet
  • Exercise (moderate)
  • Monitor weight
    • Excess maternal weight gain → check for big baby or polyhydramnios
  • Routine antenatal steps if time allows:
    • Iodine supplement
    • Vaccinations
    • Avoid certain foods
    • Avoid alcohol and smoking
    • Other routine elements if time permits

SPECIAL COUNSELLING SCENARIO (Non-compliance with injections)

  • Emphasize importance of sugar control
  • Offer options:
    • Pre-mixed insulin (twice daily)
    • Insulin pump (patch with continuous insulin and monitoring)

SUMMARY REMINDER FROM LECTURE

  • Routine steps still important
  • But key points must be covered first
  • Keep counselling bullet-pointed
  • “Strike the balance — time is limited.”

🔬 Expert Review & Enhancements

Reviewed by: clinical-documentation-expert (Australian clinical supervisor, O&G documentation standards) · fact-checked against the irStudy medical textbook RAG index.

✏️ Corrections

✏️ The note gives pre-pregnancy HbA1c targets as 'Type 1 <7%, Type 2 <6%'. This type-1-vs-type-2 split is not the Australian standard and the note itself contradicts it by saying the two types are managed the same.
Per ADIPS/RANZCOG, aim for a single pre-conception target of HbA1c <=6.5% (approx 48 mmol/mol) for all women with pre-existing diabetes, provided it can be achieved without significant hypoglycaemia. Higher risk of congenital malformation rises progressively above this.
✏️ 'High-dose folic acid' is recommended but the dose is not stated, and iodine is only mentioned in passing.
Specify folic acid 5 mg daily (high dose, indicated in pre-existing diabetes) from at least 1 month pre-conception until the end of the first trimester, PLUS iodine 150 microgram daily throughout - both are standard Australian preconception/antenatal supplementation.
✏️ Aspirin for pre-eclampsia prevention is advised with 'no need to state dose', and betamethasone timing lacks the diabetes-specific caution.
Document low-dose aspirin 100-150 mg nocte from 12-16 weeks (continued to ~36 weeks) for pre-eclampsia prophylaxis in this high-risk woman. When giving antenatal corticosteroids (betamethasone) for anticipated preterm birth, note they cause maternal hyperglycaemia requiring intensified glucose monitoring and often temporary insulin adjustment.

⭐ Suggested Enhancements

⭐ Add the crucial pre-conception medication review beyond diabetes agents: stop/switch teratogenic or not-recommended drugs (ACE inhibitors/ARBs, statins, most SGLT2/GLP-1/DPP-4 agents) to pregnancy-appropriate alternatives before conception - the note only addresses glucose-lowering agents.
⭐ Use Australian spelling and terminology consistently (anaemia not anemia, paediatric, oedema) and quantify glucose self-monitoring targets (e.g. fasting <5.0-5.3 mmol/L, 1-hour post-prandial <7.4 mmol/L, 2-hour <6.7 mmol/L per common Australian targets) rather than only 'avoid hyperglycaemia'.
⭐ Add postnatal contraception, breastfeeding encouragement (reduces offspring diabetes risk) and neonatal hypoglycaemia monitoring to the postpartum section, which currently only mentions the insulin-requirement drop.
⭐ Reference ADIPS pre-existing diabetes and pregnancy guidelines, RANZCOG, and eTG (diabetes in pregnancy) as metadata; note calcium/vitamin D where dietary intake is low.
