Vaginal Birth After Caesarean (VBAC) Counselling

Source: Dr. Amir workshop — obstetrics / class 8 (Aug 2026 drop) · Specialty: obstetrics_gynaecology

VBAC( vaginal birth after C- Section) counselling

It’s more of a yes rather than no

Benefits of VBAC

  • Shorter hospital stay.
  • Faster recovery.
  • Avoidance of a major surgery (cesarean section).
  • With two cesarean sections, you say no to vaginal deliveries.
  • Reduced risk of maternal mortality.
  • Increased likelihood of breastfeeding.

Maternal Risks

  • Biggest concern: Risk of uterine rupture (due to uterine scar).
  • Complications from vaginal birth:
    • Cuts
    • Damages
    • Incontinence
    • Anal sphincter injury

Fetal / Neonatal Points

Benefits

  • Increased likelihood of breastfeeding.

Risks

  • Higher risk of mortality.
  • Risk of hypoxic encephalopathy.

Assessment

Not a simple yes/no situation.

Factors Favoring likelihood VBAC

  • Previous vaginal birth.
  • Previous cesarean due to malpresentation.
    Key history question: “Why did you have a cesarean section last time?”
  • Spontaneous onset of labor last time.
  • Higher bishop score
  • Uncomplicated low-risk pregnancy.

Factors Reducing Likelihood of VBAC

  • No previous vaginal birth
  • Previous cesarean for
  • Dystocia or failure to progress/arrest in first stage.
  • Failed induction (oxytocin → emergency C-section).
  • Cephalopelvic disproportion.
  • Hypertensive disorders complicating pregnancy
  • Obesity.
  • Advanced maternal age.
  • Current fetal macrosomia of > 4Kg
  • Diabetes (both gestational or pre existing)

Simple definition of a good candidate for VBAC:

  • Uncomplicated low-risk pregnancy.

Contraindications to Planned VBAC

  • Previous uterine rupture.
  • Classical cesarean cut (vertical).
  • Any other contraindication to vaginal birth (e.g., placenta previa).

Important point:
 Even if planning a VBAC, complications (fetal distress, arrest in labor, etc.) may require a cesarean section.

Clinical History

  • Dates of previous cesarean section and deliveries.
  • Type of incision:
    • Ask if it was horizontal cut or vertical cut.
  • Check for:
    • History of uterine rupture.
    • Any surgical procedures on uterus.

Woman's decision will be influenced by many factors including:

  • Previous experience of a vaginal births
  • Feelings about previous CS
  • Family considerations including recovery times
  • Likelihood of VBAC (refer to Section 3 Likelihood of VBAC)
  • Unique and individual perception of risk
  • A sense that labour and vaginal birth may be empowering
  • Desire for partner's involvement
  • Scheduling convenience
  • Desire to avoid pain of labour
  • Desire for sterilisation
  • Cultural identity, values and beliefs

Trial of Labor in VBAC

Risks

  • Uterine rupture.
  • May need emergency C-section.
    • Emergency C-section is worse than planned elective C-section.
  • Instrumental deliveries.

Benefits

  • Shorter hospital stay.
  • Increased likelihood of future vaginal deliveries.

Elective Cesarean Section: Risks and Benefits

  • Surgical complications.
  • Risk of placenta previa/accreta in future pregnancies.

Risk to baby in trial of labor

  • Slight increase in risk of stillbirth.

Benefit

  • Lower risk of transient respiratory morbidity.

Most Common Indications for Cesarean Section

  • Previous cesarean section.
  • Labor dystocia.
  • Malpresentation.
  • Non-reassuring fetal status.

Counseling Point

  • Even if attempting VBAC, if problems arise, a C-section may be needed.

Absolute Contraindications of VBAC

  • Two or more previous cesarean sections.
  • Previous classical, inverted T or J incision
  • History of uterine rupture.
  • Myomectomy that breached the uterine cavity.
  • Medical/obstetric reason for a repeat cesarean section.

The success rate of TOL ranges from 55-85% for those attempting normal birth. Predictors for successful TOL include:

  • Previous vaginal delivery (85-90% success rate)
  • Spontaneous labour
  • Dilatation of > 4 cm on admission
  • Previous C/S for malpresentation/non-reassuring CT
  • > 18 months from previous C/S
  • BMI < 30
  • Birth weight <4000g
  • Caucasian
  • < 40 years
  • > 150 cm
  • Female infant.

Follow-Up Plan

26 weeks

  • The woman is reviewed by an obstetrician or midwife VBAC champion and all options reviewed and questions answered.
  • This is documented on the Women's Antenatal NBAC Assessment form.

32 weeks

  • If the placenta was low-lying at the time of the 20 weeks ultrasound it should be repeated at 32 weeks to exclude placenta previa.

36 weeks

  • A discussion should take place with women booked for elective C/S, regarding mode of birth, should they labour before their C/s date. A plan should then be documented in the notes. Consideration should be given to corticosteroids if C/S is planned for before 39 weeks.

41 weeks

  • A discussion should be had on expectant management. Membrane sweep can be offered and possible induction discussed.

Monitoring

  • During labor, monitor for signs of uterine rupture (main complication).

Conclusion

Success rates for women attempting a VBAC is approximately 63%

Benefits of vaginal birth after caesarean:

  • Earlier initiation of breastfeeding and enhanced mother- infant bonding
  • Earlier mobilisation for the mother and resumption of normal activities
  • Reduced blood loss
  • Reduced risk of deep vein thrombosis
  • Reduced risk of readmission to hospital
  • Reduce risk of placenta praevia, placenta accreta, bowel adhesions, risk of organ damage and/or cesarean section in future pregnancies
  • Shorter length of hospital stay

Risks of attempting VBAC

  • May end up needing an urgent cesarean section.
  • Risk of uterine rupture.

Contraindications

  • History of uterine rupture.
  • Classical cut.
  • Surgeries to the womb.

Main Approach

  • Take history, especially reason for previous cesarean.
  • Look for contraindications.
  • Closer to delivery, make final decision based on what the lady wants.

If VBAC is Chosen

  • Wait for spontaneous labor.
  • If it starts → proceed.
  • If induction needed → monitor progress.
  • If labor progresses well → continue.
  • If anything goes wrong → consider urgent cesarean section.

If Your next patient is a 25 year old lady who has come for antenatal visit. She has a history of a previous C/S and is requesting a vaginal birth for this pregnancy.

• Task:

• Take history from the lady

• Counsel the patient on her request

1. INTRO OPENING / AGENDA EXPLORATION

  • Start with open-ended question: “How can I help you today?”
  • Patient says: previous pregnancy had a cesarean section; this time I would like to have a vaginal delivery.
  • Explore her concern before addressing it:
    • “Can I know why you prefer to have a vaginal delivery this time?”
    • “Is there any concern you have about a cesarean section or delivery that you'd like to discuss with me?”
  • If an agenda appears (e.g., past complication), address it later in counseling.

2. HISTORY TAKING

A. OB/Antenatal History

  • Follow usual structure regardless of gestational age:
    • Have you done your bloods?
    • Dating scan?
    • Down syndrome screening?
    • If near mid-pregnancy: morphology scan to check placenta position.
    • Continue up to whatever gestational age the case gives.

B. Previous Pregnancy / Previous Cesarean

Key Questions:

  1. Reason for previous cesarean section?
    • e.g., labor took too long, baby not doing well.
  2. Type of cesarean section scar.
    • Ask: Is the scar on your stomach vertical or horizontal?
  3. Any previous surgeries on the womb (e.g., myomectomy).
  4. Any history of rupture of the womb.

C. Symptoms (optional, early pregnancy)

  • Vaginal bleeding?
  • Abdominal pain?
  • Not very relevant, but can ask if time allows.

D. Antenatal extras if time

  • Partner support.
  • Psychological component.
  • Past medical history and so on.

3. KEY POINT FROM HISTORY

  • We cannot do a VBAC if she previously had a classical cesarean section (vertical cut).

4. COUNSELING

A. Acknowledgement / Support

  • Acknowledge preference: she prefers vaginal delivery.
  • “I will support you and be here for u no matter what decision you make.”
  • I want to talk about benefits and risks of vaginal birth after cesarean.

5. BENEFITS OF VBAC (good thing first)

  1. Shorter hospital stay.
  2. Faster recovery – on feet faster.
  3. Avoiding a major surgery and its complications:
    • Infections
    • Bleeding
    • Anesthesiology complications
    • Injury to abdominal organs
  4. Decreases risk of mortality.
  5. Decreases risk of clots in the leg.
  6. Increases success rate of breastfeeding.

6. RISKS OF VBAC

Main risk (write big, write red):

  1. Uterine rupture at the site of the cesarean scar.
    • “Uterus might burst in your stomach.”
  2. There is chance of an Emergency cesarean section if things don’t go as planned.
    • Emergency surgery is riskier.
  3. Risks of vaginal delivery:
    • Trauma to private parts
    • Tears or cuts
    • Possible incontinence / leak urine
    • Prolapse
    • Increased risk of injury and mortality of the baby

7. CONTRAINDICATIONS TO VBAC

  1. There are problems during pregnancy or delivery that may prevent a vaginal delivery.

Examples:

  • If later scan shows placenta very close to the outlet of the womb → no chance for vaginal delivery.
  • If there is Arrest in labor ,contractions and cervical change stop → urgent cesarean section.
  • Fetal distress → like if u tell me baby is not kicking and I check and there is distress to the baby, we may proceed to cesarean section.

Specific contraindications:

  1. More than two cesarean sections.
  2. Classical (old method) cesarean section.
  3. History of uterine rupture.

All listed are treated as absolute contraindications.

8. FINAL DISCUSSION / PLAN

  • Cases change frequently; agendas may appear—address them if present.
  • It is still early to make a final decision.
  • Plan:
    • We usually have Another chat at 26 weeks.
    • Final plan around 36 weeks.
  • Provide reading material on risks and benefits.
  • Encourage discussion with partner.
  • “I’m here to support you in making an informed decision.”

9. IF PROCEEDING TO VBAC (EXTRA IF TIME)

  • Close monitoring during trial of labor.
    • Monitor for uterine rupture.
    • Monitor baby’s heart rate for distress.
  • If complications arise → may need emergency cesarean section.
  • Prepare patient emotionally: don’t get upset if cesarean becomes necessary.

10. ANTENATAL FOLLOW-UP (REGULAR COUNSELING)

  • Further investigations:
    • Ultrasound at 20 weeks
    • Sweet drink test
    • Swab test
  • Closer to delivery: vaccination
  • Ongoing support.

🔬 Expert Review & Enhancements

Reviewed by: clinical-documentation-expert (Australian clinical supervisor, O&G documentation standards) · fact-checked against the irStudy medical textbook RAG index.

✏️ Corrections

✏️ The notes describe uterine rupture as the main risk but never quantify it, which is essential for valid informed consent.
State the absolute figures per RANZCOG: for a planned VBAC after one lower-segment caesarean the risk of uterine rupture is approximately 0.5% (about 1 in 200), rising with induction/augmentation. Overall planned VBAC success is roughly 60-80%. Quantified risk must be documented for informed consent (AHPRA).
✏️ Predictors listed include 'Caucasian' and 'Female infant', taken from older US (MFMU) nomograms.
These ethnicity/sex-based predictors are derived from a US population and are of limited and contested applicability in Australian practice; RANZCOG counselling emphasises obstetric history (prior vaginal birth, reason for prior CS, spontaneous labour, inter-delivery interval). De-emphasise ethnicity/infant-sex predictors.
✏️ Induction of labour is described neutrally ('If induction needed, monitor progress').
Add the safety caveat: prostaglandin induction in a woman with a uterine scar increases uterine rupture risk and is generally avoided; if induction is required, mechanical methods and specialist supervision are preferred. This is a key Australian VBAC safety point that is currently missing.

⭐ Suggested Enhancements

⭐ Add the intrapartum safety bundle for planned VBAC: birth in a facility with immediate access to caesarean, continuous CTG monitoring in labour, IV access and group-and-hold, and vigilance for rupture signs (scar pain, fetal bradycardia, loss of contractions, haematuria).
⭐ Quantify the alternative (elective repeat caesarean) risks too - accreta/praevia and adhesions rising with each caesarean - so the pros/cons are numerically balanced.
⭐ Explicitly document the contraindication logic from history: classical/inverted-T/J incision, previous uterine rupture, or myomectomy breaching the cavity = planned VBAC contraindicated.
⭐ Reference the RANZCOG 'Birth after previous caesarean section' statement in the teaching notes and record the counselling on the antenatal VBAC assessment form (fix the OCR typo 'NBAC' -> 'VBAC').
⭐ Add a shared-decision closing plan with reading material, partner involvement, and a documented final decision review at ~36 weeks.
