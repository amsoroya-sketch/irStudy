Post-Coital Bleeding - History, Differentials and Cervical Ectropion

Source: Dr. Amir workshop — gynae / 2. intercourse and cervical problems (Aug 2026 drop) · Specialty: obstetrics_gynaecology

POST-COITAL BLEEDING —

Your next patient is a 35 year old lady who has presented to your general practice complaining of post coital bleeding.

Task:

  • Take history from the patient
  • Physical examination will appear on the screen
  • Explain your diagnosis and differentials to the patient

Differential List for the Exam

  1. Cervical problems
    • Cervical cancers
    • Cervical ectropion
    • Cervical polyps
    • Cervicitis
  2. Endometrial cancer
  3. Endometrial polyps + Fibroids
  4. Infections (PID/STIs/vaginal infections)
  5. Atrophic vaginitis (lactational, menopausal, premature ovarian failure)
  6. Traumatic causes
    • Violent sex
    • Lacerations after delivery, episiotomy
    • Direct trauma
  7. Dermatological conditions
    • Dermatitis, lichen planus, lichen sclerosus
  8. IUDs and other contraceptions

3. HISTORY STRUCTURE

A. Open-ended Question

  • “How can I help you today?”
  • “Can you tell me more about your bleeding?”

B. Address Concern

  • Reassurance statement.

4. EXPLORE THE BLEEDING

A. Timing Box

  • How many episodes of bleeding?
  • How long has this been happening?
  • Is it getting worse?

B. Characteristics of Bleeding (CCVO)

  • Color: bright or dark
  • Contents: clots, discharge, tissue
  • Volume: number of pads
  • Are pads fully soaked?
  • Odour (thinking of PID)

C. Effect on Life

  • How is this affecting you/your life/relationships/sexual activity?

5. FIVE Ps

1. Period

  • LMP
  • Excessive bleeding?
  • Heavy bleeding?
  • Excessive pain during periods (menorrhagia + dysmenorrhea)
  • Any pain or bleeding between periods?

2. Partner

  • Are you sexually active?
  • Ask post-coital bleeding (case)
  • Ask dyspareunia> pain on sex
  • STI structure:
    • Stable relationship?
    • Multiple partners ever?
    • Safe sex? Condoms?
    • Previous STIs?
  • Symptoms: discharge, fever (optional placement)

3. Pills

  • Any contraception?
  • Think especially IUD
  • Also other contraceptions.

4. Pregnancies

  • Any recent delivery?
    • Looking for tears/cuts from delivery causing bleeding on sex
  • Breastfeeding? (atrophic vaginitis)
  • Ask pregnancy history routinely (fits all cases)

5. Pap Smear

  • Are you up to date with cervical screening test?
  • Have u received Gardasil vaccination?

6. DIFFERENTIALS BOX (HISTORY QUESTIONS)

1. Cervical Cancer

  • Unusual/abnormal vaginal discharge?
  • Loss of weight?
  • Loss of appetite?
  • Lumps and bumps?
  • Tiredness?
  • Low back pain?

2. PID / STIs

  • Fever?
  • Vaginal discharge?
  • Abdominal pain?
  • (Sexual history already done)

3. Atrophic Vaginitis

  • Vaginal dryness?

4. Dermatological Conditions

  • Rash/redness on private parts?
  • Any ulcers?
  • Itchiness?

5. Traumatic Causes

  • Can I ask about your relationship?
  • Can i ask a Sensitive question (optional)
  • “Have you ever been subjected to violent sex?”

6. SADMA

  • Smoking (risk factor for cancers)
  • Medications (blood thinners not critical but optional)

Past medical history
Family history

PHYSICAL EXAM FINDINGS

  • Bimanual exam: normal
  • Cervix: ectropion

  • History:
    • Last cervical screening 5 years ago
    • Has not received Gardasil vaccination
  • Supports cervical problem on screening questions.

8. DIAGNOSIS EXPLANATION

  • “Most likely you have a condition called cervical ectropion.”
  • Easy explanation:
    • “The inner layer of the cervix or the mouth of your womb has rolled out and expanded.”

Reasons

  • Bleeding after sex (main feature)
  • She hasn’t done cervical screening for quite a while
  • Exam shows the red ring confirming ectropion

DIFFERENTIALS EXPLANATION TO PATIENT

(Listed exactly as taught)

  • Cervical problems at the beginning, mainly cervical cancer
  • Cervical polyps
  • Endometrial/uterine problems:
    • Endometrial cancers
    • Polyps
    • Fibroids
  • Infections
  • Dermatological conditions
  • Traumatic causes
  • Atrophic vaginitis
  • IUDs and contraceptions

ADDITIONAL EXAM TEACHING

  • Most famous cervix seen in exam = ectropion
  • If ugly, irregular lesion with superficial bleeding → think malignancy → colposcopy

🔬 Expert Review & Enhancements

Reviewed by: clinical-documentation-expert (Australian clinical supervisor, O&G / GP standards) · fact-checked against the irStudy medical textbook RAG index.

✏️ Corrections

✏️ 'Last cervical screening 5 years ago' is presented as supporting the benign ectropion diagnosis, and cervical cancer is listed but not clearly actioned.
A woman with post-coital bleeding who is due/overdue for screening requires a co-test (HPV plus liquid-based cytology together, requested regardless of HPV result), NOT a routine 5-yearly cervical screening test, because she is symptomatic. Post-coital bleeding is a cardinal symptom of cervical cancer and must be actively excluded (speculum inspection +/- colposcopy) before attributing bleeding to ectropion. The case should make explicit that ectropion is a diagnosis of exclusion here.
✏️ The management logic implicit in the case treats ectropion as the answer once seen; contraception counselling is not linked.
Cervical ectropion is usually physiological (oestrogen effect - COCP, pregnancy, adolescence) and asymptomatic ectropion needs only reassurance. If symptomatic (troublesome PCB/discharge) and other causes excluded, options include reviewing the oestrogen-containing contraceptive and, if persistent, cryotherapy/diathermy or silver-nitrate to the ectropion - referral per RANZCOG. This should be stated rather than implying ectropion alone closes the case.

⭐ Suggested Enhancements

⭐ Add mandatory beta-hCG (early pregnancy loss/ectopic can present as bleeding) and note this as a routine first step in any woman of reproductive age with PV bleeding - not currently in the case.
⭐ Explicitly document the STI screen (endocervical/self-collected NAAT for chlamydia and gonorrhoea) since cervicitis/PID and Chlamydia are common causes of PCB, and Chlamydia is a nationally notifiable, PBS-treatable infection.
⭐ Add safety-netting and referral thresholds: refer for colposcopy if the cervix looks irregular/friable/ulcerated, if PCB is persistent, or if the co-test is abnormal - align with RACGP 'red flag' guidance for non-menstrual bleeding = cancer until proven otherwise.
⭐ Add metadata linking Gardasil/HPV vaccination status (already asked) to cervical cancer risk stratification and note the two attached clinical images (ectropion vs suspicious lesion) as teaching material for the diagnosis-explanation step.
