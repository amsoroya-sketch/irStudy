Advanced Maternal Age and Obesity Antenatal Counselling

Source: Dr. Amir workshop — obstetrics / class 1 (Aug 2026 drop) · Specialty: obstetrics_gynaecology

Your next patient is a 40 year old lady who has presented to your general praçtice with a positive home pregnancy test. This is her first antenatal visit.

Task:

• Take history from the patient for 5 minutes

• Physical examination will appear on the screen at 5 minutes

• Counsel the patient

1. OPENING THE CONSULTATION

  • Start sensitively.
  • Reassure first:
    • “Many ladies with a higher age go through normal pregnancies, deliver normal babies.”
  • Then gently introduce risk:
    • “But we do have some concerns in your case because you are in the higher age group and also obese. We’ll take care of you, monitor you more closely, and support you through this pregnancy.”

2. KEY FOCUSED HISTORY

  • Obstetric history (including Is this your first pregnancy? – lecturer uses this for aspirin risk factor count).
  • Medical history: hypertension, diabetes, thrombosis.
  • Medications, supplements.
  • BMI / weight history.
  • Symptoms suggestive of complications.
  • Family history of genetic disorders.
  • Previous GDM or high blood pressure.
  • Any previous scans or tests done.

3. EXPLAINING RISKS – ADVANCED MATERNAL AGE

Always reassure first, then state:

  • “There is a higher risk of morbidities for both you and the baby.”
  • Miscarriage.
  • Chromosomal abnormalities – especially Down syndrome (lecturer’s “most important”).
  • Ectopic pregnancy.
  • Multiple pregnancies.
  • Obstetric complications:
    • Abruption
    • Placenta previa
    • Malpresentation
    • “Predetermined delivery problems”
    • Postpartum haemorrhage
  • Hypertensive disorders.
  • Gestational diabetes.
  • Congenital abnormalities.
  • IUGR.
  • Stillbirth.
  • Embolism.
  • Higher chance of caesarean section.

4. EXPLAINING RISKS – OBESITY (lecturer’s list)

  • Higher risk of:
    • Hypertension
    • Preeclampsia
    • Gestational diabetes
    • Thrombosis
  • Additional points:
    • Higher chance of inconclusive NIPT result.
    • Difficulty with scans (mentions but chooses not to emphasise).
    • Weight gain monitoring is especially important; unexpected excessive gain → “problem with the pregnancy”.

5. EXPLAINING INVESTIGATIONS

A. Down syndrome / genetic testing

  • Key point: Down syndrome screening must be emphasised.
  • For AMA:
    • “Strong recommendation: NIPT instead of combined testing because:
      • Not only Down syndrome risk is increased
      • Other genetic disorders also increased
      • NIPT checks for extra ones
  • For obesity: warn of higher chance of inconclusive NIPT.

B. Early GDM screening

  • Major key point for AMA + obesity.
  • Do it early: 14–16 weeks (end of first trimester).
  • We might even do it again at 26–28 weeks.

C. Growth ultrasounds

  • AMA: extra scans due to IUGR.
  • Every 4 weeks till 28 week
  • Every 2 weeks till 36 week
  • Every week till 40 week
  • Obesity: additional serial scans.
    • 20-week morphology + placenta
    • Extra growth scans (number depends on hospital).

D. Folic acid

  • For obesity:
    • High-dose folic acid (5 mg) (critical)

E. Aspirin for preeclampsia prophylaxis

  • AMA + obesity → several risk factors.
    • Start before 16 weeks.
    • Stop between 34 weeks and delivery.
    • Obesity may require higher dose
  • Must state consultation with obstetrician.

6. MANAGEMENT & FOLLOW-UP PLAN

1. Classification

  • “Because of your age and weight, we will categorise you as a high-risk pregnancy.”

2. Increased visit frequency

  • More frequent antenatal visits than the regular schedule.

3. Testing Plan

  • NIPT (with counselling re: inconclusive result).
  • Early OGTT at 14–16 weeks.
  • Possible repeat OGTT at 26–28 weeks due to obesity.
  • Extra growth ultrasounds throughout pregnancy.
  • 20-week morphology scan.

4. Medications

  • Aspirin prophylaxis (discuss obstetrician input).
  • High-dose folic acid (5 mg) for obesity.

5. Weight monitoring

  • “We’re going to monitor your weight gain carefully.”
  • Explanation: expected weight gain depends on baseline BMI; excessive gain → “problem to investigate.”

6. Delivery planning

  • AMA → Do not allow post-dates.
  • Plan delivery 39–40 weeks maximum.

7. COMMUNICATION TIPS

  • Be sensitive; avoid scaring the patient:
    • “You’re not going to tell this lady: ‘Oh, you’re above 40, we expect complications.’”
  • Reassure before discussing risks.
  • Emphasise:
    • “We’re going to take care of you.”
    • “We’ll be a little bit more careful.”
  • Provide “age-appropriate counselling”.
  • Take more time when explaining genetic testing in AMA.

8. COMMON EXAMINER TRAPS

  • Failing to say “high-dose folic acid” in obesity → “No passing.”
  • Insufficient emphasis on Down syndrome screening in AMA
  • Forgetting early OGTT (14–16 weeks).
  • Forgetting that the patient has multiple reasons for aspirin.
  • Forgetting extra growth scans.
  • AMA is 30-35 BMI and above
  • Talking too negatively or harshly → must counsel sensitively.
  • Not planning delivery before 40 weeks in AMA.
  • Not mentioning higher chance of inconclusive NIPT in obesity.

HISTORY TAKING

1. Introduction Phase

  • Introduce yourself.
  • Begin with an open-ended question (e.g., “How can I help you today?”).
  • Share your plan for the consultation.
  • Congratulate the patient — since she intends to keep the pregnancy and the session involves antenatal counselling.

2. The “Five Ps”

P1 — Period

  • “Tell me, when was your last period?”
  • Ask whether her periods are regular.

P2 — Partner

  • “Tell me about your partner.”
  • Still important to know partner details in antenatal history.

P3 — Previous Pregnancies

  • “Tell me about your previous pregnancies.”
  • Ask about:
    • Whether she has been pregnant before.
    • If first pregnancy (nulliparity), note this is another risk factor for preeclampsia (lecturer’s reminder).
    • If she has had previous pregnancies: ask for the outcome and other relevant details.

P4 — Pills (Not specified here)

P5 — PAP smear/ Cervical Screening History

  • Since she is 40 (advanced maternal age):
    • “When was your last cervical screening (CSD)?”
    • The lecturer explicitly highlights interest in knowing this.

3. Past Medical History & Medication History

  • Specific targeted questions because of AMA + obesity:
    • “Any history of high blood pressure?”
    • “Any history of high blood sugar levels?”

4. Family History

  • “Any family history of medical or genetic conditions?”
  • The lecturer notes this follows an existing structure and is straightforward.

6. Lifestyle History

Include the routine lifestyle components:

  • Smoking
  • Alcohol
  • Drugs
  • Exercise
  • Diet

7. Psychological Assessment

  • “Tell me, how are you coping with the pregnancy news?”
  • “How’s your mood been lately?”
  • “Any problems we have with that?”
    (These are the specific psychological questions indicated.)

Final Note from Lecturer

  • The overall structure remains a regular antenatal structure.
  • No major changes were made for AMA and obesity except:
    • Additional emphasis on querying hypertension and high blood sugar in PMH.
    • Ensuring cervical screening is checked due to age.

PHYSICAL EXAMINATION (PFE)

General Appearance :

pallor

Vital signs → Blood pressure/BMI.

Quick cardiovascular exam → S1/S2 normal? Any murmurs or added sounds?

Urine pregnancy test (confirm) – not a key point.

Urine dipstick → looking for protein.

Do NOT perform pelvic or abdominal exam at this stage.

COUNSELING

1. COUNSELLING FRAMEWORK

Lecturer emphasises: sensitive counselling, limited time, MUST hit key points.

A. Begin with respectful terminology + reassurance

  • “pregnancy above age 40 is called Advanced Maternal Age pregnancy” .
  • Reassure first:
    • “Many ladies above 40 have normal pregnancies and deliver healthy babies.”
  • “having said that, pregnancy at this age has higher risk of complications as compared to younger women.

B. RISKS – Key Points (must be stated)

Lecturer lists them clearly — MUST mention:

  1. Higher risk of miscarriage
  2. Higher risk of genetic disorders
  3. Higher risk of diabetes / high blood sugar in pregnancy
  4. Higher risk of high blood pressure and preeclampsia

2. MANAGEMENT – KEY COUNSELLING POINTS

KEY POINT 1 — Genetic Screening (higher emphasis due to AMA)

  • Because of increased genetic risk, strongly suggest Down syndrome screening.
  • Two options:
    1. Combined test (blood test + ultrasound) → checks only for Down syndrome.
    2. NIPT → blood test, preferred because:
      • More accurate
      • Checks for few other genetic disorders

KEY POINT 2 — we going to do Early Diabetes Screening (due to AMA + obesity)

  • Higher risk due to age + weight.
  • Perform Oral Glucose Tolerance Test (OGTT) / sweet drink test
  • Timing: 14–16 weeks (end of first trimester).

KEY POINT 3 — Preeclampsia Risk & Aspirin

  • Higher risk because of age + obesity.
  • Obstetrician may start aspirin:
    • Begin early pregnancy
    • Continue until third trimester
    • Stop a few weeks before delivery

KEY POINT 4 — Extra Ultrasound Scans

  • We r going to arrange few extra USG just to monitor the growth of the baby. In addition to the 20-week morphology scan, we will arrange 1–2 extra growth scans.

KEY POINT 5 — Referral to High-Risk Pregnancy Clinic

  • Purpose: more frequent visits throughout pregnancy.
  • Simply saying “high-risk clinic” is not enough; must state purpose.

KEY POINT 6 — High-Dose Folic Acid (due to obesity)

  • Because of her high weight, prescribe:
    • 5 mg folic acid daily
    • For the first 3 months
  • Purpose: prevent neural tube defects / fetal defects

(commonly forgotten key point.)

KEY POINT 7 — Close Monitoring of Weight

  • Must monitor weight gain closely because BMI is high.

KEY POINT 8 — Delivery Planning

  • Plan delivery around 39–40 weeks.
  • (Lecturer calls this “least important key point” but still required.)

3. ANTENATAL INVESTIGATIONS (routine)

After main key points, the lecturer adds routine tasks:

  • Full blood examination
  • Blood group & Rh
  • STI screening
  • Rubella, varicella
  • Urine tests
  • Routine antenatal screening profile

4. ROUTINE ANTENATAL COUNSELLING

(Given after the key points)

  • Avoid medications unless approved; tell doctor/pharmacist she is pregnant.
  • Avoid X-ray/CT unless necessary; inform radiology she is pregnant.
  • Diet precautions.
  • Avoid alcohol, drugs, smoking.
  • Exercise guidance.
  • Vaccinations.
  • Supplements:
    • Folic acid (already discussed)
    • Iodine supplement

5. LECTURER’S FINAL WARNINGS

  • AMA + obesity cases share most key points.
  • Students often fail because they mention routine lifestyle advice but forget the key points:
    • NIPT counselling
    • Early OGTT
    • Preeclampsia aspirin prophylaxis
    • Extra scans
    • High-dose folic acid
    • More frequent visits
    • Weight monitoring

🔬 Expert Review & Enhancements

Reviewed by: clinical-documentation-expert (Australian clinical supervisor, high-risk antenatal care) · fact-checked against the irStudy medical textbook RAG index.

✏️ Corrections

✏️ The examiner-traps list states 'AMA is 30-35 BMI and above', conflating advanced maternal age with body mass index.
Advanced Maternal Age (AMA) is defined by AGE, not BMI - conventionally maternal age 35 years or older at expected delivery (with escalating risk beyond 40, as in this 40-year-old patient). BMI 30 and above defines obesity (30-34.9 class I, 35-39.9 class II, 40+ class III). These are two separate risk factors and must not be merged; the statement as written is factually incorrect.
✏️ The genetic-screening counselling says the combined test 'checks only for Down syndrome', used to justify preferring NIPT.
Combined first-trimester screening also generates risk estimates for trisomy 18 and trisomy 13 (and the nuchal translucency scan screens for structural anomalies), not Down syndrome alone. NIPT is preferred in AMA because of higher detection for the common trisomies and fewer false positives, but the reason given ('combined checks only for Down syndrome') is inaccurate.
✏️ Aspirin for pre-eclampsia prophylaxis is described only as 'start early, stop a few weeks before delivery, obesity may require higher dose', without a specific dose.
Per SOMANZ/RANZCOG, low-dose aspirin 100-150 mg once daily at night should commence ideally before 16 weeks (12-16 weeks) in women with risk factors (AMA, obesity, nulliparity all present here) and continue until 36 weeks. Document the dose in full (e.g. '150 mg nocte'); avoid vague 'higher dose' phrasing. Also consider calcium supplementation in women with low dietary calcium intake.

⭐ Suggested Enhancements

⭐ Add early gestational diabetes testing detail per ADIPS: for high-risk women (AMA + obesity) perform early testing at booking (75 g OGTT, or HbA1c/fasting glucose) and, if normal, repeat the 75 g OGTT at 24-28 weeks - align the card's '14-16 weeks' with the recognised ADIPS pathway.
⭐ Add a VTE risk note: obesity plus AMA increases venous thromboembolism risk; document assessment for thromboprophylaxis (mechanical/LMWH) per RANZCOG, especially peripartum - currently absent from the card.
⭐ Quantify high-dose folic acid: folic acid 5 mg daily for the first trimester for BMI over 30 (and continue iodine 150 microgram) - write the full dose and duration to meet safe-prescribing documentation standards.
⭐ Add explicit fetal-surveillance rationale: serial growth ultrasounds for IUGR and stillbirth risk in AMA/obesity, plus planned delivery by 39-40 weeks (avoid post-dates) - link each investigation to the specific risk it mitigates.
⭐ Metadata to add: tag as high-risk pregnancy and cross-link to the Down syndrome/NIPT card and the routine antenatal counselling card to show escalation from average- to high-risk care.
