Severe Postpartum Hypertension

Source: Dr. Amir workshop — obstetrics / class 8 (Aug 2026 drop) · Specialty: obstetrics_gynaecology

POSTPARTUM HYPERTENSION

Your next patient is a 32 year old lady who gave birth 20 minutes ago. The nurse has called you and is concerned of her blood pressure which is 190/100. She had a history of gestational HTN since 39 weeks and had an lOL. She had 250mlit bleeding after delivery.

Task

  • Talk to the midwife and take history
  • Explain the reason of the high blood pressure
  • Explain your management plan

POSSIBLE CAUSES OF POSTPARTUM HYPERTENSION

  1. Pain
  2. Medications
    • Ergotamine
    • NSAIDs (for post delivery analgesics)
  3. Postpartum preeclampsia (The possibility of postpartum preeclampsia should be considered; the signs and symptoms of preeclampsia are first recognized postpartum in approximately 5% of preeclampsia cases, usually within 48 hours of delivery.)
  4. persistence of antepartum or intrapartum hypertension or may be of new onset.
  5. Large volume IV fluids
  6. Neuraxial anesthesia
  7. Loss of pregnancy associated vasodilation after delivery
  8. mobilization of extravascular fluid after delivery

Two key questions to ask the midwife:

  • What medications has she taken?
  • Did she receive IV fluids? How much?

Lab studies to consider for postpartum hypertension:

  • Serum electrolytes
  • Liver chemistries
  • Platelet count
  • creatinine, urine protein for preeclampsia

APPROACH

1. RESUSCITATION / PRIMARY SURVEY (DRSABC)

  • Introduce yourself
  • Identify who you’re talking to
  • Start with open question: “Can you tell me what has happened so far?”
  • Confirm: delivered 20 minutes ago, BP 190/100
  • This BP meets severe hypertension cutoff (≥160 systolic / ≥100 diastolic)

Step-by-step with nurse: DRSABC

  • Is the patient responsive/alert?
  • Send for help: notify obstetric registrar/consultant
  • Airway patent?
  • Breathing?
  • Circulation: TELL ME:
    • Pulse rate
    • Oxygen saturation
    • Respiratory rate
    • Blood pressure checked again (never make decisions on one reading; ask how many times it's been checked)
    • Temperature
  • Insert IV line if not already in

2. INFORMATION GATHERING

Divide into:

  • History
  • Physical Examination

A. HISTORY( Pregnancy related qs, delivery related qs, baby qs)

i. Pregnancy-related questions

  • Tell me more about Obstetric history
  • Any complications during pregnancy:
    • High blood pressure
    • High blood sugar
    • Previous H/O Kidney disease

(Increased BP/HTN at 39 weeks)

  • Did she receive treatment for hypertension?
  • Was she investigated for preeclampsia?
    • Any Protein in urine?
    • Kidney function?
    • LFTs?
  • Previous pregnancies? (Only if yes ask next que)
    • Any complications?
    • Any postpartum high BP?

ii. Delivery-related questions

  • Method/route: NVD or cesarean?
  • Term or preterm?
  • Prolonged labor? Duration?
  • Instruments used?
  • Any cuts?
  • Key special questions:
    1. What medications has she taken?
      • Specifically: ergotamine? NSAIDs?
    2. How much fluid has she received?
  • Is she in pain?
  • Any anxiety or worry?
  • Has the bleeding stopped? (250 mL reported)

iii. Baby

  • How is the baby doing?
  • APGAR score? (>7 reassuring)

iv. Symptoms of pre eclampsia

  • Blurring of vision
  • Headache
  • Dizziness
  • Chest pain
  • Shortness of breath
  • Abdominal pain

B. PHYSICAL EXAMINATION (via midwife)

  • Level of consciousness
  • Any rashes
  • Can u do Fundoscopy (if possible)
  • Abdominal exam:
    • Liver/right upper quadrant tenderness
    • Uterus: firm and contracted?
    • Uterine tenderness?
  • Can u check Reflexes
  • Clonus
  • Consider urine dipstick for protein (will be in investigations)

3. MANAGEMENT

Investigations

  • Bloods:
    • Electrolytes, creatinine, EGFR
    • Urine protein (dipstick or spot urine)
    • Full blood examination
    • Platelets
    • Hemoglobin
    • Liver function test

Treatment Components

1. IV Antihypertensive

  • Required (severe category)
  • Agents: hydralazine or labetalol or other agent
  • Target BP: 140/90

2. Pain relief

  • Paracetamol (IV or oral)
  • Avoid NSAIDs

3. Get help

  • Call consultant

4. Consider magnesium sulfate if

  • New onset non severe HTN with Headache and Blurring of vision
  • for severe hypertension with or without preeclampsia features
  • Purpose: prevent eclampsia
  • To be discussed with consultant

OTHER CONSIDERATIONS

  • Other causes (pheochromocytoma, thyrotoxicosis, kidney disease) are out of context for hypertensive disorders of pregnancy and postpartum, and would only come after all above discussion.

NOTED FINDINGS FROM RECALL

  • Baby delivered 20 minutes ago
  • Bleeding stopped
  • Uterus small and contracted
  • Labor 14 hours
  • No meconium
  • No complications
  • Baby 3.5 kg, APGAR normal
  • Mother O negative
  • Antenatal checks normal
  • No features of preeclampsia
  • Likely linked to pain or medication

🔬 Expert Review & Enhancements

Reviewed by: clinical-documentation-expert (Australian clinical supervisor, O&G documentation standards) · fact-checked against the irStudy medical textbook RAG index.

✏️ Corrections

✏️ The notes define the severe-hypertension cut-off as 'greater than or equal to 160 systolic / greater than or equal to 100 diastolic'.
Per SOMANZ (Society of Obstetric Medicine of Australia and New Zealand) the severe-hypertension threshold requiring urgent treatment is systolic greater than or equal to 160 mmHg OR diastolic greater than or equal to 110 mmHg (not 100). This patient at 190/100 is severe on the systolic criterion; correct the diastolic figure to 110.
✏️ The listed drug causing postpartum hypertension is written as 'ergotamine'.
The relevant obstetric uterotonic is ergometrine (methylergometrine, a component of Syntometrine) given for third-stage/PPH, which causes vasoconstriction and hypertension and is contraindicated in hypertensive women. 'Ergotamine' is the migraine drug and is the wrong agent - correct to ergometrine.
✏️ Magnesium sulfate is mentioned only as 'consider' without dosing, and NSAIDs are listed as a cause but avoidance is not tied to a mechanism.
State the SOMANZ magnesium regimen for severe pre-eclampsia/eclampsia prophylaxis: loading dose 4 g IV over 20 minutes then 1 g/hour infusion. Reinforce that NSAIDs should be AVOIDED postpartum in hypertensive/pre-eclamptic women as they raise blood pressure and impair renal function; use paracetamol for analgesia.

⭐ Suggested Enhancements

⭐ Name Australian first-line acute antihypertensives with routes: IV labetalol, IV hydralazine, or oral/immediate-release nifedipine, titrated to a target of less than 160/110 and aiming 130-150 / 80-100 mmHg - the case gives a 140/90 target without agents' dosing context.
⭐ Emphasise that postpartum pre-eclampsia can present or worsen up to 6 weeks postpartum (about 5% of pre-eclampsia is first recognised postpartum, usually within 48 hours) and mandates a full pre-eclampsia work-up (FBC, LFTs, platelets, creatinine, urine PCR) - already partly present; make HELLP screening explicit.
⭐ Reference the SOMANZ 'Hypertension in Pregnancy' guideline in the teaching notes to anchor Australian thresholds and drug choices.
⭐ Add escalation/communication documentation: activate a MET/obstetric emergency call, notify the obstetric consultant, insert IDC with strict fluid balance, and record repeat BP measurements (never act on a single reading) - reinforces contemporaneous EMR documentation.
⭐ Convert medication lines to ACSQHC-safe formatting (spell out 'units', no trailing zeros) since this note doubles as a documentation exemplar, and use Australian drug names (paracetamol, adrenaline if needed).
