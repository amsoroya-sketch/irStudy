Urinary Frequency (Stress vs Urge Incontinence, Atrophic Vaginitis)

Source: Dr. Amir workshop — gynae / 3. Vulvar problems (Aug 2026 drop) · Specialty: obstetrics_gynaecology

URINARY FREQUENCY

Opening of Case

  • 62-year-old lady
  • Complaint: urinary frequency
  • Take a history
  • Physical examination appears on screen at 6 minutes
  • Explain diagnosis with reasons to the patient

General Points

  • Very old AMC case
  • Understand a good structure and approach
  • Recall usually becomes an incontinence case, but still push yourself to think of differentials
  • Differential thinking especially important because the case changed this year

DIFFERENTIALS (as taught)

  • Infections
  • Diabetes
  • Bladder cancer (red flag)
  • Pelvic mass pressing on bladder

HISTORY TAKING STRUCTURE

1. Open-Ended Question

  • Ask a nice open-ended question
  • Role player often gives a vague, incomplete answer
  • Shows embarrassment → You must use confidentiality statement

Confidentiality Statement

  • “Everything we discuss today is confidential, kept between us… you’re welcome to share your concerns…”

Acknowledge Distress

  • “I understand how stressful, distressing, overwhelming this problem can be…”

2. Explore Frequency

  • Define what she means by “frequent”
  • Patients with urgency or leakage may use the word frequency
  • Polyuria also causes multiple toilet visits
  • Describe question:
    • “Can you describe a little bit more about your problem?”
    • “Are you passing urine more frequently or a larger amount of urine?”
  • If frequency:
    • “How frequent?” Once a day? Twice? Every hour?
    • “Small amount or large amount each time?”

3. Timing Box

  • Duration: “How long has this been happening?”
  • Is it on/off or always there?
  • Worse at certain times? Getting worse?
  • Alleviating/aggravating factors: “What makes it better? What makes it worse?”

4. Ask All Urinary Symptoms Early

Urinary symptoms listed in lecture:

  • Frequency
  • Urgency: “Do you feel a sudden strong urge to pass urine?”
  • Nocturia: “Do you wake up in the middle of the night to pass urine?”
  • Weak stream / dribbling
  • Sensation of incomplete emptying> do u feel u have emptied your bladder incompletely once u r done?
  • Straining: “Do you need to strain and push hard to empty your bladder?”
  • Dysuria
  • Polyuria (already asked earlier)
  • Hematuria can be in urinary symptoms or cancer red flags

DIFFERENTIAL-DRIVEN QUESTIONS

A. UTI (upper + lower)

  • Already have frequency + dysuria
  • Add:
    • Fever
    • Nausea/vomiting
    • Abdominal pain

B. Diabetes

  • Polyuria already asked
  • Ask:
    • Polydipsia: “Have you noticed any increase in your thirst?”
    • Polyphagia: “Any increase in your appetite?”
    • Recurrent infections

C. Cancers (bladder, ovarian)

  • Weight loss
  • Appetite change already asked
  • Lumps/bumps
  • Tiredness
  • Hematuria
  • Pelvic mass → think of pressure on bladder

D. Renal Calculi

  • Pain, hematuria, dysuria already overlap
  • Ask PMH/FH of stones

E. Vulval/Vaginal Conditions

Because urethra opens in vulva:

  • STIs such as herpes
  • Skin infections
  • Dermatitis
  • Ask:
    • Rashes/redness in private parts
    • Vaginal discharge

Atrophic Vaginitis

  • “Do you feel dry or any dryness in your vagina?”

INCONTINENCE SECTION (kept at end in lecture structure)

OB History

  • Number of pregnancies
  • Delivery route
  • Complicated/long deliveries
  • Instrumental deliveries
  • Episiotomy/cuts
  • Big babies

Things increasing Valsalva/intra-abdominal pressure

  • Long-term cough
  • Constipation
  • Heavy lifting occupations

5Ps (Menopausal Version)

  • Period history
  • Last final period
  • Any spotting/bleeding after

Menopausal symptoms

  • Hot flushes
  • Mood and sleep problems
  • Atrophic vaginitis

Partner history

  • Are you sexually active?
  • Ask dyspareunia
  • Ask post-coital bleeding
  • STI questions:
    • Stable relationship?
    • Multiple partners?
    • Condom use?
    • Previous STI diagnosis?

Appeals (HRT)

  • “Are you using or have you ever used HRT?”

Screening

  • Up-to-date with CST
  • Up-to-date with mammogram

SADMA Add-On (clarification from lecturer)

  • Alcohol (acts as diuretic)
  • Medications (e.g., thiazides mentioned)
  • Coffee/tea intake
  • Past medical history & family history

DIAGNOSIS VERSIONS (as taught)

Version 1 – Stress Incontinence

Findings:

  • Leak a few drops on coughing/sneezing/laughing
  • OB history: 4 vaginal deliveries, large babies, instrumental
  • Physical exam (old version):
    • Normal pelvic exam
    • Cough test negative
    • No prolapse

Diagnosis explanation:

  • Condition: Stress incontinence
  • In this condition, u Leak a few drops of urine when you coughing/sneezing/laughing and this is because weakness in the gateway of the bladder and the muscles surrounding this:
    • Pelvic floor muscles
    • Sphincter
  • Reasons:
    • Menopause (hormonal changes → weakness of muscles/tissue)
    • Previous vaginal deliveries injure muscles/gateway

Version 2 – Urge Incontinence

Findings:

  • Sudden urge → rush to toilet → leak urine before reaching
  • Large amount leakage
  • No cough/sneeze leakage
  • No constipation
  • Same 4 vaginal deliveries
  • Menopause years ago

Diagnosis explanation:

  • Condition: Urge incontinence
  • Due to “hyperactive bladder” → muscles overly sensitive
  • in this condition, u feel a sudden strong need to urinate which is because of Strong inappropriate contraction of bladder muscles causes leakage


Version 3 – Atrophic Vaginitis

Findings:

  • Has frequency
  • Does not leak urine
  • Feels dry in vagina
  • Physical exam: thin, dry vaginal walls

Diagnosis explanation:

  • Hormonal changes in menopause → vaginal walls become thin
  • Also affects urinary tubes opening in that area
  • Causes urinary symptoms like frequency/dysuria

MANAGEMENT (as taught)

General

  • Old versions asked management
  • Atrophic vaginitis mgmt already taught many times

Stress & Urge Incontinence

First-line:

  • Physiotherapy
  • Pelvic floor muscle exercises
    • Stress: Kegel exercises
    • Urge: exercises to hold bladder, increase duration and frequency between voids

Pharmacotherapy (Urge only, severe cases)

  • Use medication that causes urinary retention as side effect
  • Mentioned example: oxybutynin
  • Has side effects

Surgical management

  • Exists for both stress and urge incontinence
  • Usually after non-pharmacological methods

End of Case (as per lecture)

  • Easy case
  • Main management: pelvic floor muscle exercises via physiotherapist
  • Urge incontinence: pharmacotherapy if severe
  • Worst-case: surgical options

🔬 Expert Review & Enhancements

Reviewed by: clinical-documentation-expert (Australian clinical supervisor, O&G / AMC OSCE) · fact-checked against the irStudy medical textbook RAG index.

✏️ Corrections

✏️ Urge incontinence pharmacotherapy names only oxybutynin, described as 'medication that causes urinary retention as a side effect', with no caution in an older woman.
First-line for overactive bladder/urge incontinence is bladder training, then an antimuscarinic (oxybutynin, solifenacin) OR the beta-3 agonist mirabegron. Immediate-release oxybutynin has high anticholinergic burden and is a poor choice in the elderly (confusion, falls, dry mouth, constipation, precipitation of retention/glaucoma) - prefer mirabegron or a more selective/extended-release agent in older women. Add topical vaginal oestrogen for postmenopausal urinary/atrophic symptoms.
✏️ No baseline investigations are specified before labelling the diagnosis.
Include urinalysis/MSU (exclude UTI and haematuria - haematuria mandates work-up for bladder cancer), a bladder diary (frequency-volume chart), post-void residual (bladder scan) to exclude retention/overflow, and BSL/HbA1c to exclude diabetes. This grounds the incontinence subtype and excludes the red-flag differentials the case lists.
✏️ American spelling 'Hematuria' is used throughout.
Use Australian spelling: haematuria.

⭐ Suggested Enhancements

⭐ Add explicit red-flag/urgent-referral criteria: visible haematuria, recurrent UTI, pelvic mass, significant post-void residual/voiding difficulty, or suspected fistula (continuous leak) warrant urology/urogynaecology referral and cystoscopy.
⭐ Add conservative first-line lifestyle measures shared by both incontinence types: weight loss, reduce caffeine/alcohol, treat constipation and chronic cough, and refer for supervised pelvic floor muscle training (minimum 3 months) before medication/surgery.
⭐ Add that mixed urinary incontinence is common in a multiparous menopausal woman - treat the predominant symptom first and quantify with a bladder diary rather than assuming a single mechanism.
⭐ Add PBS/eTG anchoring for drug choices (mirabegron, antimuscarinics) and the note that surgery (e.g. mid-urethral sling for stress incontinence) is reserved for failed conservative therapy after urodynamic assessment.
⭐ Add metadata and cross-link to the prolapse PEFE station (prolapse and incontinence co-occur) and the atrophic vaginitis content in the discharge/itchiness stations.
