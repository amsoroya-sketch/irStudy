Pelvic and Systemic Examination for Amenorrhoea (PEFE)

Source: Dr. Amir workshop — gynae / 1. period problems (Aug 2026 drop) · Specialty: obstetrics_gynaecology

PEFE AMENORRHEA

PRIMARY AMENORRHEA

1. General Appearance

  • Look for PCOS features:
    • Do I see Acne
    • Do I see hair on the face
    • BMI / ask if patient is overweight
  • Genetic syndromes:
    • Turner syndrome features:
      • Webbed neck / short neck with extra skin
      • Short stature / short lady

2. Vital Signs

  • Blood pressure
  • Pulse rate
  • Respiratory rate
  • Temperature

3. Organized Sequence (Head-to-Toe)

Key Point: Tanner Staging

  • Ask examiner: “I’d like to know the Tanner staging.”
  • Examiner may ask> How to check:
    • Assess breast development
    • Assess pubic hair growth
  • Stage 1 = no breast development, no hair → no features of puberty

4. Prolactinomas / Pituitary Problems

  • Fundoscopy
  • Visual field (looking for bitemporal hemianopia)

5. Thyroid Examination

  • Ask to perform a thyroid exam

6. Abdominal Examination

  • Look for:
    • Masses
    • Tenderness

7. Pelvic Examination

  • With consent and chaperone

Inspection

  • Bleeding
  • Rashes
  • Swelling or lumps

Discussion Point

  • Most primary amenorrhea cases: patient is a virgin
  • No speculum exam discussed initially , first:

Labia Examination

  • Spread the labia:
    • Check for imperforate hymen
      • If imperforate → may see blood behind it
    • If no imperforate hymen → ask: “Is the hymen intact?”
If Hymen Intact (CRITICAL ERROR)
  • Skip speculum examination
  • Skip bimanual examination
If Hymen NOT Intact
  • Proceed to speculum exam:
    • Vaginal walls
    • Cervix
  • Then bimanual exam

8. Urine Pregnancy Test

  • Depends on history
  • If she has never had sex and hymen is intact → can skip
  • Asking it does no harm

END SUMMARY – PRIMARY AMENORRHEA

  • Key point: Tanner staging
  • Key point: Thyroid exam
  • Key point: Ask about prolactinomas
  • Do NOT proceed to speculum or bimanual exam in a virgin with intact hymen
  • Abdominal exam becomes more significant because pelvic exam cannot be completed

SECONDARY AMENORRHEA

1. General Appearance

  • PCOS features:
    • Hirsutism
    • Acne
    • BMI
  • No genetic syndromes → no webbed neck

2. Vital Signs

  • Blood pressure
  • Pulse rate
  • Temperature

3. Prolactinomas

  • Fundoscopy
  • Visual field

4. Thyroid Examination

  • Ask to perform a thyroid exam

5. Abdominal Examination

  • Masses
  • Tenderness

6. Pelvic Examination

  • With consent and chaperone

Inspection

  • Bleeding
  • Discharge
  • Redness or rashes
  • Swelling or lumps

Speculum Examination

  • Check:
    • Cervix:
      • Bleeding
      • Discharge
      • Abnormal growths
      • Dry cervix
      • Ectropion
    • Vaginal walls:
      • Example: atrophic vaginitis
      • (If in 40 YO can indicate Premature Ovarian Failure)

Bimanual Examination

  • Cervical motion tenderness: positive or negative
  • Uterus:
    • Size
    • Borders regular or not
    • Tender or not
  • Adnexal masses and tenderness

7. Urine Pregnancy Test (Key Point)

  • Most important differential: rule out pregnancy
  • If you don’t ask → you won’t pass

END SUMMARY – SECONDARY AMENORRHEA

  • Complete pelvic exam including speculum and bimanual
  • Urine pregnancy test is essential and key point

END OF AMENORRHEA EXAMINATIONS

✏️ Corrections

✏️ The note advises assessing an imperforate hymen by 'spreading the labia' and that if the 'hymen is intact' a speculum should be skipped, but the logic conflates an intact hymen with imperforate hymen.
An imperforate hymen is a specific obstructing membrane (may show a bluish bulge with haematocolpos behind it), whereas an intact/annular hymen is normal in a virgin. In a virginal adolescent, avoid speculum and bimanual examination regardless; if imperforate hymen with cyclical pain/haematocolpos is suspected, refer for gynaecological assessment rather than attempting internal examination. Clarify the wording so trainees do not equate 'intact hymen' with pathology.
✏️ American spelling 'hemianopia' style terms and general US spellings.
Use Australian spellings (e.g. bitemporal hemianopia is acceptable, but ensure oestrogen, gynaecology, paediatric, tumour throughout accompanying material).

⭐ Suggested Enhancements

⭐ Add that atrophic vaginitis found on speculum in a woman around 40 should prompt hormonal testing for premature ovarian insufficiency (FSH x2, oestradiol) and referral - the note flags this association but omits the confirmatory investigation.
⭐ Add Turner syndrome stigmata beyond webbed neck/short stature (widely spaced nipples, cubitus valgus, high-arched palate, low posterior hairline) and recommend karyotype and cardiac/renal screening if suspected - strengthens the genetic examination component.
⭐ Add explicit note that a urine/serum beta-hCG should still be documented as considered even when skipped (with reason), since ruling out pregnancy is the single most important step in secondary amenorrhoea and a common AMC mark.
