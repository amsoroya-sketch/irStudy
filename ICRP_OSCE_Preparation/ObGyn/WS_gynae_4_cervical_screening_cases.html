Cervical Screening Test (Renewed NCSP) - Counselling and Result Interpretation

Source: Dr. Amir workshop — gynae / 2. intercourse and cervical problems (Aug 2026 drop) · Specialty: obstetrics_gynaecology

CERVICAL SCREENING CASES

General Chat About Cervical Screening

New Cervical Screening Method / Schedule

  • Start at 25 years.
  • Exit testing 70–74 years.
  • Beyond 74: no reason to screen due to life expectancy.
  • Screening every 5 years (previously Pap smear every 2 years).
  • Done for sexually active people.

Definition of sexually active

  • Does NOT require intercourse.
  • Includes lesbians and any sexual activity.
  • Even if not sexually active now but have been before → still screen.
  • Even if lifelong single partner → still screen.
  • These are common misconceptions patients have.

Symptoms vs Screening

  • Screening = asymptomatic patients.
  • If symptoms suggest cervical cancer → NOT screening, different method.
  • Example: 22-year-old with post-coital bleeding still gets tested (different pathway).

Reasoning Behind New Method

Why 5-yearly instead of 2-yearly?

  • HPV → takes >10 years to progress to cervical cancer.
  • 5 years is safe; not missing cancers.

Why start at 25 instead of 18?

  • Cervical cancer rare in younger age group.
  • Screening did not change incidence or death in younger age group.
  • Australia has high HPV vaccination coverage.
  • Screening in younger age group not efficient.

Technique: Liquid Cytology (Reflex Liquid Cytology)

  • Old method: smear on slide + spray.
  • New method: sample placed in a small bottle with liquid → liquid cytology.
  • Called “reflex cytology” because:
    • Lab checks cytology only if HPV positive.
    • If HPV negative → no cytology performed.

Why HPV-focused?

  • Majority of cervical cancers caused by HPV (not all, but major portion).
  • HPV testing more efficient than cytology alone.
  • HPV occurs 10–15 years before cell changes.
  • High-risk strains: 16 and 18.

Primary and Secondary Prevention

  • Primary prevention: HPV vaccination (Gardasil) in teenagers → prevents HPV and cervical cancer.
  • Secondary prevention: Cervical screening.
  • HPV → takes 10–15 years → cell changes → precancer → cancer.
  • Screening picks HPV early → more efficient than looking at cells.

Main Flowchart Overview

  • 100 strains of HPV.

  • Grouped as:
    • 16 and 18 (highest risk)
    • Non-16 and 18

Pathway 1: HPV Negative

  • HPV negative → cytology not done.
  • Tell patient results are perfect.
  • Repeat CST in 5 years.

Pathway 2: HPV 16 and 18 Positive

  • Lab performs reflex liquid cytology, BUT:
  • Regardless of cytology:
    • Normal
    • LSIL
    • HSIL
  • Send for colposcopy.
  • Reason: highest cancer risk.

Pathway 3: HPV Non-16/18 (Lower Risk Types)

At First CST

  • Look at cytology:
    • High-grade changes (HSIL) → colposcopy.
    • Normal or LSIL → wait 12 months, repeat CST #2.

At CST #2 (12 months later)

  • If new HPV 16/18 detected → colposcopy.
  • If HPV cleared → return to 5-yearly.
  • If HPV non-16/18 persists:
    • Cytology high-grade → colposcopy.
    • Cytology normal/LSIL → wait another 12 months, CST #3.

At CST #3 (24 months from initial)

  • Cytology no longer matters.
  • If HPV still present → colposcopy.
  • If HPV cleared → return to 5-yearly.

Special Situations (No Second-Year Wait)

  • Do NOT wait the second year → send earlier for colposcopy if:
    1. Woman ≥50 years
    2. Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander women
    3. Woman overdue ≥2 years at initial screen
  • These patients are high risk or likely to be lost to follow-up.
  • AMC unlikely to ask but still included in lecture.

Practice Cases (Lecture Examples)

Case 1

  • HPV 18 positive + normal cytology → colposcopy.

Case 2

  • First test: non-16/18 + LSIL → wait 1 year.
  • Second test (12 months): non-16/18 + HSIL → colposcopy.
  • If still LSIL → wait another 12 months.
  • Third test: any HPV present (even 18) → colposcopy.

Practical Aspect: Self-Collection

History

  • Initially clinician-collected only.
  • Then limited indications for self-collection.
  • New guidelines: any patient eligible for self-collection.

Purpose

  • Increase accessibility for women uncomfortable with speculum exams.

How to Explain Self-Collection

  • Provide instructions, patient collects sample like inserting tampon.
  • Vaginal sample, not cervical.
  • Less accurate.

Lab Processing for Self-Collection

  • HPV only (not always cytology).
  • No cervical cells present.

Results and Implications

  • HPV negative → return in 5 years.
  • HPV 16/18 → colposcopy.
  • HPV non-16/18 → discuss clinician-collected sample for cytology.
    • If patient refuses → encourage return next year for repeat HPV.

Symptomatic Patients (NOT Screening)

  • Symptoms that raise concern:
    • Post-coital bleeding
    • Unusual vaginal discharge
    • Abnormal vaginal bleeding (pre- or post-menopausal)
    • Weight loss
  • Do a co-test, not a CST.

Co-test Definition

  • Same collection method as CST.
  • Must request HPV + cytology together, even if HPV negative.

Indications

  • Abnormal vaginal bleeding (premenopausal)
  • Post-coital bleeding
  • Post-menopausal bleeding
  • Persistent unexplained vaginal discharge (after ruling out infections)

Colposcopy Explanation to Patient

  • Similar procedure to speculum exam.
  • Uses a colposcope (microscope/binoculars on a stand).
  • Stays outside the body.
  • Special dyes identify abnormal cells.
  • Biopsy may be taken → mild discomfort.
  • Avoid physical activity, intercourse, swimming, baths, spas for 1–2 days post-procedure.

If Abnormal Lesion Seen During Colposcopy

  • May treat at same time.
  • Possible treatments:
    • Cone biopsy
    • Loop excision
    • Laser treatment
    • Cryotherapy (e.g., for warts)

HISTORY-TAKING STRUCTURE FOR ALL CSD CASES

1. Opening

  • Start with an open-ended question: “How can I help you today?”
  • Patient explains she received a mail to do cervical screening and wants to discuss it.
  • Identify specific concern early:
    “Do you have any specific concern you’d like me to address today?”

2. Acknowledge her concern

  • She says: “My mum had a Pap smear — I’m concerned about this new cervical screening.”
  • Tell her you will ask questions first:
    “Is it okay if I ask you a few questions before I explain more about our new method?”

CERVIX-RELATED HISTORY (PRIORITY BEFORE 5Ps)

1. Previous CST

  • “Have you ever done a CST before? Have you ever done a cervical screening before?”

2. Gardasil

  • “Can I know if you have received your Gardasil vaccination?”

3. Personal / family cervical problems

  • “Do you have any personal or family history of cervical problems or possibly cervical cancers?”

4. Symptoms

Go one by one:

  • Unusual / abnormal vaginal discharge?
  • Bleeding after sex?
  • Pain during sex / pain on intercourse?
  • Low back pain?
  • Cancer symptoms: loss of weight, loss of appetite, lumps and bumps, tiredness.

BEGIN 5Ps (ONLY RELEVANT PARTS)

P1 – Periods

  • When was your last menstrual period?
  • Can we do cervical screening if pregnant?
    • (Lecture answer: Yes, in 1st trimester; broom not brush; will not cause harm.)
  • Are the periods regular or irregular?
  • Excessive bleeding or excessive pain?
  • Pain or bleeding between periods?

P2 – Partners (HPV is sexually transmitted)

  • Are you sexually active?
  • Even if no: “Have you ever been sexually active?”
  • Are you in a stable relationship?
  • Ever had multiple partners?
  • Do you practice safe sex / use condoms?
  • Ever been diagnosed with an STI?

P3 – Pills (Contraception)

  • Ask if using any contraception.
  • Important to know about IUD (strings).
  • CST relevance:
    • Most common cause of cervical ectropion = estrogen in COCPs.
    • If she is on COCPs + ectropion → first step is stop the pills before treatment.

P4 – Pregnancies

  • Ask about number of pregnancies.
  • Age of first pregnancy (earlier age = risk factor as explained).
  • Higher number of pregnancies = risk factor (as explained in video).

P5 – Past medical history / social

  • Smoking (risk factor).
  • Alcohol (can be a risk factor).
  • Medications that affect immune system (e.g., steroids).
  • Any past medical history causing immunocompromised state (e.g., leukemia).
    • Important because sometimes immune system clearing HPV affects management.

COUNSELLING EXPLANATION – PAP vs CST

1. Opening

  • Thank and acknowledge:
    “Thank you for coming in to discuss about the cervical screening. I appreciate you coming in today.”

2. Change in Australia

  • “In 2017 we changed the way we screen for cervical cancer in Australia.”
  • And Replaced the Pap smear with the cervical screening test method(CST).
  • Has been 7–8 years, nothing new to panic about.

THE NEW METHOD

1. Who and how often

  • In new method, we Test sexually active ladies from age 25 to 74.
  • We do Screening every 5 years.

2. Comparison with old Pap smear

  • In the Old method: we used to check for cancer cells.
  • But the main difference of New method is we check for a virus called human papillomavirus (HPV).
  • HPV is a sexually transmitted infection.
  • It is the main cause of most (almost all) cervical cancers.

How the new screening works:

  • We First check for HPV.
  • If HPV positive →
    • “We do another test on the liquid sample we have collected to analyze the cells (liquid cytology, not a slide).

4. Why it is better

  • This method is More effective than Pap smear because it checks for the root cause of cervical cancer
  • HPV takes 10–15 years to cause cancerous changes.

5. Frequency

  • In New method we screen every 5 years instead of every 2 years
  • Our studies show HPV takes many years to cause cancer and 5 years frequency is safe

EXPLAIN THE PROCEDURE (LAY LANGUAGE)

Steps

  1. I will Give u privacy to undress and get ready.
  2. I will arrange a chaperone to be with us.
  3. We will Gently insert a speculum in the vagina.
  4. We first Look at the cervix for any visible abnormal lesions.
  5. Then we Take a sample with a brush-like device
  6. And we Spin and tap the brush in a liquid bottle.

Pain / discomfort

  • This is Not painful procedure.
  • Mild discomfort possible.
  • Usually does not cause bleeding; may cause mild spotting.

SELF-COLLECTION

Offer option

  • If you are not happy with the procedure:
    • I can Offer u some reading material and fact sheets, u can read about it and understand it better.
    • If u still refuse to di it → I can arrange self-collection method.

How it works

  • I will Give you a long cotton-bud-like swab.
  • U need to take the sample yourself from the vagina.
  • I will Provide printed instructions.

Steps

  • Insert swab a few centimeters into vagina.
  • Rotate it.
  • Put it in the tube and give back.

OPTIONAL (IF TIME)

When we do HPV analysis

  • We Check for high-risk strains that cause cervical cancer I.e 16 and 18.
    • If positive → colposcopy.
  • If non-16/18 (other types):
    • We Look at cells for abnormalities.

END OF CASE

  • This case focuses on:
    • Explaining why cervical screening changed
    • Explaining what happens during screening
    • Offering self-collection
    • Not a results-interpretation case

CERVICAL SCREENING – CASE 2

32-year-old lady – Return to discuss CST results

RESULTS

  • HPV 16 positive
  • HPV 18 negative
  • Non-16/18 negative
  • Cytology normal

Management: Colposcopy.

HISTORY TAKING (Same structure as Case 1)

Apply the same CST structure.

Example findings given in the script:

  • Last period 1 week ago
  • Multiple partners
  • Does not use condoms
  • On contraception
  • Never took Gardasil (needle phobia)
  • STI: never screened before
  • (If symptoms: would do co-test, not CST — but exam usually won’t give symptoms here)

EXPLAINING THE INVESTIGATION RESULTS

1. Re-explain CST (repetition from previous case)

  • “We have done a cervical screening test for you.”
  • “This test checks for a virus called human papillomavirus, which is a sexually transmitted infection and the main cause of cervical cancer.”

2. Explain strains

  • “There are more than 100 strains of the HPV virus.”
  • “Some strains have a higher risk for cervical cancer.”
  • “The high-risk strains are type 16 and 18.”

3. State her result

  • “In your results, you have tested positive for HPV 16.”
  • Difficult conversation because 16 has high risk.

4. Give the good news

  • “The good news is that there are no cancerous changes in the cells.”
  • “The cells look totally normal.”

MANAGEMENT – COLPOSCOPY

1. Reason

  • “As HPV 16 is a high-risk strain for causing cervical cancer, we need to proceed to another test called a colposcopy.”

2. Referral

  • Referral to gynaecologist (referral itself does not score).

3. Explain colposcopy (must educate patient)

Colposcopy is:

  • Very similar to previous CST procedure.
  • Privacy and chaperone provided.
  • We insert Speculum to Look closer and more carefully with a microscope.
  • We Use some chemicals like dyes and colours to reveal abnormalities in the cells.

4. What may happen during colposcopy

  • We May take a sample.
  • May treat any abnormal lesions with:
    • Laser treatment
    • Cryo treatment
    • Cone biopsy
    • Wire loop excision

End of Case 2

CERVICAL SCREENING – CASE 3

27-year-old lady – who had done a CST 12 months ago. HPV non 16-18 was + with cytology: LSIL. Repeat CST results after 12 months:

LAST YEAR’S RESULT

  • Non-16/18 positive
  • Cytology: LSIL

THIS YEAR’S RESULT

  • HPV 16 positive
  • Cytology: HSIL

Two indications for colposcopy (16 OR HSIL), but 16 alone is enough.

HISTORY TAKING

  • Same structure as before.

EXPLAINING THE RESULTS

1. Start with usual CST explanation

  • “We’ve done a cervical screening.”
  • “We’re checking for the human papillomavirus.”
  • “It is a sexually transmitted infection that causes cervical cancer.”
  • “There are 100 strains.”
  • “High-risk strains are 16 and 18.”

2. Explain LAST YEAR

  • “Last year you had a low-risk strain of HPV.”
  • “When we checked the cells, there were low-grade precancerous changes in the cells.”

Important clarification

  • LSIL and HSIL are NOT cancers.
  • They are precancerous changes.

3. Explain THIS YEAR

There are two problems:

Problem 1

  • “You have tested positive for a high-risk strain.”

Problem 2

  • “Now we can see high-grade precancerous changes.”

Reassurance

  • “This is not a cancer yet.”

MANAGEMENT

Indication

  • HPV 16 = colposcopy (regardless of cytology).
  • HSIL strengthens the indication but is not the primary reason here.

Plan

  • “I need to send you for a colposcopy.”

Explain colposcopy (same as Case 2)

  • Speculum insertion
  • Microscope (colposcope)
  • Use chemicals/dyes
  • May take samples
  • May treat abnormalities:
    • Laser
    • Cryo
    • Cone biopsy
    • Wire loop excision

End of Case 3

✏️ Corrections

✏️ Self-collection is described as 'less accurate' than clinician collection.
Under the renewed National Cervical Screening Program (universal self-collection eligibility from 1 July 2022), a self-collected vaginal sample is as accurate as a clinician-collected sample for detecting oncogenic HPV. The genuine limitation is only that a self-collected sample cannot undergo reflex liquid-based cytology (no cervical cells), so an HPV-positive non-16/18 self-collection result requires a follow-up clinician-collected sample for cytology. Avoid telling patients self-collection is 'less accurate' as this discourages an evidence-based, access-improving option.
✏️ P3 (Pills) states the most common cause of cervical ectropion is oestrogen in COCPs and that the 'first step is to stop the pills before treatment'.
Ectropion is usually physiological; asymptomatic ectropion needs reassurance only, not cessation of contraception. Ceasing an effective contraceptive should not be a routine first step - it is considered only for troublesome symptomatic ectropion after excluding other causes, and the patient's contraceptive needs must be addressed. Reword to 'consider reviewing oestrogen-containing contraception if symptomatic'.

⭐ Suggested Enhancements

⭐ Add the exact renewed-NCSP intermediate-risk pathway terminology (HPV-positive 'non-16/18' = intermediate risk -> repeat HPV test in 12 months; two consecutive intermediate-risk results or HSIL/possible high-grade cytology -> colposcopy) and cite the National Cervical Screening Program clinical guidelines / RACGP as the authoritative Australian source in metadata.
⭐ Clarify the age boundaries precisely: first CST at age 25 (or 2 years after previous Pap if transitioning), routine 5-yearly, and an exit HPV test between 70-74 years - matching the case but worth stating as a numbered rule for learners.
⭐ Add that the co-test (HPV + cytology together) for symptomatic patients also mandates examination of the cervix and consideration of colposcopy/referral, and that post-menopausal bleeding additionally requires transvaginal ultrasound +/- endometrial sampling (endometrial cancer until proven otherwise).
⭐ Include HPV vaccination status and immunosuppression as risk modifiers in the note, and add a documentation prompt to record consent, chaperone and that self-collection was offered (Australian medicolegal/quality standard).
