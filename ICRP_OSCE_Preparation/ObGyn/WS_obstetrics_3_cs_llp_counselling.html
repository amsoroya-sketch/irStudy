Low-Lying Placenta and Maternal-Request Caesarean Counselling

Source: Dr. Amir workshop — obstetrics / class 8 (Aug 2026 drop) · Specialty: obstetrics_gynaecology

CS LLP Counselling

Your next patient is a 27 year old lady, GA 20 weeks, who is here to discuss her ultrasound results. Her USD has shown a low lying placenta. She is requesting a C/S.

Task:

• Take History

• Explain the ultrasound results

• Counsel the patient regarding her request

• Agenda: worried of pain during vaginal delivery

  • Must apply patient-centered approach
  • Your job:
    • Give pros
    • Give cons / disadvantages / risks
    • Let the patient make an informed decision
  • Give time to think at the end
  • Because she is only 20 weeks, many things can change before delivery
  • Your job: educate → patient decides

1. HISTORY TAKING

Intro + Agenda finding

  • ID check, sit, introduce yourself
  • Open-ended question:
    • “How can I help you today?”
  • Patient says she is here for results & wants a C-section
  • Respond:
    • “Thank you for coming in to discuss the results and your follow-up.”
    • Ask for agenda:
      • “Is there any specific concern you have today?”
        OR
      • “Any specific concerns about delivery methods?”
        OR
      • “Can you tell me why you prefer to have a cesarean section?”

Agenda revealed

  • She is worried about the pain of delivery
  • Friends told her C-section has less pain

Assess understanding

  • “How much do you know about pain control options?”
  • Patient: doesn’t know

Obstetric History (standard structure)

  • Early pregnancy bloods – done? normal?
  • Dating scan done?
  • Down syndrome screening?
  • Morphology scan at 20 weeks – shows low-lying placenta
  • Ask about fetal movements (borderline time):
    • “Have you started feeling your baby kicking?” → No

Symptoms / Risk factors (MOST IMPORTANT)

  • Any vaginal bleeding? (most important)
  • Any abdominal pain?
  • Previous cesarean sections?
  • Previous surgeries on womb / terminations / pregnancies

Optional extra questions (if time)

  • Partner supportive?
  • Psychological coping? mood?
  • Past medical history / Family history (unlikely to have time)

Summary of History Purpose

  1. Assess agenda (pain)
  2. Complete obstetric history for low-lying placenta

2. EXPLAIN ULTRASOUND RESULTS

Explain placenta (same as previous case)

  • Placenta = tissue/bridge connecting baby to womb & mother
  • Normally placenta is towards the top of the womb

Explain finding

  • In this case:
    • Placenta is at the lower part of the womb
    • Very close to the opening/outlet
    • This is called a low-lying placenta
    • If <2 cm from the outlet, we call it low-lying

Explain other findings (if present)

  • Morphology scan:
    • Baby formed well
    • No deformities
    • No concerns
    • (Explain whatever normal findings are available)

3. COUNSELING

You need two components to pass:

A. Counseling about low-lying placenta

B. Counseling about pain + C-section request

Both MUST be completed.

A. LOW-LYING PLACENTA COUNSELING

Key point

  • We will need to Repeat ultrasound closer to delivery (around 34 weeks, can be 32–36 weeks)

Why repeat?

  • Most ladies having low-lying placentas will no longer have a problem o repeat scan because:
    • Womb grows in size and placenta moves away from the outlet
    • 9 out of 10 will have no longer low-lying placenta.
    • 1 in 10 ladies will still have low-lying placenta

If still low-lying near delivery

  • In that case, there is an Increased risk of severe bleeding during/after delivery
  • This is why we prefer a planned cesarean section at 39 weeks of pregnancy

Option of trial of labor

  • We Can consider a trial of labor and vaginal delivery
  • But I need u to understand risk of bleeding

B. CESAREAN SECTION + PAIN MANAGEMENT COUNSELING

1. Acknowledge Pain Concern

  • “I understand pain in labor can be scary and stressful.”

Pain Control Options: we have few options to help control the pain during delivery

Non-pharmacological

  1. Breathing techniques / relaxation techniques to help u cope up with pain
  2. Some Positions can decrease the pain
  3. Using TENS machine
    • Small device
    • We put Two sticky pads on the back
    • And they produce small electrical pulses → helps reduce pain

Medications

  1. Nitrous oxide gas (laughing gas)
    • Breathe in through a mask
    • Decreases pain
  2. Morphine / Pethidine injections
    • Not preferred
    • Makes baby sleepy
    • Takes ~30 min to work
    • Can cause nausea
  3. Epidural anesthesia
    • Most common
    • Medication injected close to spine in your back
    • This Numbs your stomach and private parts
    • Risks:
      • Drop in blood pressure → need IV fluids
      • Headaches, pain after
    • Will monitor baby with CTG

Environment

  • Support people allowed (partner / family)

Present options repeatedly:

    • “You have options”
    • “Think about these options”
    • “I can give you reading material about the options”

2. Cesarean Section – Pros & Cons

Risks for the mother

  • Higher risk of maternal death
  • Higher risk of hysterectomy (we may need to remove your womb)
  • Higher risk of infections
  • Higher risk of bleeding
    Higher risk of injury to organs in abdomen
  • Higher risk of anesthetic complications
  • Long-term:
    • Higher risk of rupture in next pregnancy if opt for vaginal delivery
    • Increased risk of placenta previa in next pregnancy
      • (Important since she already has low-lying placenta)

Risks for the baby

  • Increased mortality
  • Higher risk of asthma
  • Respiratory problems

Benefits / Pros

  • Less injury to private parts
  • Less need for cuts in private parts
  • Decreases risk of incontinence
  • Decreases risk of prolapse
  • No large baby passing through birth canal → less muscle/organ injury

Key closing points

  • Too early to decide and finalize a plan now
  • Things may change closer to delivery
  • Low-lying placenta may persist → we may prefer C-section ourselves

Plan

  • For now Think about the options, pros/cons
  • I will Provide u reading materials (C-section + pain relief)
  • Arrange another meeting
  • U can bring partner/family
  • Review concerns together
  • Make best decision closer to delivery

🔬 Expert Review & Enhancements

Reviewed by: clinical-documentation-expert (Australian clinical supervisor, O&G documentation standards) · fact-checked against the irStudy medical textbook RAG index.

✏️ Corrections

✏️ Under 'Risks for the baby' the notes list 'Increased mortality' for caesarean section.
This overstates the evidence. For a planned caesarean the principal neonatal risk is transient respiratory morbidity (transient tachypnoea of the newborn), higher before 39 weeks. Increased neonatal mortality is not an established consequence of elective caesarean in an uncomplicated term pregnancy and should be removed or reworded to 'respiratory morbidity, especially if delivered before 39 weeks'.
✏️ The pain-relief section presents pethidine/morphine and epidural without Australian nuance, and omits that opioids cross the placenta requiring neonatal observation.
Retain but refine: parenteral opioids (morphine/pethidine) can cause neonatal respiratory depression and reduced early feeding, so document neonatal monitoring; add remifentanil PCA as an Australian option where available. Nitrous oxide (Entonox) and epidural remain appropriate. Use Australian drug names.
✏️ Planned birth timing for a persistent low-lying (non-covering) placenta is stated as 39 weeks.
For a truly low-lying placenta that does not cover the os and is asymptomatic, RANZCOG timing is individualised, commonly 36-37 weeks if there has been bleeding, and vaginal birth may be attempted if the placental edge is >20 mm from the os. State timing as individualised rather than a fixed 39 weeks.

⭐ Suggested Enhancements

⭐ Flag that this is a maternal-request caesarean scenario: document an explicit shared decision-making / informed-consent conversation and offer a formal patient decision aid, consistent with AHPRA informed-consent expectations.
⭐ Add the evidence caveat for TENS (limited evidence of efficacy in established labour) so the candidate does not overstate benefit; keep it as an early-labour comfort option.
⭐ Add a repeat transvaginal ultrasound plan at 32-36 weeks with a clear statement that ~9/10 low-lying placentas resolve, and document what happens at each branch (resolves -> aim vaginal birth; persists -> caesarean).
⭐ Reference the RANZCOG statement on 'Caesarean section on maternal request' and the RANZCOG 'Placenta praevia and placenta accreta' guideline in the teaching notes to anchor Australian practice.
⭐ Add a safety-net and follow-up line: any antepartum bleeding, pain or contractions -> present immediately; schedule the follow-up counselling appointment with partner as documented shared care.
