# Acquired Hemophilia

**Authors:** Mobeen Z. Haider, Faiz Anwer

**Source:** StatPearls - StatPearls Publishing

## Abstract

Hemophilia is derived from hemo (blood) and philia (love) and is recognized as the most common and severe hemorrhagic disorder. Depending upon the pathophysiology, there are 3 types of hemophilia -A, B, and C, caused by the deficiency or dysfunction of factors VIII, IX, and XI, respectively. It can be hereditary, which is more common, and a rare variety is the acquired 1. Acquired hemophilia is due to the autoantibodies that develop against a coagulation factor. The antibodies mostly develop against factor VIII, commonly known as "acquired hemophilia A" (AHA). Patients with autoantibodies may present with catastrophic bleeding; they usually do not have any past medical history of bleeding episodes or disorders. Diagnosing this disease is often difficult because of its rarity and the complexity of the laboratory workup. This topic describes the clinical and biological features of this disease, in addition to the therapeutic strategies for it. Clinically, the presentation varies from life-threatening bleeds on 1 end to mild or no bleeds on the other. The presentation with life-threatening bleeding is usually seen during the initial several weeks. However, it can happen at any point during the disease if the appropriate treatment is not initiated.
