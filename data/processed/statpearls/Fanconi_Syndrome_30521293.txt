# Fanconi Syndrome

**Authors:** Ahmad S. Matarneh, Bayan Matarneh, Preeti Rout, Syed Rizwan A. Bokhari

**Source:** StatPearls - StatPearls Publishing

## Abstract

Fanconi syndrome is a disorder of the proximal renal tubule characterized by impaired reabsorption of various solutes, including glucose, phosphate, bicarbonate, amino acids, and electrolytes. This defect causes excessive urinary losses and a wide range of biochemical abnormalities. The condition may be inherited, more commonly affecting children, or acquired in adults, often due to medications, toxins, or systemic diseases. Fanconi syndrome should not be confused with Fanconi anemia—a distinct autosomal recessive disorder characterized by bone marrow failure, congenital abnormalities, and an increased risk of malignancy. Despite sharing a namesake, the 2 conditions differ completely in both pathophysiology and clinical presentation. The severity and progression of Fanconi syndrome vary based on the underlying cause. Treatment focuses on managing the primary etiology when identifiable and providing supportive care to correct fluid, electrolyte, and nutritional imbalances. Early recognition and targeted intervention are crucial to reducing complications and preserving long-term renal function.
