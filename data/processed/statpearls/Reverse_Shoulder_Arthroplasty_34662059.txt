# Reverse Shoulder Arthroplasty

**Authors:** Shady Hermena, Manjeera Rednam

**Source:** StatPearls - StatPearls Publishing

## Abstract

Reverse total shoulder arthroplasty (RTSA) is increasingly gaining popularity worldwide in treating various traumatic and degenerative glenohumeral diseases and irreparable rotator cuff arthropathies (see Image. Reverse Total Shoulder Arthroplasty). The number of performed RTSA in the United States of America has increased from 22,835 in 2011 to 62,705 in 2017. RTSA was first described by Beddow and Alloy in 1970. However, they did not publish their surgical outcomes. In 1987, Grammont et al reported the first 8 cases of rotator cuff arthropathy treated by an RTSA prototype called the “Trompette” (Medinov). RTSA was introduced in the United States of America in 1998 after being used in Europe for several years. The RTSA is a semi-constrained prosthesis with components different from the anatomical total shoulder arthroplasty prosthesis. The anatomical shoulder arthroplasty consists of a concave glenoid socket and proximal humeral ball prosthesis (see Image. Anatomical Shoulder Arthroplasty). In the reverse shoulder arthroplasty, the anatomy is reversed to a glenoid ball (glenosphere) and concave proximal humerus component.
