# Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy

**Authors:** Hajira Basit, Mohamed H. Alahmadi, Preeti Rout, Saurabh Sharma

**Source:** StatPearls - StatPearls Publishing

## Abstract

Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM) is an autosomal dominant cardiac myocyte disease caused by mutations in sarcomere and sarcomere-related protein genes encoding for elements of the contractile machinery of the heart. Characteristic cardiac structural changes include increased left ventricular wall thickness (hypertrophy), causing dynamic left ventricular outflow obstruction, diastolic dysfunction, myocardial ischemia, arrhythmias, autonomic dysfunction, and mitral regurgitation. HCM is a condition that has historically been referred to as idiopathic hypertrophic subaortic stenosis. In the United States, HCM is the most common identifiable cause of sudden cardiac death in healthy people younger than 35, including well-trained athletes. About 60% of patients with HCM have a known gene mutation of the sarcomere or sarcomere-related genes, and obtaining a detailed family history is crucial to help risk-stratify affected patients. A comprehensive patient history and thorough physical exam can also help identify patients at risk of malignant arrhythmia. These structural and functional abnormalities can produce fatigue, dyspnea, chest pain, palpitations, and syncope. Early intervention through pharmacologic means, internal cardiac defibrillator placement, and surgery, when warranted, has greatly improved the survival rates of HCM in the last few decades.
