# Auditory Brainstem Response

**Authors:** Allen Young, Jennifer Cornejo, Alycia Spinner

**Source:** StatPearls - StatPearls Publishing

## Abstract

Auditory brainstem response (ABR), or brainstem auditory Evoked Potentials (BAEP), is an objective measurement of auditory pathway function from the auditory nerve to the mesencephalon. ABR tests synchronous neural function and can estimate hearing sensitivity thresholds in individuals unable to tolerate traditional behavioral audiometry. Electrical activity from the eighth cranial nerve and neurons along the brainstem auditory pathway are recorded by surface electrodes placed on the scalp, forehead, and near the ears for 10 milliseconds after an acoustic stimulus. The readings consist of up to 7 positive wave peaks labeled I-VII with negative troughs in between. It was first introduced into audiology in the 1970s, and its importance has increased with the contemporary guidelines of newborn hearing screening for hearing loss within 3 months of age. It is now essential in diagnosing hearing loss, acoustic tumors, and cerebellopontine angle tumors.
