# Persistent Epithelial Defect

**Authors:** Priyanka Golhait, Ropfuleno Peseyie

**Source:** StatPearls - StatPearls Publishing

## Abstract

The corneal epithelium provides a smooth optical surface and maintains a protective barrier against infectious agents and damage, serving as the first line of ocular immune defense. Any breach in the continuity of the epithelium renders the eye susceptible to infection, ulcers, and even perforation. Most of the corneal abrasions re-epithelialize rapidly and uneventfully; however, they persist for longer than usual duration in the presence of certain risk factors. Beyond approximately two weeks of standard treatment, they are described as persistent epithelial defects (PEDs). Though uncommon, their management is quite challenging since they are refractory to standard therapy and require a long-term follow-up. Without appropriate treatment, they can have potentially blinding consequences like scarring, infection, vascularization, and vision loss. This article encompasses the etiopathogenesis, predisposing factors, diagnosis, current management options, and novel therapies under pipeline.
