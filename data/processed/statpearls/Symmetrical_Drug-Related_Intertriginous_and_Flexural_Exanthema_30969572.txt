# Symmetrical Drug-Related Intertriginous and Flexural Exanthema

**Authors:** Sarra Harbaoui, Hasnain A. Syed

**Source:** StatPearls - StatPearls Publishing

## Abstract

Symmetrical drug-related intertriginous and flexural exanthema (SDRIFE) is a symmetrical erythematous eruption involving the gluteal and intertriginous regions that develops following systemic drug exposure. The condition was initially described in 1984 as "baboon syndrome," named for the characteristic distribution of lesions on the buttocks and inner thighs resembling the red rump of baboons, and was associated with systemic or local exposure to contact allergens and drugs. In 2004, Hausermann proposed the term "symmetrical drug-related intertriginous and flexural exanthema" to more accurately describe eruptions occurring after systemic drug administration, irrespective of prior sensitization. SDRIFE is distinguished from other cutaneous drug reactions by its characteristic morphology, distribution, and absence of systemic manifestations.
