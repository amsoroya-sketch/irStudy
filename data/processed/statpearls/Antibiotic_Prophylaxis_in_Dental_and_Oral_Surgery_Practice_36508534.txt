# Antibiotic Prophylaxis in Dental and Oral Surgery Practice

**Authors:** Joseph O. Daley, Caitlyn M. Hollingshead, Melina Brizuela

**Source:** StatPearls - StatPearls Publishing

## Abstract

The disruption of the dental mucosa can lead to transient bacteremia. In turn, bacteremia is a risk factor for the development of infective endocarditis and other biofilm-associated infections, such as prosthetic joint infection. The first suggestion that dental procedures could be associated with infective endocarditis came in 1923. In 1935, 1 study isolated S. viridans in blood cultures in 61% of patients with dental sepsis. Recommendations for antimicrobial prophylaxis before dental procedures were first included in the American Heart Association guidelines in 1955. Antimicrobials have been offered to patients for various reasons before dental procedures and oral surgery. Recent recommendations target prophylaxis toward the most vulnerable to infection.
