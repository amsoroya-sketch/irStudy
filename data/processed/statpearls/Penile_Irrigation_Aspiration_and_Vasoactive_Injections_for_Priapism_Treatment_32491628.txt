# Penile Irrigation, Aspiration, and Vasoactive Injections for Priapism Treatment

**Authors:** Nikhil Mohan, Stephen W. Leslie, Christopher M. Deibert

**Source:** StatPearls - StatPearls Publishing

## Abstract

Penile injection and aspiration is a bedside procedure used to treat ischemic priapism. Priapism is defined as an erection of extended duration (>4 hours) that continues beyond, or is unrelated to, sexual activity. There are 3 types of priapism—ischemic, which involves a low flow of blood into the penis; nonischemic or high flow priapism, caused by increased blood flow into the penis; and stuttering priapism, which refers to recurrent episodes of ischemic priapism. Ischemic priapism is a surgical emergency, whereas nonischemic priapism is not, due to continued arterial blood flow. Causes of ischemic priapism include medication effects, vasoactive or recreational drug use, blood dyscrasias, malignancy, sickle cell disease, fat embolism, intravenous contrast reaction, neurogenic disorders, hormonal abnormalities, metabolic disease, response to toxins, and idiopathic causes. The injection of vasoactive drugs into the corpora to treat erectile dysfunction is the most common cause of ischemic priapism. As ischemic priapism is a medical and surgical emergency, prompt diagnosis and reversal treatment are required. Blunt perineal trauma is the most common cause of nonischemic priapism, which results in unrestricted arterial flow within the corpora due to a fistula. As there is no ischemia of the corporal tissues because of the continued arterial blood flow to the penis, urgent surgical treatment is not immediately required.
