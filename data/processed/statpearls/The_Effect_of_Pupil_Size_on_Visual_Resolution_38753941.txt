# The Effect of Pupil Size on Visual Resolution

**Authors:** Garrett N. Manion, Thomas J. Stokkermans

**Source:** StatPearls - StatPearls Publishing

## Abstract

The human pupil regulates the amount of light that reaches the retina and profoundly impacts vision. Pupil size, denoted by the diameter of the black circular opening at the center of the iris, is modulated by a dynamic balance between the iridic constrictor and dilator muscles. These muscles respond to various inputs, including levels of ambient light, visual focus requirements, and certain cognitive states. Pupil size has a direct effect on visual resolution and depth of field. Decreasing pupil size increases the depth of field, leading to a wider range of clear vision. Conversely, increased pupil size can enhance light sensitivity at the expense of visual resolution due to increased aberrations and a narrow depth of field. In bright light, normal pupil diameter in adults can range from 2 mm to 4 mm; in dark light, it ranges from 4 mm to 8 mm.
