# Angiokeratoma Circumscriptum

**Authors:** Heather Ivy, Carmen A. Julian

**Source:** StatPearls - StatPearls Publishing

## Abstract

Angiokeratomas are a set of disorders characterized by benign vascular ectasias of the papillary dermis associated with hyperkeratosis, papillomatosis, and acanthosis of the epidermis. Five distinct clinical presentations of angiokeratomas are known, including solitary or multiple angiokeratomas, angiokeratoma of Mibelli, angiokeratoma of Fordyce, angiokeratoma corporus diffusum, and angiokeratoma circumscriptum. Angiokeratoma circumscriptum is the rarest form of these disorders. Initial presentation is typically at birth as red-colored macules. After several years the lesion transforms into dark red to blue violaceous nodules or plaques with warty and/or hyperkeratotic features. They commonly present unilaterally on the lower extremities but have also been found to occur in other locations, including the trunk, neck, scrotum, and tongue.
