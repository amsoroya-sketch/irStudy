# Dyspnea

**Authors:** Fathmath Suha, Pranav Modi, Sandeep Sharma

**Source:** StatPearls - StatPearls Publishing

## Abstract

Dyspnea, commonly described as shortness of breath, is a subjective sensation of uncomfortable or difficult breathing arising from diverse medical, environmental, psychological, and physiological factors. Patients frequently report chest tightness, air hunger, rapid breathing, or a sensation of insufficient air. Dyspnea affects millions worldwide and may serve as the primary manifestation of respiratory, cardiac, neuromuscular, systemic, or psychogenic disorders, or a combination thereof. Psychosocial, cultural, and physiologic factors, including anxiety, depression, hormonal status, obesity, and overall fitness level, influence the perception and severity of dyspnea. Clinically, acute dyspnea develops over hours to days, subacute dyspnea evolves over several days up to 4 weeks, and chronic dyspnea persists for more than 4 weeks. Effective evaluation requires a systematic approach, beginning with a detailed history and physical examination, supplemented by targeted diagnostics such as chest imaging, electrocardiography (ECG), and limited laboratory testing. Additional assessment may include pulmonary function tests, echocardiography, or advanced imaging guided by clinical suspicion. Management prioritizes identification and treatment of the underlying cause—whether optimizing heart failure therapy, addressing airway obstruction, or providing psychological support—while ensuring symptom relief and patient safety through coordinated, interprofessional care.
