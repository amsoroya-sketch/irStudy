# Obesity in Pregnancy

**Authors:** Nowreen Haq, Venkata Sushma Chamarthi, Asia Ayabe

**Source:** StatPearls - StatPearls Publishing

## Abstract

The global prevalence of obesity has risen markedly over recent decades, prompting the World Health Organization to label it a “global epidemic”; the number of people with overweight or obesity nearly tripled between 1975 and 2016.  The World Obesity Federation projects that by 2030 more than 1.13 billion adults will have obesity, with lifetime exposure to high BMI becoming common by ages 65–70.  Parallel trends are seen in pregnancy: the prevalence of maternal obesity is increasing worldwide, with marked regional variation—particularly high among women in Pacific Island nations, the Caribbean, and the Middle East—and an estimated 39 million pregnancies annually complicated by maternal obesity. Contemporary data from 2024–2025 report obesity prevalence of 40–46% among pregnant women in some populations, representing a substantial rise over prior decades.   Rates are highest among older mothers, minority ethnic groups, and populations experiencing higher deprivation. Maternal obesity is associated with gestational hypertension, gestational diabetes, preeclampsia, preterm birth, and pregnancy loss, and it increases the risk of fetal congenital anomalies and macrosomia.  Moreover, in-utero exposure to maternal obesity is linked to adverse cardiometabolic health in offspring that can persist into adulthood.
