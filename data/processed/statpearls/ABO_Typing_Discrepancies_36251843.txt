# ABO Typing Discrepancies

**Authors:** Saubia Fathima, Robert B. Killeen

**Source:** StatPearls - StatPearls Publishing

## Abstract

ABO discrepancies pose a significant challenge in clinical practice, necessitating a thorough understanding of blood group typing to ensure transfusion safety. The ABO system, crucial in blood banking, categorizes blood into 4 primary types—A, B, AB, and O—based on the presence or absence of antigens on red blood cells (RBCs). Laboratory personnel perform forward grouping by detecting antigens on the RBC surface, whereas reverse grouping confirms results by identifying antibodies in the patient's serum. Discrepancies arise when these results do not align, potentially due to technical errors, weak antigen or antibody expression, underlying medical conditions, or the presence of subgroups or abnormal proteins. Depending on the discrepancy, up to 10% of patients tested may show weak or missing antibodies or antigens or even pan-agglutination. The presence of weak red cell antigens can prompt a delayed hemolytic transfusion reaction. Experts classify ABO discrepancies into 4 major types—type I due to weak or missing antibodies, type II due to weak or missing antigens, type III due plasma or protein abnormalities leading to pseudo-agglutination, and type IV due to miscellaneous causes leading to false reactions. These inconsistencies can lead to potentially life-threatening hemolytic reactions if not accurately identified and resolved. Clinicians must collaborate with laboratory specialists to conduct thorough serological investigations, including extended incubation, adsorption, and elution techniques, and review patient histories for recent transfusions or medical conditions that may impact blood typing. Standardized protocols, strict quality control measures, and interdisciplinary communication are vital in mitigating the risks associated with ABO discrepancies. Effectively managing ABO discrepancies is crucial for transfusion safety, minimizing adverse reactions, and enhancing patient outcomes.
