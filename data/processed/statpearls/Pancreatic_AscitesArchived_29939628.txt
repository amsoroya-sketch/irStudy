# Pancreatic Ascites(Archived)

**Authors:** Jonathan Gapp, Gilles J. Hoilat, Stephen W. Leslie, Subhash Chandra

**Source:** StatPearls - StatPearls Publishing

## Abstract

Pancreatic ascites is an intraperitoneal pancreatic fluid collection primarily caused by persistent pancreatic duct leakage or intraperitoneal pancreatic pseudocyst rupture; this condition is a complication of persistent chronic alcoholic pancreatitis. Less frequent etiologies of pancreatic ascites include biliary pancreatitis, major pancreatic duct trauma, ampullary stenosis, cystic ductal duplications, and choledocholithiasis.  The severity of this condition varies widely, depending on the location and degree of ductal injury and the presence of infection. Mild cases resolve spontaneously, but infection, persistence, and association with pancreatic duct adenocarcinoma increase morbidity and mortality. Failure of conservative measures warrants stenting to bypass the damaged or leaking pancreatic duct. Adjunctive treatment includes medical management to decrease pancreatic exocrine secretions and salvage therapy with surgery. However, comparative studies between different treatment approaches are unavailable due to the low incidence of this condition.
