# Dupuytren Contracture

**Authors:** Joel Walthall, Prashanth Anand, Uzma H. Rehman

**Source:** StatPearls - StatPearls Publishing

## Abstract

Dupuytren disease is predominantly a myofibroblastic disease that affects the palmar and digital fascia of the hand and results in contracture deformities. The fourth (ring) and fifth (small or pinky) digits are the most commonly affected. The disease begins in the palm as painless nodules that form along longitudinal lines of tension. The nodules form cords that produce contracture deformities within fascial bands and tissues of the hand. Dupuytren contracture is a benign disorder with an etiology similar to those of similar disorders such as Peyronie disease, Ledderhose disease, and Garrod disease. Dupuytren contracture is usually seen in whites, and the disorder is often bilateral; when unilateral, the right side is more likely to be involved than the left. Many individuals have a family history, with males being more likely to be affected than females.
