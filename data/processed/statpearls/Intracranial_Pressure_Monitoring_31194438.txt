# Intracranial Pressure Monitoring

**Authors:** Sunil Munakomi, Joe M. Das

**Source:** StatPearls - StatPearls Publishing

## Abstract

The normal intracranial pressure (ICP) ranges from 7 to 15 mm Hg, while it does not exceed 15 mm Hg in the vertical position. Overnight sleep monitoring is considered the “gold standard” in conscious patients. Typically, ICP lowering therapy initiates when pressure exceeds 20 to 25 mm Hg. Refractory elevated ICP reduces cerebral perfusion pressure, accounting for cerebral ischemia and initiating herniation syndromes that eventually lead to death. Implementing multimodal monitoring with adherence to ICP-guided therapy has been the cornerstone in managing severe traumatic brain injury. Thus, ICP monitoring allows for the judicious use of interventions with a defined target point, thereby avoiding potentially harmful aggressive treatment. Brain Trauma Foundation guidelines during patient care bundle approaches have shown positive outcomes and the minimized cost of acute care.
