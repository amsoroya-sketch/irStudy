# Techniques and Strategies in Ultrasound Simulation

**Authors:** Benjamin Nti, Leela Sharath Pillarisetty

**Source:** StatPearls - StatPearls Publishing

## Abstract

Point-of-care ultrasound (POCUS) continues to evolve as an essential adjunct to clinical examination in the acute care setting and other disciplines such as critical care medicine, internal medicine, and pediatrics. As this modality continues to evolve, a comprehensive training curriculum is an important foundation in medical education for acquiring and enhancing this skill set. It is well established that medical training using simulation has many advantages over traditional training techniques. To our knowledge, the current literature on POCUS simulation as a training tool is limited. A wide array of POCUS training simulation and clinical integration strategies exist. The common approach often begins with workshops and conferences with didactics, phantom, and live models to simulate bedside skills and techniques; this often translates to further learning at the bedside by scanning patients with clinically indicated diagnoses or chief complaints. As established by various oversight governing bodies such as the American Academy of Emergency Physicians, competence is based on achieving specific knowledge and skill acquisition milestones. However, learning at the bedside without adequate experience early on in training can often create a stress-induced environment, which can be detrimental to the learning environment and potentially harm the patient.
