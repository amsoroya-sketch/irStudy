# Liver Transplantation

**Authors:** Yara Dababneh, Omar Y. Mousa

**Source:** StatPearls - StatPearls Publishing

## Abstract

Cirrhosis and decompensated liver disease were the ninth leading cause of death for men in 2016 in the United States. Liver transplantation (LT) is a lifesaving gift and procedure, as well as a proven intervention in caring for individuals with acute and chronic end-stage liver disease. This procedure restores normal health, lifestyle, and extends lifespan by 15 years. The advent of liver transplantation has provided a critical safety net for treating advanced liver diseases when all other medical interventions have been exhausted. According to the Scientific Registry of Transplant Recipients data, overall patient survival is excellent, reaching 90% at 1 year following deceased donor LT and 77% at 5 years. Since LT was first attempted in 1963, there have been continuous advances and major improvements in the surgical technique, type of organ donation, with the expansion of the organ donation pool, and a significant focus on the quality of life for both recipients and donors. Still, there are important challenges, including the shortage of donor organs, the selection of LT candidates, and organ allocation.
