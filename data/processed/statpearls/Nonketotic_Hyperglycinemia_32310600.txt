# Nonketotic Hyperglycinemia

**Authors:** Conrad Krawiec, Catherine Anastasopoulou

**Source:** StatPearls - StatPearls Publishing

## Abstract

Nonketotic hyperglycinemia (NKH) is a rare genetic disease secondary to an inborn error in glycine metabolism. Due to a mutation in the glycine cleavage enzyme system, the patient cannot break down glycine, ultimately accumulating it throughout the body. The buildup of glycine primarily occurs within the spinal cord and brain; thus, this condition's initial clinical manifestations and long-term sequelae are more often of neurological origin. No known interventions are effective in altering the natural history of nonketotic hyperglycinemia, but whatever therapeutic strategies are applied can potentially reduce the comorbidities associated with this condition. It is essential that the clinician recognizes this disease and initiates early evaluation and treatment to attain the best possible outcome.
