# Human African Trypanosomiasis (Sleeping Sickness)

**Authors:** Caitlyn M. Hollingshead, Rene Bermudez

**Source:** StatPearls - StatPearls Publishing

## Abstract

Human African trypanosomiasis (HAT), or African sleeping sickness, is a potentially life-threatening infectious illness caused by the protozoans Trypanosoma brucei gambiense (T brucei gambiense) or Trypanosoma brucei rhodesiense (T brucei rhodesiense) transmitted by the tsetse fly (see Image. Vector and Organism of Human African Trypanosomiasis). This disease is endemic in sub-Saharan Africa but can be seen outside of endemic areas among travelers. These parasites produce illnesses with different clinical and epidemiological characteristics. East African HAT is caused by T brucei rhodesiense. West African HAT is caused by T brucei gambiense. Both conditions have hemolymphatic and meningoencephalitic stages. HAT is considered a neglected tropical disease and is a significant public health threat in rural Africa.
