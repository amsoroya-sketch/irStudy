# Suprapubic Bladder Catheterization

**Authors:** Theodore Poulton, Stephen W. Leslie

**Source:** StatPearls - StatPearls Publishing

## Abstract

Suprapubic catheterization is a procedure in which a drainage tube (catheter) is inserted into the urinary bladder through the lower abdominal wall just superior to the pubic symphysis. The procedure is typically performed in individuals who cannot void when urethral catheterization is contraindicated, not tolerated, or unsuccessful. Suprapubic catheterization offers an effective alternative route to drain the urinary bladder when other methods are not clinically feasible, appropriate, or tolerated. Compared with urethral catheters, suprapubic catheters are easier to change, available in a wide range of sizes, associated with fewer catheter-associated urinary tract infections, require antibiotics less often, and are generally considered more comfortable for men with long-term catheter requirements. Alternatives to suprapubic catheterization include urethral catheterization, intermittent catheterization, urinary diversion, and percutaneous nephrostomy drainage. These specialized drainage catheters are typically placed either via a percutaneous or open approach, typically guided by cystoscopic or ultrasound imaging to minimize the risk of complications.
