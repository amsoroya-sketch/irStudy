# Motivational Interviewing

**Authors:** Rachel L. Emery, Matt Wimmer

**Source:** StatPearls - StatPearls Publishing

## Abstract

Motivational Interviewing (MI) is a conversational style that facilitates collaboration between the healthcare provider and their patients. Its ultimate goal is to solve a healthcare-related problem through patient introspection and empowerment. It combines technical aspects, such as open-ended questions and empathy, with a view toward a patient-centered approach. Since the original description of MI in 1983 by William Miller, in the context of alcoholism, providers have expanded its use to a multitude of other mental and physical health conditions. However, despite the wide range of potential benefits for MI, most studies are still only applied to substance abuse cases. Research continues to determine which skills and tools are most beneficial in MI and the theories behind its mechanism of action in the primary care environment.
