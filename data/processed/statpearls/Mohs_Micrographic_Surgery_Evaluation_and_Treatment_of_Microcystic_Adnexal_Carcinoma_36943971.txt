# Mohs Micrographic Surgery Evaluation and Treatment of Microcystic Adnexal Carcinoma

**Authors:** Sujitha Yadlapati, Patrick M. Zito, James A. Rooney

**Source:** StatPearls - StatPearls Publishing

## Abstract

Microcystic adnexal carcinoma (MAC) is a rare cutaneous neoplasm of follicular and sweat gland differentiation, first described by Goldstein and Barr in 1982. It has many synonyms, including sclerosing sweat duct carcinoma, malignant syringoma, aggressive trichofolliculoma, combined adnexal tumor of the skin, and syringomatous carcinoma. Syringomatous adenoma of the nipple has also been considered MAC, although this is debated.  Microcystic adnexal carcinoma is an indolent but locally aggressive and deeply infiltrative tumor with a predilection for the head and neck area. It is most notable for its perineural invasion (upwards of 80%) and infiltration beyond clinically apparent margins. Microcystic adnexal carcinoma spreads characteristically by local invasion with rare cases of spread to regional lymph nodes. Invasion of muscle, adipose tissue, and nerves can result in significant morbidity.  The locally aggressive nature and deep infiltration make complete excision of the tumor with margin control a crucial part of management. In contrast, MAC is reported to have a fairly high recurrence rate with standard-wide local excision (60% in some case series). More favorable cure rates with lower recurrence rates are reported with Mohs micrographic surgery (MMS).
