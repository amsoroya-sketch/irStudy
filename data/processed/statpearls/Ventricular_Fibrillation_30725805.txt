# Ventricular Fibrillation

**Authors:** Robert M. Ricketts, Amandeep Goyal, Intisar Ahmed

**Source:** StatPearls - StatPearls Publishing

## Abstract

Ventricular arrhythmias are pathologic cardiac rhythms originating from the ventricular myocardium or Purkinje system. This group includes ventricular tachycardia, ventricular fibrillation, premature ventricular contractions (PVCs), and ventricular flutter. Ventricular fibrillation is a clinical emergency characterized by disorganized electrical activity within the ventricular system, resulting in uncoordinated myocardial contraction and cessation of cardiac output (see Image. Ventricular Fibrillation). Ventricular fibrillation is strongly associated with sudden cardiac death (SCD). Despite its lethality, the rhythm is treatable because it is amenable to defibrillation, a shockable rhythm associated with higher survival rates than nonshockable rhythms. Optimal outcomes depend not only on rapid recognition and defibrillation but also on identification and treatment of precipitating and reversible causes.
