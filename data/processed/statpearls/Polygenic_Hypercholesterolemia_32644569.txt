# Polygenic Hypercholesterolemia

**Authors:** Jessica Genkil, Catherine Anastasopoulou, Meenakshi Narayanaswamy, Saurabh Sharma

**Source:** StatPearls - StatPearls Publishing

## Abstract

Hypercholesterolemia is caused by genetic mutations and environmental factors, resulting in elevated serum cholesterol. Familial hypercholesterolemia is an autosomal dominant condition inherited from a single gene mutation. Polygenic hypercholesterolemia is a common condition caused by multiple genetic variants. Only 40% of patients diagnosed with familial hypercholesterolemia are found to have a mutation in genes, suggesting that the remaining patients with severe hypercholesterolemic phenotypes likely have a polygenic etiology of hypercholesterolemia. The patients in this subset are diagnosed with polygenic hypercholesterolemia. Both familial hypercholesterolemia and polygenic hypercholesterolemia elevate serum plasma low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) levels and put patients at risk of early development of atherosclerotic cardiovascular diseases (ASCVDs). Although distinguishing polygenic hypercholesterolemia from familial hypercholesterolemia poses a diagnostic challenge, diagnosing and treating patients with polygenic hypercholesterolemia due to their increased risk of cardiovascular disease (CVD) is crucial. By doing so, the likelihood of complications from the disease can be reduced.
