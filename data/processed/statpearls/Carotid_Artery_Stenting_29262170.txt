# Carotid Artery Stenting

**Authors:** Ali Kimyaghalam, Donald T. Baril

**Source:** StatPearls - StatPearls Publishing

## Abstract

Traditionally, carotid endarterectomy (CEA) has been the primary treatment for high-grade asymptomatic and symptomatic carotid artery stenosis. A carotid endarterectomy involves exposure of the carotid artery and removal of plaque, most typically from the carotid bulb and the proximal internal carotid artery, via a neck incision. However, in vascular surgery, as in many other surgical specialties, minimally invasive techniques have evolved over the years. These techniques offer the advantages of smaller incisions, reduced postoperative pain, reduced risk of postoperative wound complications, and a shorter hospital length of stay. Carotid artery stenting (CAS) is one such technique that can be performed via a transfemoral or transcarotid approach.
