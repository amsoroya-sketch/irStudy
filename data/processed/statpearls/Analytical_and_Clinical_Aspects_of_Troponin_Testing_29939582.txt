# Analytical and Clinical Aspects of Troponin Testing

**Authors:** Muhammad Zubair, Sandeep Sharma

**Source:** StatPearls - StatPearls Publishing

## Abstract

Accurate diagnosis of cardiac emergencies is a critical responsibility of emergency physicians. The extensive differential diagnosis of chest pain must be narrowed rapidly and accurately to enable timely, lifesaving interventions. In addition to the history and physical examination, several diagnostic modalities assist in distinguishing among potential causes of chest pain. Measurement of cardiac troponins (cTns) has become an integral component of cardiac evaluation and diagnosis. CTns were introduced for clinical application in 1995 following the approval of the first cardiac troponin T (cTnT) assay. Subsequent advances in assay design demonstrated that enhanced cardiac specificity and markedly improved sensitivity increased the diagnostic accuracy and frequency of cardiovascular disease detection, particularly with the development of high-sensitivity cardiac troponin (hs-cTn) assays. Progressive refinement of troponin assays has significantly improved the speed and precision of acute myocardial infarction (AMI) detection. Fourth-generation assays identified substantial myocardial injury but exhibited limited early sensitivity. High-sensitivity 5th-generation assays enabled detection of minute troponin concentrations at single-digit nanogram-per-liter levels, facilitating earlier diagnosis and the implementation of accelerated rule-out protocols. However, increased analytical sensitivity resulted in measurable elevations in nonischemic conditions such as chronic kidney disease (CKD) and heart failure. Sixth-generation assays demonstrate further improvements in analytical precision and reproducibility, enhancing differentiation between acute ischemia and other etiologies of troponin elevation and optimizing timely clinical decision-making in emergency care.
