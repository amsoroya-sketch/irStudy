# Blistering Distal Dactylitis

**Authors:** Gopikrishnan Anjaneyan, Haitham M. Saleh, Feroze Kaliyadan

**Source:** StatPearls - StatPearls Publishing

## Abstract

Blistering distal dactylitis (BDD) is a localized bacterial infection manifesting as fluid-filled lesions over the fingers, especially in children. Classically, BDD is localized to the volar fat pad of the distal phalanx of the digits, presenting as a medium-to-large nontender blister filled with a thin, white fluid. Bacterial cultures from this fluid most commonly grow Group A beta-hemolytic Streptococcus.
