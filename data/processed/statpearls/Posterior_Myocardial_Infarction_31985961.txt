# Posterior Myocardial Infarction

**Authors:** Jenna M. Lizzo, Pirbhat Shams

**Source:** StatPearls - StatPearls Publishing

## Abstract

Myocardial infarction is a major cause of chest pain and is associated with substantial morbidity and mortality. Posterior wall myocardial infarction results from disruption of blood flow to the posterior myocardium, most often caused by occlusion of the left circumflex (LCx) artery or a dominant right coronary artery (RCA). The condition frequently co-occurs with inferior or inferolateral myocardial infarction. However, an isolated posterior wall myocardial infarction presents a significant diagnostic challenge. Diagnosis is often missed even when patients exhibit classic symptoms of myocardial infarction because electrocardiographic (ECG) findings can be subtle. A low threshold for additional evaluation with posterior leads should be maintained in patients with atypical or equivocal ECG changes, particularly ST depression in precordial leads V1 to V3. Prompt recognition is essential to guide reperfusion therapy and reduce the risk of adverse outcomes, including cardiogenic shock, arrhythmias, and death. Synthesized V7 to V9 leads may facilitate earlier diagnosis of isolated posterior wall ST-elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI) and improve prognosis.
