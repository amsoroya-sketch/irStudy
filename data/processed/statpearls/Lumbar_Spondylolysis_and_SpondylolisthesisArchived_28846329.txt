# Lumbar Spondylolysis and Spondylolisthesis(Archived)

**Authors:** Alan G. Shamrock, Chester J. Donnally III, Matthew A. Varacallo

**Source:** StatPearls - StatPearls Publishing

## Abstract

Spondylolysis refers to a posterior defect of the vertebral body occurring at the pars interarticularis. Typically, this defect results from trauma or chronic repetitive loading and hyperextension. If this instability leads to translation of the vertebral body, this is spondylolisthesis. This process requires either a fracture or deformation of the posterior spinal elements resulting in elongation of the pars interarticularis. This condition can potentially occur in all age groups, with the underlying cause varying based on age. If the slip progresses to the point of neurologic compromise, then surgical intervention may be necessary to decompress and stabilize affected segments. Absent any motor deficits, a nonoperative course of analgesia, activity modification, and injections should be the initial therapeutic approach for several months.
