# HIV-1–Associated Toxoplasmosis

**Authors:** Folusakin Ayoade, Andrew Stevenson Joel Chandranesan

**Source:** StatPearls - StatPearls Publishing

## Abstract

Toxoplasmosis is the most common central nervous system (CNS) infection in patients with human immunodeficiency virus/acquired immunodeficiency syndrome (HIV/AIDS) who are not on appropriate prophylaxis. The usual manifestation of cerebral toxoplasmosis is typically 1 or more CNS mass lesions. Other opportunistic conditions seen in AIDS that may present with mass-like lesions include primary CNS lymphoma (typically associated with the Epstein-Barr virus) and progressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy (PML), which is caused by John Cunningham polyomavirus.
