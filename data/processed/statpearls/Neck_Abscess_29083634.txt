# Neck Abscess

**Authors:** Dawlat Mohamed, Rachel H. McDowell, Ryan Winters, Matthew J. Hyser

**Source:** StatPearls - StatPearls Publishing

## Abstract

Infections of the deep neck spaces represent a potentially life-threatening clinical entity that can be challenging to diagnose, localize, and manage due to the complex anatomy and proximity of vital structures. The neck contains multiple fascial planes and potential spaces that communicate with the mediastinum and skull base, allowing infections to spread rapidly and unpredictably. These spaces encompass critical components of the airway, gastrointestinal tract, and major vascular and neural structures, making even small abscesses clinically significant. Because the affected tissues are often deep and not externally visible or palpable, diagnosis relies heavily on a high index of suspicion and appropriate imaging modalities such as contrast-enhanced computed tomography or magnetic resonance imaging. The source of infection determines not only the clinical presentation but also the microbiologic profile, route of spread, and potential complications, including airway obstruction, jugular vein thrombosis, mediastinitis, and septic embolization. Neck pain is the most common presenting symptom, but it carries a broad differential diagnosis that may delay definitive management. Odontogenic infections remain the leading etiology of deep neck abscesses; however, the specific teeth and fascial spaces involved markedly influence the clinical pattern, severity, and prognosis, as distinct anatomic pathways of spread dictate the course of disease. Other significant sources include tonsillar or pharyngeal infections, salivary gland infections, cervical spine pathology, and, less commonly, cutaneous, branchial cleft, or traumatic origins. Understanding the fascial anatomy and interconnections of the neck spaces is essential for accurate localization, timely surgical drainage, and targeted antimicrobial therapy. Early multidisciplinary collaboration, particularly between surgeons, infectious disease specialists, radiologists, and anesthesiologists, is crucial for optimizing airway management, preventing complications, and improving patient outcomes.
