# Opioid Anesthesia

**Authors:** Nicolas Ferry, Laura E. Hancock, Joseph Maxwell Hendrix, Sandeep T. Dhanjal

**Source:** StatPearls - StatPearls Publishing

## Abstract

Opioid anesthesia involves the use of opioid medications as primary or adjunct agents for pain control during surgical procedures and in the postoperative period. Despite the ongoing opioid crisis, particularly in the United States, opioids remain essential in anesthesia due to their potent analgesic properties. They are FDA-approved for use across all surgical phases—preinduction, induction, maintenance, and postoperative recovery—and can be administered via multiple routes, though intravenous delivery is most common. Frequently used agents include fentanyl, morphine, and hydromorphone, with more potent options like sufentanil and remifentanil reserved for specific intraoperative uses.
