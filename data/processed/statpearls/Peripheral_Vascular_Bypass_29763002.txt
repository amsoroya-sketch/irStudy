# Peripheral Vascular Bypass

**Authors:** Joshua S. Braganza, Ali Kimyaghalam, Shivik K. Patel

**Source:** StatPearls - StatPearls Publishing

## Abstract

Peripheral vascular bypass (PVB) is the surgical restoration of blood flow to an occluded or disrupted arterial segment, excluding vessels of the heart, brain, and lungs. First described in the 1940s with foundational contributions from Drs. Carrel, Kunlin, and LeRiche, PVB remains a cornerstone in the management of advanced peripheral arterial disease (PAD). While most PAD patients are asymptomatic and do not require intervention, those with claudication, rest pain, nonhealing wounds, or progressive disease may develop chronic limb-threatening ischemia (CLTI), a condition demanding timely revascularization. Evaluation begins with noninvasive testing—ultrasound, ankle-brachial indices (ABIs), and pulse volume recordings—followed by angiography to determine candidacy for endovascular therapy or the need for surgical bypass. Despite growth in minimally invasive options, surgical bypass continues to provide superior long-term patency and limb-salvage benefits in anatomically complex or endovascular-refractory disease. This activity offers a comprehensive, evidence-based overview of peripheral vascular bypass, integrating contemporary surgical techniques with current recommendations from the Society for Vascular Surgery (SVS) and the European Society for Vascular Surgery (ESVS). This activity examines patient selection, preoperative risk optimization, conduit and target-vessel considerations, intraoperative strategy, postoperative monitoring, and mechanisms of graft failure. Emphasis is placed on long-term surveillance and limb-preservation strategy, as well as the essential multidisciplinary collaboration among vascular surgeons, interventionalists, podiatrists, wound-care specialists, nurses, and rehabilitation professionals. Through this synthesis, clinicians will gain an advanced understanding of best practices guiding peripheral vascular bypass in modern vascular care, enabling more effective, evidence-driven, and patient-centered management.
