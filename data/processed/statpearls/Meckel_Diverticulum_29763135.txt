# Meckel Diverticulum

**Authors:** Jason An, Christopher P. Zabbo

**Source:** StatPearls - StatPearls Publishing

## Abstract

The incomplete obliteration of the omphalomesenteric duct in the developing embryo causes Meckel diverticulum. It is the most common congenital anomaly of the gastrointestinal tract. The incomplete obliteration of the duct results in a diverticulum in the small intestine (see Image. Meckel Diverticulum). Often, these are completely asymptomatic. Acid secretion from the ectopic gastric mucosa within the diverticulum can result in gastrointestinal bleeding and abdominal pain. It is useful to remember the rule of 2s. Meckel diverticulum occurs in 2% of the population; 2% are symptomatic, children are usually less than 2 years, affects males twice as often as females, is located 2 feet proximal to the ileocecal valve, is 2 inches long or less, and can have 2 types of the mucosal lining.
