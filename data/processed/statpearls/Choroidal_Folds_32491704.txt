# Choroidal Folds

**Authors:** Manit Agrawal, Koushik Tripathy

**Source:** StatPearls - StatPearls Publishing

## Abstract

Choroidal folds were first described by Nettleship in 1884 in a patient with papilloedema due to a space-occupying lesion. Choroidal folds appear as a series of subretinal alternating dark and bright lines, grooves, or striae. They are usually arranged parallelly in a horizontal fashion but can be vertical, oblique, circumferential, or irregular. They rarely extend beyond the equator. They may be unilateral or bilateral. The folds usually broaden with time, becoming smoother and whiter in appearance. In the early stages, they may be as narrow as small blood vessels but gradually become wider with smoother edges. When the neurosensory retina is also involved in the fold, it is called a chorioretinal fold.
