# Strangulation Injuries

**Authors:** Roberta J. Dunn, Kunal Sukhija, Richard A. Lopez

**Source:** StatPearls - StatPearls Publishing

## Abstract

Strangulation injuries encompass a diverse range of traumatic pathology resulting from mechanical force applied externally to the neck and surrounding structures. As a form of asphyxia, these injuries can impair cerebral oxygen delivery through compression of cervical blood vessels or tracheal occlusion. Death occurs rapidly unless the compressing force is relieved. Relying solely on external signs may lead to missed diagnoses of underlying damage. While “choking” is often used colloquially to describe strangulation, the term more accurately refers to internal or partial occlusion of the proximal airway in medical contexts. Individuals who experience strangulation injuries are at high risk for various biopsychosocial sequelae. A significant proportion of these injuries result from domestic and other forms of violence. Strangulation is also a common injury pattern in completed and attempted suicide. History and toxicological analysis may reveal the presence of various substances, either intentionally ingested or otherwise. Strangulation injuries can also arise from both consensual and nonconsensual sexual activity. Additionally, martial arts, combat sports, military training, and law enforcement actions may involve external neck compression through specific holds or detainment maneuvers. Pediatric populations are particularly vulnerable to strangulation injuries due to both anatomical differences and psychosocial factors that increase the risk of long-term morbidity and mortality in survivors. External influences, including social media, have been identified as contributing stressors leading to suicide attempts among adolescents. Effective identification, diagnosis, and treatment require an interprofessional approach. While medical evaluation and treatment are initiated, concurrent involvement of mental health professionals, social workers, and law enforcement, when appropriate based on jurisdiction, is essential. Additional personnel and tailored interventions may be necessary depending on the specific injury pattern and psychosocial context. In cases where strangulation results in death, forensic evaluation carries significant legal implications.
