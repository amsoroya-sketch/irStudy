# Mohs Micrographic Surgery: Chemoprophylaxis Options for Nicotinamide and Oral Retinoids

**Authors:** Abigail L. Hunter, Rene Bermudez

**Source:** StatPearls - StatPearls Publishing

## Abstract

The Mohs surgeon should be familiar with nonsurgical options for treating nonmelanoma skin cancers (NMSCs) and medical options for preventing the formation of new skin cancers. Patients not well-suited for cutaneous surgery due to their overall clinical health can be offered alternative treatment modalities such as topical or systemic chemotherapy or radiation. Patients with extensive sun exposure over the years, certain genodermatoses, chronic radiation exposure, and immunosuppression are at a greater risk of developing NMSCs than those without these risk factors. In patients with increased risk factors, chemoprophylaxis, or agents used to slow the growth of NMSCs, can be utilized to prevent the formation of NMSCs and, subsequently, the associated morbidity and mortality. Basal cell carcinoma (BCC) is the most prevalent NMSC in the United States, with an annual incidence of approximately 2 million. Although death from BCC is rare, the tumors can be associated with significant morbidity and cost. The second most common NMSC, squamous cell carcinoma (SCC), has an annual incidence of approximately 700,000 cases in the United States, of which approximately 2% result in death. In addition to preventing the morbidity and mortality associated with these NMSCs, evidence suggests that preventive measures, such as chemoprophylaxis, can save the healthcare system money. A 2015 study analyzed the economic burden of skin cancer treatments and found that the average annual cost of these treatments increased from $3.6 billion in 2002 to 2006 and from $ 8.1 billion in 2007 to 2011. Preventing new NMSC formation can reduce healthcare expenditures and improve patient outcomes, including morbidity and mortality. Nicotinamide and oral retinoids are 2 systemic chemopreventive medications used in populations at high risk of developing NMSCs. A survey sent to the American College of Mohs Surgeons found that 76.9% of the respondents recommend nicotinamide to their patients for NMSC prevention. These 2 medications are essential tools the Mohs surgeon can utilize to decrease morbidity and mortality in patients with multiple NMSCs.
