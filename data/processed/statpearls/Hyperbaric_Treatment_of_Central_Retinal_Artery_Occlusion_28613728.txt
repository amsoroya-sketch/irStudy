# Hyperbaric Treatment of Central Retinal Artery Occlusion

**Authors:** Stephen Hendriksen, Jeffrey S. Cooper

**Source:** StatPearls - StatPearls Publishing

## Abstract

Central retinal artery occlusion (CRAO) is a relatively rare emergency and potentially devastating eye condition. Patients present with sudden, painless monocular vision loss. The prognosis for visual recovery is poor, and up until recently, no treatment could be offered to restore at least some functional vision to the affected eye. The retina has the highest oxygen consumption rate of any organ in the body at 13 mL/100 gm per minute. Retinal tissue is not tolerant of hypoxia. Until 2006, when hyperbaric oxygen therapy was approved for central retinal artery occlusion treatment, ophthalmologists had no treatment options for these patients. It is still a vastly underutilized modality, partially due to a lack of availability and knowledge in the ophthalmology community.
