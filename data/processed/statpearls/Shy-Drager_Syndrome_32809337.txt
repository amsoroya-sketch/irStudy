# Shy-Drager Syndrome

**Authors:** Christopher Fecek, Shivaraj Nagalli

**Source:** StatPearls - StatPearls Publishing

## Abstract

Shy Drager syndrome was originally classified in 1960 as a neurological disorder that resulted in orthostatic hypotension of unknown etiology. Since then, its naming convention and the understanding of its etiology have undergone several revisions. In a 1998 consensus statement, the term "Shy Drager syndrome" was formally taken out of use and placed under the categorization of "multiple system atrophy" (MSA). Multiple system atrophy has been further categorized into MSA-P and MSA-C, depending on whether parkinsonian or cerebellar features predominate, respectively. As indicated in its name, the anatomical hallmark of MSA is atrophy. Specifically, the cerebellum, pons, and putamen are the primary areas of brain involvement. MSA-P and MSA-C differ in the amount of involvement in each of these areas of the brain. In the spinal column, both MSA-P and MSA-C include the intermediolateral area of the spinal cord in addition to Onuf's nucleus and the vagal nucleus.
