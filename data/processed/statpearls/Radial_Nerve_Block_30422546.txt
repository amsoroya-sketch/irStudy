# Radial Nerve Block

**Authors:** Muhammad I. Durrani, Samaresh Dasgupta

**Source:** StatPearls - StatPearls Publishing

## Abstract

Radial nerve anesthesia rests upon disrupting impulse conduction in the nerve with drugs such as local anesthetics that can be given locally to block specific nerves and can be reversed as needed (see Table. Local Anesthetic Duration and Maximum Dosage). Disrupting nerve impulses and conduction can be done in any body region where nerves are easily accessible for visualization and injection. A radial nerve block provides anesthesia or analgesia to the hand, specifically the dorsal radial side. This includes anesthesia dorsally to the thumb, index and middle fingers, and the lateral aspect of the ring finger. It can be useful as a stand-alone or adjunctive therapy for interventions of the hands and fingers, palliation of acute pain involving the radial nerve's distribution, diagnosis and treatment of radial tunnel syndrome, and a diagnostic prognostication procedure in instances of injury to the radial nerve. This block is especially useful for the clinician as an alternative to procedural sedation and escalating opioid analgesic requirements for acutely painful conditions such as fractures, lacerations, and burns.
