# Pit and Fissure Sealants

**Authors:** Athira Sreedevi, Melina Brizuela, Shamaz Mohamed

**Source:** StatPearls - StatPearls Publishing

## Abstract

Pits and fissures on the occlusal surfaces of posterior teeth are significantly more susceptible to caries than smooth surfaces. This vulnerability is due to their intricate morphology, which impairs effective cleaning and encourages plaque accumulation. Additionally, enamel in these areas receives less protection from topical fluorides compared to smooth surfaces, further increasing their caries risk. Pit and fissure sealants act as a protective physical barrier, preventing the accumulation of food particles and microorganisms. Their use has proven effective in preventing the initiation of caries and arresting early noncavitated lesions. Among all teeth, permanent first molars exhibit the highest prevalence of caries, followed by second molars. These teeth are especially vulnerable because caries often begin shortly after eruption, and they are the first permanent molars to erupt. The long-term effectiveness of sealants relies heavily on their retention and correct application.
