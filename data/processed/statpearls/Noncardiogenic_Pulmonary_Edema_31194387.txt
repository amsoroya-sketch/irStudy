# Noncardiogenic Pulmonary Edema

**Authors:** Nicholas Waters, Sunil Munakomi

**Source:** StatPearls - StatPearls Publishing

## Abstract

Pulmonary edema is caused by fluid accumulation in the lung parenchyma. This impairs gas exchange and leads to rapid deterioration in respiratory status, which can result in respiratory failure if unaddressed. The condition has multiple etiologies, which can be separated into cardiogenic and noncardiogenic causes. Noncardiogenic pulmonary edema is an inflammatory process that increases pulmonary capillary permeability, allowing fluid to move into the pulmonary and interstitial spaces, disrupting gas exchange and lung function. Conversely, cardiogenic pulmonary edema results from left-sided congestive heart failure, leading to elevated pulmonary venous pressures. The distinction can be made by history, physical examination, and pulmonary capillary wedge pressure measurement, which is elevated (>18 mm Hg) in cardiogenic but not noncardiogenic pulmonary edema. Pulmonary edema requires prompt recognition and management tailored to the underlying cause.
