# Nuclear Medicine Stress Test

**Authors:** Shwetha Gopal, Thomas F. Heston, Christie Murphy

**Source:** StatPearls - StatPearls Publishing

## Abstract

Coronary artery disease (CAD) remains the leading cause of death in the United States, accounting for approximately 840,768 deaths in 2016. Globally, cardiovascular disease is the leading cause of mortality, responsible for an estimated 17.6 million deaths each year. Cardiac stress testing has a critical role in the diagnosis of known or suspected CAD. In the United States, approximately 8 million nuclear stress tests are performed annually. Please see StatPearls' companion resource, "Pharmacologic Stress Testing," for more information. Although stress testing can be conducted using various methods, exercise electrocardiography (ECG) and exercise or pharmacologic stress testing combined with imaging—such as echocardiography or radionuclide myocardial perfusion imaging (rMPI)—are the most commonly performed and widely accessible. These tests aid in diagnosing CAD, risk stratification, and guiding therapy. An intravenous radioactive tracer is administered, and gamma photon emissions are detected using single-photon emission computed tomography (SPECT) or positron emission tomography (PET). Myocardial perfusion images are acquired at rest and following stress—induced either by exercise or, preferably, pharmacological vasodilators. The 2 sets of images are then compared to assess myocardial perfusion, viability, and global left ventricular systolic function, providing insight into the presence and extent of CAD.
