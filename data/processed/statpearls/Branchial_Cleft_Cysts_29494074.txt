# Branchial Cleft Cysts

**Authors:** Andrew E. Sutton, Julie Goldman

**Source:** StatPearls - StatPearls Publishing

## Abstract

Branchial cleft anomalies can develop at any age and are generally benign, though they often resemble other medical conditions. These abnormal structures typically present as neck masses that fluctuate in size, worsening with upper respiratory tract infections and improving during recovery. Most cases involve 2nd branchial cleft cysts, which appear as nontender, fluctuant masses along the anterior border of the sternocleidomastoid (SCM), sometimes posing a diagnostic challenge for clinicians and pathologists. These cysts result from the incomplete obliteration of branchial sinuses during embryonic development. While many remain asymptomatic, some become infected or form abscesses, leading to enlargement, pain, and drainage. Diagnosis relies on clinical suspicion, as no specific tests or imaging studies are universally recommended. Definitive treatment involves elective surgical excision, though urgent intervention may be required in cases of airway compromise. In some instances, an endoscope-assisted approach may be preferred. With appropriate management, most patients recover without complications. Rarely, branchiogenic carcinoma has been reported within branchial cleft cysts, though it remains unclear whether these cases represent true malignancies or cystic lymph node metastases.
