**Authors:** Emily H. Garmon, Joseph Maxwell Hendrix, Martin R. Huecker

**Source:** StatPearls - StatPearls Publishing

## Abstract

Precise pain control during medical procedures represents a significant advancement in modern medicine, with local anesthetics serving as cornerstone agents in clinical practice. These agents achieve targeted nerve blockade by selectively blocking voltage-gated sodium channels while preserving consciousness and cognitive function. A comprehensive understanding of nerve impulse transmission and the mechanism by which local anesthetics interrupt this process is crucial for clinicians across various specialties. Local anesthetics exhibit a hierarchical pattern of neural blockade, starting with small autonomic fibers, followed by sensory pathways, and ultimately affecting motor function at higher concentrations. This sequential blockade allows clinicians to achieve precise levels of anesthesia tailored to the needs of different procedures—from simple dermal interventions to complex regional nerve blocks. The versatility of these agents extends across various administration routes, including topical, infiltrative, and regional methods. Each route requires specific considerations to ensure optimal efficacy and safety while administering anesthesia to patients.
