# Calcium Deposition and Other Renal Crystal Diseases

**Authors:** Prathap Kumar Simhadri, Stephen W. Leslie

**Source:** StatPearls - StatPearls Publishing

## Abstract

Crystals are groups of ions, atoms, and molecules that can form into homogeneous solids and have a fixed distance between the constituent parts, creating an orderly, repeating 3-dimensional pattern. The molecular components of crystals are filtered from the plasma through the kidneys and concentrated in the renal tubules. The kidneys are a primary site of crystalline deposition, receiving 25% of total cardiac output. These deposits primarily occur in the renal tubules, interstitium, or both. They are associated with the development of acute kidney injury (AKI) or progressive chronic kidney disease (CKD), which can present with microscopic hematuria, crystalluria, and sub-nephrotic range proteinuria.
