# Niacin Deficiency

**Authors:** Sasa Redzic, Muhammad F. Hashmi, Vikas Gupta

**Source:** StatPearls - StatPearls Publishing

## Abstract

Niacin or vitamin B3 are generic terms for nicotinic acid and nicotinamide (niacinamide). Niacin was initially referred to as the anti-black tongue factor due to niacin's effect on dogs. In humans, niacin was discovered through the niacin deficiency condition pellagra. In the 1700s, pellagra first appeared in Italy, and the name translates into "pella," meaning skin, and "agra," meaning rough. In the early 1900s, pellagra was prevalent in the Southern United States due to the low availability of corn, the primary dietary source of niacin. In 1937, Elvehjem and his colleagues isolated the vitamin and demonstrated that pure nicotinic acid and nicotinic acid amide would reverse the black tongue and pellagra. Today, niacin deficiencies are uncommon in industrialized nations primarily due to sufficient dietary intake; however, specific populations remain at risk of this mostly eradicated condition. Previously nutritional deficiency was the commonest cause of pellagra in the world. However, this is no longer true in developed countries, where it has been nearly eradicated as a manifestation of primary nutritional deficiency. Instead, it now presents a consequence of chronic alcoholism, malabsorption syndrome, adverse drug effects, and anorexia nervosa.
