# HIV-Related Endocrinopathies

**Authors:** Sri Ram Teja T. Sathi, Catherine Anastasopoulou

**Source:** StatPearls - StatPearls Publishing

## Abstract

An estimated 37.9 million people were living with HIV at the end of 2018, of which 24.7 million could access anti-retroviral therapy (ART). While HIV was once considered a dreadful infection, the introduction of effective ART has transformed HIV into a chronic disease. Reducing HIV-related mortality with ART has meant an increased incidence of common health issues and conditions associated with aging, many of which have an endocrine basis. Individuals infected with HIV exhibit endocrine system dysfunction, ranging from subtle alterations in laboratory values without symptoms to overt glandular failure. Virtually every endocrine organ system can be involved. Although many of these changes can be seen with any significant systemic disease, some changes appear specific to HIV infection. During the early AIDS pandemic, endocrinopathic manifestations of HIV infection were mostly secondary to opportunistic infections and HIV-related neoplasms directly involving endocrine organs. However, with the widespread use of ART and the associated reduction in the incidence of the late manifestations of AIDS, much attention has been paid to endocrine dysfunction due to the direct effects of HIV infection, immune reconstitution, and the use of ART.
