# The Current Role of Medical Simulation in Psychiatry

**Authors:** Paroma Mitra, Dimy Fluyau

**Source:** StatPearls - StatPearls Publishing

## Abstract

Worldwide, medical education went through significant changes due to concerns for the safety of patients. Medical errors result in as many as 98000 deaths per year in the United States of America. Changes in instructional methods have resulted in ingenious medical curricula, and one of the changes was the universally accepted proficiency in clinical skills, which constitute an essential learning outcome. The challenge for medical undergraduates is the application of theoretical knowledge to the management of patients. Simulation-based learning becomes a necessary tool as a new teaching method for undergraduate and postgraduate education. Simulation of real-life experiences develops skills for better communication between members, rapid thinking, able to cope with new crises medical situations to make medical decisions on the spur of the moment. Simulation can take part in many settings, including a medical floor, classroom, or even online. Required specific skills and attitudes are part of the training in psychiatry. These skills and approaches must be experienced and not merely memorized. Medical simulation in psychiatry via appropriate training may be particularly adaptable to this situation.
