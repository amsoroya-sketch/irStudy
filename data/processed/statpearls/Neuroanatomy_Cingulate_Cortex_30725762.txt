## Abstract

The cingulate cortex is a fascinating area of the human brain that has been the subject of neuroanatomical and therapeutic investigation owing to its role in various functions and pathologies. It is a paired structure located on the medial surface of the cerebral hemisphere. It is perhaps best known as part of the limbic system (see Image. Principal Systems of Association Fibers in the Cerebrum). It is involved in many vital neural circuits, including the reward centers, amygdala, lateral prefrontal cortex, parietal cortex, motor areas, spinal cord, hippocampus, and limbic regions. Although previously viewed as a mysterious territory of the brain, evidence from cognitive and fMRI studies has established a better understanding of the cingulate cortex in the last decade. Its functional role continues to be heavily studied to this day. The following sections provide further insight into the cingulate cortex's structure, function, and clinical and surgical significance.
