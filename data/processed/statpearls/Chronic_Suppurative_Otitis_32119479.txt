# Chronic Suppurative Otitis

**Authors:** Digna C. Rosario, Magda D. Mendez

**Source:** StatPearls - StatPearls Publishing

## Abstract

Otitis media is a common cause of fever in the pediatric population. Chronic suppurative otitis media, also known as chronic otitis media, is a stage of ear disease characterized by an ongoing infection of the middle ear without an intact tympanic membrane. This disease is a chronic inflammation of the middle ear and mastoid cavity. The characteristic presentation is chronic or persistent otorrhoea over 2 to 6 weeks through a perforated tympanic membrane. The Eustachian tube plays an important role in this disease, and dysfunction of this tube is found in 70% of patients undergoing middle ear surgery. When the Eustachian tube is dysfunctional, pressure equilibration in the middle ear is impaired, leading to disturbed middle ear aeration and the classic symptoms of chronic suppurative otitis media. Acquired hearing loss is also characteristically found in patients with this condition and, if left untreated, can lead to further morbidity and mortality.
