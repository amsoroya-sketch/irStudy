# Chondroid Syringoma

**Authors:** Abigail L. Meckley, Angelia Stepien

**Source:** StatPearls - StatPearls Publishing

## Abstract

Chondroid syringoma, also known as a mixed tumor of the skin, is a rare, typically benign neoplasm that arises from the sweat glands in the skin. These tumors predominantly occur in the head and neck region, especially on the nose, cheeks, and upper lip, though they can also be found on the extremities. In 1892, Nasse described the histopathologic components of both epithelial and mesenchymal elements, resembling the structure of mixed tumors in other tissues, such as pleomorphic adenoma of the salivary gland. The term chondroid syringoma was suggested by Hirsch and Helwig in 1961. Chondroid syringomas generally grow slowly and present as painless, firm nodules. While most are benign, there have been rare cases of malignancy. Treatment typically involves surgical excision, and the prognosis is generally good following complete removal of the tumor.
