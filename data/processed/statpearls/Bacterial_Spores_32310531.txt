# Bacterial Spores

**Authors:** Marina Basta, Pavan Annamaraju

**Source:** StatPearls - StatPearls Publishing

## Abstract

Bacterial species have different coping mechanisms for selective harsh environmental conditions. One of the most common coping mechanisms is forming spores to protect themselves against ecological degrading agents. Bacterial spores are the most dormant form of bacteria since they exhibit minimal metabolism, respiration, and reduced enzyme production. Typically, Gram-positive bacteria are best known for producing intracellular spores called endospores as a survival mechanism. Endospores are highly retractile and thick-walled structures formed inside bacterial cells. It is most common for Bacillus species and Clostridium species to create endospores. B. cereus is a member of the Bacillus species and is well-known for its ability to cause foodborne illness due to its spores surviving various temperatures. Similarly, C. perfringens spores are acid-soluble proteins that resist chemicals and heat.
