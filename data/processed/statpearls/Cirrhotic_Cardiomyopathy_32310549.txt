# Cirrhotic Cardiomyopathy

**Authors:** Lauren A. Lyssy, Michael P. Soos

**Source:** StatPearls - StatPearls Publishing

## Abstract

Cirrhotic cardiomyopathy (CCM) is a significant complication in patients with cirrhosis and is characterized by systolic and diastolic dysfunction and structural changes such as chamber enlargement in the absence of preexisting heart disease. The term "CCM" was first introduced in 1989. CCM is characterized by an impaired cardiac response to stress, a hyperdynamic circulatory state, diastolic and systolic dysfunction, and various electrophysiological abnormalities, most notably QT prolongation. This condition affects up to 50% of patients with cirrhosis, regardless of etiology. Alcohol use disorder and hemochromatosis are additional risk factors for developing cardiac disease. As CCM manifests under stress, it remains an underrecognized condition with no standardized treatment regimen established. Significant improvement, or even reversal, of CCM has been noted in patients after liver transplantation.
