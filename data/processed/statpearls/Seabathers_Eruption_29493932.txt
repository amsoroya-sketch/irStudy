# Seabathers Eruption

**Authors:** Joseph Prohaska, Phuong Daniels, Alexa Lum, Alexandra Taylor

**Source:** StatPearls - StatPearls Publishing

## Abstract

Seabather’s eruption (SBE), also referred to as “sea lice,” is a pruritic dermatitis that occurs in a bathing suit distribution and at sites of friction following ocean exposure. The eruption is due to 2 saltwater cnidarian species: the thimble jellyfish (Linuche unguiculata) and the sea anemone (Edwardsiella lineata). Both organisms are sufficiently small to become entrapped beneath swimwear. Pressure and exposure to freshwater trigger the discharge of nematocysts, specialized stinging organelles. Nematocysts release antigenic toxins that induce a host immune response. Larval-stage nematocysts of L unguiculata and E lineata elicit both types I and IV hypersensitivity reactions, producing the scattered pruritic red macules characteristic of SBE. L unguiculata has been most commonly reported along the southeastern coast of the U.S., the Gulf of Mexico, and the Caribbean, with additional reports from Brazil and Papua New Guinea. E lineata has been implicated in SBE along the U.S. East Coast, from the mid-Atlantic to New York. Evidence suggests that other life stages of L unguiculata may also contribute to SBE. Incidence is highest during spring and summer, particularly in May and June. Children younger than 15 years are at elevated risk, likely due to increased exposure to the ocean. Surfers also exhibit an increased susceptibility, with lesions frequently occurring in areas of friction such as the chest, axilla, and abdomen. A prior history of SBE predisposes individuals to recurrent, more severe presentations.
