# Lisch Nodules

**Authors:** Vijayalakshmi A. Senthilkumar, Koushik Tripathy

**Source:** StatPearls - StatPearls Publishing

## Abstract

Waardenburg, in 1918 first described the pigmented iris hamartomas. Karl Lisch, an Austrian ophthalmologist, reported the association of these iris hamartomas with neurofibromatosis type 1 (NF1) in 1937. The term Lisch nodule was used first by Riccardi in 1981 in a formal publication. Lisch nodules (LNs) are the melanocytic hamartomas found in 90% to 100% of adults with NF1 and are the most consistent features of NF1. A German pathologist named Friedrich Daniel Von Recklinghausen identified NF1 as a genetic disease. NF1 considered a neurocristopathy, is an autosomal dominant disorder with a high mutation rate characterized by pathological hamartomas proliferation of neural crest-derived tissues. LNs, as such, do not cause any visual disturbances, but the associated life-threatening as well as vision-threatening conditions, merit discussion.
