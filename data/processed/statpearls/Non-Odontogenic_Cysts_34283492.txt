# Non-Odontogenic Cysts

**Authors:** Reed McKinney, Caitlyn Menicucci, Melina Brizuela

**Source:** StatPearls - StatPearls Publishing

## Abstract

Nonodontogenic cysts of the oral cavity constitute a heterogeneous group of epithelium-lined lesions arising from tissues not involved in tooth formation. Many of these growths are asymptomatic and identified incidentally during routine clinical or radiographic evaluation. However, presentation, histopathology, and management vary considerably. Most nonodontogenic cysts are developmental, arising from persistent remnants of embryonic epithelial structures that later contribute to cyst formation. Although typically benign, these lesions warrant clinical recognition. The masses can resemble odontogenic cysts, inflammatory swellings, or neoplasms, and misdiagnosis may result in inappropriate intervention or delayed identification of pathology. Early and accurate detection is essential to guide management and inform patient counseling. This activity addresses 5 clinically significant nonodontogenic cysts: the nasopalatine duct cyst, nasolabial cyst, palatal cyst of the neonate, oral lymphoepithelial cyst, and epidermoid cyst. The epidemiology, pathogenesis, clinical features, diagnostic evaluation, and management strategies are presented, emphasizing precise diagnosis for optimal patient outcomes.
