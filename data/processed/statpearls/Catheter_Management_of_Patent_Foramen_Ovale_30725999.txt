# Catheter Management of Patent Foramen Ovale

**Authors:** Kristen N. Brown, Bishoy ElBebawy, Ashish H. Shah, Arun Kanmanthareddy

**Source:** StatPearls - StatPearls Publishing

## Abstract

Patent foramen ovale (PFO) is a small opening between the top 2 chambers of the heart (see Image. Patent Foramen Ovale). However, it is one of the most common cardiac defects. Several large, population-based studies estimated no differences in incidence (at about 9.2%) between patients with stroke and patients without a stroke. Patent foramen ovale is present in one-fifth of the population. They affect males and females equally. The size grows with age, whereas the prevalence diminishes with increasing age.  PFO has correlations with both cardiac and extra-cardiac disorders. Its strongest association seen in the literature is with cryptogenic stroke. The PFO provides a conduit for emboli to shunt from right to left. Most present incidentally on echocardiogram following stroke workup. The contribution of the PFO to the burden of cryptogenic stroke is well-characterized. Several large, population-based studies estimate that transcatheter closure of PFO in addition to medical therapy is superior to medical therapy alone in preventing recurrent cryptogenic stroke; it bears mention that PFO closure in these patients has not demonstrated a reduction in the risk of recurrent TIA or all-cause mortality. Studies have found a higher rate of postoperative atrial fibrillation following PFO closure device placement, the long-term effects of which have yet to be studied.  Data exists showing that PFO closure for cryptogenic stroke can be cost-effective.  One study demonstrated that the effectiveness of PFO closure depends on the device used. The gold standard for diagnosis of PFO remains a transoesophageal echocardiogram with a bubble test after a sustained Valsalva maneuver (see Video. Transesophageal Echocardiogram). Once identified, the structure can undergo an assessment to determine the need for treatment. There are surgical, transcatheter, and medical therapies available for treatment. The percutaneous transcatheter approach is becoming more favored, as most patients prefer the less invasive procedure.
