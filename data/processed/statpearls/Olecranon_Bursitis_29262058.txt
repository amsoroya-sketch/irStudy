# Olecranon Bursitis

**Authors:** Jon Pangia, Muhammad Taqi, Tara J. Rizvi

**Source:** StatPearls - StatPearls Publishing

## Abstract

Olecranon bursitis is inflammation of the olecranon bursa, a synovial-lined sac located over the dorsal aspect of the proximal ulna (olecranon process) at the elbow. This bursae is one of the most common type to become inflamed, presenting as swelling, pain, and sometimes erythema over the posterior elbow. Olecranon bursitis has long been recognized as a frequent cause of elbow swelling, especially in individuals with repetitive elbow trauma or pressure, such as manual laborers and athletes. The condition can be acute or chronic, and is classified as either septic (infectious) or aseptic (noninfectious). Septic cases are most often caused by Staphylococcus aureus and are associated with trauma or skin breaks. The annual incidence is estimated at 10 to 29 per 100,000, with higher rates in populations exposed to repetitive elbow trauma, such as military recruits. The olecranon bursa is a thin, fluid-filled sac situated between the skin and the olecranon process of the ulna. The bursa's primary function is to reduce friction between the skin and the underlying bone during elbow movement. The bursa is superficial, making it susceptible to direct trauma and infection. Olecranon bursitis most commonly results from microtrauma or repetitive pressure on the elbow, leading to inflammation and fluid accumulation. Other mechanisms include acute trauma, infection (septic bursitis), crystal deposition (eg, gout), hemorrhage, and systemic inflammatory conditions. Risk factors include men, older age, elevated body mass index, hyperlipidemia, and smoking. Septic bursitis typically follows a skin break, allowing bacteria to enter the bursa, whereas aseptic bursitis is usually related to mechanical irritation or underlying systemic disease.
