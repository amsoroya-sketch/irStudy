# Bagassosis

**Authors:** Alpha Madu, Tariq Sharman

**Source:** StatPearls - StatPearls Publishing

## Abstract

Bagassosis belongs to the group of respiratory conditions classified as interstitial lung diseases or hypersensitivity pneumonitis. This condition presents similarly to other forms of extrinsic allergic alveolitis, eg, farmer's lung.  Bagassosis develops as a result of exposure and inhalation of bagasse, the residual fibrous material following sugar extraction from sugar cane. This seemingly wasted product of sugar production has now found use in manufacturing other end-products, including building materials, biofuel, pulp, paper, furniture, brick, ship, automobiles, and fertilizers. Historically, bagassosis was first described by Jamison and Hopkins in 1941, following its initial report in a hardboard factory in New Orleans. Subsequently, other reports followed from other American states (mainly in the South) and other countries, including Italy, India, and the Philippines. Castleden and Hamilton-Paterson were the first to use the term "bagassosis" after observing it in 4 factory workers in London in 1942.  In the past, bagasse was processed manually in a time-consuming process before the technological revolution of the 1950s and '60s. However, the machine processing of bagasse led to the dispersal of particle-laden dust around the machines. Soon after introducing these machines, the first cases of bagassosis were reported, predictably in those close to the machines, eg, the workers who fed the machines and the engineers who supervised them. Other workers, including those who handled the finished products, were not affected.
