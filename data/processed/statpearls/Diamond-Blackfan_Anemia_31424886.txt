# Diamond-Blackfan Anemia

**Authors:** Robert B. Killeen, Christina Wills

**Source:** StatPearls - StatPearls Publishing

## Abstract

Anemia refers to a hemoglobin level below the normal range for a given age group. Classification typically falls into 3 categories based on the mean corpuscular volume (MCV) of red blood cells. Iron deficiency anemia remains the most prevalent type across all age groups worldwide. In contrast, a rare anemia with a genetic origin—Diamond-Blackfan anemia is a congenital condition that features pure red cell aplasia and frequently accompanies congenital skeletal abnormalities. Diamond-Blackfan anemia presents as a chronic anemia, typically characterized by either macrocytic or normocytic features. In approximately 40% to 45% of cases, inheritance follows an autosomal dominant pattern, while 55% to 60% arise sporadically (see Image. Diamond-Blackfan Anemia). A smaller subset of cases involves autosomal recessive inheritance. Although possible, the occurrence of hydrops remains exceedingly rare.
