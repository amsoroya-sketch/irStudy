# Intrauterine Device Placement and Removal

**Authors:** Emily L. Lanzola, Mary Auber, Kari Ketvertis

**Source:** StatPearls - StatPearls Publishing

## Abstract

The intrauterine device (IUD) is one of the most effective contraception options available today, with failure rates as low as certain sterilization methods. In the United States, 2 types of IUDs currently available—the copper-containing IUD and levonorgestrel-containing IUD, which have similar rates of preventing pregnancy, with failure rates of 0.08% and 0.02%, respectively. These devices are more than 99% effective in preventing pregnancy. Additional benefits of IUDs include efficacy, ease of use, reversible nature, and patient satisfaction, especially for women seeking long-term use and cost-effectiveness.  In the United States, the use of long-acting reversible contraception (LARC) has increased since 1995. This use has continued to rise annually, with 14% of females using contraception opting for LARC methods. With the increased use of LARC, there has been a significant decrease in the number of unplanned pregnancies.
