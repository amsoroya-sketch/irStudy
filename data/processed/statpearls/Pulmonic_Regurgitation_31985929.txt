# Pulmonic Regurgitation

**Authors:** Venkata Satish Pendela, Rania Ayyad

**Source:** StatPearls - StatPearls Publishing

## Abstract

The pulmonary valve is located at the junction of the distal end of the right ventricular outflow tract and the pulmonary artery and comprises 3 semilunar leaflets, which are of equal dimensions. These leaflets are joined by 3 commissures, the attachments between the pulmonic wall and the leaflets. The pulmonic valve is not attached to papillary muscles, as seen with atrioventricular valves. The microscopic structure of the leaflets consists of 5 layers from the ventricular end to the arterial end called lamina ventricular, lamina radialis, lamina spongiosa, lamina fibrosa, and lamina arterial. The pulmonic valves help deliver deoxygenated blood from the right ventricle to the lung vasculature during systole when they open completely. They close completely during diastole to prevent regurgitant flow. By far, the most common cause of pulmonic regurgitation is the dilation of the valve ring.
