# Lateral Patellar Compression Syndrome

**Authors:** Amin Abukar, Daniel Li

**Source:** StatPearls - StatPearls Publishing

## Abstract

Lateral patellar compression syndrome (LPCS), also known as excessive lateral pressure syndrome, is a distinct cause of anterior knee pain characterized by an abnormally high pressure on the lateral patellofemoral joint without patellar instability. This condition occurs when a tight lateral retinaculum and related soft tissues tether the patella laterally, causing chronic overload of the lateral patellar facet. Patients typically have anterior knee pain that worsens with activities, such as stair climbing, squatting, or prolonged sitting (the "theater sign"). Unlike patellar instability, LPCS involves a stable patella (no true subluxation or dislocation episodes), but with painful maltracking due to lateral tilt. Over time, this increased lateral pressure can lead to cartilage wear (chondromalacia) on the lateral patellar facet and, if unaddressed, to patellofemoral osteoarthritis. Early recognition of LPCS is important, as targeted conservative management can often relieve symptoms and prevent progression to irreversible cartilage damage. This review provides an in-depth, clinically oriented overview of LPCS for orthopedic surgeons, with emphasis on pathophysiology, diagnosis, and management strategies tailored to subspecialty practice.
