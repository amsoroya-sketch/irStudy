# Mediastinal Trauma

**Authors:** Leila Sabbagh, Reza Askari, Sharven Taghavi

**Source:** StatPearls - StatPearls Publishing

## Abstract

Mediastinal trauma refers to an injury involving the central compartment of the thoracic cavity, which houses several critical anatomical structures. The mediastinum is often categorized into 3 clinically relevant sections: anterior, middle, and posterior. The anterior contains the thymus and internal thoracic branches surrounded by connective and adipose tissue that cushions the vital cardiac structures underlying it in the middle mediastinum. The middle contains the pericardium, heart, ascending aorta, and great vessels, and trachea/main bronchi. The posterior mediastinum contains the esophagus, descending aorta, intrathoracic vena cava, thoracic duct, and azygos/hemiazygos veins. In trauma surgery, particular attention to mediastinal injury should be paid for penetrating injuries to the “cardiac box,” whose anatomical borders are the midclavicular lines laterally, the sternal notch superiorly, and the tip of the xiphoid inferiorly. Given the importance of these structures and their proximity, traumatic injury to this area can be immediately life-threatening. A large portion of individuals with trauma who experience mediastinal injuries may not make it to the hospital alive, and survival is contingent upon minimizing he time from injury to treatment. In the past, mandatory operative exploration was the primary practice. However, all patients should be treated according to the principles of Advanced Trauma Life Support (ATLS). With advancements in modern diagnostic and radiologic modalities (such as combined arteriography, esophagoscopy, bronchoscopy, and echocardiography), unnecessary explorations can now be avoided.
